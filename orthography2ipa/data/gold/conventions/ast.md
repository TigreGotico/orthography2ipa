# Transcription conventions: Asturian (ast)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, including stressed monosyllables. Unstressed words (see function_words) get no mark. Placement follows the spelling: (1) a written acute marks the stressed vowel; (2) with no acute, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the penult; (3) with no acute, a word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the last syllable. Examples: casa [ˈkasa], homes [ˈomes], canten [ˈkanten], cantar [kanˈtaɾ], ciudá [θjuˈða], fácil [ˈfaθil], árbol [ˈaɾβol], Uviéu [uˈβjeu̯], pan [ˈpan]. A diphthong or triphthong (see glides) counts as one syllable; an accented ⟨í ú⟩ next to a vowel is a hiatus: díes [ˈdi.es]. Put ˈ at the syllable onset. A single intervocalic consonant goes to the following syllable, as do obstruent+liquid clusters (pr, bl, tr) and the digraphs ll, rr, ch, ñ; other clusters split: cantar [kanˈtaɾ], llibru [ˈʎiβɾu], fuerza [ˈfweɾθa]. Adverbs in -mente take primary stress on -men- and secondary ˌ on the adjective's stressed syllable: rápidamente [ˌrapiðaˈmente]. A host before a hyphenated enclitic keeps its own stress: dá-yla [ˈda] [i̯la]. Asturian has no phonemic vowel length, no geminates (⟨ll rr⟩ are single consonants) and no case endings, so none of these affects stress.

Source: ALLA, Normes Ortográfiques 2021, §1.9.1 (accent rules), pp. 40-47; ALLA, Gramática de la Llingua Asturiana 2001, ch. V (word stress). Stress on -mente adverbs follows the Hispanic tradition (Alarcos Llorach 1994).

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed and get no ˈ. Definite articles: el, la, lo, los, les and elided l: el [el], la [la], les [les], l [l]. Prepositions: a, de, en, con, pa, per, por, ente, ensin, hasta, dende, sobre, tres, escontra, and elided d: de [de], con [kon], pa [pa]. The only stressed preposition is según [seˈɣun]. Conjunctions: y [i], o [o], ni [ni], que [ke], si [si], pero [peɾo], porque [poɾke], mas [mas], and elided qu [k]. Relatives and conjunctions written without an accent are unstressed: que, cuando [kwando], onde [onde], como [komo]. Clitic pronouns: me, te, se, nos, vos, lu, la, lo, los, les, y and its combinations ylu, yla, ylo, ylos, yles, and yos: me [me], se [se], lu [lu]. The preverbal negator nun is unstressed: nun [nun]. Prenominal monosyllabic possessives mio, to, so are unstressed: la mio casa [la] [mio] [ˈkasa]. These are stressed: indefinite un, una, unos, unes ([ˈun], [ˈuna]); tonic pronouns yo, tu, él, ella, nós, vós, ellos ([ˈʝo], [ˈtu], [ˈel], [ˈnos]); accented interrogatives qué, cuándo, ónde, cómo ([ˈke], [ˈkwando]); nuestru, vuestru; the stand-alone negator non [ˈnon]; and every verb form, auxiliaries and copula included: ye [ˈʝe], foi [ˈfoi̯], hai [ˈai̯], ha [ˈa], tien [ˈtjen].

Source: ALLA, Gramática de la Llingua Asturiana 2001, ch. V (stressed and unstressed words). For the indefinite article and auxiliaries it follows the Hispanic tradition of Alarcos Llorach 1994 (indefinite un and all verb forms stressed).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Asturian has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Final vowels and consonants are written the same before a pause, at a comma and sentence-medially. A pause matters only as the context for the stop allophones of /b d g/ (see other).

Source: ALLA, Gramática de la Llingua Asturiana 2001, ch. II-III (no pausal alternations described).

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its own place. Only two consonant processes are applied across word boundaries. (1) Word-final /n/ takes the place of a following word-initial consonant: [m] before /p b m/ and [ŋ] before /k g/: un pan [ˈum] [ˈpan], en casa [eŋ] [ˈkasa], un gatu [ˈuŋ] [ˈɡatu]. (2) The stop or spirant choice for /b d g/ at the start of a word depends on the preceding word's last sound (see other): la vaca [la] [ˈβaka], el día [el] [ˈdia], el gatu [el] [ˈɣatu]. Punctuation and the sentence start count as a pause. Elided words split off by the apostrophe are written as consonant-only words with the same values: l'agua [l] [ˈaɣwa], qu'entamar [k] [entaˈmaɾ]; d' follows the /d/ rule, so de casa d'Uviéu gives [ð] after a vowel. Not written: vowel contraction across words (synalepha), resyllabification, linking, and s-voicing. Write la escuela [la] [esˈkwela] with both vowels, and los homes [los] [ˈomes].

Source: ALLA, Gramática de la Llingua Asturiana 2001, III.1 (consonant variants: nasal assimilation, /b d g/ allophones) and III.2.1 (elision with the apostrophe); Normes 2021 §1.1.7, p. 14. Not marking synalepha follows broad Hispanic practice (Alarcos Llorach 1994).

## Glides and diphthongs

Unstressed ⟨i u⟩ next to a vowel in the same word are glides: [j w] before the nucleus and [i̯ u̯] after it. Examples: tierra [ˈtjera], fuerza [ˈfweɾθa], güei [ˈɡwei̯], voi [ˈboi̯], Uviéu [uˈβjeu̯], cantáu [kanˈtau̯], estudiéis [esˈtuðjei̯s]. The mixed diphthongs ⟨ui iu⟩ are rising: ruina [ˈrwina], ciudá [θjuˈða]. Written ⟨í ú⟩ stay full vowels in hiatus: díes [ˈdies], ríu [ˈriu̯]. The consonant ⟨y⟩ is [ʝ], never a glide: yo [ˈʝo], mayu [ˈmaʝu]. The conjunction y is always [i]. The enclitic -y and its combinations are [i] after a consonant-final host or alone (nun-y [nun] [i], dán-ylo [ˈdan] [ilo]) and [i̯] after a vowel-final host (dio-y [ˈdjo] [i̯], dá-yla [ˈda] [i̯la]).

Source: ALLA, Gramática de la Llingua Asturiana 2001, II.2, p. 24 (glides [j w] / [i̯ u̯]); Normes 2021 §1.6.1-1.6.2, p. 26 (diphthongs, mixed diphthongs, triphthongs) and §1.2.1.2, p. 15 (vocalic y).

## Other choices

### vowels

Write only the five vowels [a e i o u]. Do not write the open variants [ę ǫ], and do not write central-Asturian metaphony (raising before final -u). Final unstressed -u, -i, -e are written as spelled: gatu [ˈɡatu], pexe [ˈpeʃe], xorra [ˈʃora].

Source: ALLA, Normes 2021 §1.2, p. 15; ALLA, Gramática 2001, II.2, p. 24. Metaphony is a regional feature not in the standard (García Arias 2003).

### /b d g/ allophones

/b d g/ are stops [b d ɡ] after a pause or a nasal, and /d/ also after /l/. Elsewhere, including after /l/ for b and g, after /ɾ s/ and between vowels, they are [β ð ɣ]. This applies inside words and across word boundaries. Examples: verde [ˈbeɾðe], falda [ˈfalda], alba [ˈalβa], ganga [ˈɡaŋɡa], llobu [ˈʎoβu]. ⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩ are both /b/.

Source: ALLA, Gramática de la Llingua Asturiana 2001, III.1 (consonant variants).

### rhotics

Write [r] for ⟨rr⟩, for word-initial ⟨r⟩ and for ⟨r⟩ after ⟨l n s⟩. Every other ⟨r⟩ is [ɾ], including before a consonant and word-finally: perru [ˈperu], ríu [ˈriu̯], honra [ˈonra], pera [ˈpeɾa], cantar [kanˈtaɾ].

Source: ALLA, Normes 2021 §1.7.19.1, p. 34.

### consonant symbols

Write ⟨x⟩ [ʃ], ⟨ch⟩ [tʃ], ⟨ll⟩ [ʎ] (no yeísmo), ⟨ñ⟩ [ɲ] and ⟨y⟩ [ʝ]. ⟨z⟩ and ⟨c⟩ before e, i are [θ] (no seseo). ⟨h⟩ is silent; ⟨u⟩ in gue gui que qui is silent, while ⟨ü⟩ is [w]. Examples: xente [ˈʃente], llingua [ˈʎiŋɡwa], ñeru [ˈɲeɾu], zapatu [θaˈpatu], quier [ˈkjeɾ].

Source: ALLA, Normes 2021 §1.7, pp. 27-34; §1.2.2.2, p. 15; §1.7.20, p. 34.

### written dialect letters

Write ⟨ḷḷ⟩ and ⟨l.l⟩ as [ʎ] (central value); ⟨ts⟩ as [ts]; ⟨yy⟩ as [kʲ]; ⟨ḥ⟩ and ⟨h.⟩ as [h]. Examples: vaḷḷe [ˈbaʎe], cutsu [ˈkutsu], muyyer [muˈkʲeɾ], ḥuente [ˈhwente].

Source: ALLA, Normes 2021 §1.1.2-1.1.5, pp. 13-14; ALLA, Gramática 2001, I, pp. 16-17.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
