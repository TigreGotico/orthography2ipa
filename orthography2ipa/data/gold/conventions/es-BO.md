# Transcription conventions: Bolivian Spanish (es-BO)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Spanish lexical stress, taken from the spelling. A word with a written accent is stressed on that syllable: café [kaˈfe], árbol [ˈaɾbol], teléfono [teˈlefono]. Without an accent, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkasa], hablan [ˈablan], martes [ˈmaɾtes]. Any other unaccented word is stressed on the final syllable: hablar [aˈblaɾ], ciudad [sjuˈdad]. Write ˈ immediately before the stressed syllable. Syllabify within the word with maximal onsets: padre [ˈpadɾe], obrero [oˈbɾeɾo]. Every stressed word gets ˈ, including stressed monosyllables: sol [ˈsol], es [ˈes]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark: la [la], para [paɾa]. Words with enclitics keep the written stress: dámelo [ˈdamelo], diciéndole [diˈsjendole]. Adverbs in -mente take ˌ on the adjective's stress and ˈ on -men-: fácilmente [ˌfasilˈmente]. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels and no case endings, so these do not apply. Geminates do not apply either, except a written double consonant pronounced double: innato [inˈnato]. A written cc is [ks]: acción [akˈsjon]. Secondary stress is not marked otherwise.

Source: Hualde 2005, The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on stress and accentuation rules

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ: definite articles el la lo los las [el la lo los las]. Prenominal possessives mi tu su mis tus sus [mi tu su]. Object and reflexive clitics me te se le les lo la los las nos os. All simple prepositions except según: a, ante [ante], con, de, desde [desde], en, entre [entɾe], hacia [asja], hasta [asta], para [paɾa], por, sin, sobre [sobɾe]. Conjunctions y [i], e, ni, o, u, que [ke], pero [peɾo], sino, si, porque [poɾke], aunque [awnke]. Relative (non-interrogative) que, quien, cuando, donde, como. Treatment terms don, doña. Stressed, with ˈ: según [seˈɣun] written [seˈɡun]. Indefinite articles un una unos unas [ˈun ˈuna]. Demonstratives este [ˈeste], ese. Subject and prepositional pronouns yo [ˈʝo], tú [ˈtu], él [ˈel], ella [ˈeʎa], nosotros, mí [ˈmi], ti [ˈti]. Stressed possessives nuestro [ˈnwestɾo], mío. Interrogatives and exclamatives qué [ˈke], quién [ˈkjen], cómo [ˈkomo], dónde, cuándo. Numerals, adverbs including no [ˈno], muy [ˈmui̯] and tan [ˈtan]. All verb forms, including auxiliaries and the copula: ha [ˈa], he [ˈe], es [ˈes], está [esˈta], había [aˈbia].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on stress (stressed vs. unstressed word classes); Quilis-style list as summarised there

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word before a comma or at sentence end is transcribed exactly as elsewhere: final vowels and highland coda /s/ are kept, as in las casas [las ˈkasas].

Source: Hualde 2005; Lipski 1994, chapter on Bolivia (highland retention of /s/)

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its own place and keeps its own segments. No synalepha or vowel elision is written: la amiga [la aˈmiga], de esa [de ˈesa], mi hijo [mi ˈixo]. No glide formation is written across words: y entonces [i enˈtonses]. No resyllabification is written, and stress marks are placed by word-internal syllabification only: los otros [los ˈotɾos]. No linking marks are used. The one cross-word process written is nasal place assimilation, which changes the final nasal of the first word and also applies inside words. Write /n/ as [m] before p b m: un beso [ˈum ˈbeso], en Potosí [em potoˈsi], campo [ˈkampo]. Write it as [ŋ] before k ɡ x: en casa [eŋ ˈkasa], tango [ˈtaŋɡo], ángel [ˈaŋxel]. Write it as [n] elsewhere, with no [ɱ], dental or palatal nasal variants: un fuego [ˈun ˈfweɡo]. Highland final /n/ is alveolar, not velarized, so [n] stays before a vowel or pause: pan [ˈpan], son así [ˈson aˈsi]. Voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants is not written: mismo [ˈmismo], los días [los ˈdias].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on nasals (place assimilation) and on syllable/resyllabification; Lipski 1994, chapter on Bolivia (alveolar final /n/ in the highlands)

## Glides and diphthongs

Unstressed i, u (and y) next to another vowel in the same word form a diphthong. Before the vowel (rising), write [j w]: bien [ˈbjen], tierra [ˈtjer̝a], bueno [ˈbweno], cuidado [kwiˈdado], ciudad [sjuˈdad]. After the vowel (falling), write [i̯ u̯]: aire [ˈai̯ɾe], hoy [ˈoi̯], muy [ˈmui̯], reina [ˈrei̯na], causa [ˈkau̯sa], deuda [ˈdeu̯da]. Initial hie- is [ʝe]: hielo [ˈʝelo]. Initial hue- is [we]: hueso [ˈweso]. gü is [ɡw]: pingüino [piŋˈɡwino]. Accented í, ú and two strong vowels are in hiatus, written with no glide: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis], baúl [baˈul], teatro [teˈatɾo], poeta [poˈeta]. Spanish has no long vowels, so no length marks are used. Glides are never formed across word boundaries.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on vowel sequences (diphthongs [j w] vs. [i̯ u̯], hiatus)

## Other choices

### stops /b d ɡ/

Broad phonemic stops [b d ɡ] in all positions; the approximant allophones are not written. b and v are both [b]: vaca [ˈbaka], lobo [ˈlobo], nada [ˈnada], lago [ˈlaɡo]. Final -d is written [d]: verdad [beɾˈdad], usted [usˈted].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on voiced stops (allophony described but not transcribed in this broad scheme)

### seseo

c before e/i and z are [s]; there is no /θ/: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato], hacer [aˈseɾ].

Source: Lipski 1994, chapter on Bolivia; Fontanella de Weinberg 1992

### coda /s/

Highland norm: coda /s/ is kept as [s] everywhere. The lowland (Santa Cruz) aspiration or loss in the note is not written: estas cosas [ˈestas ˈkosas].

Source: Lipski 1994, chapter on Bolivia

### ll vs y

Highland Bolivian distinction is kept. ll is [ʎ]: calle [ˈkaʎe], llamar [ʎaˈmaɾ], ella [ˈeʎa]. y and hi+vowel before a vowel are [ʝ]: mayo [ˈmaʝo], yo [ˈʝo]. This departs from the note, which calls /ʎ/ only possible in rural areas; Lipski describes it as general in the highlands, with yeísmo mainly in the lowlands.

Source: Lipski 1994, chapter on Bolivia; Hualde 2005, chapter on palatals

### rhotics

The trill (written rr, word-initial r, and r after n, l, s) is written as the Andean assibilated [r̝]: perro [ˈper̝o], rojo [ˈr̝oxo], honra [ˈonr̝a], Israel [isr̝aˈel]. All other r is the tap [ɾ], including coda r and the tr cluster: pero [ˈpeɾo], mar [ˈmaɾ], tres [ˈtɾes].

Source: Lipski 1994, chapter on Bolivia (assibilated /r̄/); Hualde 2005, chapter on rhotics; spec note

### velar fricative and other consonants

j and g before e/i are velar [x]: jugar [xuˈɡaɾ], gente [ˈxente]. h is silent: hora [ˈoɾa]. ch is [tʃ]: chico [ˈtʃiko]. ñ is [ɲ]: año [ˈaɲo]. qu and c before a/o/u are [k]: queso [ˈkeso]. gu before e/i is [ɡ]: guerra [ˈɡer̝a]. x is [ks]: taxi [ˈtaksi]. x in México and other native names is [x]: [ˈmexiko].

Source: Hualde 2005; spec note (velar [x])

### vowels

Five vowels [a e i o u] as written. Substrate raising of e/o in Quechua/Aymara bilinguals and Andean unstressed vowel reduction or devoicing are not written: noche [ˈnotʃe], pues [ˈpwes].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on vowels; Lipski 1994, chapter on Bolivia (bilingual and unstressed-vowel variation, not written in this broad scheme)

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
