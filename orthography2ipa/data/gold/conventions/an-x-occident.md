# Transcription conventions: Western Aragonese (an-x-occident)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Put one primary stress mark ˈ at the start of the stressed syllable of every stressed word, including stressed monosyllables: [ˈfeɾ] fer, [ˈjo] yo. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. No secondary stress is marked.

Placement follows the spelling:
(1) A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel: café [kaˈfe], país [paˈis], cantábanos [kanˈtabanos].
(2) With no written accent, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkasa], cantan [ˈkantan], chuegos [ˈtʃweɡos].
(3) With no written accent, a word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the last syllable: cantar [kanˈtaɾ], paret [paˈɾet], zagal [θaˈɡal].

Syllabify V.CV inside a word. Obstruent + liquid clusters are onsets: otro [ˈotɾo]. Other clusters split: cantar [kan.ˈtaɾ].

The Aragonese tendency to shift stress off the antepenult is lexical. Where the orthography has no accent, apply rules 2 and 3 and do not guess.

Vowel length, geminates and case endings do not apply: the lect has none.

Source: Hualde 2005 (The Sounds of Spanish), chapter on word stress, for the default rule and the use of the written accent. Nagore Laín 2001 (Gramática de la Lengua Aragonesa), sections on accentuation, for the Aragonese paroxytone tendency.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed and carry no ˈ:
- definite articles: o, a, os, as, lo, la, los, las, el
- object and reflexive clitics: me, te, se, lo, la, li, les, nos, tos, ne/en, bi/i
- prenominal possessives: mi, tu, su, mis, tus, sus
- all simple prepositions, including polysyllabic ones: a, de, en, con, ta, t', per, por, entre, sobre, dica, sin
- conjunctions: y/e, u, que, si, pero, pos, como when it means 'as', and quan when it is not a question
- relative que

Examples: a casa [a ˈkasa]; lo veigo [lo ˈbejɡo]; pero no [peɾo ˈno]; dica Echo [dika ˈetʃo].

These are stressed and carry ˈ:
- nouns, adjectives, adverbs, numerals and demonstratives
- subject and prepositional pronouns: yo, tu, el, ella, nusatros, mi, ti, ell when stressed
- interrogatives and exclamatives: qué, quí, án, cuan
- indefinite articles: un, una
- all verb forms, including the auxiliaries ha, ye, yera, son and estar

Examples: un can [ˈun ˈkan]; ¿qué fas? [ˈke ˈfas]; ha cantau [ˈa kanˈtau].

A hyphenated enclitic is a separate unstressed word, and the host keeps its own stress: da-me-lo [ˈda me lo].

Source: Hualde 2005, section on stressed and unstressed words (the Spanish pattern of Navarro Tomás). The Aragonese clitic and article inventory follows Nagore Laín 2001, chapters on the article and pronouns.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. The lect has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written the same at a comma, at sentence end and sentence-internally.

Source: Not applicable. Nagore Laín 2001 and Hualde 2005 describe no pausal alternations for Aragonese or Spanish.

## Connected speech

Write only one cross-word process: place assimilation of a word-final nasal to the first consonant of the next word.
- Final n becomes m before p, b, m: en Benás [em beˈnas]; un pan [ˈum ˈpan].
- Final n becomes ŋ before k, ɡ, x: en casa [eŋ ˈkasa].
- The same rule applies inside words: cinco [ˈθiŋko].

Do not write any of these: synalepha or vowel elision across words (de agua stays [de ˈaɡwa]), voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants, resyllabification, or linking marks.

Elision that the spelling shows with an apostrophe is written as its own word, with only the remaining sounds: d'a [d a]; l'agua [l ˈaɡwa]; m'en [m en].

Each word stays in its place, separated by single spaces.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on nasals (nasal place assimilation) and on vowel sequences across words, read as a broad phonemic transcription. Elided clitic forms follow Nagore Laín 2001, section on apostrophized forms.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic i/y and u in diphthongs as j and w, in both rising and falling diphthongs. Never use i̯ or u̯.
- Rising: tierra [ˈtjera], puen [ˈpwen], güello [ˈɡweʎo].
- Falling: aire [ˈajɾe], rei [ˈrej], causa [ˈkawsa], cantau [kanˈtaw].

A written accent on i or u marks a hiatus: país [paˈis], baúl [baˈul].

The consonant spelled y or hi- before a vowel at the start of a syllable is ʝ, not j: yera [ˈʝeɾa], mayo [ˈmaʝo], hierba [ˈʝeɾba].

The conjunction y is always [i].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on glides and diphthongs, for the j/w versus ʝ distinction. The diphthong inventory follows Nagore Laín 2001, section on phonology.

## Other choices

### Palatal lateral (disagrees with the note)

The note says /ʎ/ has merged with /ʝ/ (yeísmo). Published descriptions of Western Aragonese (Ansó, Echo) record /ʎ/ as distinct, so write ll as ʎ: muller [muˈʎeɾ], castiello [kasˈtjeʎo]. Write y/hi- as ʝ.

Source: Nagore Laín 2001, phonology section. Also Kuhn 1935 (Der hocharagonesische Dialekt) and Alvar 1953 (El dialecto aragonés) on the cheso and ansotano varieties.

### Outcome of Latin J-/G- and the letters ch, x, j (disagrees with the note)

The note claims Castilian [x] for Latin J-. Native words in these varieties have /tʃ/, and the spelling shows it.
- Write ch as tʃ: chen [ˈtʃen], choven [ˈtʃoben], Chaca [ˈtʃaka].
- Write x as ʃ: baxo [ˈbaʃo].
- Write j, and g before e or i, as x only where the spelling uses them, as in Castilian loans: jamón [xaˈmon].
Transcribe what the spelling encodes; do not convert ch to x.

Source: Nagore Laín 2001, phonology section. Penny 2002, chapter on the dialects of medieval Iberia (Navarro-Aragonese outcomes of J-).

### Coronal fricatives

Keep /θ/ distinct from /s/.
- Write z, and c before e or i, as θ: zagal [θaˈɡal], cinco [ˈθiŋko].
- Write s as plain s; the apico-alveolar quality is not shown.

Source: Nagore Laín 2001, phonology section. Hualde 2005, chapter on fricatives.

### Voiced stops

Write b, d, ɡ in every position. Do not write the approximants β ð ɣ: lobo [ˈlobo], cantada [kanˈtada]. b and v are both b.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on obstruents (spirantization treated as allophonic and left out of a broad transcription).

### Rhotics

Write r for rr, and for r at the start of a word or after n, l, s: rei [ˈrej], tierra [ˈtjera]. Write ɾ everywhere else, including at the end of a word, as long as the r is spelled: cantar [kanˈtaɾ]. Do not drop a spelled infinitive -r.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on rhotics. Nagore Laín 2001, phonology section.

### Silent letters

h is silent: hue [ˈwe] reading spelled forms. qu and gu before e or i are k and ɡ: que [ke]. ü is w: güello [ˈɡweʎo].

Source: Nagore Laín 2001, orthography and phonology sections.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
