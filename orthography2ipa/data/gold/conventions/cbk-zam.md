# Transcription conventions: Chavacano (Zamboangueño) (cbk-zam)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every polysyllabic content word. Monosyllables are never marked. This departs from the specification note, which renders no stress. Published descriptions treat stress as lexical and contrastive, so the transcription marks it. Placement follows the Spanish orthographic rule. (1) A written acute marks the stressed vowel: comprá [komˈpɾa], llamá [jaˈma]. (2) Otherwise, a word ending in a vowel, ⟨n⟩ or ⟨s⟩ is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkasa], grande [ˈɡɾande], ustedes [usˈtedes]. (3) Otherwise, a word ending in another consonant is stressed on the last syllable: papel [paˈpel], mujer [muˈheɾ]. Verbs derived from Spanish infinitives are always stressed on the final syllable, even when the accent is not written: ya come [ja koˈme], ta anda [ta anˈda]. Philippine-origin words take the penult by default: bata [ˈbata], kame [ˈkame], sila [ˈsila]. There are no phonemic long vowels, so length is never written. There are no geminates: ⟨ll⟩, ⟨rr⟩ and ⟨cc⟩ are single segments or a cluster, never doubled. There are no case endings.

Source: Forman 1972, ch. 2 (phonology: Spanish-patterned lexical stress, final-stressed verb roots); Lipski 1987, pp. 93-99; orthographic acute convention per chavacano_wiki (Alphabet).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Leave these unstressed and unmarked, even when polysyllabic: TMA particles ya, ta, ay/hay, the negator no, articles el, la, un, una, the plural marker maga, prepositions de, na, con, para, por, and conjunctions y, o, pero, cuando, si, que. Examples: maga [maɡa], para [paɾa], pero [peɾo], cuando [kwando]. A sample phrase: ya anda el maga bata na escuela [ja anˈda el maɡa ˈbata na esˈkwela]. These words carry full stress and take ˈ when polysyllabic: personal pronouns (ele [ˈele], kame [ˈkame], kita [ˈkita], sila [ˈsila], ustedes [usˈtedes]), demonstratives (este [ˈeste], ese [ˈese], aquel [aˈkel]), adverbs, and interrogatives (donde [ˈdonde], cosa [ˈkosa]). Monosyllabic pronouns are unmarked like all monosyllables: yo [jo], tu [tu].

Source: Forman 1972, ch. 2-3 (TMA particles ya, ta, ay; negation no; maga; pronoun set); stress treatment follows the Spanish weak/strong word distinction as applied in Lipski 1987.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Chavacano has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written in the same form at a comma, at sentence end and sentence-internally: Bueno. [ˈbweno].

Source: Forman 1972, ch. 2 (no pausal alternations described).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form. No cross-boundary process is written: no synalepha or vowel elision (de este [de ˈeste]), no liaison or resyllabification (el anak [el aˈnak]), and no nasal place assimilation across words (un poco [un ˈpoko], con kame [kon ˈkame]). Word-internal nasal assimilation is written: ⟨n⟩ before a velar is [ŋ] (tengo [ˈteŋɡo], blanco [ˈblaŋko]). ⟨n⟩ before /p b/ inside a word is [m] (invierno [imˈbjeɾno]). Coda /s/ stays [s]; it is never aspirated or dropped (mas [mas], ustedes [usˈtedes]).

Source: Lipski 1987, pp. 93-99 (retention of coda /s/, conservative segmental realisation); Forman 1972, ch. 2.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the non-syllabic members of diphthongs as consonantal [j] and [w], never i̯ or u̯. Rising diphthongs: bueno [ˈbweno], tiene [ˈtjene], cuando [kwando]. Falling diphthongs: aire [ˈajɾe], auto [ˈawto], hoy [oj]. Consonantal ⟨y⟩ and ⟨ll⟩ are [j]: yo [jo], llamá [jaˈma]. This is yeísmo; never write ʎ. ⟨hu⟩ before a vowel is [w]: huevo [ˈwebo]. In Philippine words, ⟨w⟩ is [w] and ⟨y⟩ is [j]. A vowel sequence with a written accent on i or u is hiatus and stays two vowels: día [ˈdia], tía [ˈtia]. Two non-high vowels in a row are also hiatus: idea [iˈdea].

Source: Lipski 1987, pp. 93-99 (yeísmo, ⟨ll⟩ = [j]); chavacano_wiki (Alphabet); Forman 1972, ch. 2 (five-vowel system /a e i o u/).

## Other choices

### voiced stops

⟨b⟩, ⟨v⟩, ⟨d⟩ and ⟨g⟩ (before a o u, and ⟨gu⟩ before e i) are always the stops [b d ɡ], including between vowels. Never write β ð ɣ: lado [ˈlado], agua [ˈaɡwa], uva [ˈuba].

Source: Lipski 1987, pp. 93-99 (non-lenition of intervocalic /b d ɡ/); chavacano_wiki.

### ⟨j⟩, ⟨g⟩ before e i, ⟨h⟩

⟨j⟩, and ⟨g⟩ before e or i, are [h], never x: mujer [muˈheɾ], gente [ˈhente]. ⟨h⟩ is silent in Spanish-derived words (hace [ˈase], hombre [ˈombɾe]) but is [h] in Philippine-derived words.

Source: Lipski 1987, pp. 93-99 ([h] for ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩ + front vowel).

### seseo and other consonant letters

⟨c⟩ before e or i, ⟨z⟩ and ⟨s⟩ are all [s]; never write θ: cielo [ˈsjelo], cabeza [kaˈbesa]. Other letters: ⟨ch⟩ = [tʃ] (muchacho [muˈtʃatʃo]); ⟨ñ⟩ = [ɲ]; ⟨qu⟩ and ⟨k⟩ = [k]; ⟨x⟩ = [ks]; ⟨ng⟩ in Philippine-derived words = [ŋ].

Source: Lipski 1987, pp. 93-99; chavacano_wiki (seseo).

### rhotics

Write the trill [r] for ⟨rr⟩, for word-initial ⟨r⟩ and for ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s: perro [ˈpero], rio [ˈrio], honra [ˈonra]. Write the tap [ɾ] elsewhere: pero [peɾo], comer [koˈmeɾ].

Source: Spanish-based distinction as retained in Forman 1972, ch. 2.

### vowel quality and glottal stop

Write the five vowels /a e i o u/ exactly as spelled. Do not write the substrate-driven e~i and o~u variation in unstressed syllables: noche [ˈnotʃe], not [ˈnotʃi]. Do not write a glottal stop, even in Philippine-origin words, unless the orthography spells it.

Source: Forman 1972, ch. 2 (five-vowel system); variation noted in Lipski 1987, but not transcribed here.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
