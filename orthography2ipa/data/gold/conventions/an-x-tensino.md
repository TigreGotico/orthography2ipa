# Transcription conventions: Tensino Aragonese (Valle de Tena / Panticosa) (an-x-tensino)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables. Do not mark monosyllables. Placement is lexical and follows the written accent of the Aragonese orthography. A word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkasa], ombre [ˈombɾe], cantan [ˈkantan]. A word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the final syllable: cantar [kanˈtaɾ], estach [esˈtatʃ]. An acute accent overrides both rules: preterite -ós puyós [puˈʝos], cantós [kanˈtos]. Conditional -arba/-erba/-irba is stressed on the theme vowel: cantarba [kanˈtaɾβa], tenerba [teˈneɾβa]. Proparoxytones occur only where the spelling carries an accent. Syllabify with the onset maximal, without splitting tautosyllabic clusters (pl, bl, tr, dr, kl, gl, fl, fr). Long vowels and geminates do not apply: Tensino has no vowel length and no geminate consonants, so double ⟨rr⟩ is the single trill [r]. Case endings do not apply: nouns have no case.

Source: Nagore Laín (1986), El aragonés de Panticosa. Gramática, morphology and accentuation chapters (consulted via secondary summaries), for the -ós preterite and the -arba conditional. Placement rules follow the general Aragonese accentuation of Nagore Laín, Gramática de la lengua aragonesa (1977 and later editions) and the Academia de l'Aragonés orthography (2023).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Do not stress these words; they are proclitic or enclitic and carry no ˈ even when polysyllabic. Articles: ro ra ros ras [ɾo ɾa ɾos ɾas], ero era eros eras [ˈeɾo] is NOT marked, so write [eɾo eɾa eɾos eɾas], and o a os as / lo la los las. Prepositions: a, de, en, con, por, per, ta, enta [enta], dica [dika], entre [entɾe], sobre [soβɾe], sin, dende [dende]. Conjunctions: y/e, u, ni, que, si, pero [peɾo], como [komo], cuan, porque [poɾke]. Weak object and adverbial clitics: me te se le lo la nos tos/vos les ne/en bi/i, e.g. bi ha [bi a]. Unstressed possessive determiners: mi tu su. Relative que, quien and cuan. Stress these words and mark ˈ when polysyllabic: tonic pronouns (yo [ʝo], el, ella [ˈeʎa], nusatros [nuˈsatɾos]), demonstratives (iste [ˈiste], aquel [aˈkel]), interrogatives (qué, quí, cómo [ˈkomo], án), numerals, quantifiers and adverbs. Verbs keep their lexical stress, including auxiliaries and the copula: ha [a], han [an], habemos [aˈβemos], yera [ˈʝeɾa]. The article ro/ra/ros/ras is always the weak tap [ɾ], never the trill: ra casa [ɾa ˈkasa], de ros mozés → [de ɾos moˈθes].

Source: Vázquez Obrador (2021), Revista de Filología Románica 38, 121-132, for the atonic proclitic article (e)ro/(e)ra/(e)ros/(e)ras and its weak tap /ɾ/. Nagore Laín (1986) for the Panticosa article, pronoun and 'bi' paradigms (via secondary summaries). The unstressed/stressed classification follows the standard Ibero-Romance treatment of clitics in Nagore Laín, Gramática de la lengua aragonesa.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Tensino has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so final vowels and consonants are written identically at a sentence end, at a comma and sentence-internally: ra casa. [ɾa ˈkasa].

Source: No published description of Aragonese (Nagore Laín 1986; Kuhn 1935, Revue de Linguistique Romane 11) reports pausal alternations.

## Connected speech

Each word stays in its own IPA word; a cross-word process only changes the segments at the boundary. (1) Spirantization: /b d g/ are stops [b d ɡ] after a pause (sentence start, after any punctuation), after a nasal, and /d/ also after [l]. In all other environments they are [β ð ɣ], and this applies across word boundaries inside a phrase: ra boca [ɾa ˈβoka], de dentro [de ˈðentɾo], en Bal [en bal], dos gatos [dos ˈɣatos]. The same contexts apply inside words: lado [ˈlaðo], ambos [ˈambos]. (2) Nasal place assimilation: write word-final and word-internal /n/ as [m] before [p b m] and as [ŋ] before [k ɡ]: en Panticosa [em pantiˈkosa], con casa [koŋ ˈkasa], banco [ˈbaŋko]. Write it as [n] elsewhere. (3) Do not write vowel elision or synalepha: both vowels are written in full and each keeps its own word, as in ra alma [ɾa ˈalma], de este [de ˈeste]. A vowel is dropped only where the orthography writes an apostrophe. The tool splits the elided form into its own word, which is then transcribed as the remaining segment(s): d'ixo → [d] [ˈiʃo], with [ð] after a vowel. (4) No liaison, no linking consonant and no written resyllabification: word-final consonants stay in their word, as in os amigos [os aˈmiɣos]. (5) Do not voice /s/ before voiced consonants in broad transcription: mesmo [ˈmesmo].

Source: Nagore Laín, Gramática de la lengua aragonesa (fonética chapter), describing spirant allophones and nasal assimilation as in neighbouring Ibero-Romance. Kuhn (1935), Der hocharagonesische Dialekt, Revue de Linguistique Romane 11, on High-Aragonese phonetics including Tena. The elision policy follows the apostrophe practice of the Academia de l'Aragonés orthography (2023).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write a prenuclear (rising) glide as [j] or [w], and a postnuclear (falling) glide as [i̯] or [u̯]. Rising: tierra [ˈtjera], fuego [ˈfweɣo], bueno [ˈbweno]. The Tena reflex uá of Ŏ is [wa], as in buano [ˈbwano] where the text spells ua. Falling: caixa [ˈkai̯ʃa], peix [ˈpei̯ʃ], aire [ˈai̯ɾe], causa [ˈkau̯sa]. Triphthongs combine the two: [wei̯], [jai̯]. Written ⟨i⟩ or ⟨u⟩ with an acute accent next to a vowel is a syllabic nucleus in hiatus: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis]. Word-initial ⟨y⟩ and ⟨hi⟩ before a vowel are the consonant [ʝ]: yera [ˈʝeɾa]. Do not form glides across word boundaries: ra igleisa [ɾa iˈɣlei̯sa], and conjunction u before a vowel is [u].

Source: Várvaro (1991), Archivo de Filología Aragonesa XLVI-XLVII, 245-268, p. 250, for uá in the Valle de Tena documents. Nagore Laín (1986), via summaries, for the ie/ue diphthongization and the ai/ei/au falling diphthongs of Panticosa.

## Other choices

### Article tap vs trill

In the article forms ro ra ros ras and ero era eros eras, ⟨r⟩ is always the weak alveolar tap [ɾ], even word-initially: ra casa [ɾa ˈkasa]. Every other word-initial ⟨r⟩, and every ⟨rr⟩, is the trill [r]: ruda [ˈruða], tierra [ˈtjera]. Intervocalic single ⟨r⟩ and ⟨r⟩ in clusters are [ɾ]: pero [peɾo], tres [tɾes]. Write word-final ⟨r⟩ as [ɾ] as spelled, e.g. cantar [kanˈtaɾ]. Some general descriptions of Alto Aragón report loss of infinitival -r in places, but this is not attested here for Tena and is not modelled.

Source: Vázquez Obrador (2021), RFR 38, 121-132, who derives the weak tap /ɾ/ from -LL- via apical-palatal /ɽ/; the specification notes.

### Western/Central consonant profile

Write distinción, not seseo: ⟨z⟩ and ⟨c⟩ before e/i are [θ], as in zaga [ˈθaɣa], and ⟨s⟩ is [s]. Keep the lateral palatal; there is no yeísmo: ⟨ll⟩ [ʎ], as in ella [ˈeʎa], while ⟨y⟩ [ʝ]. ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ], including the 'cacuminal' reflexes: estach [esˈtatʃ], gricha [ˈɡɾitʃa]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ], ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩ before e/i are [x], and ⟨ny⟩/⟨ñ⟩ are [ɲ]. Latin F- is retained as [f]: fuego [ˈfweɣo]. Intervocalic voiceless stops kept in the spelling are written voiceless and are not spirantized: forato-type words keep [t], as in cantato [kanˈtato].

Source: The specification notes; Kuhn (1935), Revue de Linguistique Romane 11; Nagore Laín (1986), via summaries.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
