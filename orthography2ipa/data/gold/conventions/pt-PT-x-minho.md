# Transcription conventions: Minhoto Portuguese (pt-PT-x-minho)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every content word, including monosyllables: casa [ˈkazɐ], café [kɐˈfɛ], mar [ˈmaɾ]. Stress is lexical and is read off the spelling. (a) A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed vowel: lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃pɐdɐ], você [buˈse]. (b) With no accent, words ending in -a, -e, -o, -em or -am, with or without a final -s, take penultimate stress: falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], homem [ˈɔmẽj̃]. (c) All other unaccented words take final stress: falar [fɐˈlaɾ], azul [ɐˈz̺uɫ], aqui [ɐˈki]. In this table the sibilants of ⟨casa, você⟩ are apical [z̺, s̺], following 'other: sibilants'; those examples write plain [z, s] for readability. Mark no secondary stress. In stressed position the full vowel system [i e ɛ a ɐ ɔ o u] and the nasal vowels appear. Elsewhere vowels reduce, following 'other: unstressed vowels'. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply. Portuguese has no phonemic length. ⟨ss⟩ and ⟨rr⟩ are digraphs for single consonants. There is no case morphology.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, chapter on word stress; Cunha & Cintra 1984, chapter on orthographic accentuation.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Write the following without a stress mark, with reduced vowels: (a) Definite articles: o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ]. (b) Simple prepositions: de [dɨ], em [ẽj̃], a [ɐ], por [puɾ], para [pɐɾɐ], com [kõ], sem [sẽj̃]. Write para in full, not as pra. (c) Contractions: do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], ao [aw], à [a], pelo [pɨlu]. (d) Conjunctions and complementisers: e [i], que [kɨ], se [sɨ], mas [mɐʃ], ou [ow], nem [nẽj̃]. (e) Relative que, onde and quem when non-interrogative. (f) All clitic pronouns, whether proclitic or hyphen-joined: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [sɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], nos [nuʃ], vos [buʃ], o [u], a [ɐ], lo [lu], la [lɐ]. Example: dá-me [ˈda mɨ]. Write the following with stress: (a) auxiliaries and copulas, which keep their lexical stress: é [ˈɛ], está [ɨʃˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], foi [ˈfoj]; (b) indefinite articles: um [ˈũ], uma [ˈumɐ]; (c) negation: não [ˈnɐ̃w̃]; (d) tonic pronouns: eu [ˈew], ele [ˈelɨ], mim [ˈmĩ]; (e) possessives, demonstratives, numerals, interrogatives such as quê [ˈke] and quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], and adverbs.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, discussion of clitics and the prosodic word; Cunha & Cintra 1984, chapters on articles, pronouns and prepositions.

## Pausal forms

Pausal forms do not apply. Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written in the same form at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside a phrase. Final unstressed -e stays [ɨ] and is never deleted: noite [ˈnojtɨ]. A word-final sibilant before a pause is [ʃ], as before a voiceless consonant: as casas. [ɐʃ ˈkaz̺ɐʃ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, treatment of syllable-final fricatives.

## Connected speech

Write only one sandhi process: the voicing of a word-final sibilant (⟨s, z, x⟩). Write the change on the first word; the second word does not change. (a) Before a vowel-initial word the final sibilant is [z̺]: os amigos [uz̺ ɐˈmiɡuʃ]. (b) Before a voiced consonant it is [ʒ]: os dias [uʒ ˈdiɐʃ]. (c) Before a voiceless consonant or a pause it is [ʃ]: os pais [uʃ ˈpajʃ]. Do not write other processes. (a) Vowel elision and fusion across words are not written: de água [dɨ ˈaɡwɐ], not [ˈdaɡwɐ]. (b) Glide formation of [i u ɨ] before a following vowel is not written. (c) Resyllabification and linking of final [ɾ] or [ɫ] are not written: mar azul [ˈmaɾ ɐˈz̺uɫ]. (d) Nasal assimilation across words is not written. Each word keeps its citation form apart from the sibilant rule.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, sections on sandhi and syllable-final fricatives; apical value of the prevocalic sibilant after Cintra 1971 p.93.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the non-syllabic part of a falling diphthong as [j] or [w], never i̯ or u̯: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], céu [ˈsɛw], foi [ˈfoj]. In nasal diphthongs write a tilde on both the vowel and the glide: mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃]. Write rising [w] only after ⟨q, g⟩ where the ⟨u⟩ is pronounced: quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. Other prevocalic i and u are written as full vowels: história [iʃˈtɔɾiɐ], piano [piˈɐnu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, chapter on glides and diphthongs.

## Other choices

### sibilants

In onset position the graphemes ⟨s, ss, c(e,i), ç, z⟩ all give apico-alveolar sibilants. Voiceless ones are [s̺]: sapato [s̺ɐˈpatu], passo [ˈpas̺u], cinco [ˈs̺ĩku], caça [ˈkas̺ɐ]. Voiced ones are [z̺]: casa [ˈkaz̺ɐ], fazer [fɐˈz̺eɾ]. Never write laminal [s z] in onset. In coda the sibilant is [ʃ] or [ʒ], as in pan-European Portuguese: mesmo [ˈmeʒmu], festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ]. ⟨ch⟩ and ⟨x⟩ give [ʃ] in this table (Baixo-Minho), not the Alto-Minho/Transmontano affricate [tʃ]: chave [ˈʃabɨ]. ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g(e,i)⟩ give [ʒ].

Source: Cintra 1971 p.93 and pp.11-13 (CVC reprint); Álvarez Pérez 2014 pp.37-38.

### betacism

Write ⟨v⟩ and ⟨b⟩ alike as [b] in every position: vinho [ˈbiɲu], vaca [ˈbakɐ], livro [ˈlibɾu]. The spirant [β], and likewise [ð ɣ], is an allophone and is not written in this broad transcription, although the specification lists it as a realisation.

Source: Cintra 1971 p.88; Álvarez Pérez 2014 pp.35-37.

### rhotics

Write word-initial ⟨r⟩, ⟨rr⟩, and ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s as the alveolar trill [r]: rio [ˈriu], carro [ˈkaru], honra [ˈõrɐ]. This follows the northern traditional pronunciation, not the Lisbon uvular [ʁ]. Write ⟨r⟩ between vowels and in coda as [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], mar [ˈmaɾ]. The uvular coda variants listed in the specification are not written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, inventory of rhotics and dialectal variation.

### diphthongs ou and ei

Write ⟨ou⟩ as [ow]: outro [ˈowtɾu], falou [fɐˈlow]. Do not write the southern monophthong [o]. Write ⟨ei⟩ as [ej]: leite [ˈlejtɨ], primeiro [pɾiˈmejɾu]. Do not write the Lisbon form [ɐj]. Write ⟨em/ens⟩ as [ẽj̃], not [ɐ̃j̃].

Source: Cintra 1971 (northern preservation of ou); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, note on Lisbon centralisation of [ej].

### unstressed vowels

Reduce unstressed vowels as in standard European Portuguese. Unstressed ⟨a⟩ is [ɐ]. Unstressed ⟨e⟩ is [ɨ], or [i] word-initially before a sibilant: escola [iʃˈkɔlɐ]. Unstressed ⟨o⟩ is [u]. Unstressed ⟨i u⟩ stay [i u]. Final -e is [ɨ] and final -o is [u]: verde [ˈbeɾdɨ], gato [ˈɡatu]. Stressed ⟨e⟩ before a palatal stays [e], not the Lisbon [ɐ]: espelho [iʃˈpeʎu]. The Porto diphthongisation [je, wo] is not written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, chapter on vowel reduction; the exclusion of diphthongisation follows the specification notes.

### laterals and palatals

Write onset ⟨l⟩ as [l] and coda ⟨l⟩ as velarised [ɫ]: lado [ˈladu], sol [ˈs̺ɔɫ]. Write ⟨lh⟩ as [ʎ] and ⟨nh⟩ as [ɲ]: filho [ˈfiʎu], vinho [ˈbiɲu]. Write stops as [b d ɡ] in all positions, without spirant symbols.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, consonant inventory.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
