# Transcription conventions: Extremaduran Spanish (Extremeño) (es-ES-x-extremadura)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Lexical stress follows standard Spanish rules, applied to the spelled word before any aspiration or deletion. A written accent marks the stressed vowel. Otherwise, words ending in a vowel, -n or -s stress the penultimate syllable, and all other words stress the final syllable. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every stressed word of two or more syllables: estás [ehˈtah], tienes [ˈtjeneh], ciudad [θjuˈða], cansado [kanˈsao], comida [koˈmia]. Stressed monosyllables carry no mark: más [mah], sol [sol]. Stress stays on its original syllable when a consonant is deleted. Adverbs in -mente take ˌ on the adjective's stressed syllable and ˈ on -men-: rápidamente [ˌrapiðaˈmente]. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings. Vowels meeting across a word boundary are never written long: la ala stays [la ˈala].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on stress and accentuation; Zamora Vicente 1960, chapter on Extremaduran (stress unaffected by consonant loss).

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following words are unstressed and never carry ˈ, even when polysyllabic: definite articles (el la lo los las); prenominal mi tu su and their plurals; object and reflexive clitics (me te se nos os lo la le los las les); all prepositions except según (a de en con por para desde hasta sobre entre sin: para [paɾa], desde [dehðe]); conjunctions (y e o u ni que pero si porque aunque); and non-interrogative que, quien, cual, donde, como, cuando: cuando viene [kwando ˈβjene]. The following are stressed: indefinite articles (un una unos unas: una [ˈuna]); numerals; subject and prepositional pronouns (yo tú él ella nosotros mí ti: nosotros [noˈsotɾoh]); demonstratives; postnominal and full possessives (nuestro [ˈnwehtɾo], mío [ˈmio]); interrogatives and exclamatives (qué cómo dónde cuándo: cómo [ˈkomo]); adverbs; and all verbs, including the auxiliaries haber, ser and estar (hemos [ˈemoh], han [an]). Write each word in its full spelled form. Do not write reduced forms such as pa for para unless the text spells them.

Source: Hualde 2005, section on stressed and unstressed words in the stress chapter.

## Pausal forms

Spanish has no pausal forms. There are no final short vowels to drop, no case endings and no nunation (tanwīn), and a word has the same shape before a pause as anywhere else. Final /s/ is written [h] before a pause as well: vamos. [ˈbamoh]. Do not write the vowel opening or [h] loss heard at pause in some speech. A pause matters in one way only. After the start of the sentence or a comma, and after a nasal, voiced /b d g/ are stops. After a lateral, /d/ is also a stop. Everywhere else /b d g/ are approximants: Dame, gato. [ˈdame ˈgato]; la boda [la ˈβoða].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on spirantization of voiced obstruents; Zamora Vicente 1960, chapter on Extremaduran (aspiration).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its own place. Processes across the boundary change only the edge segments. (1) Spirantization: the choice of [b d g] or [β ð ɣ] at the start of a word depends on the last sound of the previous word. Use a stop after a pause or a nasal, and also for /d/ after /l/; use an approximant elsewhere, including after [h]: los dedos [loh ˈðeðoh]; un beso [um ˈbeso]; el dedo [el ˈdeðo]. (2) Nasal place assimilation: a final /n/ takes the place of a following consonant. Write [m] before bilabials, [ɱ] before f, [ŋ] before velars, and [n] elsewhere and before vowels or a pause: en Badajoz [em baðaˈhoh], un gato [uŋ ˈgato], con fe [koɱ ˈfe]. (3) Final /s/ stays [h] before a vowel too, with no relinking to [s]: los otros [loh ˈotɾoh]. (4) Synalepha, vowel elision and resyllabification are not written, and there are no syllable dots: la otra [la ˈotɾa], de esto [de ˈehto]. (5) Word-initial r is [r]. Within a word, r after n, l or s is also [r].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on nasal assimilation, spirantization and vowel sequences; Zamora Vicente 1960, chapter on Extremaduran (aspiration before vowels).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write a prevocalic glide in a diphthong as [j w] and a postvocalic glide as [i̯ u̯]: bien [bjen], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [θjuˈða], aire [ˈai̯ɾe], rey [rei̯], causa [ˈkau̯sa]. The yeísta consonant from ll or onset y is [ʝ], distinct from the glide: calle [ˈkaʝe], yo [ʝo], hielo [ˈʝelo]. Write y as [ʝ] after a pause or a nasal too, never [ɟʝ]. The conjunction y is [i] in every position. Vowels left in hiatus by deletion of /d/ are written as full vowels, not glides: cansado [kanˈsao], not [kanˈsau̯].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on glides and diphthongs and on the palatal obstruent (yeísmo).

## Other choices

### syllable-final /s/

Write /s/ as [h] at the end of every word and before every consonant, in all of Extremadura, Cáceres included: estos [ˈehtoh], más [mah], las casas [lah ˈkasah]. This follows the dialectological description of general aspiration and departs from the note that Cáceres preserves [s] more. Do not write gemination or devoicing of the following consonant. Onset /s/ stays [s]. In x before a consonant, the s part also aspirates: extremo [ehˈtɾemo]; before a vowel x is [ks]: examen [ekˈsamen].

Source: Zamora Vicente 1960, chapter on Extremaduran (aspiración de -s implosiva).

### syllable-final /θ/

Write word-final and preconsonantal z as [h], like /s/: luz [luh], voz [boh], diez [djeh]. Onset c and z before a vowel stay [θ].

Source: Zamora Vicente 1960, chapter on Extremaduran.

### seseo versus distinción

Use distinción everywhere: c before e or i, and z, are [θ]; s is [s]. cien [θjen], zapato [θaˈpato], casa [ˈkasa]. The seseo of southeastern Badajoz (Zafra, Jerez de los Caballeros) is not written.

Source: Zamora Vicente 1960, chapter on Extremaduran; Hualde 2005, chapter on dialect variation in Spanish consonants.

### /x/

Write j, and g before e or i, as [h] before every vowel. This follows the dialectological description of general aspiration in Extremadura rather than the note's restriction to back vowels: jamón [haˈmon], gente [ˈhente], ojo [ˈoho]. The letter h is silent: humo [ˈumo]. Do not write the rural aspiration of h from Latin F.

Source: Zamora Vicente 1960, chapter on Extremaduran (aspiración de j).

### /d/ deletion

Delete word-final /d/: ciudad [θjuˈða], verdad [beɾˈða], usted [uhˈte]. Delete intervocalic /d/ only in the endings -ado -ada -ido -ida -udo -uda and their plurals: cansado [kanˈsao], nada [ˈnaa], comida [koˈmia], comidos [koˈmioh]. The remaining vowels stay full vowels; this departs from the note's [koˈmiə]. Keep /d/ as [ð] elsewhere: todo [ˈtoðo], cada [ˈkaða].

Source: Zamora Vicente 1960, chapter on Extremaduran; Hualde 2005, chapter on /d/ weakening.

### coda liquids

Keep coda /ɾ/ and /l/ distinct and present: comer [koˈmeɾ], el árbol [el ˈaɾβol], alto [ˈalto]. Do not write r and l neutralization or loss of the infinitive -r. Write [ɾ] for single r between vowels and in codas, and [r] for rr and in the positions given under connected_speech.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on rhotics and laterals; the rural forms in Zamora Vicente 1960 are not followed.

### vowels

Write five vowels [a e i o u] with no openness or nasal marks. Do not write vowel opening after aspirated /s/, or the Portuguese-contact quality noted for Badajoz: los perros [loh ˈperoh].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on vowels.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
