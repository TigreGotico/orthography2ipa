# Transcription conventions: Aragonese (an)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Free lexical stress, one primary stress per word, marked with ˈ before the stressed syllable on every word of two or more syllables. Stressed monosyllables are not marked. Place ˈ before the whole legal onset: [ˈpjedɾa], [faˈblaɾ], [ˈfejto]. If the spelling has a written accent, follow it: [aɾaɡoˈnes], [ˈdia]. With no written accent, use the default rule. Words ending in a vowel, -n or -s stress the penult: [ˈfiʝo], [ˈbweno], [ˈkasas]. Words ending in any other consonant stress the last syllable: [tʃiˈkot], [kanˈtaɾ], [aˈmistat]. Plurals in -s, -z or -tz keep the stress of the singular: chicotz [tʃiˈkots]. Adverbs in -ment get a single primary stress on -ment: [kjetaˈment]. Do not apply the dialectal shift of proparoxytones to paroxytones; stress the standard spelling form. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Aragonese has no vowel length, no phonemic geminates and no case endings.

Source: OA2023, Ortografía de l'aragonés, section on accentuation; Nagore Laín 2001, Gramática de la Lengua Aragonesa, phonology chapter (accent); GBA2021 phonology section.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Function words carry no stress mark and keep full vowels; there is no reduction. Unstressed: articles o, a, os, as, lo, la, los, las, ro, ra, ros, ras, un, una, unos, unas. Unstressed: simple prepositions a, de, en, con, per, ta, enta, dende, entre, sobre, sin, cara: [enta], [dende], [entɾe]. Unstressed: conjunctions e, y, u, o, que, si, ni, pero, pos, como (conjunctive), cuan: [peɾo], [komo]. Unstressed: clitic pronouns me, te, se, lo, la, li, nos, vos, los, las, les, and adverbial-partitive en, ne, bi, i. Unstressed: preposed possessive and short determiners. Unstressed: auxiliary forms of haber (he, has, ha, emos, hemos, etc.) and the relative que. Stressed, marked when polysyllabic: subject and prepositional pronouns (yo, tu, ell, ella [ˈeʎa], nusatros [nuˈsatɾos]), demonstratives (iste [ˈiste], ixe [ˈiʃe], aquel [aˈkel]), possessives after the article (a mía [ˈmia]), interrogatives and exclamatives with or without a written accent (qué, cómo [ˈkomo]), numerals, negation no, and forms of ser and estar as main verbs. Hyphenated enclitics are separate unstressed words; the host keeps its citation stress: fer-lo → [feɾ] [lo].

Source: Nagore Laín 2001, chapters on articles, pronouns and prepositions; GBA2021, morphology sections; general Ibero-Romance prosody as in Hualde 2005, chapter on stress (function-word cliticisation).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Aragonese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written in its citation form at a comma, at the end of a sentence and medially alike, and final vowels and consonants are kept: [kanˈtaɾ], [tʃiˈkots], [ˈkasa].

Source: Nagore Laín 2001, phonology chapter; GBA2021.

## Connected speech

Write no cross-word processes. Each word is transcribed in its citation form. Omit: synalepha, vowel elision, resyllabification, nasal place assimilation across words, voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants, and spirantisation. So 'a casa' is [a] [ˈkasa] and 'os ombres' is [os] [ˈombɾes]. Elision is marked in the spelling with an apostrophe, and the elided form is its own word, written as its consonant alone: l'aragonés → [l] [aɾaɡoˈnes]; d'o → [d] [o]; m', t', s', n' → [m], [t], [s], [n]. The contracted articles ro, ra, ros, ras after a vowel and contractions like d'o, t'o, en o are transcribed exactly as written. Inside a word, write /n/ as [ŋ] before /k ɡ/: [ˈlenɡwa] → [ˈleŋɡwa], [ˈbaŋko]. Write no other assimilation. Infinitival final -r is pronounced [ɾ] as in the standard, even though many spoken varieties drop it.

Source: OA2023, sections on the apostrophe and contractions; Nagore Laín 2001, phonology and article chapters; Hualde 2005, chapters on nasals and syllabification, for the Ibero-Romance treatment that is here restricted to the word.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic i and u as [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. Rising diphthongs: piedra [ˈpjedɾa], bueno [ˈbweno], lengua [ˈleŋɡwa], agua [ˈaɡwa]. Falling diphthongs: feito [ˈfejto], muito [ˈmujto], aire [ˈajɾe], causa [ˈkawsa], beure [ˈbewɾe]. Triphthongs: bueis [bwejs]. An accented í or ú is a syllable nucleus, giving a hiatus with no glide: día [ˈdia], baúl [baˈul]. ⟨y⟩ before a vowel is the palatal consonant [ʝ]: fiyo [ˈfiʝo], yo [ʝo]. ⟨y⟩ as the conjunction or word-final after a vowel is [i] or [j] respectively: rey [rej]. ⟨u⟩ in ⟨qu⟩ and ⟨gu⟩ before e/i is silent; ⟨ü⟩ is [w].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on glides and diphthongs (j w notation); Nagore Laín 2001, phonology chapter on diphthongs; GBA2021.

## Other choices

### c/z: θ versus s

Standard Aragonese has /θ/ for ⟨z⟩ and for ⟨c⟩ before e/i: zapato [θaˈpato], cinco [ˈθiŋko], plaza [ˈplaθa]. ⟨s⟩ is /s/. Do not use seseo, which belongs only to eastern (Ribagorzan) varieties. This departs from the spec note, which is self-contradictory and says the sounds become /s/.

Source: Nagore Laín 2001, phonology chapter; GBA2021; OA2023.

### b/v

⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩ are both /b/; there is no /v/ in the standard. vaca [ˈbaka], vida [ˈbida]. Write the stops /b d ɡ/ in every position and do not write the spirant allophones [β ð ɣ]. This departs from the spec note claiming a partial /v/ contrast.

Source: Nagore Laín 2001, phonology chapter; Penny 2002 on Ibero-Romance betacism; Hualde 2005 for b/v merger.

### Palatal and affricate digraphs

⟨ch⟩ = /tʃ/: chicot [tʃiˈkot]. ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩ before e/i in native words, as written in the etymological spelling, = /tʃ/: chent or gent [tʃent]. /x/ is used only in evident Castilian loans and proper names. ⟨x⟩ = /ʃ/: xa [ʃa]. ⟨ix⟩ after a vowel = /ʃ/ with silent i: baixo [ˈbaʃo]. Word-initial ix- = [iʃ]: ixe [ˈiʃe]. ⟨ll⟩ = /ʎ/: ella [ˈeʎa]. There is no yeísmo. ⟨ny⟩ and ⟨ñ⟩ = /ɲ/: anyo [ˈaɲo]. ⟨tz⟩ = /ts/: chicotz [tʃiˈkots]. /dz/ is not used in the standard. ⟨h⟩ is silent.

Source: OA2023, chapters on consonant graphemes; Nagore Laín 2001, phonology chapter; GBA2021.

### Rhotics

/r/ is written for ⟨rr⟩, word-initial ⟨r⟩, and ⟨r⟩ after l, n or s: ruello [ˈrweʎo], honra [ˈonra]. Every other ⟨r⟩, including word-final ⟨r⟩, is /ɾ/: [ˈpeɾo], [kanˈtaɾ].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on rhotics; Nagore Laín 2001.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
