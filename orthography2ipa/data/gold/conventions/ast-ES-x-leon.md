# Transcription conventions: Leonese (ast-ES-x-leon)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ at the start of the stressed syllable in every stressed word of two or more syllables. Monosyllables never get a mark, whether stressed or not. Placement follows the orthography. A written acute accent (á é í ó ú) marks the stressed vowel: /aˈθi/, /ˈarbole/, /kanˈθjon/. Without an accent, a word ending in a vowel, a diphthong ending in i or u, -n or -s is stressed on the penultimate syllable: /ˈkasa/, /ˈʎobu/, /ˈkantan/, /ˈkasas/. Without an accent, a word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the last syllable: /kanˈtaɾ/, /berˈdaθ/. Syllabify with the maximal onset. Clusters of a stop or f with l or ɾ, and the sounds /tʃ ʎ ɲ ʃ/, begin a syllable: /ˈpjedɾa/, /ˈkaʎe/, /ˈpaʃaɾu/. A diphthong belongs to one syllable, so the mark goes before its onset: /ˈbweno/. Unaccented final -i and -u count as vowels, so the penultimate rule applies: /ˈtʃobu/. A verb with an enclitic written joined to it keeps the verb's stressed syllable: /ˈdime/, /diˈθiaʎe/. Leonese has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so none of these is written. Double letters are digraphs (ll /ʎ/, rr /r/), not geminates.

Source: Orthographic accent rules of the Propuesta de Norma Ortográfica para el Leonés (c. 2010), which follow the Asturian-Leonese and Spanish model. Stress is described as lexical and free in Morala & Egido (2009); syllabification follows Quilis (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, ch. on la sílaba.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and never carry ˈ, even when they have two syllables: definite articles (el, la, lo, los, las, l'); contractions of preposition and article (al, del, nel, pol, pela, pelos, col, cola); prepositions (a, de, en, con, por, pa, pe, sin, so, sobre, hasta, dende, ente, pente); conjunctions (y/e, ou/o, ni, que, si, pero, porque, mas); relative que, onde, cuando, cuantu when non-interrogative; unstressed object pronouns (me, te, se, nos, vos, lu, la, lo, los, las, le/ye/lle, les/yes/lles); short prenominal possessives (mi, tu, su, mios, tous); and forms of haber used as perfect auxiliary. Examples: /de la ˈkasa/, /pa ˈbeɾlu/, /pero ˈnun ˈkeɾe/ (nun itself is stressed but monosyllabic). The following are stressed and carry ˈ when they have two or more syllables: nouns, adjectives, lexical verbs, copula and other verbs not used as auxiliary, adverbs including the negator nun, indefinite articles (/ˈuna/), numerals, tonic pronouns (/ˈnosotɾos/, ˈél is monosyllabic so no mark), demonstratives (/ˈeste/, /aˈkel/), and interrogative or exclamative words (/ˈkwandu/ in questions). A hyphenated enclitic is its own word and is written unstressed. Its host keeps its own stress: ⟨da-ye⟩ → /ˈda/ written da, then /je/.

Source: Navarro Tomás (1918, 1957 ed.), Manual de pronunciación española, §§ on palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas; Quilis (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, ch. on el acento. Applied to the Leonese function words listed in Morala & Egido (2009) and the Propuesta de Norma Ortográfica.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Leonese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word is written the same at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside a phrase, and final vowels are kept as spelled: /ˈʎobu/ everywhere.

Source: Morala & Egido (2009); Menéndez Pidal (1906), El dialecto leonés; neither describes any pausal alternation.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form and do not write processes across word boundaries. Nasal place assimilation is not written: /en ˈbaka/, not [em]. Voicing of /s/ before a voiced consonant is not written: /los ˈdedos/. Synalepha and vowel merger across words are not written: /la ˈaɡwa/ (where spelled so), /ke ˈel/. There is no linking symbol and no resyllabification across words. The only elision written is the one the orthography shows with an apostrophe. Because the apostrophe splits words, an elided form is written as its own reduced word: ⟨l'agua⟩ → /l/ /ˈaɡwa/; ⟨d'ellos⟩ → /d/ /ˈeʎos/; ⟨qu'él⟩ → /k/ /el/. Within a word, allophonic processes are also left unwritten, such as spirantization of /b d ɡ/ and nasal assimilation (see other).

Source: Broad phonemic convention of Martínez-Celdrán, Fernández-Planas & Carrera-Sabaté (2003), 'Castilian Spanish', JIPA 33(2), which writes such processes only in narrow transcription. Apostrophe elisions follow the Propuesta de Norma Ortográfica.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write a prevocalic (rising) glide as j or w and a postvocalic (falling) glide as i̯ or u̯. Rising: /ˈpjedɾa/, /ˈbweno/, /ˈɡweʝu/ (güeyu), /kanˈθjon/. Falling: /ˈkei̯ʃu/ (queixu), /ˈkou̯sa/ (cousa), /ˈbai̯le/, /ˈpau̯sa/, /ˈboi̯/. Triphthongs combine the two: /ˈbwei̯/. The western ei/ou diphthongs are written i̯ and u̯ wherever they are spelled; monophthongize only where the text spells e/o. An accented í or ú marks hiatus and is a full vowel: /ˈdia/, /ˈrio/, /pɾoˈiβe/ is wrong, write /pɾoˈibe/. The conjunction ⟨y⟩ is /i/, and i̯ after a vowel within the word: ⟨hai/hay⟩ → /ai̯/. Prevocalic consonantal ⟨y⟩ is the fricative /ʝ/, not a glide, following the note, including the verb ⟨ye⟩ → /ʝe/ and ⟨yera⟩ → /ˈʝeɾa/.

Source: Martínez-Celdrán, Fernández-Planas & Carrera-Sabaté (2003), JIPA 33(2), which uses [j w] for onglides and [i̯ u̯] for offglides. Diphthong inventory, including western ei/ou, from Menéndez Pidal (1906), El dialecto leonés, and Morala & Egido (2009). The ⟨y⟩=/ʝ/ value is from the specification notes.

## Other choices

### Final unstressed vowels

Write final vowels exactly as spelled, with no reduction or raising beyond the spelling. Final -u is /u/ (/ˈʎobu/, /ˈpaʃaɾu/), final -o is /o/, final -e is /e/ and final -i is /i/. Plural -es and -as stay /es/ and /as/. Do not convert -u to -o or -e to -i to match another dialect area.

Source: Menéndez Pidal (1906), El dialecto leonés, on final -u and -i; Morala & Egido (2009); Propuesta de Norma Ortográfica, western base.

### Voiced stops

Always write /b d ɡ/, never [β ð ɣ], in every position: /ˈlobu/ is wrong only if the word is spelled with ll, so write /ˈʎobu/; /ˈnada/; /ˈaɡwa/. Both ⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩ are /b/: /ˈbaka/ (vaca). There is no labiodental /v/.

Source: Martínez-Celdrán, Fernández-Planas & Carrera-Sabaté (2003), JIPA 33(2), where approximants are allophones; Morala & Egido (2009) for b/v merger.

### Rhotics

Write ⟨rr⟩, word-initial ⟨r⟩ and ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s as trill /r/: /ˈrio/, /ˈpero/ vs /ˈpero/ is wrong, write /ˈperu/ only if spelled perru, and write /onˈrar/. Write single ⟨r⟩ elsewhere, including syllable- and word-final position, as tap /ɾ/: /ˈpeɾa/, /kanˈtaɾ/, /ˈpoɾta/.

Source: Quilis (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, ch. on vibrantes; the Leonese rhotic system is the same per Morala & Egido (2009).

### Coronal fricatives and palatals

Write ⟨c⟩ before e or i, and ⟨z⟩, as /θ/ (distinción, no seseo): /ˈθinku/, /berˈdaθ/. Write ⟨s⟩ as /s/, ⟨x⟩ as /ʃ/ (/ˈʃentʃa/ only if so spelled), ⟨ch⟩ as /tʃ/ (/ˈtʃobu/, /ˈtʃamaɾ/) and ⟨ñ⟩ as /ɲ/ (/ˈaɲu/). Write ⟨ll⟩, including Leonese initial L-, as /ʎ/ (/ˈʎuna/), even though western areas realize it as [tʃ], [ts] or a retroflex. Keep /ʎ/ and /ʝ/ distinct, with no yeísmo: /ˈkaʎe/ vs /ˈmaʝu/. Write ⟨j⟩, and ⟨g⟩ before e or i, as /x/ where they occur in the text. Write ⟨h⟩ as silent.

Source: Specification notes (from Morala & Egido 2009 and the Propuesta de Norma Ortográfica). Dialectal realizations of /ʎ/ are described in Menéndez Pidal (1906) and Zamora Vicente (1967), Dialectología española, ch. on leonés; the broad phoneme is kept here.

### Nasals

Write nasals as spelled: ⟨m⟩ is /m/ and ⟨n⟩ is /n/, also before labials and velars within the word and word-finally. Examples: /ˈkampu/, /ˈteŋɡo/ is wrong, write /ˈtenɡo/; /kanˈθjon/. Velar or labial assimilation of /n/ is not written.

Source: Broad phonemic convention of Martínez-Celdrán, Fernández-Planas & Carrera-Sabaté (2003), JIPA 33(2); Quilis (1993) on neutralization of coda nasals.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
