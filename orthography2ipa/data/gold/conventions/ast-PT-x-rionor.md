# Transcription conventions: Rionorese (ast-PT-x-rionor)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and falls on one of the last three syllables. Write ˈ before the stressed syllable of every content word of two or more syllables. Put ˈ before the whole onset of that syllable, taking the largest legal onset (pl, bɾ, tʃ, ɲ, ʎ count as onsets). Never mark monosyllables, whether content or function words. Placement follows these rules in order. (1) A written acute or circumflex accent marks the stressed vowel: ⟨bós⟩ needs no mark; ⟨sábado⟩ [ˈsabɐdu]. (2) The diphthongs ⟨ie iê uô uâ⟩ arise only under stress, so the syllable that has one is stressed: ⟨piedra⟩ [ˈpjedɾɐ], ⟨puârta⟩ [ˈpwɐɾtɐ], ⟨ruôdra⟩ [ˈrwɔdɾɐ], ⟨baliente⟩ [bɐˈljente]. (3) Otherwise, a word ending in a vowel, in -s, or in -n/-m (plural or verbal endings) stresses the penult: ⟨piquenu⟩ [piˈkenu], ⟨tchamaban⟩ [tʃɐˈmaban]. (4) A word ending in any other consonant (-r, -l, -z) stresses the final syllable: ⟨tchamar⟩ [tʃɐˈmaɾ], ⟨benhir⟩ [beˈɲiɾ]. A lexicon entry overrides rules 2–4. Rionorese has no phonemic vowel length, no geminates and no case endings, so the length, geminate and case parts of this topic do not apply. Write ⟨rr⟩ as the trill [r], not as a geminate, and never use ː.

Source: Carvalho & Dias 1955 (O Falar de Rio de Onor), which describes the shared Western Ibero-Romance (Leonese/Portuguese) penult/final stress pattern; Macias 2003, pp. 25–26, for diphthongization being restricted to tonic syllables.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, so never marked with ˈ, and given their reduced vowels: definite articles (el, l, la, lu, lus, las, al, del), clitic and object pronouns, including hyphen-joined ones (me, te, se, lu, la, le, mos, bos, les), and proclitic possessives (mi, tu, su). Also unstressed: prepositions (a, de, en, cun, por, pa, para, antre, sobre), conjunctions and complementizers (i, que, se, ou, mas, porque, cumo, quando when relative), the negator nun, and the auxiliaries haber/ser/star when they are monosyllabic. Examples: ⟨prendie-lu⟩ → [ˈpɾendje] [lu]; ⟨de la casa⟩ → [de] [lɐ] [ˈkasɐ]; ⟨para el⟩ → [pɐɾɐ] [el]. Stressed, with ˈ when polysyllabic: tonic subject and prepositional pronouns (nós, bós, eilhes/eilles), demonstratives (iesse, aquel), interrogatives used as questions (quando?, cumo?), polysyllabic auxiliary forms (habia [ɐˈbiɐ], staba [sˈtabɐ]), and all adverbs. The vowels of unstressed function words follow the unstressed-vowel convention under 'other': que [ke], lu [lu], la [lɐ].

Source: Carvalho & Dias 1955, which describes the proclitic/enclitic article and pronoun system; Macias 2003, pp. 25–27 (examples prendie-lu, al conseio). The split between stressed and unstressed words follows the general Asturleonese/Portuguese treatment of clitics.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Rionorese has no pausal forms, no case endings and no nunation. Words at the end of a sentence or before a comma are written exactly as in citation form: ⟨muârtu.⟩ [ˈmwɐɾtu], ⟨puârtas,⟩ [ˈpwɐɾtɐs].

Source: Not applicable to Ibero-Romance; no pausal alternations are reported in Carvalho & Dias 1955 or Macias 2003.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form, and write no sandhi across word boundaries. Final -s stays [s] before vowels and voiced consonants, because the lect has no sibilant voicing contrast: ⟨las otras⟩ [lɐs] [ˈotɾɐs], never [lɐz]. Write no vowel elision or synalepha unless the orthography shows it with an apostrophe; an apostrophe already splits the words, so ⟨l'home⟩ → [l] [ˈɔme]. Do not write spirantization of b d g across the boundary or inside words: ⟨la bida⟩ [lɐ] [ˈbidɐ], not [ˈβiðɐ]. Word-final -n stays [n] and does not assimilate to the next consonant: ⟨un pouco⟩ [un] [ˈpowku]. Do not mark resyllabification, since no syllable dots or linking symbols are used.

Source: The sibilant voicing merger is from Macias 2003, p. 27. The other processes are allophonic in descriptions of the lect (Carvalho & Dias 1955), and this broad transcription leaves them out, as the specification notes do with [β].

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vowels as the consonant symbols j and w, never as i̯ or u̯. Rising diphthongs: ⟨ie/iê⟩ [je], as in ⟨pierna⟩ [ˈpjeɾnɐ] and ⟨iera⟩ [ˈjeɾɐ]; ⟨uô⟩ [wɔ], as in ⟨ruôdra⟩ [ˈrwɔdɾɐ]; ⟨uâ⟩ [wɐ], as in ⟨fuârte⟩ [ˈfwɐɾte]. Other ⟨i/u⟩ + vowel sequences in the same syllable also take j/w: ⟨quando⟩ [ˈkwandu], ⟨agua⟩ [ˈaɡwɐ]. Falling diphthongs: ⟨ai⟩ [aj], ⟨ei⟩ [ej], ⟨oi⟩ [oj], ⟨ou⟩ [ow], ⟨eu⟩ [ew]. Examples: ⟨suxeitu⟩ [suˈʃejtu], ⟨pouco⟩ [ˈpowku], ⟨conseio⟩ [kunˈseju]. The yod that comes from lateral vocalization is written j: ⟨mior⟩ [ˈmjoɾ].

Source: Macias 2003, pp. 25–27 (ie, uô, uâ, l-yotization in conseio, mior; ou in pouco), following Carvalho & Dias 1955. The j/w notation follows IPA usage for Ibero-Romance glides.

## Other choices

### betacism

Write both ⟨v⟩ and ⟨b⟩ as [b] in all positions; [v] never occurs: ⟨ber⟩ [beɾ], ⟨bira⟩ [ˈbiɾɐ], ⟨bergonha⟩ [beɾˈɡoɲɐ]. Do not write the intervocalic allophone [β].

Source: Macias 2003, p. 26, fn. 28.

### palatal affricate vs fricative

⟨tch⟩ → [tʃ], as in ⟨tchamar⟩ [tʃɐˈmaɾ] and ⟨matchada⟩ [mɐˈtʃadɐ]. ⟨ch⟩ → [ʃ]. ⟨x⟩ → [ʃ], as in ⟨dixu⟩ [ˈdiʃu]. Write ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩+e,i also as [ʃ], because there is no voicing contrast and the lect spells suxeitu: ⟨suxeitu⟩ [suˈʃejtu].

Source: Macias 2003, pp. 26–27.

### sibilants and seseo

Write the single merged sibilant [s] for ⟨s ss ç z⟩ and ⟨c⟩+e,i in every position, including between vowels. Never write [z] or [θ], and never use apical diacritics: ⟨casa⟩ [ˈkasɐ], ⟨cinco⟩ [ˈsinku], ⟨fazer⟩ [fɐˈseɾ]. The note does not depart from the published descriptions here.

Source: Macias 2003, p. 27 (voicing merger); Vasconcelos 1900, EPM II p. 54 (seseo of the NW border falar).

### initial ⟨ll⟩

Follow the specification and write ⟨ll⟩ as [ʎ], as in ⟨lluna⟩ [ˈʎunɐ]. Write single ⟨l⟩ as [l], and spelled yod (⟨i⟩) as [j]. Do not palatalize an initial ⟨l⟩ that is not spelled ⟨ll⟩. Retaining initial /ʎ/ is medium confidence: Macias 2003 is silent on it.

Source: Frías-Conde 2005, p. 4, for initial /ʎ/ in the zone; Macias 2003, p. 27, for yod outcomes.

### unstressed vowels

Final unstressed ⟨o/u⟩ → [u], as in ⟨muârtu⟩ [ˈmwɐɾtu]. Every unstressed ⟨a⟩ → [ɐ], as in ⟨puârta⟩ [ˈpwɐɾtɐ] and ⟨tchamar⟩ [tʃɐˈmaɾ]. Unstressed ⟨e⟩ → [e], with no reduction to [ɨ]. Pretonic ⟨o⟩ → [u], as in ⟨conseio⟩ [kunˈseju]. Unstressed ⟨i⟩ → [i]. Stressed ⟨a⟩ → [a].

Source: Macias 2003, pp. 25–26 (final -o > u; closed -a, as the uâ description implies); Carvalho & Dias 1955.

### mid-vowel height and nasality

⟨é ó⟩ → [ɛ ɔ] and ⟨ê ô⟩ → [e o]. Unaccented stressed ⟨e o⟩ → [e o] unless a lexicon entry gives otherwise. Write a coda nasal as the consonant spelled, with no nasal vowel diacritic: ⟨un⟩ [un], ⟨tiempo⟩ [ˈtjempu].

Source: Carvalho & Dias 1955; Macias 2003. This spells out what the descriptions say about nasality, which is unlike Portuguese nasal vowels.

### rhotics and palatals

⟨r⟩ between vowels and in codas → [ɾ]. ⟨rr⟩ and word-initial ⟨r⟩ → the apical trill [r], not uvular: ⟨ruôdra⟩ [ˈrwɔdɾɐ]. ⟨nh⟩/⟨ñ⟩ → [ɲ], as in ⟨benhir⟩ [beˈɲiɾ]. ⟨lh⟩ → [ʎ].

Source: Carvalho & Dias 1955; Macias 2003, p. 26 (bergonha, benhir).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
