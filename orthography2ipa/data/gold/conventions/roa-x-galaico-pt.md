# Transcription conventions: Galaico-Portuguese (roa-x-galaico-pt)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and inherited from Latin. Put it on the syllable that carried the Latin stress, which is the same syllable that carries it in the modern Galician or Portuguese cognate. Where an intervocalic consonant has been lost, stress the vowel that continues the Latin stressed vowel: mercee /meɾˈt͡see/ (mercēde), door /doˈoɾ/ (dolōre), seer /seˈeɾ/ (sedēre), soo /ˈsoo/ (sōlu). As a check: a word ending in a vowel or in -s is normally paroxytone (cantiga /kãˈtiɡa/, filhos /ˈfiʎos/). A word ending in -r, -l, -z or a stressed nasal is normally oxytone (amor /aˈmoɾ/, coraçon /koɾaˈt͡sõ/, senhor /seˈɲoɾ/). Verb endings in -an, -en or -on stay paroxytone (aman /ˈamã/, cantaron /kãˈtaɾõ/). Medieval accent marks are unsystematic, so ignore them. Write ˈ before the stressed syllable of every word of two or more syllables, and before every stressed monosyllable (Deus /ˈdeu̯s/, ben /ˈbẽ/). Unstressed monosyllables (see function_words) carry no mark. There is no secondary stress mark. The lect has no phonemic vowel length, so never write ː. Double vowel letters are two syllables in hiatus, not long vowels. There are no geminates: ⟨ss⟩ is /s/ and ⟨rr⟩ is /r/ (see other). There are no case endings, so that part of the topic does not apply.

Source: Maia 1986 (treatment of Latin stress and hiatus from consonant loss); Teyssier 1982 (Old Portuguese hiatus forms such as door, mercee); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, chapter on word stress, for the inherited pattern that the modern languages keep.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ when monosyllabic: definite articles o a os as and their contractions do da dos das no na nos nas ao aos pelo/polo pola; prepositions a de en con per por sen and so; conjunctions e ou que se nen ca mais (meaning 'but'); clitic pronouns me te se lhe lhi lhes lhis o a os as nos vos, including when hyphenated after the verb; relative and complementiser que; negator non directly before a verb or clitic. Examples: /o/ /a/ /de/ /ẽ/ /kõ/ /e/ /ke/ /me/ /ʎe/ /nõ/. Stressed, always marked ˈ: subject and tonic pronouns (eu /ˈeu̯/, tu /ˈtu/, el /ˈel/, min /ˈmĩ/, ti /ˈti/, si /ˈsi/, and nós /ˈnɔs/, vós /ˈvɔs/ when they are not clitics); possessives, including after the article (a mia senhor /a ˈmia seˈɲoɾ/); the indefinite and numeral un/ũu /ˈũ/; all forms of aver, seer/ser, estar, including auxiliaries (é /ˈɛ/, á /ˈa/); non when it ends a clause or stands alone /ˈnõ/. Polysyllabic function words such as pera, sobre, quando, como, porque take their lexical stress mark (/ˈpeɾa/, /ˈkwãdo/). Unstressed function words never take open-mid vowels: o /o/, os /os/.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (the chapter on clitics and prosodic words, for the proclitic/enclitic status of articles, prepositions and object pronouns); Freixeiro Mato 1998 (the section on unstressed words, palabras átonas, which gives the same inventory for Galician).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. The lect has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so transcribe every word identically at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside the sentence. Final unstressed vowels keep their full quality everywhere: -e /e/, -o /o/, -a /a/ (filho /ˈfiʎo/, noite /ˈnoi̯te/).

Source: Maia 1986; Teyssier 1982 (neither describes a pausal alternation for the medieval language).

## Connected speech

Do not write any cross-word process that the spelling does not show. Write each word in its citation form, differing only in the stress marking set out under function_words. Specifically: final -s stays /s/ before a vowel (no [z] liaison), with no resyllabification and no nasal or place assimilation across the boundary. Synalepha and elision in verse are not written unless the scribe wrote them. Where the text spells an elision with an apostrophe (d'amor, m'eu, qu'el), the elided item is its own word, and you transcribe just what is written: d' becomes /d/, m' becomes /m/, qu' becomes /k/, beside /aˈmoɾ/, /ˈeu̯/, /ˈel/. Do not write syllable dots or linking ties.

Source: Broad phonemic practice following Teyssier 1982. Modern sandhi such as /s/ voicing before vowels (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000) is not projected back onto the medieval language.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write falling-diphthong offglides as non-syllabic i̯ and u̯: ei /ei̯/ (lei /ˈlei̯/), ou /ou̯/ as a true diphthong (ouro /ˈou̯ɾo/), ai /ai̯/, oi /oi̯/, ui /ui̯/, eu /eu̯/ (Deus /ˈdeu̯s/), au /au̯/, iu /iu̯/. Nasal diphthongs: ⟨ão⟩ /ãu̯/ (mão /ˈmãu̯/), ⟨ãe ães⟩ /ãi̯ ãi̯s/ (cães /ˈkãi̯s/), ⟨õe⟩ /õi̯/. Write the labial onglide after /k ɡ/ as w: quando /ˈkwãdo/, guarda /ˈɡwaɾda/. Before e and i, ⟨qu gu⟩ are plain /k ɡ/: que /ke/, guisa /ˈɡiza/. Any other sequence of i or u plus a vowel is a hiatus of two full vowels: mia /ˈmia/, Maria /maˈɾia/, lũa /ˈlũa/, irmãa /iɾˈmãa/. Never use j as a glide, because ⟨i j⟩ before a vowel at the start of a syllable is the consonant /ʒ/ (see other).

Source: Teyssier 1982 (the diphthong ou still undiminished, and the nasal diphthongs); Maia 1986 (the inventory of diphthongs in Galician-Portuguese documents); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (falling diphthongs analysed as vowel plus glide). The notation i̯ u̯ is the house choice.

## Other choices

### sibilants and affricates

Follow the spelling with the medieval values, using tie bars throughout. ⟨ç⟩, and ⟨c⟩ before e or i, is /t͡s/ (coraçon /koɾaˈt͡sõ/, cinco /ˈt͡sĩko/). ⟨z⟩ is /d͡z/ in every position, including word-final (fazer /faˈd͡zeɾ/, vez /ˈved͡z/). ⟨ss⟩ is /s/, and so is ⟨s⟩ at the start of a word, after a consonant or at the end of a word (passar /paˈsaɾ/, senhor /seˈɲoɾ/). Single intervocalic ⟨s⟩ is /z/ (casa /ˈkaza/). ⟨ch⟩ is /t͡ʃ/ (chamar /t͡ʃaˈmaɾ/) and ⟨x⟩ is /ʃ/ (dixe /ˈdiʃe/). The apical versus dental place of s/z against t͡s/d͡z is not marked. The note writes /ts/ without a tie; this set writes /t͡s/ to match /d͡z/.

Source: Teyssier 1982 (the medieval four-sibilant system and ch /t͡ʃ/ against x /ʃ/); Maia 1986.

### ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩ before front vowels

Write /ʒ/: ja /ʒa/, gente /ˈʒẽte/, hoje/oje /ˈoʒe/. Some accounts reconstruct an earlier affricate /d͡ʒ/ parallel to /t͡ʃ/. This set does not use it.

Source: Teyssier 1982; Maia 1986. The /d͡ʒ/ reconstruction found in older handbooks is not followed.

### labials

Keep /b/ and /v/ apart exactly as spelled. Consonantal ⟨u⟩ or ⟨v⟩ is /v/ (aver /aˈveɾ/, uos/vos /vos/), and ⟨b⟩ is /b/ (bever /beˈveɾ/). Do not spirantise. ⟨f⟩ is /f/ (filho /ˈfiʎo/).

Source: Maia 1986; Teyssier 1982.

### rhotics

Write /ɾ/ for single ⟨r⟩ between vowels and in syllable codas (amor /aˈmoɾ/). Write /r/ for ⟨rr⟩, for word-initial ⟨r⟩, and for ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s (rei /ˈrei̯/, terra /ˈtɛra/, onra /ˈõra/).

Source: Maia 1986; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (tap/trill distribution, which the medieval language already shows).

### palatals

⟨lh⟩ is /ʎ/ and ⟨nh⟩ is /ɲ/ (filho /ˈfiʎo/, senhor /seˈɲoɾ/). For Galician-style ⟨ll⟩ and ⟨nn⟩ (and ⟨ñ⟩), write /ʎ/ and /ɲ/ only where the modern cognates have a palatal, as in fillo /ˈfiʎo/. Otherwise write /l n/, as in Latinising anno /ˈano/.

Source: Maia 1986 (the scribal variants for the palatals in Galician documents); Teyssier 1982 (the Occitan origin of lh and nh).

### vowel quality and reduction

Stressed syllables have seven vowels, /a ɛ e i ɔ o u/. Choose open or close mid by etymology and the modern reflex: Latin ĕ and ŏ give /ɛ ɔ/, Latin ē ĭ ō ŭ give /e o/ (terra /ˈtɛra/, porta /ˈpɔɾta/, mesa /ˈmeza/, boca /ˈboka/). Unstressed syllables have /a e i o u/, with no reduction to ɐ, ɨ or u and no raising of final vowels. This departs from Mateus & d'Andrade's modern European Portuguese reduction.

Source: Maia 1986; Teyssier 1982. The vowel reduction in Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 is post-medieval and is not applied.

### nasality

Write a nasal vowel with tilde, and no following nasal consonant, for spelled ⟨ã õ ẽ ĩ ũ⟩ and for a vowel followed by ⟨m⟩ or ⟨n⟩ in the coda, whether word-final or before a consonant (non /nõ/, ben /ˈbẽ/, tempo /ˈtẽpo/, cantar /kãˈtaɾ/). Write intervocalic ⟨m n⟩ as onset /m n/ with an oral vowel before it (amor /aˈmoɾ/). Nasal vowel plus vowel stays a hiatus (lũa /ˈlũa/, irmãa /iɾˈmãa/). The note calls nasal vowels not fully phonemicised; this set still writes them uniformly, following the medieval -m/-n/tilde spellings.

Source: Teyssier 1982 (nasal vowels and the medieval nasal hiatus forms); Maia 1986 (the interchangeable -m, -n and tilde spellings).

### silent and variant letters

⟨h⟩ is silent (home /ˈɔme/, ha /ˈa/). ⟨y⟩ is the vowel /i/, or the glide i̯ after a vowel. ⟨i j⟩ before a vowel at the start of a syllable is /ʒ/ (ia /ʒa/). ⟨g⟩ before a, o or u, and ⟨gu⟩ before e or i, are /ɡ/.

Source: Maia 1986 (graphic variation in documents).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
