# Transcription conventions: Sanabrese (ast-x-sanabria)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and falls on one of the last three syllables, as in the rest of Asturleonese. Write the primary-stress mark ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables that is not in the unstressed list under function_words. Do not mark stress on monosyllables. Mark no secondary stress. Default placement: a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s (plural or verb ending) is stressed on the penult. A word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the final syllable. A written accent overrides both defaults. Infinitives are oxytone: falar faˈlar, chover tʃoˈβer is wrong, write tʃoˈber. In a diphthong the mark goes before the whole syllable and stress falls on the syllabic vowel: piedra ˈpjedɾa, cuorpo ˈkworpu, peito ˈpei̯tu, castiello kasˈtjeʎu. In a hiatus the stressed i or u carries the stress: teníe teˈnie. Vowel length is not distinctive, so length is never written. There are no geminates; ll is the single consonant /ʎ/: palla ˈpaʎa. There are no case endings.

Source: Frías-Conde (c. 2005), El Habla de Sanabria, pp. 2–3 (stressed versus final vowel systems, tonic diphthongs). Krüger (1923), El dialecto de San Ciprián de Sanabria (RFE, Anejo), accentuation. García Arias (2003), Gramática histórica de la lengua asturiana, on Asturleonese accent placement.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Write the following words with no stress mark, even when they have two syllables. Articles: el, la, los, las, lo, l', o, a, os, as. Prepositions: a, de, en, con, por, pa, pra, sin, so, ta. Conjunctions: y, e, ou, o, que, si, ni, mas, pero, cuando when it is not interrogative, como when it is not interrogative. Object and reflexive clitics: me, te, se, nos, vos, os, lle, lles, lo, la, los, las, le, les, y. Proclitic possessives: mi, tu, su, mios, tous. Negation: nun, non, nin. Relative que. Example: pero ˈ… is written peɾo, para is written paɾa. Write the following words with stress: subject and prepositional pronouns, written ˈjo, ˈtu, ˈel, ˈnos, ˈmi when they stand after a preposition; demonstratives; interrogatives and exclamatives; numerals; quantifiers; adverbs; and every form of ser, tar and haber with two or more syllables. Monosyllabic auxiliaries (hai, ha, he, ye, ten) take no mark under the monosyllable rule. An enclitic separated by a hyphen is its own word with no stress, and its host keeps its own stress: dá-lle ˈda ʎe. An apostrophised article or pronoun is written as its bare consonant: l' becomes l.

Source: Krüger (1923), El dialecto de San Ciprián de Sanabria, on articles and pronouns. García Arias (2003), Gramática histórica de la lengua asturiana, on clitics and unstressed forms in Asturleonese. Zamora Vicente (1967), Dialectología española, chapter on leonés (article and pronoun forms).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Sanabrese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written in the same form at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside a phrase.

Source: Frías-Conde (c. 2005), El Habla de Sanabria, pp. 2–4. No pausal alternation is described for Asturleonese.

## Connected speech

Write every word in its citation form. Write no cross-word sandhi: no vowel elision, no synalepha, no resyllabification of a final consonant onto the next word, no voicing of final /s/ before a voiced sound, and no nasal place assimilation across words. Example: los ojos stays los ˈɔxos is wrong, write los ˈoxos; en casa en ˈkasa. Elision is written only when the spelling itself elides with an apostrophe: l'auga is written as two words, l and ˈau̯ɣa is wrong, write l ˈau̯ga. Inside a word, write phonemes, not allophones. /b d ɡ/ stay b d ɡ and are not written as β ð ɣ: ruoda ˈrwoda, fuogo ˈfwoɡu. /n/ before a consonant is written n: cantar kanˈtar. /m/ is written m only where the word has /m/.

Source: Broad phonemic practice applied to the inventory in Frías-Conde (c. 2005), El Habla de Sanabria, p. 3. Allophonic spirantisation and assimilation are described in Krüger (1923) but are left out of a broad transcription.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the non-syllabic element of a rising diphthong as j or w: tierra ˈtjera, piedra ˈpjedɾa, ñiebla ˈɲjebla, cuorpo ˈkworpu, lluogo ˈʎwoɡu, puode ˈpwode, ñuoite ˈɲwoi̯te, cuarda (Guadramil) ˈkwarda. Write the second element of a falling diphthong as i̯ or u̯: feito ˈfei̯tu, lleite ˈʎei̯te, diñeiro diˈɲei̯ɾu, cheirar tʃei̯ˈɾar, moito ˈmoi̯tu, auga ˈau̯ɡa. A sequence of two syllabic vowels is a hiatus and gets no glide: teníe teˈnie, falarí (Rionor) falaˈɾi. Tonic Ŏ is written wo, never we, wherever the spelling has ⟨uo⟩. Consonantal ⟨y⟩ in an onset is the fricative /ʝ/, not a glide: mayo ˈmaʝu.

Source: Frías-Conde (c. 2005), El Habla de Sanabria, p. 3 (tonic diphthongs ie and uo, Guadramil ua, Rionor -í) and p. 4 (ei and oi from kt). Krüger (1923) for the falling diphthongs ei and ou.

## Other choices

### Final unstressed vowels

Follow the three-way final system. Write unstressed final /e/ as e, final /a/ as a, and the reduced back vowel as u, whether the spelling has ⟨o⟩ or ⟨u⟩: fillo ˈfiʎu, forno ˈfornu, llobo ˈʎobu, noche ˈnotʃe, chuvia ˈtʃubja. The centralised quality the source writes /U/ is shown in broad IPA as plain u. Plural -os is written -us: llobos ˈʎobus. This departs from the orthographic ⟨o⟩ used in the note's examples.

Source: Frías-Conde (c. 2005), El Habla de Sanabria, pp. 2–3 (final vowels e, a, U).

### Sibilants and palatals

Keep all four sibilant phonemes distinct: ⟨s⟩ = s, ⟨x⟩ = ʃ, ⟨z⟩ and ⟨c⟩ before e or i = θ, ⟨ch⟩ = tʃ. Examples: facer faˈθer, chave ˈtʃabe, xente ˈʃente. Initial L- and ⟨ll⟩ are /ʎ/, never merged with /ʝ/ (no yeísmo): lluz ˈʎuθ, muller muˈʎer. Initial ⟨ñ⟩ is ɲ: ñabo ˈɲabu. Latin F- is f: falar faˈlar.

Source: Frías-Conde (c. 2005), El Habla de Sanabria, pp. 3–4 (inventory and comparative table). The four-way sibilant system is inferred from the inventory there.

### Rhotics

Write the trill r word-initially, after n, l or s, and for ⟨rr⟩. Write the tap ɾ between vowels and in onset clusters. Write r in coda position, including at word end: ruoda ˈrwoda, tierra ˈtjera, piedra ˈpjedɾa, chorar tʃoˈɾar, facer faˈθer.

Source: Krüger (1923), El dialecto de San Ciprián de Sanabria, consonant description. This is the general Asturleonese and Ibero-Romance pattern in García Arias (2003).

### Sub-zone variants

Transcribe the general Sanabrese form the spelling shows, and transcribe sub-zone forms only where the spelling has them. Spelled oit (Vigo de Sanabria) is written oi̯t: moito ˈmoi̯tu. Spelled eit is written ei̯t: peito ˈpei̯tu. Spelled ua or uo (Guadramil) is written wa or wo: muarto ˈmwartu. Spelled -í (Rionor) is written i, and spelled -íe is written ie.

Source: Frías-Conde (c. 2005), El Habla de Sanabria, pp. 3–4 (Vigo de Sanabria, Guadramil, Rionor).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
