# Transcription conventions: São Tomé and Príncipe Portuguese (pt-ST)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is placed by the standard Portuguese lexical rule, read from the spelling. (1) A written acute or circumflex accent marks the stressed syllable: café [kaˈfɛ], médico [ˈmɛdiko], você [voˈse]. (2) Words ending in -a, -e, -o, with or without -s, and in -am, -em, -ens are stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkaza], falam [ˈfalãw̃], homens [ˈɔmẽj̃s]. (3) Otherwise, stress falls on the last syllable. This covers endings in -i, -u, -l, -r, -z, -x, -im, -um, -ão and oral diphthongs: papel [paˈpɛl], falar [faˈlaɾ], aqui [aˈki], irmão [iɾˈmãw̃]. Write ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: pé [ˈpɛ], é [ˈɛ]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. No secondary stress is marked. Stressed mid vowels follow the orthographic accent (é ó = [ɛ ɔ], ê ô = [e o]). Without an accent they take their standard lexical quality: festa [ˈfɛsta], pessoa [peˈsoa], porta [ˈpɔɾta]. The open tendency in the notes is a phonetic trend and is not written beyond this. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: the lect has no phonemic length and no case morphology, and rr, ss are digraphs for single consonants.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), The Phonology of Portuguese, ch. 6 (word stress), for the stress rule. Hagemeijer (2009) and R. Gonçalves (2010, MA thesis, Univ. Lisboa) on São Tomé Portuguese, which keeps standard lexical stress.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed proclitics or enclitics, written without ˈ and with full, unreduced vowels: definite articles o a os as [o a os as]; the prepositions de em a por para com and their contractions do da no na ao à pelo pela; the conjunctions e ou que se mas nem; the relative que; clitic pronouns me te se lhe nos vos o a lo la. Examples: de [de], que [ke], me [me], para [paɾa], no [no]. The conjunction e is [e], never [i]. The following carry stress: subject and strong pronouns (eu [ˈew], ele [ˈele], mim [ˈmĩ], você [voˈse]); indefinite articles (um [ˈũ], uma [ˈuma]); demonstratives; possessives; numerals; the negator não [ˈnãw̃]; question words; all verb forms, including auxiliaries and copulas (é [ˈɛ], está [esˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], vai [ˈvaj]).

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 on clitics and prosodic words. Unreduced vowels in clitics follow the five-vowel unstressed system described for São Tomé Portuguese by Brandão (org.) (2018), Duas variedades africanas do português, chapters on vowels.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word at a comma or at the end of a sentence is written exactly as elsewhere, with no deletion of final vowels: casa [ˈkaza], dele [ˈdele].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000); no São Tomé Portuguese description reports pausal alternations.

## Connected speech

Only sibilant sandhi is written. A word-final s/z is written [z] before a word starting with a vowel or a voiced consonant: os amigos [oz aˈmiɡos], dois dias [ˈdojz ˈdias]. It is written [s] before a voiceless consonant and before a pause: os pés [os ˈpɛs]. Word-internal coda s follows the same voicing rule: mesmo [ˈmezmo], gosta [ˈɡɔsta]. It is never palatalised to [ʃ ʒ], as the notes also state. Word-final r stays [ɾ] before a vowel: falar a [faˈlaɾ a]. Vowel elision or crasis across words (de a, que é) is not written: each word keeps its full form, de amigo [de aˈmiɡo]. Nasal place assimilation is not written. Every process changes only the words at the boundary; words are never joined.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 3 (fricative sandhi). The non-palatal coda [s] follows Bouchard (2017), Linguistic variation and change in the Portuguese of São Tomé (PhD, NYU), and Brandão (org.) (2018) on coda /S/ in São Tomé Portuguese.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write nonsyllabic glides as [j w], and as [j̃ w̃] in nasal diphthongs. Never use i̯ u̯. Falling diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], meu [ˈmew], noite [ˈnojte], mãe [ˈmãj̃], são [ˈsãw̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃]. An unstressed i or u before a vowel in the same word is written as a rising glide: água [ˈaɡwa], história [isˈtɔɾja], quando [ˈkwãdo]. A stressed í or ú, or one marked by an accent, stays a syllabic vowel in hiatus: saída [saˈida], país [paˈis].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 (glides and diphthongs), applied with the IPA symbols [j w].

## Other choices

### unstressed vowels

Use five unstressed vowels [i e a o u], written with the quality the spelling indicates. There is no [ɨ] or [ɐ] and no deletion. Unstressed e and o are not raised to [i u], even word-finally: grande [ˈɡɾãde], menino [meˈnino], cidade [siˈdade]. Unstressed a is [a]: casa [ˈkaza].

Source: This follows the notes and Brandão (org.) (2018) on pretonic and postonic mid vowels in São Tomé Portuguese, where mid realisations predominate. Some speakers raise postonic finals; that variation is not written.

### rhotics

Write the tap [ɾ] for single r between vowels, in onset clusters and in coda: caro [ˈkaɾo], prato [ˈpɾato], porta [ˈpɔɾta]. Write the alveolar trill [r] for word-initial r, for rr, and after n, l, s: rua [ˈrua], carro [ˈkaro], honra [ˈõra]. Infinitive and other final r is always written [ɾ], never deleted.

Source: The trill choice follows the notes. Bouchard (2017) reports uvular [ʁ] spreading among younger speakers and frequent deletion of coda r; this transcription follows the alveolar norm instead.

### nasal vowels

Write nasal vowels with a tilde and leave the nasal consonant of the coda unwritten: campo [ˈkãpo], fim [ˈfĩ], ontem [ˈõtẽj̃]. Nasal a is [ã], not [ɐ̃]: ano [ˈano] with an onset n, but canto [ˈkãto].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2. The [ã] quality follows the absence of [ɐ] reported for African varieties (Gonçalves, P. 1996, for the PALOP comparison).

### coronal stops and laterals

t and d are never affricated before i: tipo [ˈtipo], dia [ˈdia]. Coda l is [l], not [ɫ] or [w]: Brasil [bɾaˈzil]. lh = [ʎ], nh = [ɲ], ch = [ʃ], j and g before e/i = [ʒ].

Source: Hagemeijer (2009) and R. Gonçalves (2010) on São Tomé Portuguese, which follows the European pattern here.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
