# Transcription conventions: Colombian Spanish (Paisa/Medellín) (es-CO-x-paisa)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, including stressed monosyllables: sol [ˈsol], mesa [ˈmesa], papel [paˈpel], árbol [ˈarβol], música [ˈmusika]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark: el sol [el ˈsol]. The stress is lexical, so read it from the spelling. A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel. Without an accent, stress falls on the penult if the word ends in a vowel, -n or -s, and on the final syllable otherwise: hablan [ˈaβlan], ciudad [sjuˈðað]. Voseo verb forms are oxytone as spelled: vos tenés [ˈbos teˈnes], hablás [aˈβlas], sos [ˈsos], vení [beˈni], decime [deˈsime]. Enclitics do not move the stress: dámelo [ˈdamelo]. Adverbs in -mente carry one mark only, on the adjective's stressed syllable: rápidamente [ˈrapiðamente]. Place ˈ at the syllable boundary. Onsets are maximal, so muta cum liquida clusters stay together: otro [ˈotɾo], hablar [aˈβlaɾ]. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Spanish has no phonemic length and no case endings. Identical vowels in a word are two syllables: leer [leˈeɾ], creer [kɾeˈeɾ].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on word stress (lexical stress, orthographic accent rules, unstressed vs stressed words); voseo forms per Fontanella de Weinberg 1992, section on voseo in Colombia.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ: definite articles el la los las lo; prenominal possessives mi tu su mis tus sus; object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos los las les. Also unstressed: all simple prepositions except según (a ante bajo con contra de desde en entre hacia hasta para por sin sobre tras); conjunctions y e o u ni que si pero sino porque como; relatives que quien donde cuando como without a written accent; the title don/doña. Examples: con la casa [kon la ˈkasa], para mi papá [paɾa mi paˈpa], que te lo dé [ke te lo ˈðe], desde aquí [dezde aˈki]. Write para in full [paɾa]; do not write colloquial pa. Stressed, with ˈ: indefinite articles un una unos unas [ˈun], [ˈuna]; subject and prepositional pronouns yo vos él ella usted nosotros ustedes ellos mí ti [ˈɟʝo], [ˈbos], [ˈel], [ˈmi]; según [seˈɣun]. Also stressed: interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent qué cómo dónde cuándo quién [ˈke], [ˈkomo]; demonstratives, numerals, tonic possessives (mío, nuestro), adverbs (no [ˈno], más [ˈmas], muy [ˈmui̯]). All verb forms are stressed, including the auxiliaries and copulas haber, ser and estar: ha comido [ˈa koˈmiðo], es [ˈes], está [esˈta]. Pairs that differ only in the accent follow the spelling: tu [tu] vs tú [ˈtu], el [el] vs él [ˈel], si [si] vs sí [ˈsi], mi [mi] vs mí [ˈmi], que [ke] vs qué [ˈke].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on stress (section on stressed and unstressed words); Quilis-style closed list of átonos as summarized there.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Spanish has no pausal forms, no final short-vowel loss, no case endings and no nunation. Every word has the same form at a comma, at the end of the sentence and in the middle. The only effect of punctuation is that a comma or sentence boundary counts as a pause for the positional allophones under connected_speech and other: after a comma, write stop [b d ɡ] and affricate [ɟʝ].

Source: Hualde 2005 (no pausal morphology in Spanish; the pause as the conditioning environment for stop and affricate allophones).

## Connected speech

Write the following processes across word boundaries, inside each word's own IPA. (1) Spirantization: /b d ɡ/ are stops [b d ɡ] after a pause and after any consonant, in the Colombian highland pattern. They are approximants [β ð ɣ] after a vowel or glide, written without the lowering diacritic, within and across words: la boca [la ˈβoka], un beso [um ˈbeso], los dedos [loz ˈdeðos], el gato [el ˈɡato], algo [ˈalɡo], barba [ˈbaɾba], cada [ˈkaða], de Bogotá [de βoɣoˈta]. (2) Nasal place assimilation: /n/ is [m] before p b m and [ŋ] before k ɡ h, within and across words. It stays [n] elsewhere, including before f: un poco [um ˈpoko], en casa [eŋ ˈkasa], tengo [ˈteŋɡo], en fin [en ˈfin]. (3) Sibilant voicing: /s/ is [z] before a voiced consonant (b d ɡ m n l r ɟʝ), within and across words: mismo [ˈmizmo], los mismos [loz ˈmizmos], es grande [ˈez ˈɡɾande]. Do not write the following: resyllabification of a final consonant onto a following vowel (los otros stays [los ˈotɾos]); synalepha, vowel merger or gliding across words (la amiga [la aˈmiɣa], te espero [te esˈpeɾo], mi hijo [mi ˈixo] is not merged); elision of words or segments. The conjunction y is always [i] and e is [e]: y ella [i ˈeɟʝa].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on the allophones of /b d ɡ/ (including the note that Colombian varieties keep stops after non-nasal consonants), nasal assimilation, /s/ voicing, and vowel sequences across words. The Colombian stop pattern is also described in Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 on Andean Colombian consonantism.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write a prenuclear (rising) glide as [j] or [w]: tierra [ˈtjera], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [sjuˈðað], cuatro [ˈkwatɾo], huevo [ˈweβo]. Write a postnuclear (falling) glide as [i̯] or [u̯]: aire [ˈai̯ɾe], causa [ˈkau̯sa], hoy [ˈoi̯], muy [ˈmui̯], ley [ˈlei̯], deuda [ˈdeu̯ða]. In ui/iu, the second element is the nucleus: cuida [ˈkwiða], viuda [ˈbjuða]. An accented high vowel is a nucleus in hiatus: río [ˈrio], país [paˈis], día [ˈdia]. Non-high vowel sequences stay in hiatus, without the rustic raising to a glide: teatro [teˈatɾo], poeta [poˈeta], peor [peˈoɾ]. Other unaccented high-vowel sequences are diphthongs: cliente [ˈkljente], viaje [ˈbjaxe] (see /x/ in other). Consonantal /ʝ/ spelled y, ll or hi+vowel is not a glide: see the yeísmo entry in other. Word-final y after a vowel is [i̯]: rey [ˈrei̯].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on diphthongs, glides and hiatus (its [j w] vs [i̯ u̯] notation for on-glides vs off-glides).

## Other choices

### seseo

c before e/i and z are [s], with no /θ/: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato], ciudad [sjuˈðað]. Write the Paisa apicoalveolar tendency as plain broad [s], not [s̺].

Source: Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 (seseo in all American Spanish); Penny 2002, chapter on American Spanish; Hualde 2005, on the apical /s/ of Antioquia.

### coda /s/

Paisa keeps coda /s/ as a sibilant: write [s], or [z] before a voiced consonant per connected_speech. Never write aspiration [h] or deletion: los dos [loz ˈðos] is wrong. Write los dos [loz ˈdos] (stop after consonant), estos [ˈestos], más [ˈmas].

Source: Fontanella de Weinberg 1992, on conservative highland/Andean Colombian /s/; spec note 'conservative coda /s/'.

### yeísmo and /ʝ/

ll and y (consonantal), and hi before a vowel, are one phoneme /ʝ/; never write [ʎ]. Write the affricate [ɟʝ] after a pause and after any consonant, within and across words: yo [ˈɟʝo] (sentence-initial), con yuca [koŋ ˈɟʝuka], el llavero [el ɟʝaˈβeɾo]. Write [ʝ] after a vowel or glide, within and across words: calle [ˈkaʝe], la llave [la ˈʝaβe], mayo [ˈmaʝo]. This is the positional rule of the published descriptions. The spec note's 'strong affrication' refers to the [ɟʝ] contexts and is not a reason to write [ɟʝ] between vowels.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on palatals (/ʝ/ allophony and yeísmo); Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 (yeísmo in Colombia).

### /x/

Write j, and g before e/i, as glottal [h], following descriptions of Colombian Spanish, including Antioquia, where the weak glottal realization is general: hijo [ˈiho], gente [ˈhente], viaje [ˈbjahe], México [ˈmehiko]. Use [h] also in the earlier glides examples (cliente stays [ˈkljente]). Read any [x] above as [h]. A preceding /n/ is [ŋ]: monja [ˈmoŋha].

Source: Fontanella de Weinberg 1992, on /x/ in Colombia; Hualde 2005, on dialectal [h] for /x/. This departs from the velar [x] of conservative peninsular descriptions.

### rhotics

Single r between vowels and in codas is a tap [ɾ]: pero [ˈpeɾo], hablar [aˈβlaɾ], verde [ˈbeɾde]. rr, word-initial r and r after n l s are a trill [r]: perro [ˈpero], rosa [ˈrosa], honra [ˈonra], Israel [izraˈel]. Word-initial r keeps [r] after a vowel-final word: la rosa [la ˈrosa]. Do not write assibilated rhotics.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on rhotics.

### /d/ in -ado and word-finally

Keep intervocalic /d/ as [ð] in all words, including -ado participles: cansado [kanˈsaðo]. Keep word-final /d/ as [ð]: usted [usˈteð], verdad [beɾˈðað]. Final /d/ is weak and often dropped in Colombian casual speech, but the transcription writes the careful standard form.

Source: Hualde 2005, on the weakening of /d/; Fontanella de Weinberg 1992, on retention of intervocalic consonants in Andean Colombian.

### other segments

Write ch as [tʃ] (mucho [ˈmutʃo]), ñ as [ɲ] (año [ˈaɲo]), x between vowels as [ks] (taxi [ˈtaksi]), qu/c as [k], h as silent. Write five vowels [a e i o u], with no reduction of unstressed vowels.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on vowels and on the consonant inventory.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
