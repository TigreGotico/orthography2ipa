# Transcription conventions: Cearense Portuguese (pt-BR-x-ce)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, including stressed monosyllables: casa [ˈkazɐ], café [kaˈfɛ], pé [ˈpɛ]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Secondary stress is not written. Stress is lexical and follows the orthography. A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed vowel: pássaro [ˈpasaɾu], você [vɔˈse]. Without an accent mark, stress falls on the last syllable when the word ends in r, l, z, x, i, u, a nasal diphthong or a tilde vowel: falar [faˈla], Brasil [bɾaˈziw], aqui [aˈki], pão [ˈpɐ̃w̃]. Otherwise it falls on the penult, including words ending in -a, -e, -o, -am, -em and -s: costas [ˈkahtɐs], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so these do not apply. Double rr is the single sound [h], not a geminate: carro [ˈkahu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (word stress); Silva 2002 (stress and accentuation).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ and reduced vowels: definite articles o a os as [u ɐ us ɐs]. Prepositions and contractions: de do da dos das [dʒi du dɐ dus dɐs], em no na [ĩ nu nɐ], ao à aos às [aw a aws as], por [poh], pelo pela [pelu pelɐ], para [paɾɐ], pra [pɾɐ], com [kũ], sem [sẽj̃]. Conjunctions: e [i], ou [o], nem [nẽj̃], mas [mas], se [si], and complementiser or relative que [ki]. Clitic pronouns, whether free or hyphen-split: me te se lhe nos vos lhes [mi tʃi si ʎi nus vus ʎis], o a os as lo la [u ɐ us ɐs lu lɐ]. Function words do not undergo pretonic vowel opening. Stressed, with ˈ: all nouns, adjectives, adverbs and verbs, including auxiliaries and the copula: é [ˈɛ], está [ihˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], vai [ˈvaj], foi [ˈfoj]. Also stressed: não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], indefinite articles um uma [ˈũ ˈumɐ], numerals, demonstratives (esse [ˈesi]), possessives (meu [ˈmew]), and tonic or subject pronouns (eu [ˈew], ele [ˈeli], mim [ˈmĩ]). Interrogatives are stressed (quê [ˈke], onde [ˈõdʒi]), and so are polysyllabic prepositions and conjunctions other than para, pelo and pela (sobre [ˈsobɾi], até [aˈtɛ], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], como [ˈkomu]).

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (clitics and prosodic words); Callou & Leite 2001 (unstressed monosyllables).

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Before a pause (sentence end or comma), each word takes its ordinary word-final form. Final unstressed vowels are written [i u ɐ]; they are not dropped or devoiced in the transcription: cidade [siˈdadʒi]. Final /S/ is [s] and final /R/ follows the rhotics rule.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000; Silva 2002.

## Connected speech

Each word keeps its own IPA string, and only these boundary processes are written. (1) Word-final /S/ before a vowel-initial word is written [z] on the first word: os olhos [uz ˈɔʎus], mas é [maz ˈɛ]. Before a consonant or a pause it stays [s], following the note: as casas [as ˈkazɐs]. (2) Word-final /R/ before a vowel-initial word is written as the tap [ɾ], including in infinitives: amor eterno [aˈmoɾ ɛˈtɛhnu], falar isso [faˈlaɾ ˈisu]. (3) Vowel sandhi is not written: elision, degemination and diphthongisation across words leave both words with their full vowels, as in de água [dʒi ˈaɡwɐ]. (4) Coda /l/ stays [w] before a vowel. (5) Palatalisation and pretonic rules apply inside the word only. No word is merged with or moved into its neighbour.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 7 (sandhi); Silva 2002 (connected speech); final [s] before consonant per the specification note.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as [j w], never as i̯ u̯. Nasal glides are [j̃ w̃]. Examples: pai [ˈpaj], céu [ˈsɛw], noite [ˈnojtʃi], mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], pão [ˈpɐ̃w̃]. Final -em is [ẽj̃] and final -am is [ɐ̃w̃]: bem [ˈbẽj̃], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. Coda /l/ is [w]: sal [ˈsaw], alto [ˈawtu]. Unstressed i/u next to a vowel inside a word is a glide: história [ihˈtɔɾjɐ], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. Stressed i/u is a vowel in hiatus: saúde [saˈudʒi], dia [ˈdʒiɐ]. Monophthongisation: ou is always [o] (sou [ˈso]); ei is [e] before [ɾ ʃ ʒ] (feira [ˈfeɾɐ]); ai is [a] before [ʃ] (caixa [ˈkaʃɐ]). A glide [j] is inserted between a stressed vowel and word-final /S/: três [ˈtɾejs], paz [ˈpajs], nós [ˈnɔjs], arroz [aˈhojs].

Source: Silva 2002 (diphthongs, monophthongisation and glide epenthesis); Callou & Leite 2001.

## Other choices

### pretonic mid vowels

Pretonic e/o in content words is open [ɛ ɔ]: você [vɔˈse], morar [mɔˈɾa], peteca [pɛˈtɛkɐ], eterno [ɛˈtɛhnu]. There are three exceptions. (a) When the immediately following vowel is stressed [i] or [u], e/o raise to [i u] (harmony): menino [miˈninu], coruja [kuˈɾuʒɐ]. (b) In a nasal syllable the vowel is [ẽ õ]: sentir [sẽˈtʃih]. (c) Word-initial es-/ex- before a consonant is [ih], and des- is [dʒih]: estado [ihˈtadu], desculpa [dʒihˈkuwpɐ]. Pretonic a is [a].

Source: Cardoso et al. 2014 (ALiB pretonic-vowel isoglosses); Silva 2002 (Northeastern pretonic vowels); specification note.

### post-tonic vowels

Word-final unstressed e/o/a are written [i u ɐ]: fome [ˈfõmi], carro [ˈkahu], casa [ˈkazɐ]. Word-medial post-tonic e/o are written [e o]: número [ˈnumeɾu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000; Callou & Leite 2001.

### palatalisation of /t d/

/t d/ become [tʃ dʒ] before any [i], whether from i, final unstressed e, or raised e, and before [j]: tio [ˈtʃiu], cidade [siˈdadʒi], de [dʒi], pátio [ˈpatʃju]. Palatalisation is retained as the Fortaleza prestige norm, following the note.

Source: Cardoso et al. 2014 (ALiB palatalisation isoglosses); specification note.

### coda /S/

Word-internal coda /S/ before a consonant is written [h], whether the consonant is voiced or voiceless: costas [ˈkahtɐs], mesmo [ˈmehmu], desde [ˈdehdʒi]. Word-final /S/ is [s], or [z] before a vowel. Neither the chiado [ʃ ʒ] nor [ɦ] is used.

Source: Specification note; Callou & Leite 2001 (coda /S/ variation).

### rhotics

Strong r (word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s) is [h]: rua [ˈhuɐ], carro [ˈkahu]. Word-internal coda r is [h]: porta [ˈpɔhtɐ]. Intervocalic r and r in onset clusters is [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], três [ˈtɾejs]. Word-final r is dropped in verb forms (falar [faˈla], comer [kɔˈme]) and written [h] in other words (mar [ˈmah], amor [aˈmoh]). Before a vowel-initial word, word-final r is always [ɾ].

Source: Callou & Leite 2001 (rhotic variation by region); Silva 2002.

### nasal vowels

Vowels before a coda nasal letter or with a tilde are written as nasal vowels with no nasal consonant: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], lindo [ˈlĩdu], ponte [ˈpõtʃi]. Stressed vowels before a following m, n or nh are nasalised, with a as [ɐ̃] and e/o as [ẽ õ]: cama [ˈkɐ̃mɐ], fome [ˈfõmi]. Unstressed vowels before a nasal consonant are not marked: banana [baˈnɐ̃nɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 8 (nasality); Silva 2002.

### palatal consonants and epenthesis

lh is written [ʎ] and nh is written [ɲ]: olho [ˈoʎu], banho [ˈbɐ̃ɲu]. A medial obstruent with no vowel before another consonant gets an epenthetic [i], which triggers palatalisation: ritmo [ˈhitʃimu], advogado [adʒivɔˈɡadu], pneu [piˈnew].

Source: Silva 2002 (epenthesis and palatals); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
