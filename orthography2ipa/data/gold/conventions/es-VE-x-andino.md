# Transcription conventions: Andean Venezuelan Spanish (es-VE-x-andino)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical, and the spelling shows where it falls. A written accent marks the stressed vowel. Otherwise, a word ending in a vowel, n or s is stressed on the penult, and any other word on the final syllable. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable, including any glide or onset cluster: casa [ˈkasa], tienen [ˈtjenen], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [sjuˈdad], árbol [ˈaɾbol], café [kaˈfe], hablamos [aˈblamos], construir [konsˈtɾwiɾ]. Mark every stressed word, monosyllables included: sol [ˈsol], pan [ˈpan]. Leave unstressed function words unmarked (see function_words). Adverbs in -mente take ˈ on -men- and ˌ on the stress of the base: rápidamente [ˌrapidaˈmente]. Write no syllable dots. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels: identical vowels in hiatus are written twice, as in leer [leˈeɾ] and lee [ˈlee]. Spanish has no geminates: heteromorphemic doubled consonants are written as spelled, as in innato [inˈnato]. Case endings do not apply to Spanish.

Source: Standard Spanish accentuation as in Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, chapter on the accent; marking practice after Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP, ch. 13 (stress). Andean Venezuelan Spanish does not differ in stress placement (Lipski 1994, Latin American Spanish, chapter on Venezuela).

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and carry no ˈ, even when polysyllabic. Definite articles: el, la, los, las, lo, as in la casa [la ˈkasa]. All simple prepositions except según: a, de, en, con, por, para, sin, sobre, entre, hasta, desde, hacia, contra, as in para ti [paɾa ˈti] and hasta mañana [asta maˈɲana]. Conjunctions: y [i], e, o, u, ni, pero, que, si, porque, as in pero no [peɾo ˈno]. Unaccented relatives: que, quien, donde, cuando, como. Object clitics: me, te, se, le, les, lo, la, los, las, nos, as in me dijo [me ˈdixo]. Prenominal possessives: mi, tu, su, mis, tus, sus, nuestro, nuestra, as in mi casa [mi ˈkasa]. The following are stressed: indefinite articles un, una, unos, unas, as in un libro [ˈun ˈlibɾo]; subject and prepositional pronouns: yo [ˈʝo], él [ˈel], mí [ˈmi], usted [usˈted]; demonstratives; accented interrogatives and exclamatives: qué [ˈke], dónde [ˈdonde]; según [seˈɡun]; all verb forms, auxiliaries included: ha [ˈa], he [ˈe], está [esˈta]; negative no [ˈno].

Source: Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, inventory of palabras tónicas and átonas, which treats all verb forms and the indefinite articles as tonic. Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1957), Manual de pronunciación española, §§ on palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas, agrees except on some auxiliaries. Where they differ, follow Quilis.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, no case endings and no nunation. Every word has the same form at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside a phrase: verdad [beɾˈdad], los ojos [los ˈoxos]. Do not write final devoicing or any final weakening.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP. Retention of final /s/ and /d/ in the Andean Venezuelan highlands is noted in Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Venezuela.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form; write no process across a word boundary. Vowel sinalefa is not written: la amiga [la aˈmiɡa], casa azul [ˈkasa aˈsul]. Resyllabification of a final consonant is not written: los otros [los ˈotɾos]. Nasal place assimilation is not written: un beso [ˈun ˈbeso], en casa [en ˈkasa]. Voicing of /s/ before a voiced consonant is not written: los mismos [los ˈmismos]. The conjunction y is always [i]: y ella [i ˈeʝa]. Inside a word, too, nasals are written as spelled (n or m) and not assimilated: tengo [ˈtenɡo], cambio [ˈkambjo].

Source: Sinalefa, resyllabification and assimilation are described in Navarro Tomás (1918/1957), Manual de pronunciación española, and Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapters on syllable structure and connected speech. Leaving them unwritten is a choice of this broad, word-by-word transcription.

## Glides and diphthongs

An unstressed i or u next to another vowel in the same word is a glide. Write the prenuclear (rising) glide as j or w: bien [ˈbjen], piano [ˈpjano], cuatro [ˈkwatɾo], Venezuela [beneˈswela]. Write the postnuclear (falling) glide as i̯ or u̯: aire [ˈai̯ɾe], hoy [ˈoi̯], voy [ˈboi̯], auto [ˈau̯to], deuda [ˈdeu̯da]. In ui and iu the second vowel is the nucleus: muy [ˈmwi], cuidado [kwiˈdado], ciudad [sjuˈdad]. An accented í or ú is a full vowel in hiatus: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis], baúl [baˈul]. Word-initial hie- is [ʝe]: hierba [ˈʝeɾba]. Word-initial hue- is [we]: hueso [ˈweso].

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP, ch. on glides and diphthongs, which uses [j w] prenuclear and [i̯ u̯] postnuclear. The resolution of ui/iu follows the same work.

## Other choices

### seseo and yeísmo

Write c before e/i, z and s as s: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato]. Write ll and consonantal y as ʝ: calle [ˈkaʝe], yo [ˈʝo]. Write ch as tʃ, ñ as ɲ, v and b as b. Do not write h.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish (pan-Latin-American baseline), as in the specification notes.

### syllable-final /s/

Write coda and final s as s everywhere: estos [ˈestos], mismo [ˈmismo]. Do not write aspiration [h] or deletion. The Andean highlands retain /s/, unlike coastal Venezuelan Spanish.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Venezuela (Andean region conservative in /s/); Obediente Sosa, E. (1998), Fonética y fonología, Universidad de Los Andes, Mérida.

### voiced stops /b d ɡ/

Write b d ɡ in all positions. Do not write spirant allophones [β ð ɣ]. Intervocalic and final d is always written: cansado [kanˈsado], verdad [beɾˈdad]. The highland zone shows less d-weakening and more stop realizations after consonants than coastal varieties.

Source: Broad convention. Highland retention is noted in Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapters on Colombia and Venezuela.

### final /n/

Write word-final n as n, not ŋ: pan [ˈpan], también [tamˈbjen]. Velarization is characteristic of coastal Venezuelan Spanish, not of the Andes.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Venezuela; Obediente Sosa (1998), Fonética y fonología.

### /x/

Write j, and g before e/i, as x: hijo [ˈixo], gente [ˈxente]. A weaker glottal [h] is reported for Venezuela but is not written in this broad transcription.

Source: es-419 baseline as in Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish. Venezuelan [h] is noted in Lipski (1994), chapter on Venezuela.

### rhotics

Write the tap as ɾ: pero [ˈpeɾo], mar [ˈmaɾ]. Write the trill as r for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l or s: perro [ˈpero], rosa [ˈrosa], honra [ˈonra].

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on rhotics. Andean Venezuela keeps the alveolar trill (Lipski 1994).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
