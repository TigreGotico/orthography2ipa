# Transcription conventions: Algerian Arabic (ar-DZ)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Count syllables on the surface form, after syncope and schwa epenthesis. Mark primary stress with ˈ on every word of two or more syllables. Leave monosyllables unmarked: ktaːb, ktəb, fhəmt, biːt. Words in the unstressed function-word list are also left unmarked (see function_words). RULE: (1) Stress the final syllable if it has a long vowel followed by a consonant (VːC), or if it ends in two consonants (VCC): məkˈtuːb, mˈsaːkin is NOT final (-kin is short, so rule 2 applies), ʒəbˈdaːt. (2) Otherwise stress the penult: ˈtlaːta, ˈkətbu, ˈkaːtbət, ˈmsaːkin, kətˈbətha. The antepenult is never stressed. LONG VOWELS: length is written as ː in any position except word-finally. A long vowel makes its syllable heavy but attracts stress only through rules 1 and 2. Word-final vowels are always written short and unstressed: ʒa, mʃa, ˈkətbu, ˈtlaːta. GEMINATES are written as doubled letters. They close the preceding syllable and are split by the stress mark: kətˈtəb, ˈsˤɑħħa, ˈmərra. CASE ENDINGS: none; the dialect has no case or tanwīn, so nothing is added. PLACEMENT of ˈ: put it before the onset of the stressed syllable. A word-initial cluster is all onset (ˈtlaːta, ˈmsaːkin). In a medial cluster, only the last consonant is onset (məkˈtuːb). Proclitics written in the word (l-, b-, f-, w-) count as part of the word's first onset or syllable (ˈltlaːta, lməkˈtuːb).

Source: General Arabic rightmost-heavy stress typology as summarised in Watson 2002, ch. 4 (stress). Maghrebi weak-stress and final-vowel shortening follow Marçais 1902 (Tlemcen) and Grand'Henry 1972 (Cherchell). Boucherit 2002 is the reference for Algiers but was not consulted to a folio, so no page is claimed. Descriptions of Maghrebi stress differ, and some treat it as non-lexical; this convention adopts the weight rule for consistency.

## Stressed and unstressed words

UNSTRESSED: never take ˈ, even when they have two syllables. These are: the article l-/assimilated C- and the proclitics b-, f-, l-, w-, ma-, which are spelled in the host word and stressed with it. They also include, when written as separate words: the prepositions fi, mən, ʕla, mʕa, ʕənd, ħta/ħətta, kiːma, baʃ, mtaʕ/ntaʕ; the conjunctions w, wəlla, bəlli, ʔila/ila, lukaːn; the relative əlli; the negator ma and the postposed ʃ/ʃi; the genitive particle ntaʕ with its suffixes when used as a clitic possessive (ntaʕi, ntaʕu). Examples: ʕla ˈtˤɑbla, mən ˈdaːr, bəlli ʒa. STRESSED by the normal rule: independent pronouns (ˈaːna, nta, ˈntiːja, ˈhuwwa, ˈhiːja, ħna, ˈntuːma, ˈhuːma); demonstratives (ˈhaːda, ˈhaːdi, ˈhaːdu); interrogatives (wəʃ, ʃkuːn, kiːˈfaːʃ, ˈwaʃta, wiːn); maʃi; the presentative/aspectual raːh with suffixes (ˈraːni, ˈraːh, ˈraːhi); auxiliary kaːn and its forms (ˈkaːnu); the future particles (ˈraːjəħ, ˈɣaːdi). Monosyllables in either class carry no mark anyway.

Source: Function-word inventory follows Marçais 1902 and Grand'Henry 1972 for western/central Algerian sedentary speech, and Holes 2004 (chapter on dialect structure) for the Maghrebi clitic and particle system. Boucherit 2002 is cited for Algiers usage, without a folio.

## Pausal forms

Pausal forms do not apply as a separate rule. The dialect has no case endings and no tanwīn; ‹ـً› ‹ـٍ› ‹ـٌ› are ignored if they appear. Final short vowels are not dropped or added at a sentence end or a comma. Every word is written in the same shape wherever it stands. Word-final vowels are always short: ʒa, ˈkətbu. TĀʾ MARBŪṬA is written -a everywhere: ˈtˤɑbla, ˈmərra, ˈtlaːta. There are two exceptions: (a) when the noun is the first member of a construct directly followed by its possessor noun, write -ət (or -t where the stem already ends in a vowel-consonant sequence that allows it), as in ˈtˤɑblət ˈdaːr and mərt ʒaːri. (b) Before a pronominal suffix, write -t-: mərtu, ˈmədrəsti. This departs from a note that frames -a/-at as pause vs construct: in this dialect -a is not pause-bound, and the construct vowel is reduced to ə by syncope.

Source: Boucherit 2002 (tāʾ marbūṭa -a vs construct -at; no folio). Construct reduction to -ət and absence of case and tanwīn follow Marçais 1902 and Holes 2004 (dialect morphology). Final-vowel shortening follows Marçais 1902.

## Connected speech

No cross-word process is written. Each word keeps its own citation shape, including word-internal syncope and epenthesis. No ə is inserted or deleted at word edges because of the neighbour. There is no voicing or place assimilation across spaces and no liaison or resyllabification. Emphasis spread stops at the word boundary. So: ˈkətbu ktəb, not ...kətbu‿ktəb; mən ˈdaːr, not məd daːr. WORD-INTERNAL processes that ARE written: (1) Article assimilation. l- becomes a copy of a following coronal t d tˤ dˤ s z sˤ ʃ ʒ n r l: ʃʃəms, ttlaːta, ʒʒmaʕa, nnaːs. Otherwise it stays l: lbaːb, lqəlb, lktaːb. The article takes no prothetic vowel, even before a cluster. (2) Syncope of unstressed short vowels in open syllables, with ə inserted only to break a three-consonant sequence inside the word: ktəb, ˈkətbu. (3) Emphasis spread within the word (see other).

Source: Article assimilation and syncope/epenthesis follow Marçais 1902 and Boucherit 2002 (no folio). Treating ʒ as an assimilating letter follows the Maghrebi pattern described in Holes 2004. Restricting written processes to the word follows the tool's segmentation.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the glides as the consonant symbols j and w in all positions, never y, i̯ or u̯: ˈjəktbu, wəld, ʃaːj, ʒaːw, ˈhuwwa, ˈhiːja. Old diphthongs *ay and *aw are written monophthongised to iː and uː, as in old-urban Algiers: biːt, ziːt, juːm, muːt. This applies unless the spelling or the word is a recent Classical/Bedouin form kept with a glide, in which case write aj/aw: ˈsˤajf. Sequences of a vowel plus j or w are consonant codas and count toward syllable weight like any consonant.

Source: Monophthongisation in sedentary Algerian follows Marçais 1902 and Boucherit 2002 (no folio). The glide-as-consonant analysis follows Watson 2002 (segmental inventory chapter).

## Other choices

### qāf

‹ق› → q everywhere (old-urban Algiers): qaːl, lqəlb. ‹ڨ› and ‹ڭ› → ɡ, in Arabic words and French loans alike: ɡaːʕ, ɡruːp, ɡaraːʒ. The reflex follows the spelling; ɡ is not substituted for a ‹ق›-spelled word, even where Bedouin/rural ɡ is common.

Source: Boucherit 2002 (q urban, ɡ spreading; no folio); Grand'Henry 1972; Saadane & Habash 2015 §4.1 p.70 for ‹ڨ› = [ɡ]. ‹ڭ› follows the lahgtna dataset count, which records spellings only.

### ǧīm and interdentals

‹ج› → ʒ (not dʒ): ʒa, ʒʒmaʕa. ‹ث› → t, ‹ذ› → d, ‹ظ› and ‹ض› → dˤ: ˈtlaːta, dhəb, dˤɑhr.

Source: Watson 2002 p.16 (ʒ); Boucherit 2002; Marçais 1902 (merger of interdentals).

### vowel inventory and emphasis

Short vowels are a, ə, u, with short i written ə. Long vowels are aː, iː, uː. Emphatic consonants are written tˤ, sˤ, dˤ, zˤ; r and l are never marked emphatic. In any word containing tˤ, sˤ, dˤ, zˤ or q, write every a/aː of that word as ɑ/ɑː: ˈtˤɑblɑ, ˈsˤɑħħɑ, qɑːl. The domain is the word only. ɑ is otherwise not used, and there is no imāla: ktaːb, not ktɛːb.

Source: Watson 2002 (emphasis spread, ch. on emphasis). Absence of imāla in Algiers per Boucherit 2002 and Marçais 1902. Word-sized domain is chosen for consistency.

### French loans

Keep p and v. Write French r/ʁ as r. Keep the oral mid vowels e and o in loans. Front rounded vowels map to y→i and ø/œ→ə. Nasal vowels map to vowel + n. Loans otherwise follow the stress and emphasis rules: ɡaːtˤo, ɡraːʒ → ɡaˈraːʒ, ˈpaːrki, viːz → viːza.

Source: Holes 2004 (French adstrate in Maghrebi); the lahgtna dataset for attested loan spellings. Not in Boucherit 2002 to a folio.

### hamza

Do not write ʔ in native dialect words, where it is lost: ˈaːna, kla, ˈmra. Write ʔ only in learned Classical words where the spelling keeps an internal hamza and the word is clearly borrowed from MSA: suʔaːl.

Source: Marçais 1902; Holes 2004 (hamza loss in the dialects).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
