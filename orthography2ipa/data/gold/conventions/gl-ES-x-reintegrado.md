# Transcription conventions: Galician (reintegrationist orthography) (gl-ES-x-reintegrado)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Galician stress is lexical and free; most words are paroxytone, and stress is never predicted from the reintegrationist spelling alone. Put primary stress on the syllable that carries it in standard Galician. A written acute or circumflex shows that syllable (café [kaˈfɛ], árvore [ˈarβoɾe], avô [aˈβo]). Without an accent, give the word its standard Galician stress: nouns in -çom/-om are oxytone (naçom [naˈθoŋ]), and infinitives in -r are oxytone (falar [faˈlaɾ]). Put ˈ before the stressed syllable, with the syllable's whole onset after it (outro [ˈou̯tɾo], cidade [θiˈðaðe]). Mark ˈ on every stressed word, monosyllables included (sol [ˈsɔl], mar [ˈmaɾ], é [ˈɛ]). Words that are unstressed under function_words get no mark. Secondary stress ˌ is written only on the base of -mente adverbs, which keeps its stressed vowel quality (novamente [ˌnɔβaˈmente]); it is written nowhere else. Galician has no phonemic long vowels and no geminates: <ss> is a single [s] (isso [ˈiso]), and ː is never written. Galician has no case endings.

Source: Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2) pp. 119-122 (stress and vowels); Regueira, X.L. (2010) Dicionario de pronuncia da lingua galega (ILG/RAG) for the stress and vowel quality of individual words; Freixeiro Mato, X.R. (2006) Gramática da lingua galega I: Fonética e fonoloxía, chapter on accent.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed words carry no ˈ. They are: definite articles o a os as [o a os as]; the article allomorphs lo la los las and no na nos nas; preposition+article contractions (do da dos das, no na, ao á, polo pola, co coa) and cumha; simple prepositions a, de, em, com, por, para, sem, até, sobre, entre, desde; the conjunctions e [e], ou [ou̯], nem [neŋ], mas [mas], que [ke], se [se], porque, como when comparative; the relative que; and all atonic object and reflexive clitics, whether they precede the verb or follow it after a hyphen (me te se lhe lhes nos vos che o a os as lo no) (dá-lhe [ˈda] [ʎe], vou-no ver [ˈbou̯] [no] [ˈbeɾ]). Stressed words get ˈ. They are: indefinite articles um umha [ˈuŋ] [ˈuŋa]; possessives (o meu [o] [ˈmeu̯], minha [ˈmiɲa]); demonstratives; subject and tonic pronouns (eu [ˈeu̯], el [ˈel], mim [ˈmiŋ]); interrogatives que quem como onde when asking; the negation nom [ˈnoŋ]; and every form of ser, estar, haver, ter and ir, auxiliary or not (tem [ˈteŋ], há [ˈa]). An unstressed vowel in a function word keeps its full quality and never reduces to ə.

Source: Freixeiro Mato 2006 (Fonética e fonoloxía, section on atonic words); Álvarez, R. & Xove, X. (2002) Gramática da lingua galega, chapters on articles and atonic pronouns; Regueira 1996 (sample transcription).

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Galician has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word has the same form at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside a phrase: final unstressed vowels stay [e a o] (noite [ˈnoi̯te]), and final nasals stay [ŋ].

Source: Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2) pp. 119-122.

## Connected speech

Only two processes cross word boundaries, and each changes only the word it applies to. (1) Spirantization of /b d ɡ/. Write [b d ɡ] after a pause (including the first word of the sentence) and after a nasal; /d/ is also [d] after /l/. Write [β ð ɣ] everywhere else, including after a vowel, /ɾ/ or /s/ at the end of the preceding word: a boca [a] [ˈβoka], um gato [ˈuŋ] [ˈɡato], de dia [de] [ˈðia], vinho [ˈbiɲo] (initial). (2) A word-initial <r> is [r] (o rio [o] [ˈrio]). Do not write the following: assimilation of the final nasal to the place of the next consonant (um porco [ˈuŋ] [ˈpɔɾko], not [um]); vowel elision or synaloepha between words (de abril [de] [aˈβɾil], with both vowels written); resyllabification; voicing of final /s/ before a voiced sound (os dous [os] [ˈðou̯s]). Never use a linking tie or ‿. A final nasal before a vowel stays [ŋ] and does not move into the next syllable.

Source: Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2) pp. 119-122 (allophones of /b d ɡ/, word-final velar nasal); Freixeiro Mato 2006 (fonética sintáctica).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the second element of a falling diphthong with non-syllabic i̯ u̯: pai [ˈpai̯], seis [ˈsei̯s], rei [ˈrei̯], coisa [ˈkoi̯sa], auga [ˈau̯ɣa], outro [ˈou̯tɾo], meu [ˈmeu̯]. Write a glide before the nucleus (a rising diphthong) as [j w]: quatro [ˈkwatɾo], história [isˈtɔɾja], piano [ˈpjano]. A written accent on i or u marks a hiatus, so both vowels are syllabic: país [paˈis], saúde [saˈuðe], dia [ˈdia]. <nh> and <lh> are the single consonants [ɲ ʎ] and are never followed by a glide: vinho [ˈbiɲo], filho [ˈfiʎo].

Source: Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2) pp. 119-122 (diphthongs); Regueira 2010, Dicionario de pronuncia, for diphthong versus hiatus in individual words.

## Other choices

### vowel inventory and quality

Stressed syllables have seven vowels /i e ɛ a ɔ o u/, and the mid vowels take their standard Galician quality, whatever the spelling shows: porta [ˈpɔɾta], boca [ˈboka], terra [ˈtɛra], avó [aˈβɔ]. Unstressed syllables have five vowels [i e a o u]; the only exception is the ˌ base of -mente adverbs. Final unstressed vowels are [e a o], never [ɪ ɐ ʊ ə]: casa [ˈkasa], leite [ˈlei̯te]. Do not apply Portuguese vowel reduction.

Source: Regueira 1996 pp. 119-122; Regueira 2010 (Dicionario de pronuncia).

### nasals

Word-final nasal: final <m> or <n> is [ŋ] (bem [ˈbɛŋ], som [ˈsoŋ], naçom [naˈθoŋ]). No nasal vowels are written. Spellings borrowed from the Portuguese standard keep the Galician pronunciation (nação and não are [naˈθoŋ] and [ˈnoŋ]). Intervocalic <mh> in umha, algumha and nengumha, and Portuguese-style uma, are all [ŋ]: [ˈuŋa]. <nh> is always [ɲ] (minha [ˈmiɲa]); it is never the [ŋ] it spells in the RAG norm.

Source: Regueira 1996 (velar nasal); AGAL 1983 (umha, <nh>, <lh>).

### sibilants and fricatives

Galician has no /z/ and no /ʒ/. <s> and <ss> are [s] in every position (casa [ˈkasa], mesa [ˈmesa]). <j>, <x>, and <g> before e or i are [ʃ] (janela [ʃaˈnɛla], gente [ˈʃente], xente [ˈʃente]). <ç>, <z>, and <c> before e or i are [θ] (fazer [faˈθeɾ], praça [ˈpɾaθa]). Use distinción: write no seseo, and no gheada ([ɡ ɣ] for <g>, never [ħ x]).

Source: Regueira 1996 pp. 119-122 (standard consonant inventory); the spec note on <j>.

### other consonants

<ch> is the affricate [tʃ] (chave [ˈtʃaβe]), not Portuguese [ʃ]. <lh> is [ʎ], with no yeísmo (olho [ˈoʎo]). <v> and <b> are both /b/: write [b] or [β] following the spirantization rule (vaca [ˈbaka], a vaca [a] [ˈβaka]). <h> is silent (homem [ˈɔmeŋ]).

Source: Regueira 1996 pp. 119-122.

### rhotics

Write the trill [r] for word-initial <r>, for <rr>, and for <r> after l, n or s (honra [ˈonra]). Write the tap [ɾ] intervocalically, in onset clusters and in the coda, including word-final <r> before a vowel (caro [ˈkaɾo], falar ali [faˈlaɾ] [aˈli]).

Source: Regueira 1996 pp. 119-122.

### verb + enclitic allomorphs

When the spelling drops a verb's final -r or -s before -lo, -la, -los or -las, transcribe the verb as written, with its stress kept, and the clitic as a separate unstressed word: fazê-lo [faˈθe] [lo], comemo-lo [koˈmemo] [lo].

Source: AGAL 1983 (enclitic allomorphs); Álvarez & Xove 2002.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
