# Transcription conventions: North-Western Catalan (ca-x-occidental)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, including stressed monosyllables: juny [ˈdʒuɲ], molt [ˈmol], mar [ˈmaɾ]. Place it by the IEC rules. A written accent (à é è í ó ò ú) always marks the stressed vowel. An unaccented word ending in a vowel, vowel+s, -en or -in stresses the penult: porta [ˈpɔɾtɛ], cases [ˈkazes], jove [ˈdʒoβe], examen [eɡˈzamen]. All other unaccented endings, including consonant+s plurals, stress the last syllable: aixecar [ajʃeˈka], xiquet [tʃiˈket], xiquets [tʃiˈkets]. Rising diphthongs after q/g count as one syllable (llengua [ˈʎenɡwɛ]), and falling diphthongs count as one (remei [reˈmej]). Diaeresis ï ü marks hiatus, not stress: veïna [beˈinɛ]. The stress mark goes before the syllable onset, with maximal legal onsets: obrir [oˈβɾi], esquerra [esˈkera]. Mark no secondary stress; -ment adverbs take one mark on -ment: ràpidament [rapiðaˈment]. Vowel quality: stressed e/o are close or open by lexeme, following the DCVB North-Western form (tres [ˈtɾɛs], cos [ˈkɔs], dos [ˈdos], gos [ˈɡos], cadena [kaˈðenɛ]). Unstressed vowels never reduce: write the five unstressed vowels [a e i o u] as spelled (pessebre [peˈsebbɾe], portava [poɾˈtaβɛ]). Final unstressed -a of any word of two or more syllables is [ɛ], also before a hyphenated enclitic: dia [ˈdiɛ], compra-la [ˈkompɾɛ la]. Catalan has no vowel length and no case endings. Geminates are written as doubled consonant letters with no length mark, only where the pronunciation has them: tl [ll] (ametlla [aˈmellɛ]), tll [ʎʎ] (bitllet [biˈʎʎet]), tm [mm], tn [nn], and b/g before l [bb ɡɡ] (poble [ˈpɔbble], regla [ˈreɡɡlɛ]). l·l is a single [l] (col·legi [koˈledʒi]). tg/tj are a single [dʒ] (metge [ˈmedʒe]).

Source: IEC 2016, Gramàtica, ch. 3 (l'accent), for placement. Wheeler 2005 §3.1 for stress and §2.3 for no unstressed reduction in the Western block. Recasens 1996 and Veny 1982 for North-Western final -a = [ɛ]. Wheeler 2005 §5.2 for pre-lateral gemination. DCVB for lexical mid-vowel quality.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The IEC 'mots àtons' are written with no stress mark and with full, unreduced vowels. They are:
- definite articles el [el], la [la], els [els], les [les] and North-Western lo [lo], los [los];
- the personal article en [en], na [na];
- the contractions al, als, del, dels, pel, pels;
- the atonic prepositions a [a], de [de], en [en], per [peɾ], amb [am];
- the conjunctions i, o [o], ni [ni], que [ke] and conditional si [si];
- relative/conjunctive que, qui, on, com, quan;
- the possessive determiners mon, ton, son, ma, ta, sa, mos, tos, sos, mes, tes, ses;
- all weak pronouns, proclitic or hyphenated enclitic: em/me, et/te, es/se, ens/nos/mos, us/vos, el/lo, la, els/los, les, li [li], ho [o], hi [i], en/ne.
Monosyllabic function words keep [a]: la [la], ma [ma], not [ɛ]. Apostrophized forms are consonant-only words: l' [l], d' [d], m' [m], t' [t], s' [s], n' [n], 'l [l], 'm [m], 'n [n], 's [s], 'ns [ns]. Example: l'home [l ˈɔme]; de la casa [de la ˈkazɛ]; dóna-li-ho [ˈdonɛ li o].
The following carry stress (ˈ):
- un, una, uns, unes (una porta [ˈunɛ ˈpɔɾtɛ]);
- demonstratives, strong pronouns (jo [ˈdʒo], ell [ˈeʎ]) and stressed possessives (meu [ˈmew]);
- interrogatives (què [ˈkɛ], qui [ˈki]);
- the tonic prepositions and conjunctions sense, sobre, contra, entre, fins, des, cap, però, perquè;
- negation no [ˈno] and all adverbs and numerals;
- every verb form, including the auxiliaries haver and ser and the periphrastic-past vaig, vas, va (va menjar [ˈba menˈdʒa], ha vingut [ˈa βinˈɡut]).

Source: IEC 2016, Gramàtica, §6.2 and the list of 'mots àtons'. Wheeler 2005 §3.1. Veny 1982 for the North-Western article lo/los and the reinforced pronoun forms.

## Pausal forms

Catalan has no pausal forms, no case endings and no nunation (tanwīn), so nothing is added or dropped at a comma or at the end of a sentence. Word-final -r loss and final-cluster simplification are not pausal: they apply in every position. The only pause-sensitive process is final obstruent devoicing. Before a pause (sentence end or comma), a word-final obstruent is voiceless: verd [ˈbeɾt], fred [ˈfɾet], mig [ˈmitʃ], amics [aˈmiks]. Other positions follow connected_speech.

Source: Wheeler 2005 §5.3 (final-obstruent devoicing) and §10.2–§10.3.

## Connected speech

Write these processes inside each word's own IPA form; never join or drop words.
(1) Spirantization: /b d ɡ/ (b, v, d, g, gu) are [β ð ɣ] after any vowel or continuant consonant, within and across words. They are stops [b d ɡ] after a pause and after a nasal, and /d/ is also a stop after /l/: la boca [la ˈβokɛ], de vegades [de βeˈɣaðes], un dia [un ˈdiɛ], el dia [el ˈdiɛ], els dies [elz ˈðies]. Utterance-initially, de is [de]; after a vowel it is [ðe].
(2) Voicing at word edges: a word-final obstruent is voiceless before a pause or a voiceless consonant. It is voiced before a voiced consonant: set dies [ˈsɛd ˈdies], peix gros [ˈpejʒ ˈɣɾos]. Before a vowel, final sibilants voice (els homes [elz ˈɔmes], dos amics [ˈdoz aˈmiks]) but final stops stay voiceless (set anys [ˈsɛt ˈaɲs]).
(3) Nasal place assimilation, within and across words: n is [m] before a bilabial and [ŋ] before a velar; it stays [n] elsewhere. Examples: un poble [um ˈpɔbble], en Pere [em ˈpeɾe], un gat [uŋ ˈɡat], canvi [ˈkambi].
(4) Word-final cluster simplification: drop a final stop after a nasal or lateral (-nt, -lt, -mp, -nc, -ng, -mb), also before a vowel and also before plural -s. Examples: molt alta [ˈmol ˈaltɛ], molts [ˈmols], cinc [ˈsiŋ], amb [am], sant [ˈsan]. Keep -rt, -st, -ct etc.: fort [ˈfɔɾt], est [ˈɛst].
(5) Word-final -r is deleted in infinitives and in polysyllables stressed on the last syllable: aixecar [ajʃeˈka], fer [ˈfe], fuster [fusˈte], pitjor [piˈdʒo]. It is kept as [ɾ] in the atonic preposition per [peɾ] and in non-verb monosyllables (mar [ˈmaɾ]). There is no liaison of a deleted -r before a following word, except before a vowel-initial hyphenated enclitic (ho, hi, en): fer-ho [ˈfeɾ o], menjar-lo [menˈdʒa lo].
(6) No vowel elision, fusion or synalepha is written across word boundaries: both vowels stay in full (casa antiga [ˈkazɛ anˈtiɣɛ]). The exception is the conjunction i (see glides). Orthographic elision is already shown by the apostrophe words. Identical consonants across a boundary are both written (les sabates [les saˈβates]).

Source: Wheeler 2005: §5.2 for spirantization; §5.3 for devoicing and cross-word voicing assimilation; §10.2–§10.5 for final-rhotic loss, cluster simplification, nasal assimilation and phrase-level lenition. Veny 1982 for the isoglosses; for lateral-cluster simplification this follows the spec's note and the 4catac data rather than Veny's Eastern-only statement. Bonet & Lloret 1998, ch. 5, for cross-word vowel contact.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vowels as the consonant symbols [j] and [w], never as i̯ u̯.
Falling diphthongs: ai [aj], ei [ej]/[ɛj], oi [ɔj]/[oj], ui [uj], au [aw], eu [ew]/[ɛw], iu [iw], ou [ɔw]/[ow]. Examples: aire [ˈajɾe], remei [reˈmej], ciutat [sjuˈtat] → write [siwˈtat], meu [ˈmew], nou [ˈnɔw].
Rising diphthongs exist only after q/g + u: quatre [ˈkwatɾe], llengua [ˈʎenɡwɛ], pingüí [pinˈɡwi].
Intervocalic i is [j]: noia [ˈnɔjɛ], joia [ˈdʒɔjɛ]. Word-initial i + vowel is [j]: iogurt [joˈɣuɾt].
The ix digraph is [jʃ]: caixa [ˈkajʃɛ], peix [ˈpejʃ], baix [ˈbajʃ]. Word-final -ig is [tʃ] with no glide: maig [ˈmatʃ], mig [ˈmitʃ].
Other i/u + vowel sequences are hiatus with full vowels: dia [ˈdiɛ], suau [suˈaw], veïna [beˈinɛ].
Across words, only the conjunction i glides: it is [j] immediately before a vowel-initial word (i ara [j ˈaɾɛ]) and [i] elsewhere. Clitic hi and ho never glide: hi ha [i ˈa].

Source: Wheeler 2005 §2.5 (diphthong inventory) and §5.1 (ix, -ig). Veny 1982 for Western [jʃ]. Bonet & Lloret 1998, ch. 5, for gliding of the conjunction i and for clitic vowels resisting gliding. IEC 2016, ch. 3, for the diaeresis.

## Other choices

### ⟨x⟩, ⟨j/g⟩ and affricates

Values of x: word-initial or after a consonant [tʃ] (xiquet [tʃiˈket], panxa [ˈpantʃɛ]); in ix [jʃ]; in learned words [ks]/[ɡz] (taxi [ˈtaksi], examen [eɡˈzamen]). j and g+e/i are [dʒ] in all positions (juny [ˈdʒuɲ], pujar [puˈdʒa]). tx is [tʃ] and tg/tj are [dʒ], single. tz is [dz].

Source: Wheeler 2005 §5.1. Veny 1982 (Western [tʃ], [dʒ]). Recasens 1996.

### Betacism

v is always /b/, realized [b] or [β] by the spirantization rule: vi [ˈbi], la vida [la ˈβiðɛ]. There is never [v]. This follows the spec; some southern Tortosí and Ribagorçan speakers keep [v], but that is not written.

Source: Recasens 1996. Veny 1982. Wheeler 2005 §5.1.

### Rhotics

Write the trill [r] for word-initial r, r after n/l/s, and rr: roda [ˈroðɛ], honra [ˈonrɛ], terra [ˈtera]→[ˈterɛ]. Write the tap [ɾ] elsewhere, including retained word-final r: porta [ˈpɔɾtɛ], per [peɾ].

Source: Wheeler 2005 §5.1. Recasens 1996.

### Sibilants and other consonant symbols

Sibilants: intervocalic s and z are [z]; ss, ç, word-initial s and s/c after a consonant are [s] (casa [ˈkazɛ], massa [ˈmasɛ]).
Other digraphs: ll is [ʎ], ny is [ɲ], h is silent, qu/gu before e/i are [k]/[ɡ].
l is plain [l] in all positions; its velarization is not written.
Final -ig follows glides.

Source: Wheeler 2005 §5.1. Ladefoged & Maddieson 1996 for the symbol set.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
