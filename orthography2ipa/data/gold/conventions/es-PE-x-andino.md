# Transcription conventions: Andean Peruvian Spanish (es-PE-x-andino)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write one primary stress mark ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every polysyllabic stressed word. Place stress by Spanish orthography. A written accent marks the stressed vowel. Otherwise, a word ending in a vowel, n or s takes stress on the penult: casa [ˈkasa], hablan [ˈablan], martes [ˈmaɾtes]. Any other final consonant takes stress on the last syllable: ciudad [sjuˈdad], comer [koˈmeɾ]. A written accent overrides this: árbol [ˈaɾbol], canción [kanˈsjon], música [ˈmusika]. Put the mark before the whole onset, including a glide: tienda [ˈtjenda], muestra [ˈmwestɾa]. Stressed monosyllables carry no mark: sol [sol], más [mas]. Adverbs in -mente get a secondary mark ˌ on the stressed syllable of the adjective and a primary mark on -men-: rápidamente [ˌrapidaˈmente]. Other one-word compounds get only the stress of the last element: paraguas [paˈɾaɡwas]. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Spanish has no phonemic length and no case. Identical vowels across a morpheme boundary are written twice: leer [leˈeɾ], creer [kɾeˈeɾ]. A double nasal is written twice: innato [inˈnato].

Source: Standard Spanish accentuation rules (RAE & ASALE 2010, Ortografía de la lengua española, ch. on tilde); stress and -mente double stress per Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on stress; Quilis (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, ch. on accent.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words are unstressed and carry no mark even when polysyllabic: definite articles el la lo los las; prenominal possessives mi tu su nuestro (in the sense 'our', before a noun); object and reflexive clitics me te se nos os lo la le los las les; all simple prepositions except según: para [paɾa], desde [desde], hacia [asja]; conjunctions y e o u ni que pero si (conditional) porque aunque; non-interrogative relatives que quien donde cuando como: cuando llega [kwando ˈʎeɡa]. These words are stressed and marked when polysyllabic: indefinite articles un una unos: una casa [ˈuna ˈkasa]; según [seˈɡun]; subject and prepositional pronouns: ella [ˈeʎa], nosotros [noˈsotɾos], mí, ti, él; demonstratives: este [ˈeste]; postnominal possessives: mío [ˈmio]; interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent: dónde [ˈdonde], qué; numerals; and all verb forms, auxiliaries included: había [aˈbia], está [esˈta], estado [esˈtado]. A clitic split off by a hyphen or written in the text as a separate word gets no stress mark. A clitic written fused to a verb stays in that word, and the word keeps the verb's stress: dámelo [ˈdamelo].

Source: Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1957), Manual de pronunciación española, section on palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas; Quilis (1993), Tratado, ch. on accent; Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on stress.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Words before a comma or at the end of a sentence are written exactly as elsewhere. Do not write the phrase-final vowel devoicing or reduction reported for Andean speakers: casas. [ˈkasas], not [ˈkasḁs].

Source: No pausal morphology in any description of Spanish. Unstressed vowel devoicing in Andean Spanish (Lipski 1994, Peru chapter; Delforge 2008 on Cusco) is excluded as sub-phonemic.

## Connected speech

Write no process across word boundaries. Each word keeps its citation form: no synalepha or vowel elision (la escuela [la esˈkwela], not [lesˈkwela]); no resyllabification marks; no voicing of word-final /s/ before a vowel or voiced consonant (los años [los ˈaɲos], not [loz]); no nasal place assimilation across the boundary (un beso [un ˈbeso], not [um]). Word-final /n/ stays alveolar [n], not velar [ŋ] as on the coast. This matches the Andean tendency toward less synalepha and less linking than coastal varieties. Inside words, broad transcription does not write predictable allophony either: tengo [ˈtenɡo], cambio [ˈkambjo] (orthographic m before b written m).

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Peru chapter (Andean retention of /s/, alveolar /n/, reduced vowel linking); Cerrón-Palomino, R. (2003), Castellano andino; broad-transcription practice of Hualde (2005).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write an unstressed i/u next to another vowel in the same syllable as [j] and [w], in both rising and falling diphthongs: bien [ˈbjen], bueno [ˈbweno], aire [ˈajɾe], causa [ˈkawsa], hoy [ˈoj], muy [ˈmuj], ciudad [sjuˈdad]. Do not use i̯ u̯. The conjunction y is [i]. An accented í or ú forms a hiatus and is syllabic: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis], baúl [baˈul]. Initial hie- and hue- are written [ʝe] and [we]: hielo [ˈʝelo], hueso [ˈweso]. Consonantal y is the fricative [ʝ], distinct from the glide [j]: mayo [ˈmaʝo], ya [ʝa].

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on glides and diphthongs (using j w throughout for consistency rather than his j/i̯ split); RAE & ASALE (2011), Nueva gramática: Fonética y fonología.

## Other choices

### ll versus y (lateral palatal)

Write orthographic ll as [ʎ] and consonantal y as [ʝ], keeping them distinct: calle [ˈkaʎe], llama [ˈʎama], ella [ˈeʎa], but mayo [ˈmaʝo], yo [ʝo]. This departs from the stub note, which models yeísmo (ll -> /ʝ/). The published descriptions of the Andean highlands report /ʎ/ retained.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Peru chapter (Andean highland /ʎ/ retention, Quechua/Aymara support); Escobar, A. (1978), Variaciones sociolingüísticas del castellano en el Perú.

### Rhotics

Write the trill phoneme as the assibilated [ʐ]. This covers rr, word-initial r, and r after n, l or s: carro [ˈkaʐo], rojo [ˈʐoxo], honra [ˈonʐa], Israel [isʐaˈel]. Write the tap [ɾ] everywhere else, including in clusters and in syllable-final and word-final position: pero [ˈpeɾo], tres [tɾes], comer [koˈmeɾ]. Do not write final-/ɾ/ assibilation or the tr cluster as [tʂ].

Source: Lipski (1994), Peru and Andean chapters (assibilated /r/); Cerrón-Palomino (2003), Castellano andino.

### Sibilants and seseo

Write c before e or i, z, and s as [s] (seseo), never [θ]: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato]. Write /s/ as [s] in every position. Andean Spanish does not aspirate or drop it: mismo [ˈmismo], las casas [las ˈkasas]. ch is [tʃ]: chico [ˈtʃiko].

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Andean zone (/s/ retention); seseo per the specification note and Lipski (1994).

### Velar fricative and h

Write j, and g before e or i, as [x]: jefe [ˈxefe], gente [ˈxente]. Orthographic h is silent: hablar [aˈblaɾ]. x between vowels is [ks]: examen [ekˈsamen]. In México and other place names with the old spelling, x is [x]: [ˈmexiko].

Source: Lipski (1994), Peru chapter (velar [x] in Andean Peru).

### Voiced stops b d g

Write /b d ɡ/ as [b d ɡ] in all positions, without the approximant allophones [β ð ɣ]: lado [ˈlado], agua [ˈaɡwa]. b and v are both [b]: vaca [ˈbaka]. Write g as [ɡ].

Source: Broad phonemic convention, per Hualde (2005), ch. on voiced stops (spirantization treated as allophonic).

### Vowels

Write five vowels [a e i o u] as in the spelling. Do not write the e/i and o/u raising or alternation of Quechua-dominant bilinguals, which is socially marked and outside the standard: poco [ˈpoko], not [ˈpuku]. Do not write unstressed vowel devoicing.

Source: Lipski (1994), Peru chapter; Cerrón-Palomino (2003), Castellano andino (vowel raising in bilingual speech, treated as non-standard).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
