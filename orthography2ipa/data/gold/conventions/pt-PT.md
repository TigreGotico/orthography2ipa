# Transcription conventions: European Portuguese (pt-PT)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every content word, including monosyllables: sim [ˈsĩ], bom [ˈbõ], pé [ˈpɛ]. Place stress by spelling. (1) A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed syllable: café [kɐˈfɛ], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃pɐdɐ], órfão [ˈɔɾfɐ̃w̃]. (2) Without an accent, words ending in -a, -e, -o, -as, -es, -os, -am, -em, -ens are penultimate: casa [ˈkazɐ], noite [ˈnojtɨ], cantam [ˈkɐ̃tɐ̃w̃], homem [ˈɔmɐ̃j̃]. (3) Without an accent, words ending in -i, -is, -u, -us, -l, -r, -z, -x, -im, -ins, -um, -uns, -om, -ons, or in ã, ão, ãe, õe (plus -s), are final: aqui [ɐˈki], papel [pɐˈpɛɫ], falar [fɐˈlaɾ], feliz [fɨˈliʃ], irmão [iɾˈmɐ̃w̃]. Keep -am (unstressed) apart from -ão (stressed): falaram [fɐˈlaɾɐ̃w̃], falarão [fɐlɐˈɾɐ̃w̃]. The stress mark goes before the onset, with maximal legal onsets (obstruent+r/l clusters): amigo [ɐˈmiɡu], obra [ˈɔbɾɐ]. A preconsonantal sibilant stays in the coda of the preceding syllable: gosto [ˈɡoʃtu], estar [iʃˈtaɾ]. Do not mark secondary stress. Stressed mid-vowel quality is lexical when unaccented: é/ó are [ɛ ɔ] and ê/ô are [e o]; otherwise follow dictionary pronunciation (Infopédia): porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ], boca [ˈbokɐ]. EP has no phonemic long vowels. It has no geminates: ⟨ss rr⟩ are digraphs for [s ʁ]. It has no case endings. None of these three bear on stress.

Source: Cunha & Cintra 1984, chapter on accentuation (graphic accent and stress rules). Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, chapter on word stress (cited, not consulted for this entry). Vigário 2003 for prosodic-word status. Stress-mark placement follows Cruz-Ferreira 1995/1999 transcriptions.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words are unstressed: no ˈ, and their vowels reduced. Definite articles: o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ]. Crasis forms keep an open vowel but stay unmarked: à [a], às [aʃ], ao [aw], aos [awʃ]. Monosyllabic prepositions: de [dɨ], em [ɐ̃j̃], por [puɾ], com [kõ], sem [sɐ̃j̃]. Para is unstressed but written unreduced [pɐɾɐ], never [pɾɐ]. Preposition+article contractions: do [du], da [dɐ], dos [duʃ], das [dɐʃ], no [nu], na [nɐ], nos [nuʃ], pelo [pɨlu], pela [pɨlɐ], num [nũ]. Conjunctions and complementisers: e [i], ou [o], que [kɨ], se [sɨ], mas [mɐʃ], nem [nɐ̃j̃]. Clitic object and reflexive pronouns, including hyphenated enclitics and mesoclitics, each written as their own word: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [sɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], lhes [ʎɨʃ], nos [nuʃ], vos [vuʃ], o/a/os/as [u ɐ uʃ ɐʃ], lo/la [lu lɐ], no/na [nu nɐ]. Examples: dá-me [ˈda mɨ], comê-lo [kuˈme lu], dar-lhe-ia [ˈdaɾ ʎɨ ˈiɐ]. The final element of a mesoclitic carries its own stress. These words are stressed and marked: indefinite articles um [ˈũ], uma [ˈumɐ], uns [ˈũʃ], umas [ˈumɐʃ] (and so numa [ˈnumɐ], but num [nũ] stays unmarked by convention as a contraction). Prepositions of two or more syllables other than para: sobre [ˈsobɾɨ], entre [ˈẽtɾɨ], contra [ˈkõtɾɐ], desde [ˈdeʒdɨ], até [ɐˈtɛ]. Tonic pronouns: eu [ˈew], tu [ˈtu], ele [ˈelɨ], nós [ˈnɔʃ], mim [ˈmĩ], você [voˈse]. Demonstratives, possessives and não [ˈnɐ̃w̃]. All verb forms, including auxiliaries and copulas: é [ˈɛ], está [iʃˈta], tem [ˈtɐ̃j̃], há [ˈa], foi [ˈfoj].

Source: Vigário 2003, chapters on clitics and the prosodic word (articles, monosyllabic prepositions, conjunctions and pronominal clitics as unstressed elements). Reduced forms as in Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (cited, not consulted) and Cruz-Ferreira 1995 transcribed passage.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply in the Arabic sense. EP has no case endings or nunation, and no separate pausal form is written at a comma or sentence end. Every word keeps its citation form before a pause. Final unstressed ⟨e⟩ is always written [ɨ], never deleted: noite [ˈnojtɨ], pode [ˈpɔdɨ]. Final ⟨s z x⟩ is [ʃ]: mais [ˈmajʃ], luz [ˈluʃ]. Final ⟨l⟩ is [ɫ]: Portugal [puɾtuˈɡaɫ]. Final ⟨r⟩ is [ɾ]: mar [ˈmaɾ]. The note's 'word-final [ɨ] often deletable' describes real casual-speech deletion, but it is not transcribed.

Source: Cruz-Ferreira 1995, pp. 90-94 (citation-form transcriptions with final [ɨ] and coda [ʃ]). Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 on final-vowel deletion as an optional casual-speech process (cited, not consulted).

## Connected speech

Only one cross-word process is written: the voicing and resyllabification of a word-final sibilant (⟨s z x⟩). It is [ʃ] before a pause or a voiceless consonant: as casas [ɐʃ ˈkazɐʃ]. It is [ʒ] before a voiced consonant: os gatos [uʒ ˈɡatuʃ], mas não [mɐʒ ˈnɐ̃w̃], mais bonito [ˈmajʒ buˈnitu]. It is [z] before a vowel or a vowel-initial word, including h-initial words: os amigos [uz ɐˈmiɡuʃ], dez horas [ˈdɛz ˈɔɾɐʃ]. The changed segment stays at the end of its own word. Not written: (1) vowel elision, crasis or glide formation between words beyond what the spelling shows, so de abril is [dɨ ɐˈbɾiɫ] and que é is [kɨ ˈɛ], not [kjɛ]; (2) nasal or place assimilation across words; (3) intervocalic spirantisation of /b d ɡ/, which keep their stops: a boca [ɐ ˈbokɐ]. Word-final ⟨r⟩ stays [ɾ] before a vowel: mar alto [ˈmaɾ ˈaɫtu]. Word-final ⟨l⟩ stays [ɫ] before a vowel: mal amado [ˈmaɫ ɐˈmadu]. Word-initial ⟨r⟩ is always [ʁ], even after a vowel: o rato [u ˈʁatu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, chapters on the syllable and post-lexical processes (sibilant voicing and resyllabification, spirantisation; cited, not consulted). Cruz-Ferreira 1995, pp. 90-94, connected passage. Spirantisation is omitted per the specification note.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as [j w], never i̯ u̯. In nasal diphthongs both elements carry the tilde: [j̃ w̃]. Oral falling diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], noite [ˈnojtɨ], céu [ˈsɛw], foi [ˈfoj]. ⟨ei⟩ is Lisbon [ɐj]: primeiro [pɾiˈmɐjɾu], lei [ˈlɐj]. ⟨ou⟩ is the monophthong [o]: ouro [ˈoɾu], falou [fɐˈlo]. Nasal diphthongs: mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], pão [ˈpɐ̃w̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], final -am [ɐ̃w̃], final -em/-ém [ɐ̃j̃], final -ens [ɐ̃j̃ʃ]; muito is [ˈmũj̃tu]. ⟨qu gu⟩ before ⟨a o⟩ are [kw ɡw]: quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], água [ˈaɡwɐ], quatro [ˈkwatɾu]. Before ⟨e i⟩ the ⟨u⟩ is silent: quer [ˈkɛɾ], guerra [ˈɡɛʁɐ]. This corrects the specification's ⟨gu⟩ gap: write água [ˈaɡwɐ], and keep the nasal ⟨u⟩ in segundo [sɨˈɡũdu] and algum [aɫˈɡũ]. Unstressed ⟨i u⟩ before a vowel after a consonant are written as vowels, in hiatus: história [iʃˈtɔɾiɐ], piano [piˈɐnu], suave [suˈavɨ].

Source: Cruz-Ferreira 1995, pp. 90-94 / 1999, pp. 126-130 (glide symbols j w; Lisbon [ɐj]; nasal diphthongs). Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, chapter on nasality and diphthongs (cited, not consulted). Nasal diphthong forms follow the specification note.

## Other choices

### unstressed vowel reduction

Unstressed ⟨a⟩ is [ɐ], ⟨e⟩ is [ɨ], ⟨o⟩ is [u]: cidade [siˈdadɨ], pequeno [pɨˈkenu], morar [muˈɾaɾ]. Word-initial unstressed ⟨e⟩, including es-/ex-/em-/en-, is [i] (or [ẽ] when nasal): estar [iʃˈtaɾ], exame [iˈzɐmɨ], entrar [ẽˈtɾaɾ]. Unstressed ⟨i u⟩ stay [i u]. Lexically unreduced pretonic vowels keep [a ɛ ɔ] as in Infopédia: atual [aˈtwaɫ], hospital [ɔʃpiˈtaɫ], padeiro [paˈdɐjɾu]. Nasal vowels never reduce.

Source: Cruz-Ferreira 1995, pp. 90-94. Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, chapter on vowels (cited, not consulted). Lexical exceptions per Infopédia pronunciations.

### stressed a before nasals and before palatals

Stressed ⟨a⟩ before a nasal consonant onset is [ɐ]: cama [ˈkɐmɐ], ano [ˈɐnu]. Present falamos is [fɐˈlɐmuʃ] but preterite falámos is [fɐˈlamuʃ]. Stressed ⟨e⟩ before [ʎ ɲ] is Lisbon [ɐ]: espelho [iʃˈpɐʎu], venho [ˈvɐɲu], telha [ˈtɐʎɐ].

Source: Cruz-Ferreira 1995 (Lisbon norm). Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (cited, not consulted).

### nasal vowels

A vowel before a coda ⟨m n⟩ is written as a nasal vowel with no nasal consonant: ⟨an am⟩ [ɐ̃], ⟨en em⟩ [ẽ], ⟨in im⟩ [ĩ], ⟨on om⟩ [õ], ⟨un um⟩ [ũ]. Examples: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], tempo [ˈtẽpu], sim [ˈsĩ], ponte [ˈpõtɨ]. Word-final -am, -em, -ens are diphthongs (see glides).

Source: Specification note, after Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 'Nasality'. Cruz-Ferreira 1995.

### rhotics

⟨rr⟩ and ⟨r⟩ word-initially or after ⟨n l s⟩ are the uvular [ʁ]: rato [ˈʁatu], carro [ˈkaʁu], honra [ˈõʁɐ]. All other ⟨r⟩, intervocalic, in clusters and in codas, is the tap [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], prato [ˈpɾatu], mar [ˈmaɾ]. Cruz-Ferreira writes the uvular as a trill ʀ; this set uses ʁ throughout as a broad symbol for the Lisbon uvular, a choice of symbol rather than of sound.

Source: Cruz-Ferreira 1995, pp. 90-94 (uvular rhotic, written ʀ). Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (cited, not consulted).

### laterals and sibilants

Onset ⟨l⟩ is [l]. Coda and word-final ⟨l⟩ is [ɫ]: alto [ˈaɫtu], sol [ˈsɔɫ]. ⟨lh⟩ is [ʎ] and ⟨nh⟩ is [ɲ]. Onset sibilants are plain alveolar: ⟨ss ç⟩, ⟨c⟩ before ⟨e i⟩ and initial ⟨s⟩ are [s]; intervocalic ⟨s⟩ and ⟨z⟩ are [z]: isso [ˈisu], caso [ˈkazu]. Coda sibilants are [ʃ ʒ] by voicing of the following sound: casca [ˈkaʃkɐ], mesmo [ˈmeʒmu]. ⟨x⟩ is lexical ([ʃ ks z s]): xadrez [ʃɐˈdɾeʃ], táxi [ˈtaksi], exemplo [iˈzẽplu], próximo [ˈpɾɔsimu].

Source: Cruz-Ferreira 1995, pp. 90-94, and 1999, pp. 126-127 (alveolar s z; coda ʃ ʒ; velarised coda l). Specification note.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
