# Transcription conventions: Chistabín Aragonese (Gistaín/Chistau valley) (an-x-chistabin)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write a primary-stress mark ˈ before the stressed syllable of every content word of two or more syllables. Do not mark monosyllables, and do not mark the unstressed function words listed under function_words. Place stress from the spelling, before final-r deletion applies, using these rules in order. (1) A written accent (á é í ó ú, or à è ò where the source uses them) marks the stressed vowel: pacién [paˈθjen], mía [ˈmia]. (2) If the word ends in a single vowel, or in a vowel plus -n or -s, stress the penult: casa [ˈkasa], cabán·na [kaˈbanːa], casas [ˈkasas]. (3) If the word ends in a falling diphthong (vowel plus unaccented i/u, optionally followed by -s or -n), stress the last syllable: cantau [kanˈtaw], dormiu [doɾˈmiw], cantaus [kanˈtaws]. (4) If the word ends in any other consonant or consonant cluster (-r, -l, -z, -t, -d, -ch, -m, consonant+s), stress the last syllable: comer [koˈme], cantam [kanˈtam], pastors [pasˈtoɾs]. Final -r is deleted after stress is placed, so infinitives come out oxytone: cantar [kanˈta]. Vowel length is not phonemic, so no vowel is ever written long. The geminates l·l and n·n are written Cː. When the geminate opens the stressed syllable, the stress mark goes before the geminate: bel·lota [beˈlːota], pel·lejo [peˈlːexo]. When the stressed syllable precedes it, the mark goes before that syllable: pen·na [ˈpenːa]. The lect has no case endings.

Source: Accent rules of the Aragonese orthography as applied in Blas Gabarda & Romanos Hernando 2008 (dictionary headwords); Nagore Laín 1989, Gramática de la lengua aragonesa, phonology chapter (free lexical stress, paroxytone default, oxytone infinitives and -au/-iu participles). Geminate placement follows the spec notes' examples, taken from the retazosgistain summary of Blas Gabarda & Romanos 2008 and Mott 1989.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Words in the following classes are unstressed and get no ˈ, even when they have two syllables. Definite articles: el, la, es, las and the contracted a/as (a+article): el [el], las [las]. Prepositions: a, de, en, con, per, ta, enta [enta], dende [dende], entre [entre], sobre [sobɾe], sin. Conjunctions: y/e, u, que, si, pero [peɾo], como [komo] when comparative. Proclitic and enclitic object pronouns and adverbial clitics: me, te, se, lo, la, los, las, le, les, nos, bos, ne/en, bi/i. Prenominal possessives: mi, tu, su, mis, tus, sus. Relatives and conjunctive que, cuan, do. Forms of aber used as an auxiliary: he, has, ha, em, tz, han [an]. These words are stressed and marked when polysyllabic: subject and strong pronouns (yo, tu, ell [eʎ], nusatros [nuˈsatɾos]), demonstratives (iste [ˈiste]), interrogatives (qué, cuán, dó, ¿cómo? [ˈkomo]), the negator no, quantifiers and numerals (dos, muito [ˈmujto]), all forms of ser/estar and other verbs used as main verbs, and postnominal or pronominal possessives (mío [ˈmio]). A hyphenated enclitic is its own word and is unstressed: comer-lo → [koˈme] [lo].

Source: No Chistabín-specific description of sentence stress is available. This follows Navarro Tomás 1918, Manual de pronunciación española, ch. on 'palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas' (§§ on unstressed articles, prepositions, conjunctions, clitics, auxiliaries), applied to Aragonese as in Nagore Laín 1989. Article inventory per the spec notes / Blas Gabarda & Romanos 2008.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Aragonese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Words are written identically before a pause and inside the sentence, and final vowels are never dropped or changed at a pause. Final-r deletion is not pausal; it applies to every word-final ⟨r⟩ in every position.

Source: Nagore Laín 1989 (no pausal morphophonology in Aragonese); spec notes for final-r deletion.

## Connected speech

Each word is transcribed in its citation form, and no cross-word process is written. (a) Synalepha and vowel elision across words are not written: casa alta [ˈkasa] [ˈalta], never [ˈkasalta] or [ˈkas]. (b) Elision is written only where the spelling has an apostrophe; the elided item is then its own word, given only its consonant: l'aigua → [l] [ˈajɣwa], d'ella → [d] [ˈeʎa], n'i → [n] [i]. (c) Nasal place assimilation across words is not written: en casa [en] [ˈkasa]. Inside words, ⟨n⟩ is written [n] and ⟨m⟩ is written [m] as spelled; there is no [ŋ] or [ɱ]. (d) /s/ voicing before voiced consonants is not written: es dias [es] [ˈdias]. (e) Spirantization of /b d ɡ/ is not written; always write [b d ɡ]: lo dedo [lo] [ˈdedo]. (f) There is no liaison: deleted final -r is never restored before a vowel or a clitic: comer-ne → [koˈme] [ne], fer a casa → [fe] [a] [ˈkasa]. (g) Resyllabification is not marked, and no syllable dots are used.

Source: Broad-transcription practice of Navarro Tomás 1918 and Quilis 1993, Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas (allophonic assimilation, voicing and spirantization left to narrow transcription), applied to Aragonese as in Nagore Laín 1989. Non-restoration of -r follows the spec notes and the Chistabín infinitive forms (comé) reported in the summary of Blas Gabarda & Romanos 2008.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic i and u as [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. Rising diphthongs: ie [je], ue [we], ia [ja], io [jo], ua [wa], ui [wi]: tierra [ˈtjera], fuego [ˈfweɡo], buixo [ˈbwiʃo], familia [faˈmilja]. Falling diphthongs: ai [aj], ei [ej], oi [oj], au [aw], eu [ew], iu [iw]: aire [ˈajɾe], cantau [kanˈtaw], dormiu [doɾˈmiw]. Initial hi-/hu- before a vowel is [j]/[w]: hierba [ˈjeɾba], hueso [ˈweso]. An accented í or ú is a syllable nucleus, giving hiatus: país [paˈis], mía [ˈmia]. ⟨ix⟩ is read as follows. (a) Word-initially and before a consonant, ⟨i⟩ is a syllabic prosthetic vowel: ixufrina [iʃuˈfɾina], ixo [ˈiʃo]. (b) After a, e or o, ⟨i⟩ is the glide [j]: caixigo [kajˈʃiɡo], baixo [ˈbajʃo]. (c) After u, ⟨ui⟩ is [wi] with syllabic i: buixo [ˈbwiʃo]. Glides are never formed across word boundaries: y as conjunction is [i], and la y-griega has no linking glide.

Source: Glide symbols and the ue diphthong follow Várvaro 1991, AFA XLVI-XLVII, p. 251 (Gistaín ué), and Nagore Laín 1989. The ⟨ix⟩ glide/prosthesis reading follows the spec notes (ixufrina [iʃuˈfɾina], buixo [ˈbwiʃo]) after the summary of Blas Gabarda & Romanos 2008 / Mott 1989. Some normative readings treat the i of post-vocalic ⟨ix⟩ as purely graphic ([kaˈʃiɡo]); this set follows the pronounced-glide reading reported for the valley.

## Other choices

### Word-final r deletion

Every word-final ⟨r⟩ is written ∅ in every position, after stress has been placed from the spelling: comer [koˈme], cantar [kanˈta], fer [fe], pastor [pasˈto]. ⟨r⟩ elsewhere is kept. Published descriptions document the loss chiefly in infinitives, and in some agentive -dor/-or forms. The spec note applies it to every final ⟨r⟩, and this set follows the note for consistency, including monosyllabic nouns such as mar [ma].

Source: Spec notes; summary of Blas Gabarda & Romanos 2008 and Mott 1989 (comère > comé).

### Rhotics

⟨rr⟩, and ⟨r⟩ word-initially or after n, l or s, is the trill [r]: rio [ˈrio], tierra [ˈtjera], honra [ˈonra]. Every other surviving ⟨r⟩, intervocalic, in a cluster or in coda, is the tap [ɾ]: cara [ˈkaɾa], ixufrina [iʃuˈfɾina], verde [ˈbeɾde].

Source: Nagore Laín 1989; Navarro Tomás 1918 (shared Ibero-Romance distribution).

### Geminates

Only spelled ⟨l·l⟩ and ⟨n·n⟩ are long: [lː], [nː], as in bel·lota [beˈlːota], cabán·na [kaˈbanːa]. Plain ⟨ll⟩ is the palatal [ʎ] (ell [eʎ], cullar [kuˈʎa]). Plain ⟨n⟩ and ⟨l⟩ are short. No other consonant is written long.

Source: Spec notes after the summary of Mott 1989 and Blas Gabarda & Romanos 2008.

### Consonant symbols

⟨z⟩, and ⟨c⟩ before e or i, are [θ]: cinco [ˈθinko]. ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ]: chen [tʃen]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ]. ⟨ny⟩ and ⟨ñ⟩ are [ɲ]. ⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩ are both [b]. ⟨g⟩ is [ɡ]. ⟨j⟩, and ⟨g⟩ before e or i in Castilian loans, is [x]. ⟨h⟩ is silent. ⟨qu⟩ and ⟨gu⟩ before e or i are [k] and [ɡ]. Initial f- is preserved as [f]: fuella [ˈfweʎa], fer [fe]. Intervocalic voiced stops in words such as caixigo or cadiera are written as spelled, [ɡ d]: cadiera [kaˈdjeɾa].

Source: Spec notes (distinción, ⟨ch⟩ = [tʃ], F- preserved); Nagore Laín 1989 for the orthography-to-phoneme correspondences.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
