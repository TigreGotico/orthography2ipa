# Transcription conventions: Llanero (Eastern Plains) Spanish (es-CO-x-llanero)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is lexical and follows standard Spanish orthography. A written accent marks the stressed vowel. Without one, a word ending in a vowel, n or s is stressed on the penult, and any other word on the final syllable. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable: casa [ˈkasa], ciudad [sjuˈdad], árbol [ˈaɾbol], café [kaˈfe], llanero [ʝaˈneɾo]. Every stressed monosyllable takes ˈ: sol [ˈsol], ya [ˈʝa]. Mark no secondary stress, except that -mente adverbs take two primary marks: rápidamente [ˈrapidaˈmente]. Do not write syllable dots. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so these do not apply. Write a doubled vowel letter as two vowels: creer [kɾeˈeɾ]. Write cc as [ks]: acción [akˈsjoŋ].

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, Cambridge UP, ch. 13 (stress); Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, ch. on accent.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Write these without a stress mark: definite articles (el la lo los las); all simple prepositions except según (a de en con por para sin sobre hasta desde); conjunctions and complementizers (y o pero que si como cuando porque ni); unstressed possessives before a noun (mi tu su mis tus sus nuestro- as a determiner); object and reflexive clitics (me te se lo la le nos los las les); relative pronouns without a written accent (que quien donde cual); and forms of auxiliary haber in compound tenses (he ha han había). Write these stressed: indefinite articles un una unos unas; subject and prepositional pronouns (yo tú él ella usted nosotros mí ti sí); demonstratives; interrogatives and exclamatives with a written accent (qué cómo dónde cuándo); según; numerals; negative no; and ser and estar, which are full verbs. Examples: la casa [la ˈkasa], se lo dijo [se lo ˈdiho], un perro [ˈun ˈpero], ha llegado [a ʝeˈɡado], ¿qué quieres? [ˈke ˈkjeɾeh]. The conjunction y is always [i]: él y ella [ˈel i ˈeʝa].

Source: Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1977), Manual de pronunciación española, §§ on palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas; Quilis (1993), ch. on accent; Hualde (2005), §13.2. Indefinite articles are treated as stressed, following Hualde and Quilis.

## Pausal forms

There is no pausal form. Spanish has no case endings and no nunation (tanwīn), and final vowels are never dropped before a pause or a comma. The coda rules in 'other' apply in the same way before a pause as inside the sentence: final /s/ is [h] and final /n/ is [ŋ]. Examples: los perros. [loh ˈpeɾoh], vienen. [ˈbjeneŋ].

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Longman, chapters 'Colombian Spanish' and 'Venezuelan Spanish' (lowland/llanos coda treatment); Hualde (2005) for the absence of pausal morphology.

## Connected speech

Each word stays in its own place and keeps its own vowels. Write no synalepha, no vowel merger and no resyllabification: la otra [la ˈotɾa], todo el día [ˈtodo el ˈdia], los amigos [loh aˈmiɣos] is wrong, write [loh aˈmiɡoh]. Word-final /s/ is [h] before vowels as well as before consonants (see 'other'). The one process written across a boundary is place assimilation of word-final /n/ before a consonant in the next word. Write [m] before p b m f: un peso [ˈum ˈpeso], en Bogotá [em boɡoˈta]. Write [ŋ] before k ɡ h: en casa [eŋ ˈkasa]. Write [n] before other consonants: un día [ˈun ˈdia]. Before a vowel or a pause, final /n/ is [ŋ]: en eso [eŋ ˈeso]. Word-internal nasals assimilate the same way: campo [ˈkampo], tengo [ˈteŋɡo].

Source: Hualde (2005), ch. 10 (nasal assimilation) and ch. 15 (sandhi); Lipski (1994), Venezuelan and Colombian lowland chapters, for velar [ŋ] before vowel and pause.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write a prevocalic (rising) glide as [j w]: tierra [ˈtjera], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [sjuˈdad]. Write a postvocalic (falling) glide as non-syllabic [i̯ u̯]: aire [ˈai̯ɾe], hoy [ˈoi̯], auto [ˈau̯to], muy [ˈmui̯]. In ui and iu, the first vowel is the glide: cuida [ˈkwida]. Word-initial hie- and orthographic y or ll before a vowel are the consonant [ʝ]: hielo [ˈʝelo], yo [ˈʝo]. Word-initial hue- is [we]: huevo [ˈwebo]. Do not write the rural [ɡw] variant. Vowels in hiatus stay full vowels: día [ˈdia], país [paˈih].

Source: Hualde (2005), ch. 7 (glides and diphthongs), using the j/w vs i̯/u̯ convention of Spanish phonetic tradition (RFE alphabet adapted to IPA).

## Other choices

### Coda /s/ (departs from the stub note)

Lipski describes the llanos on both sides of the border as lowland, Caribbean-like speech. The stub note models only seseo, so this rule goes beyond it. Write /s/ in any syllable coda as [h]: inside a word, word-finally before a consonant, before a vowel and before a pause. Never write it as deleted. Examples: este [ˈehte], mismo [ˈmihmo], las casas [lah ˈkasah], dos años [ˈdoh ˈaɲoh]. Onset /s/ stays [s]: casa [ˈkasa].

Source: Lipski (1994), 'Venezuelan Spanish' (llanos aspirate and elide /s/) and 'Colombian Spanish' (eastern llanos aligned with the lowland/coastal type); cf. Montes Giraldo, J.J. (1982), 'El español de Colombia: propuesta de clasificación dialectal', Thesaurus 37.

### Seseo

Write c before e or i, z, and s all as [s]: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato], hace [ˈase]. In a coda these follow the [h] rule: luz [ˈluh], hizo [ˈiso].

Source: Lipski (1994); stub note.

### Yeísmo

Write ll and consonantal y as [ʝ] in every position, including after n and l: calle [ˈkaʝe], llano [ˈʝano], conyugal [koŋʝuˈɡal] is wrong, write [konʝuˈɡal].

Source: Lipski (1994); stub note.

### Velar fricative

Write j, and g before e or i, as [h]: joven [ˈhobeŋ], gente [ˈhente], México [ˈmehiko].

Source: Lipski (1994), Colombian and Venezuelan chapters ([h] for /x/).

### Voiced obstruents

Write b and v as [b], d as [d] and g as [ɡ] in every position. Do not mark spirant allophones. Do not write the frequent loss of intervocalic or final /d/. Examples: lado [ˈlado], verdad [beɾˈdad], agua [ˈaɡwa], vaca [ˈbaka].

Source: Broad-transcription convention, phonemic level, following Hualde (2005), ch. 9. Lipski (1994) notes /d/ weakening, which is deliberately not written.

### Rhotics

Write the trill [r] for word-initial r, for rr, and for r after n, l or s. Write the tap [ɾ] elsewhere, including in codas, with no lateralization or aspiration. Examples: río [ˈrio], perro [ˈpero], honra [ˈonra], pero [ˈpeɾo], comer [koˈmeɾ].

Source: Hualde (2005), ch. 11; Lipski (1994).

### Other consonant symbols

Write ch as [tʃ], ñ as [ɲ] and x as [ks]. Write h as nothing. Examples: noche [ˈnotʃe], año [ˈaɲo], examen [ekˈsameŋ], hora [ˈoɾa].

Source: Hualde (2005), ch. 9–10.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
