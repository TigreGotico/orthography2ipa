# Transcription conventions: Andean Bolivian Spanish (es-BO-x-andino)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Spanish stress is lexical, and the spelling shows where it falls. A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel. Otherwise, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s takes penultimate stress, and any other word takes final stress; final -y counts as a consonant. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable, using Spanish syllabification (V.CV, V.C(l/ɾ)V, VC.CV): casa [ˈkasa], árbol [ˈaɾbol], canción [kanˈsjon], Bolivia [boˈlibja], otro [ˈotɾo], estoy [esˈtoj], comer [koˈmeɾ]. Mark ˈ on every stressed word of two or more syllables. Never mark a monosyllable: sol [sol], más [mas]. Adverbs in -mente keep both stresses, with ˌ on the adjective and ˈ on -men-: rápidamente [ˌrapidaˈmente]. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Spanish has no phonemic length, gemination or case. Spelled double consonants are written as two consonants with no length mark: acción [akˈsjon], innato [inˈnato].

Source: Hualde 2005, The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on word stress (stress rules, -mente compounds, syllabification); Lipski 1994 treats Andean stress as the general Spanish system.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words are unstressed and carry no ˈ, even when they have two syllables: definite articles (el la los las lo); object and reflexive clitics (me te se lo la le nos les); prenominal possessives (mi tu su nuestro); all prepositions except según; conjunctions and complementizers (y o pero que si ni porque aunque mientras); and non-interrogative que, quien, donde, cuando, como. Examples: para [paɾa], pero [peɾo], desde [desde], hasta [asta], sobre [sobɾe], nuestra casa [nwestɾa ˈkasa]. These words keep their lexical stress: indefinite articles (una [ˈuna], unos [ˈunos]), demonstratives (este [ˈeste]), subject and tonic pronouns (ella [ˈeʎa], nosotros [noˈsotɾos]), interrogatives and exclamatives (cuándo [ˈkwando], dónde [ˈdonde]), según [seˈɡun], numerals, and all verbs including auxiliaries (había [aˈbia], estaba [esˈtaba]).

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on stress (stressed vs. unstressed words), following the Navarro Tomás / Quilis inventory of atonic words.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so every word has the same form at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside a phrase. The Andean devoicing of final unstressed vowels before a pause is not written: casas [ˈkasas] everywhere.

Source: Hualde 2005 (no pausal alternations in Spanish); Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (vowel devoicing as phonetic, not phonemic).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form, and write no process across a word boundary. Vowels in contact keep their own syllables, with no synalepha or vowel merger: de ella [de ˈeʎa], la otra [la ˈotɾa]. Final consonants are not resyllabified and keep their quality: /s/ stays [s] before a vowel or voiced consonant (los amigos [los aˈmiɡos], mis manos [mis ˈmanos]). Nasals do not assimilate in place: un beso [un ˈbeso], en casa [en ˈkasa]. Highland Andean speech is described as keeping word boundaries, often with a glottal onset on a following vowel-initial word, and as resisting resyllabification, which supports this choice. Neither the glottal onset nor any phonetic assimilation is written.

Source: Lipski 1994, Bolivia and Andean chapters (resistance to resyllabification, weak synalepha, retained /s/); Hualde 2005 (synalepha and nasal assimilation as phonetic, post-lexical processes, not written in broad transcription).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the non-syllabic high vowels of diphthongs and triphthongs as [j] and [w], in both rising and falling diphthongs. Do not use i̯ or u̯. Examples: tierra [ˈtjera], bueno [ˈbweno], aula [ˈawla], rey [rej], muy [mwi], ruido [ˈrwido], Bolivia [boˈlibja], buey [bwej]. Write the palatal consonant spelled y or hi- before a vowel as [ʝ], not [j]: yo [ʝo], mayo [ˈmaʝo], hielo [ˈʝelo]. Write hiatus shown by a written accent, and hiatus between strong vowels, as two syllables: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis], poeta [poˈeta]. Spell gü as [ɡw]: pingüino [pinˈɡwino].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on diphthongs and glides (broad [j w] for glides; [ʝ] for the consonantal palatal).

## Other choices

### ll vs. y (lleísmo)

Write ll as [ʎ], distinct from y = [ʝ], because the highland Bolivian standard keeps the lateral palatal. This departs from the specification's yeísmo note (ll->/ʝ/). Examples: calle [ˈkaʎe], pollo [ˈpoʎo], ella [ˈeʎa], against mayo [ˈmaʝo], yo [ʝo].

Source: Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (retention of /ʎ/ in the Andean highlands); Hualde 2005 (lleísta Andean regions). Disagrees with the stub note.

### seseo

Write c before e or i, and z, as [s]: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [ˈsapato], hacer [aˈseɾ].

Source: Lipski 1994; specification note.

### /s/

Write /s/ as [s] in every position. It is never aspirated or dropped: las cosas [las ˈkosas], este [ˈeste].

Source: Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (retained, strongly sibilant /s/ in the highlands).

### rhotics

Write the trill [r] for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l or s: perro [ˈpero], rojo [ˈroxo], Enrique [enˈrike]. Write the tap [ɾ] everywhere else, including word-finally and in clusters: pero [ˈpeɾo], comer [koˈmeɾ], tres [tɾes]. The Andean assibilated realizations of /r/, final /ɾ/ and /tɾ/ are not written.

Source: Hualde 2005 (rhotic contrast); Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (assibilated rhotics, treated as allophonic).

### vowels

Write five full vowels [a e i o u] as spelled, in both stressed and unstressed syllables. Andean weakening or devoicing of unstressed vowels, and the e/i and o/u interchange heard from bilingual speakers, are not written: pescado [pesˈkado], oficina [ofiˈsina].

Source: Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (unstressed vowel reduction as phonetic; e/i, o/u merger as a bilingual feature, not the monolingual standard); Hualde 2005.

### voiced stops

Write b and v as [b], d as [d], and g before a, o or u (and gu before e or i) as [ɡ], in all positions. Their approximant allophones are not written. Intervocalic /d/ is kept, including in -ado: cansado [kanˈsado], vaso [ˈbaso], lago [ˈlaɡo], guerra [ˈɡera].

Source: Hualde 2005 (spirantization as allophony); Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (retention of intervocalic /d/ in the highlands).

### velar fricative and other letters

Write j, and g before e or i, as [x], never [h]: gente [ˈxente], México [ˈmexiko]. Treat h as silent: hasta [asta], hablar [aˈblaɾ]. Write ch as [tʃ], ñ as [ɲ], and qu before e or i as [k]. Write intervocalic x as [ks]: examen [ekˈsamen]. Write word-final n as alveolar [n], not velar: pan [pan]. Write conjunction y as [i].

Source: Hualde 2005; Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (velar [x], alveolar final /n/ in the highlands).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
