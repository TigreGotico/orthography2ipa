# Transcription conventions: Paulistano Portuguese (pt-BR-x-sp)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is lexical and is marked with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable. It is written on every stressed word, monosyllables included: pé [ˈpɛ], dá [ˈda], é [ˈɛ]. It is left off unstressed function words (see function_words). Placement follows the spelling. (1) A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed vowel: café [kaˈfɛ], você [voˈse], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃pɐdɐ], número [ˈnumeɾʊ]. (2) Without an accent mark, words ending in -a, -e, -o, -as, -es, -os, -am, -em, -ens are stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkazɐ], grandes [ˈɡɾɐ̃d͡ʒɪs], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], homem [ˈɔmẽj̃]. (3) Without an accent mark, words ending in -i, -u, -is, -us, -l, -r, -z, -x, -im, -um, -ns other than -ens, or a nasal diphthong written with til are stressed on the final syllable: aqui [aˈki], Brasil [bɾaˈziw], amor [aˈmoɾ], falar [faˈla], feliz [feˈlis], então [ĩˈtɐ̃w̃]. Secondary stress is never marked. Portuguese has no phonemic vowel length, no geminates and no case endings, so nothing is lengthened or doubled. Double spellings are single sounds: carro [ˈkaxʊ], osso [ˈosʊ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (word stress); Barbosa & Albano 2004, pp. 227-232 (stress marking in the illustrative transcription).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed clitics carry no ˈ and take reduced vowels. Articles: o [ʊ], a [ɐ], os [ʊs], as [ɐs]. Prepositions: de [d͡ʒɪ], em [ĩ], com [kõ], sem [sẽj̃], por [poɾ], para [paɾɐ], pra [pɾɐ], a [ɐ]. Contractions: do [dʊ], da [dɐ], no [nʊ], na [nɐ], dos [dʊs], pelo [pelʊ], pela [pelɐ], ao [aw], à [a]. Conjunctions and complementizers: e [ɪ], ou [o], mas [mas], nem [nẽj̃], que [kɪ], se [sɪ]. Object and reflexive clitics: me [mɪ], te [t͡ʃɪ], se [sɪ], lhe [ʎɪ], nos [nʊs], lo [lʊ], la [lɐ]. Stressed words carry ˈ and full vowels. These are subject and prepositional pronouns (eu [ˈew], você [voˈse], ele [ˈelɪ], nós [ˈnɔs], mim [ˈmĩ]), possessives and demonstratives (meu [ˈmew], isso [ˈisʊ]), the indefinite article or numeral (um [ˈũ], uma [ˈumɐ]), não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], and all forms of auxiliaries and copulas (é [ˈɛ], está [isˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], foi [ˈfoj], vai [ˈvaj]). Interrogatives carry stress only when accented in the spelling: quê [ˈke], porquê [poɾˈke]. Unaccented que and por que are unstressed.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (clitics as non-stress-bearing elements); Barbosa & Albano 2004 (reduced forms in the transcribed passage); Silva 2002 (unstressed vowel reduction in monosyllables).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Each word is written the same at a comma, at sentence end and in mid-sentence. The final unstressed vowels [ɪ ʊ ɐ] appear in every position, so no extra devoicing, lengthening or deletion is marked before a pause. The only pause-sensitive choice is coda /S/, written [s] before a pause (see connected_speech).

Source: Barbosa & Albano 2004; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (no pausal morphology).

## Connected speech

Only one cross-word process is written: the voicing of word-final /S/, which stays alveolar. It is written [s] before a voiceless consonant or a pause: os pratos [ʊs ˈpɾatʊs]. It is written [z] before a voiced consonant: as mesas [ɐz ˈmezɐs]. It is also [z] before a vowel-initial word, where it resyllabifies but stays written on its own word: os amigos [ʊz aˈmiɡʊs], mas ele [maz ˈelɪ]. Everything else keeps its citation form across boundaries. Vowel sandhi (elision or coalescence, as in de outro, casa amarela) is not written: de outro [d͡ʒɪ ˈowtɾʊ]? No: ou is written [o], so [d͡ʒɪ ˈotɾʊ]. Coda /l/ stays [w] before vowels: mal-estar [ˈmaw isˈta]. Non-infinitive final /R/ stays a tap [ɾ] before vowels: amor eterno [aˈmoɾ eˈtɛɾnʊ]. Infinitive final /R/ stays dropped: falar inglês [faˈla ĩˈɡles]. Same-consonant sequences are not degeminated: as salas [ɐs ˈsalɐs]. No word is ever merged with its neighbour.

Source: Barbosa & Albano 2004 (alveolar coda sibilant with voicing assimilation in São Paulo); Silva 2002 (sibilant sandhi and resyllabification); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 4.

## Glides and diphthongs

Non-syllabic vowels are written with the consonant symbols [j w], never i̯ u̯. Falling oral diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], meu [ˈmew], foi [ˈfoj], noite [ˈnojt͡ʃɪ]. Vocalized coda /l/: sol [ˈsɔw], alto [ˈawtʊ]. Nasal diphthongs carry a tilde on both elements: mão [ˈmɐ̃w̃], mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. Post-tonic i or u before a final vowel is a rising glide, and palatalization applies before [j]: história [isˈtɔɾjɐ], água [ˈaɡwɐ], pátio [ˈpat͡ʃjʊ]. Qu and gu before a pronounced u are [kw ɡw]: quando [ˈkwɐ̃dʊ]. Pretonic and stressed i or u next to a vowel are in hiatus: piano [piˈɐ̃nʊ], saúde [saˈud͡ʒɪ]. Spelled ou is monophthongized to [o]: outro [ˈotɾʊ], falou [faˈlo]. Spelled ei stays [ej]: primeiro [pɾiˈmejɾʊ]. No glide is inserted before final /S/: três [ˈtɾes], nós [ˈnɔs]. Insertion is weaker in São Paulo than in Rio.

Source: Barbosa & Albano 2004 (glide notation, nasal diphthongs); Silva 2002 (ou monophthongization, rising diphthongs, glide epenthesis as a regional trait); Callou & Leite 2001.

## Other choices

### Rhotics

Strong /r/ is written [x] word-initially, after n, l, s and for spelled rr: rato [ˈxatʊ], carro [ˈkaxʊ], honra [ˈõxɐ]. The note allows [ʁ~x~h]. Coda /R/ is always a tap [ɾ], never retroflex [ɻ] and never a fricative: porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ], mar [ˈmaɾ]. Exception: infinitive-final r is not written: fazer [faˈze].

Source: Barbosa & Albano 2004 (São Paulo speaker). Contrast Amaral 1920 §6b, where retroflex r is caipira, not capital.

### Unstressed vowels

Final unstressed vowels, with or without -s, are [ɪ ʊ ɐ]: leite [ˈlejt͡ʃɪ], livros [ˈlivɾʊs]. Pretonic e and o keep [e o], with no harmony raising: menino [meˈninʊ], coruja [koˈɾuʒɐ]. Non-final post-tonic vowels keep their quality: número [ˈnumeɾʊ]. Word-initial unstressed es- or ex- before a consonant is [is]: estado [isˈtadʊ]. Initial en- or em- is [ĩ]: entender [ĩtẽˈde].

Source: Silva 2002 (pretonic mid vowels in São Paulo); Barbosa & Albano 2004; spec note on initial eN.

### Nasalization before nasal consonants

A stressed vowel before a heterosyllabic m, n or nh is nasalized, with stressed a written [ɐ̃]: cama [ˈkɐ̃mɐ], cena [ˈsẽnɐ]. Pretonic vowels before a nasal consonant stay oral: banana [baˈnɐ̃nɐ], camisa [kaˈmizɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 7; Silva 2002.

### Palatal nasal

Nh is written [ɲ]: banho [ˈbɐ̃ɲʊ], senhor [seˈɲoɾ]. The variable [j̃] mentioned in the note is not written. Lh is written [ʎ]: filho [ˈfiʎʊ].

Source: Barbosa & Albano 2004 (consonant inventory); departs from the note's optional [j̃].

### Palatalization and clusters

/t d/ become [t͡ʃ d͡ʒ] before every [i ɪ j] within the word: tia [ˈt͡ʃiɐ], cidade [siˈdad͡ʒɪ]. Epenthetic vowels in consonant clusters are not written: advogado [advoˈɡadʊ], pneu [ˈpnew].

Source: Barbosa & Albano 2004; Silva 2002 (epenthesis as a phonetic, variable process).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
