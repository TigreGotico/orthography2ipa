# Transcription conventions: Bracarense Portuguese (pt-PT-x-braga)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every lexically stressed word, monosyllables included: [ˈpjɛ] pé, [ˈbow] vou, [kɐˈfjɛ] café, [ˈfjɛbɾɨ] febre, [ˈowtɾu] outro. Do not mark secondary stress. Unstressed function words (see function_words) carry no mark. Stress is lexical, but spelling predicts it. A written acute or circumflex accent marks the stressed vowel: [ˈmuz̺ikɐ] música, [buˈs̺je] você. A tilde marks it if no other accent is present: [kɐˈnɐ̃w̃] canção. Otherwise, words ending in -a, -e, -o (with or without -s), -am or -em(-s) stress the penult: [ˈkaz̺ɐ] casa, [ˈfalɐ̃w̃] falam. Words ending in -i, -u, -l, -r, -z, -x, -im, -um, -om, -ão or an oral diphthong stress the final syllable: [fɐˈz̺jeɾ] fazer, [ɐˈniɫ] anil, [kɐˈpɐ̃w̃] capão. Every stressed oral mid vowel takes the Bracarense onset glide: [e]→[je], [ɛ]→[jɛ], [o]→[wo], [ɔ]→[wɔ]. This also applies word-initially: [ˈjɛ] é, [ˈwobu] ovo. Unstressed syllables never take the glide. Portuguese has no phonemic vowel length and no geminates: ⟨ss⟩ and ⟨rr⟩ each write one consonant, [ˈpas̺u] passo, [ˈkaru] carro. Portuguese has no case endings.

Source: Stress placement follows Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (word stress). The tonic diphthongisation follows Cintra 1971, Boletim de Filologia 22, Baixo-Minho/Douro-Litoral isogloss, and Segura 2013, pp. 85-142. Its extension to the open mids follows Brissos 2018, pp. 196-201, and Brissos & Rodrigues 2016.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following words are unstressed: write them with no ˈ, with reduced vowels, and never with the tonic glide. Definite articles: o a os as [u ɐ uʃ ɐʃ]. Preposition+article contractions: do da no na ao à pelo pela [du dɐ nu nɐ aw a pɨlu pɨlɐ]. Simple prepositions: de em a por para com sem [dɨ ẽj̃ ɐ puɾ pɐɾɐ kõ s̺ẽj̃]. Conjunctions: e que se mas ou nem [i kɨ s̺ɨ mɐʃ ow nẽj̃]. Relative que [kɨ]. Object and reflexive clitics: me te se lhe nos vos o a lo la [mɨ tɨ s̺ɨ ʎɨ nuʃ buʃ u ɐ lu lɐ]. The following words are stressed and marked: the indefinite article um uma [ˈũ ˈumɐ]; tonic and subject pronouns eu tu ele ela nós mim ti si [ˈjew ˈtu ˈjelɨ ˈjɛlɐ ˈnwɔʃ ˈmĩ ˈti ˈs̺i]; possessives [ˈmjew] meu; demonstratives; interrogatives, e.g. [ˈkje] quê; negative não [ˈnɐ̃w̃]. All verb forms are stressed, auxiliaries ser, estar, ter, haver and ir included: [ˈjɛ] é, [ˈtjẽj̃] tem, [ˈbow] vou. A hyphenated clitic is its own word and stays unstressed: diz-me [ˈdiʒ mɨ], dá-lo [ˈda lu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (clitics and prosodic words). The stressed forms 'quiê' and 'mieu' follow Portuguese With Leo 2021 (8 accents). bou/vou with [ow] follows Portuguese With Leo 2022 (Braga).

## Pausal forms

Pausal forms do not apply: Portuguese has no case endings and no nunation. Every word is written the same way at a sentence end, at a comma and medially. Final unstressed vowels are always written in full, never deleted: [ˈdwos̺ɨ] doce, [ˈlibɾu] livro. Before any pause, word-final ⟨s z x⟩ is [ʃ]: [ɐʃ ˈkaz̺ɐʃ] as casas. Final r is [ɾ]. The variable paragogic vowel after infinitives ('faláre') is not written: [fɐˈlaɾ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2-3 (coda sibilants, final vowels). The paragoge is excluded as variable per Portuguese With Leo 2022.

## Connected speech

The only process written across word boundaries is word-final sibilant sandhi, applied to the word on the left; every word otherwise keeps its citation form. Final ⟨s z x⟩ becomes [ʃ] before a voiceless consonant or a pause, [ʒ] before a voiced consonant, and [z̺] before a vowel (resyllabified): [uʃ ˈpatuʃ] os patos, [ˈmajʒ ˈdwos̺ɨ] mais doce, [uz̺ ˈwɔbuʃ] os ovos, [ˈdiʒ mɨ] diz-me. Do not write elision or sandhi of final vowels: [dɨ ˈawɡwɐ] de água, not [d ˈawɡwɐ]. Do not write a glide or [w] for a final [u] before a vowel. Do not write crasis beyond the orthographic contractions. Final l stays [ɫ] even before a vowel: [ˈmaɫ ẽj̃ˈtẽdidu]. Final r stays [ɾ]. No assimilation of nasals is written. Spirantisation of /b d ɡ/ to [β ð ɣ] is not written anywhere, within words or across them: [ɐ ˈbakɐ] a vaca.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2-3 (sibilant sandhi, resyllabification). The apical value of the prevocalic sibilant follows Cintra 1971, pp. 11-13 of the Camões reprint.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vowels as [j] and [w], never i̯ or u̯; convert Brissos's [i̯ɛ u̯ɔ] to [jɛ wɔ]. Tonic diphthongisation: [ˈpjɛ] pé, [ˈtwɔkɨ] toque, [ˈmjedu] medo. Falling diphthongs keep the full glide, ⟨ei⟩ as [ej] and ⟨ou⟩ as [ow] in all positions, never Lisbon [ɐj] or [o]: [ˈlejtɨ] leite, [ˈbow] vou, [ˈowtɾu] outro, [ˈpaj] pai, [ˈojtu] oito. The tonic onset glide is added before ⟨eu éu⟩ ([ˈmjew] meu, [ˈs̺jɛw] céu). It is not added before ⟨ei ou oi ói⟩, nor where the stressed vowel already follows a glide ([kjˈɛtu] is wrong; write [ˈkjɛtu] quieto, [ˈawɡwɐ] água). Nasal diphthongs carry a tilde on both elements: [ɐ̃w̃] ão, [ẽj̃] em, [õj̃] õe. Unstressed i/u before a vowel in the same word is written [j]/[w]: [iʃˈtwɔɾjɐ] história. Stressed i/u in hiatus stays a vowel: [ˈdiɐ] dia.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 (glides and diphthongs). Diphthong preservation follows Portuguese With Leo 2022. 'mieu' follows Portuguese With Leo 2021.

## Other choices

### Open vs close mid vowels

Take the lexical open or close value of stressed spelling-unmarked ⟨e o⟩ from standard EP dictionaries; do not use the spec's open default. Then apply the glide: [ˈs̺jedu] cedo, [ˈmjedu] medo, [ˈwobu] ovo, [ˈwɔbuʃ] ovos, [ɐˈbwɔ] avó, [ɐˈbwo] avô. This departs from the spec note, which lets cedo and medo escape.

Source: Cintra 1971 (close [e o] → [je wo]); Brissos 2018, pp. 196-201; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2.

### Betacism

⟨v⟩ and ⟨b⟩ are both written [b] in all positions: [ˈbakɐ] vaca, [buˈs̺je] você, [ˈlibɾu] livro. [v] is never written, and [β] is not written in broad transcription.

Source: Cintra 1971, p. 87; Segura 2013; Portuguese With Leo 2022.

### Alveolar sibilants

Every non-coda alveolar sibilant is apico-alveolar, whatever the spelling (⟨s ss ç c z⟩ and sandhi [z]): [s̺] voiceless, [z̺] voiced. Examples: [ˈpas̺u] passo, [ˈkaz̺ɐ] casa, [s̺iˈdadɨ] cidade, [ˈkaʃ̺ɐ] is wrong; write [ˈkajʃɐ] caixa (⟨x⟩ stays [ʃ]). Coda sibilants stay [ʃ ʒ]. This departs from the spec note, which makes only ⟨ss⟩ apical: Cintra places the Baixo-Minho among the varieties that keep only apico-alveolar sibilants.

Source: Cintra 1971, pp. 11-13 of the Instituto Camões reprint.

### Rhotics

Strong r (word-initial ⟨r⟩, ⟨rr⟩, and ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s) is written as the alveolar trill [r]: [ˈrwɔdɐ] roda, [ˈkaru] carro, [ˈũɾɐ] is wrong; write [ˈõrɐ] honra. Every other r is written as the tap [ɾ]: [ˈkaɾu] caro, [fɐˈlaɾ] falar. Do not write uvular [ʁ]; it is the Lisbon and younger urban form.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 (rhotic variants, alveolar trill as conservative/regional); Segura 2013.

### Stressed e before palatals and ⟨em⟩

Before [ʎ ɲ ʃ ʒ j], stressed ⟨e⟩ keeps its mid value and takes the glide, never Lisbon [ɐ]: [ˈtjeʎɐ] telha, [iʃˈpjeʎu] espelho. Final and stressed ⟨em ém⟩ is [ẽj̃], not [ɐ̃j̃]: [ˈbẽj̃] bem, [ˈtjẽj̃] tem. Nasal vowels never take the tonic onset glide, except in the verb forms listed in function_words.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 (identifies [ɐ] before palatals and [ɐ̃j̃] as Lisbon); Segura 2013. Portuguese With Leo 2021 records 'beim'.

### Unstressed vowels, ⟨l⟩ and ⟨ch⟩

Unstressed vowels reduce as in standard EP: ⟨a⟩ [ɐ], ⟨e⟩ [ɨ] (initial es- [iʃ]), ⟨o⟩ [u]. Examples: [mɨˈniɲu] menino, [iʃˈtaɾ] estar, [puˈbwo] povo. ⟨l⟩ is [l] in onsets and [ɫ] in codas: [ˈs̺aɫ] sal. ⟨ch⟩ is [ʃ], not the rural northern affricate [tʃ]: [ˈʃwob] is wrong; write [ˈʃwobɐ] chove.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2-3; Cintra 1971 (affricate [tʃ] restricted to Trás-os-Montes and Alto-Minho).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
