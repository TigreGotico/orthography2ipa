# Transcription conventions: Coastal Ecuadorian Spanish (es-EC-x-costa)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and follows standard Spanish orthography. A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel. Without one, stress falls on the penultimate syllable when the spelling ends in a vowel, n or s, and on the final syllable otherwise. Coastal aspiration or loss of final /s/, /d/ or /n/ never moves the stress: 'casas' [ˈkasah], 'verdad' [beɾˈða], 'canción' [kanˈsjoŋ], 'árbol' [ˈaɾβol]. Write ˈ immediately before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: 'sol' [ˈsol], 'pan' [ˈpaŋ]. Unstressed words (see function_words) get no mark. Do not mark secondary stress, with one exception: adverbs in -mente keep both stresses, so 'rápidamente' is [ˈrapiðaˈmente]. Enclitics written solid with a verb keep the verb's stress: 'dámelo' [ˈdamelo], 'diciéndole' [diˈsjendole]. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply. Doubled letters are written as single segments ('innato' [iˈnato]), and vowels that meet are never written long.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, Cambridge UP, chapter on word stress; Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, chapter on the accent; Real Academia Española (2010), Ortografía de la lengua española, accentuation rules.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed words carry no ˈ mark. They are: definite articles el la lo los las [el la lo loh lah]; object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos os les [me te se lo la le noh oh leh]; all simple prepositions except 'según', e.g. a de en con por para sin sobre hasta [a ðe eŋ koŋ poɾ paɾa siŋ soβɾe ahta]; conjunctions y e o u ni que si pero aunque porque como (conjunctive) [i e o u ni ke si peɾo awŋke poɾke komo]; prenominal possessives mi tu su nuestro [mi tu su nwehtɾo]; and non-interrogative relatives que quien donde cuando [ke kjeŋ dondeh kwando] (write the form as phonology requires in context). Stressed words carry ˈ. They are: indefinite articles un una unos unas [ˈuŋ ˈuna ˈunoh ˈunah]; subject and prepositional pronouns yo tú él ella usted nosotros mí ti [ˈʝo ˈtu ˈel ˈeʝa uhˈte noˈsotɾoh ˈmi ˈti]; demonstratives; interrogatives and exclamatives qué quién dónde cómo [ˈke ˈkjeŋ ˈdonde ˈkomo]; numerals; 'según' [seˈɣuŋ]; tonic possessives mío suyo [ˈmio ˈsuʝo]; and every verb form, auxiliaries included: he ha es está hay [ˈe ˈa ˈeh ehˈta ˈaj].

Source: Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1977), Manual de pronunciación española, CSIC, sections on 'palabras acentuadas' and 'palabras inacentuadas'; Quilis (1993) Tratado, chapter on the accent; Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on stress (stressed vs. unstressed words).

## Pausal forms

Spanish has no pausal morphology. Case endings, final short-vowel deletion and nunation (tanwīn) do not apply, and final vowels are always written in full: 'casa' [ˈkasa]. A pausal context is the end of the sentence or any punctuation mark (comma, semicolon, colon, dash, parenthesis). The following coastal Ecuadorian word-final realisations apply there, and they are the same as before a consonant-initial word. Final /s/ is [h]: '...los niños.' [loh ˈniɲoh]. Final /n/ is velar [ŋ]: '...un pan,' [ˈuŋ ˈpaŋ]. Final orthographic d is deleted: '...la verdad.' [la βeɾˈða], 'usted' [uhˈte]. Final /ɾ/ and /l/ stay [ɾ] [l]: 'comer' [koˈmeɾ], 'papel' [paˈpel]. At a pause the next word starts with stop allophones of /b d ɡ/: '..., dice' [ˈdise]. This departs from the stub notes, which model only seseo and yeísmo. The coastal /s/, /n/ and /d/ realisations are taken from the dialect descriptions cited.

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Longman, chapter on Ecuador (coastal /s/ aspiration and elision, velar /n/); Boyd-Bowman, P. (1953), 'Sobre la pronunciación del español en el Ecuador', Nueva Revista de Filología Hispánica 7: 221–233; Toscano Mateus, H. (1953), El español en el Ecuador, Revista de Filología Española anejo 61.

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its own place, and cross-word processes only change the edge segments of the words on either side of the boundary. Coda /s/ inside a word or at the end of a word becomes [h] before a consonant or pause: 'este' [ˈehte], 'los dos' [loh ˈðoh]. It stays [s] before a vowel-initial word: 'los amigos' [los aˈmiɣoh], 'más o menos' [ˈmas o ˈmenoh]. Write [h] uniformly and never write zero for /s/, although speakers often elide it. Word-final /n/ takes the place of a following consonant, and is [ŋ] before a vowel or pause. Examples: 'un peso' [ˈum ˈpeso], 'en Guayaquil' [eŋ gwaʝaˈkil], 'en todo' [en ˈtoðo], 'con agua' [koŋ ˈaɣwa]. Inside a word /n/ also assimilates: 'cambio' [ˈkambjo], 'tengo' [ˈteŋgo]. /b d ɡ/ are stops [b d ɡ] after a pause and after a nasal, and /d/ is also a stop after /l/. Everywhere else, across word boundaries too, they are [β ð ɣ]: 'la boca' [la ˈβoka], 'el dedo' [el ˈdeðo], 'mis gatos' [mih ˈɣatoh]. Intervocalic /d/ in -ado is written [ð]: 'cansado' [kanˈsaðo]. Vowels meeting across words are not merged, elided or glided; both are written: 'la amiga' [la aˈmiɣa], 'mi hijo' [mi ˈiho], 'de usted' [de uhˈte]. Do not mark resyllabification, and write no syllable dots or linking ties.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Ecuador chapter (coastal /s/ realisation and velarisation of /n/); Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapters on nasal assimilation, spirantisation of /b d ɡ/ and vowel sequences; Navarro Tomás (1918), Manual, sections on 'enlace' and 'sinalefa'.

## Glides and diphthongs

Inside a word, a non-syllabic high vowel before the nucleus (rising diphthong) is written [j] [w]: 'bien' [ˈbjen], 'bueno' [ˈbweno], 'ciudad' [sjuˈða]. After the nucleus (falling diphthong) it is written [i̯] [u̯]: 'aire' [ˈai̯ɾe], 'hay' [ˈai̯], 'auto' [ˈau̯to], 'hoy' [ˈoi̯], 'muy' [ˈmui̯]. A stressed í or ú forms a hiatus and is a full vowel: 'día' [ˈdia], 'país' [paˈih], 'reúne' [reˈune]. Word-initial hie-, hia- and orthographic y are the consonant [ʝ]: 'hielo' [ˈʝelo], 'yo' [ˈʝo]. Word-initial hue- is [we]: 'hueso' [ˈweso]. Never form glides across word boundaries. The conjunction 'y' is always [i], and 'u' is always [u].

Source: Martínez-Celdrán, E., A.M. Fernández-Planas & J. Carrera-Sabaté (2003), 'Castilian Spanish', Journal of the International Phonetic Association 33(2): 255–259 (rising j w vs. falling i̯ u̯); Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on glides and diphthongs.

## Other choices

### seseo and yeísmo

These follow the stub notes. Orthographic c before e/i, z and s are all /s/: 'cielo' [ˈsjelo], 'zapato' [saˈpato]. Orthographic ll and y (consonantal) are both [ʝ] in every position, with no [ʎ], [ʒ] or [ɟʝ]: 'calle' [ˈkaʝe], 'llover' [ʝoˈβeɾ], 'cónyuge' [ˈkoɲʝuhe]. The highland Andean [ʎ]/[ʒ] distinction is not used on the coast.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, general chapters and Ecuador chapter; Lipski (2011), 'Geographical and Social Varieties of Spanish', in Hualde, Olarrea & O'Rourke (eds.), Handbook of Hispanic Linguistics, pp. 1–26.

### velar fricative

Orthographic j, and g before e/i, are written as glottal [h], not [x]: 'gente' [ˈhente], 'caja' [ˈkaha], 'México' [ˈmehiko]. Aspirated /s/ and /x/ therefore share the symbol [h].

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Ecuador chapter (coastal /x/ as [h]); Boyd-Bowman (1953), NRFH 7.

### rhotics and laterals

Write the trill [r] for word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s: 'perro' [ˈpero], 'rojo' [ˈroho], 'Enrique' [enˈrike]. Write the tap [ɾ] elsewhere, including in coda: 'pero' [ˈpeɾo], 'puerta' [ˈpweɾta]. Coda /ɾ/ and /l/ are kept distinct and written as spelled. Do not write the lambdacism or rhotic loss reported for some rural Afro-Ecuadorian coastal speech (Esmeraldas): 'mar' [ˈmaɾ], 'sol' [ˈsol].

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Ecuador chapter; Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on rhotics.

### vowels and affricate

Use five vowels [i e o a u] with no reduction or devoicing in unstressed syllables. Unstressed vowels are not weakened as in the highlands: 'pues' [ˈpweh], 'noches' [ˈnotʃeh]. Orthographic ch is [tʃ] and ñ is [ɲ]: 'chico' [ˈtʃiko], 'año' [ˈaɲo].

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Ecuador chapter (highland unstressed-vowel reduction absent on the coast); Quilis (1993), Tratado, vowel and consonant chapters.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
