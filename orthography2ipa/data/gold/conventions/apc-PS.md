# Transcription conventions: Palestinian Arabic (apc-PS)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable, on every stressed word, including monosyllables (ˈbeːt). Count only the stem and its suffixes. The article il- and the attached proclitics w-, b-, l-, ʕa-, la- are outside the stress domain. The verbal prefixes bi-/b-, t-, y- belong to the verb and are counted. Light syllable = CV; heavy = CVː or CVC; superheavy = CVːC or CVCC. Apply the first rule that fits: (1) A final superheavy syllable takes stress: kaˈtabt, ʃaˈbaːb, ʒaˈbalteːn. (2) Otherwise a heavy penult takes stress: muˈʕallim, kaˈtabti, ˈbeːti, ʒaˈmiːle. (3) Otherwise stress the antepenult, never further back: ˈkatabat, ˈmadrase, ˈʃaʒara. Words of two light syllables stress the first: ˈkatab, ˈwalad. Proclitics never take stress: ilˈwalad, wilˈmadrase, bilˈbeːt. Long vowels make a syllable heavy wherever they stand. Geminates are written as doubled consonants and close the preceding syllable: ˈʔawwal, muˈʕallim. Place ˈ before the first consonant of the stressed syllable; with a geminate this is the first half: maˈħalle, ħamˈmaːm. Suffixes shift stress by the same rule: ˈkatab → kaˈtabna → katabˈnaːh. There are no case endings in the dialect, so they play no part. A final short vowel left from Classical diacritics is not written.

Source: Levantine/Palestinian stress rule as given by Kenstowicz (1981, Palestinian vowel syncope and stress) and Abu-Salim (1982), summarised in Hayes (1995, Metrical Stress Theory, ch. on Palestinian Arabic) and Watson (2011, 'Word stress in Arabic', Blackwell Companion to Phonology). The exclusion of the article follows the standard Levantine treatment (Cowell 1964 for Damascus, as reported).

## Stressed and unstressed words

No ˈ (unstressed in connected speech): the article il-/l-; the proclitics w-, b-, l-, ʕa-, la-, fa-; bare prepositions such as min, fi, ʕan, maʕ, ʕala, ʕind, la, ʔabl, baʕd, zeːy (min ilˈbeːt, ʕala ˈʔeːʃ); conjunctions and complementizers w, ʔaw, bas, ʔinno, ʔinnu, ʔiza, laʔinno, ħatta; the relative illi; the vocative ya; the negators ma and miʃ; the future/progressive particles raħ, ʕam; possessive suffixes and object clitics, which are written inside the host word (ˈbeːto, ʃafˈtak). Stressed, with ˈ: prepositions that carry a pronoun suffix (ʕaˈleːh, ˈmaʕo, ˈʕindi, ˈminno); independent pronouns (ˈʔana, ˈʔinte, ˈʔinti, ˈhuwwe, ˈhiyye, ˈʔiħna, ˈʔintu, ˈhumme); demonstratives (ˈhaːda, ˈhaːdi, haˈdoːl); interrogatives (ˈʃu, ˈweːn, ˈleːʃ, ˈmiːn, ˈkiːf, ʔadˈdeːʃ); all forms of kaːn, including auxiliary kaːn (ˈkaːn, ˈkaːnat); the existential ˈfiː; and every content word. Exception: miʃ standing alone as an answer, or contrastively stressed, is written ˈmiʃ.

Source: Follows the Levantine clitic/function-word treatment in Cowell (1964, as reported in Almbark & Hellmuth 2015) and the Palestinian grammar of Bauer (1926, Das palästinische Arabisch). The pronoun and demonstrative forms follow the urban koine described by Fadda (2016:27-30).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply as a separate category: Palestinian Arabic has no case endings and no tanwīn, so no pausal form is written anywhere. Every word has the same form at a comma, at sentence end and inside the sentence. Classical case vowels and tanwīn present in the diacritized input are not transcribed. The exception is frozen adverbs in -an, which keep -an everywhere: ˈʔabadan, taʔˈriːban, ˈfiʕlan. Final long vowels of the stem are written short word-finally (ˈʔana, ˈkatabu, ˈʃaːfu) and long before a suffix (ʃaˈfuːh). Tāʾ marbūṭa is written -e everywhere (ˈmadrase, ʒaˈmiːle). It is written -a after the gutturals ħ ʕ x ɣ h ʔ, after the emphatics tˤ dˤ sˤ zˤ, and after r (ˈsaːʕa, ˈʃarika, ˈmaraʔa, ˈʔasˤsˤa). In construct and before suffixes it is written -it/-at (madrasit ilˈbanaːt, madˈrasto).

Source: Absence of case and tanwīn and the shortening of final vowels follow the standard description of urban Palestinian/Levantine (Fadda 2016:27-30; Bauer 1926). Raising of the feminine ending to -e with -a after gutturals, emphatics and r follows the general Levantine imāla pattern as described for Palestinian by Bauer (1926).

## Connected speech

Only one cross-word process is written: elision of the article vowel. The article (attached to its noun in the script, so part of the noun's word) is written il- utterance-initially and after a consonant-final word. After a vowel-final word it is written l-, without i: fi lˈbeːt, ˈʔana lmuˈʕallim, but min ilˈbeːt, ˈbeːt ilˈwalad. The vowel of illi elides in the same way: ilˈbint ˈilli, but ˈhiyye lli. Assimilation of the article to coronal ('sun') consonants is written always, because it is word-internal: iʃˈʃams, inˈnaːs, issaˈjjaːra. Nothing else is written across a word boundary: no voicing or place assimilation, no epenthetic vowel between words, no linking or resyllabification marks, no ‿. Each word keeps its citation form apart from article elision, and each word stays in its own slot.

Source: Article and helping-vowel elision as described for Levantine by Cowell (1964, as reported) and for Palestinian by Bauer (1926). Sun-letter assimilation is common to all descriptions of the urban koine (Fadda 2016:27-28).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as the consonants j and w. Never write i̯ or u̯, and never write long vowels as vowel+glide (iː, not ij; uː, not uw). Classical /aj aw/ monophthongize to eː oː (ˈbeːt, ˈjoːm, ˈsˤoːtˤ, ˈleːʃ). They are kept as aj, aw only before a glide, where the glide is geminate (ˈʔawwal, ˈħajje, saˈjjaːra). They are also kept as aj, aw where the input has an unstressed diphthong preserved in a learned word (ˈtawdʒiːh is not used; write tawˈʒiːh). Word-initial and intervocalic glides are consonants: ˈwalad, ˈjimʃi, ˈhiyye → write ˈhijje, ˈhuwwe. A word-final glide after a short vowel is written as a consonant: ʃˈwaj.

Source: Fadda (2016:30): /aj aw/ → [eː oː] except before a glide (bajt → beːt, sˤawt → sˤoːtˤ). Long-vowel inventory /iː eː aː oː uː/ per Fadda (2016:30), after Al-Wer (2011:4), consistent with Almbark & Hellmuth (2015 §1.1).

## Other choices

### Variety chosen (qaf)

Transcribe the urban koine (Jerusalem/Jaffa/Nazareth) throughout. ق → ʔ: ˈʔaːl, ʔalb → ˈʔalb, taʔˈriːban. Do not write the rural k, the Bedouin/Gaza ɡ or the qeltu q. The spec note lists four variants; this convention fixes one, the prestige urban form the note lists first. In learned MSA loanwords that keep q in urban speech, write q: ilˈqurʔaːn, ˈqaːnuːn.

Source: Fadda (2016:28), after Al-Wer & Herin (2011:60); Cotter (2016:152).

### jim

ج → ʒ (urban): ʒaˈmiːl, ˈʒabal, ˈdaraʒ. Never dʒ.

Source: Fadda (2016:27-28): Ammani/urban Palestinian koine has [ʒ], 'identical to other Levantine cities'.

### Interdentals

Write the urban stops. θ → t, ð → d, ðˤ → dˤ in native words: ˈtalaːte, ˈdahab, dˤˈdˤuhur → write ˈdˤuhur. In learned/MSA words, θ → s, ð → z, ðˤ → zˤ: ˈʔiza 'if', ˈzˤaːhir, ˈmasalan. Never write θ, ð or ðˤ.

Source: Cotter (2016:150-154), after Bergsträßer (1915, Map 1) and Palva (1984:361-62); Fadda (2016:27-28). The learned sibilant reflexes follow the general Levantine description (Cowell 1964, as reported).

### kaf

ك → k in all positions (urban): ˈkalb, ˈkiːf, ˈʔinte ˈkbiːr. Do not write the rural tʃ.

Source: Urban koine per Fadda (2016:27-28); the rural kaf-shift in the spec note is not followed.

### Short vowels and emphasis

Write short vowels only as i a u, with no e, o or ə for short allophones: ˈkitib, ˈʔuxt. The exceptions are the feminine ending -e and final -e in pronouns and demonstratives, which are written e as a fixed lexical form: ˈʔinte, ˈhuwwe → write ˈhuwwe, ˈhaːde not used; write ˈhaːda. Write emphatics as tˤ dˤ sˤ zˤ. Do not mark emphasis spreading or backing on vowels: ˈtˤaːleb is written ˈtˤaːlib.

Source: Fadda (2016:30): short /i u a/. Almbark & Hellmuth (2015 §3-4): mid short vowels and schwa are allophones of /i u/, so a broad transcription writes i u.

### Pronominal suffixes

Use the urban forms: 3sg.m -o (ˈbeːto, ˈʃaːfo), 3sg.f -ha, 2sg.m -ak, 2sg.f -ik, 1sg -i / -ni, 1pl -na, 2pl -kum, 3pl -hum. After a vowel, 3sg.m is lengthening of the vowel + h: ʃaˈfuːh, ʕaˈleːh.

Source: Bauer (1926), urban Palestinian; consistent with the Ammani koine in Fadda (2016).

### Hamza and gemination

Write the glottal stop ʔ wherever it is pronounced, including word-initially before a vowel (ˈʔana, ˈʔinte). The article is written il without ʔ. Write geminates as doubled letters, never with ː: ˈʔawwal, ħamˈmaːm.

Source: General Levantine transcription practice (Cowell 1964, as reported; Fadda 2016).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
