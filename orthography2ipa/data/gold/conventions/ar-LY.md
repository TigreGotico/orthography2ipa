# Transcription conventions: Libyan Arabic (ar-LY)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ on every word that carries stress, including monosyllables. Place ˈ before the whole onset of the stressed syllable, word-initial clusters included: [ˈktəb], [ˈʃʃəms]. Syllabify medial VCCV as VC.CV and VCCCV as VCC.CV. Write geminates as doubled consonants, never with ː; they close the preceding syllable. Count from the right edge of the word as spoken, after syncope, with suffixes and the negative -ʃ included. (1) Stress the final syllable if it is superheavy (VːC, VCC, VːCC): [kˈtaːb] is wrong, write [ˈktaːb]; [mak.ˈtuːb]; [ma.ˈktəbʃ]. (2) Otherwise stress the penult: [ˈɡa.lib] 'heart', [ˈɡaː.lib] 'mould', [ˈmad.rsa], [ˈʕam.ma]. (3) Stress the antepenult only if the penult is light and the antepenult is heavy: [ˈmak.ta.ba]. Syncope means this case rarely arises. Case endings do not exist in the dialect. Classical words used inside a sentence take their dialect form without case vowels and are stressed by the same rule.

Source: Pereira 2010 (Le parler arabe de Tripoli, stress section; not consulted to the page), with the general Arabic weight-sensitive rule in Watson 2002 ch. on syllable and stress. Light-syllable syncope that shapes the counted forms follows Benkato 2020 p.200 and Watson 2002 p.13.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Write without ˈ these monosyllabic or clitic grammatical words, which are unstressed in connected speech. Prepositions: [fi], [mən], [ʕla], [ʕan], [b], [l]. Conjunctions: [w], [u], [walla], [ʔaw]. The relative [əlli]. The vocative [ja]. The genitive exponent [mtaːʕ] when it stands before a noun. The negator [ma]. The future and intention particle [bʃ] / [baʃ]. When any of these is a separate word, it carries no ˈ: [fi ˈlbeːt], [mən ˈtˤrabləs], [w ˈhuːwa]. The article and proclitics written attached in the orthography stay inside their host word and never take the stress, which falls on the host by the stress rule: [ˈlbeːt], [ˈʒʒməl], [bˈlɡalam] is wrong, write [blˈɡa.lam]. Write these with ˈ, because they are stressed: independent pronouns ([ˈaːna], [ˈhuːwa], [ˈhiːja]); demonstratives ([ˈhaːda], [ˈhaːdi]); interrogatives ([ˈʃənu], [ˈkeːf], [ˈweːn]); auxiliaries and aspect verbs ([ˈkaːn], [ˈɡaːʕid]); the negator [ˈmuʃ] when it stands alone before a predicate; and all content words.

Source: Pereira 2010 and Pereira 2011 EALL 'Tripoli Arabic' (pronouns, particles, negation). The unstressed status of proclitic particles follows the general Arabic dialect description in Watson 2002 and Holes 2004.

## Pausal forms

Pausal forms in the Classical sense do not apply: the dialect has no case endings, no nunation and no final short case vowels. Transcribe every word in the same form at a sentence end, at a comma and sentence-internally. Write tāʾ marbūṭa as [-a] when the word is free: [ˈmad.rsa]. Write it as [-t] in construct and before suffixes: [ˈmad.rsət ˈlbnaːt]; the [-t] is grammatical, not pausal. Fixed adverbs borrowed with tanwīn keep the [-an] the dialect pronounces, everywhere: [ˈʃuk.ran], [ˈɡaː.lə.ban] is wrong; use the dialect form [ˈdaːj.man] only if it is spoken that way. Do not drop any segment at a pause. The phonetic raising of final -a (imāla) reported for Tripoli is not written.

Source: Pereira 2010 and Pereira 2011 EALL (absence of case inflection; tāʾ marbūṭa alternation). Imāla is left unwritten as a broad-transcription choice.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its own lexical form. Cross-word assimilation, elision, linking and resyllabification are not written. Each word keeps its own syllabification and ˈ: [ˈkaːn fi ˈlbeːt], never with the [n] assimilated or the vowel of [fi] elided. Do not spread emphasis across a word boundary. Within the word, write these processes: article assimilation to sun letters, ʒ included ([ˈʒʒməl], [ˈddaːr], [ˈssuːɡ]; moon letters keep [l]: [ˈlɡa.lam], [ˈlbeːt]); syncope of short vowels in light open syllables ([ˈktəb], [ˈkətbət] is wrong, write [ˈkətbət] only if that is the dialect form, and never write *[ˈka.tab]); emphasis backing. Do not insert a prothetic vowel before word-initial clusters: write [ˈktaːb], not [əkˈtaːb].

Source: Benkato 2020 p.200 (Tripoli ktəb vs Benghazi kitab) and Watson 2002 p.13 for syncope. Pereira 2011 EALL for ʒ as a sun letter. Leaving cross-word sandhi unwritten is a broad-transcription choice made for consistency.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as the consonants [j] and [w], never as [i̯] or [u̯]. Write Classical /aj/ and /aw/ as the monophthongs [eː] and [oː], which is the urban Tripoli mainline: [ˈseːf] 'sword', [ˈloːn] 'colour', [ˈzoːz] 'two', [ˈbeːt], [ˈjoːm]. Do not write the residual diphthong variant [sajf]. Write a sequence as [aj] or [aw] only where a glide is a geminate or stands before a vowel: [ˈʕaj.jaːt], [ˈhuːwa], [ˈhiːja], [ˈdaːj.man]. Write a glide between a long vowel and a following vowel only where the dialect has it lexically. Do not insert glides as a hiatus breaker elsewhere.

Source: Pereira 2010 pp.66-67 and Pereira 2011 EALL (monophthongization in Tripoli, with diphthong retention as a residual variant).

## Other choices

### qāf

Write ق as [ɡ]: [ˈɡa.lib], [ˈɡaːl], [ˈsuːɡ]. Write [q] only in recent Literary Arabic borrowings that the speaker clearly pronounces with q, for example [ˈqaː.nuːn] in legal register. Default to [ɡ] when in doubt.

Source: Pereira 2011 EALL; Watson 2002 p.17.

### jīm

Write ج as the fricative [ʒ] and treat it as a sun letter: [ˈʒʒməl], [ˈʒaːmʕa]. Do not use the eastern affricate [dʒ] (Owens 1984 for Benghazi). Write [dʒ] only in Italian loans: [ɡriːˈdʒuː] is wrong, write [ˈɡriː.dʒu] following the stress rule.

Source: Pereira 2011 EALL for Tripoli; Benkato 2020 p.201 for the Italian loans.

### interdentals

Write the Tripoli merger: ث → [t], ذ → [d], ظ → [dˤ]: [ˈtluːt] 'three', [ˈhaːda] 'this', [ˈdˤɑhr] 'noon'. Do not write the eastern and Bedouin [θ ð ðˤ] (Owens 1984).

Source: Benkato 2020 p.201; Watson 2002 p.15.

### emphatics and backing

Write the emphatics as [tˤ dˤ sˤ zˤ]. Write /a aː/ as [ɑ ɑː] when immediately before or after an emphatic in the same word, and only there: [ˈtˤɑːləb], [ˈsˤɑbaħ], but [ˈmad.rsa]. Do not write backing next to r, q or ɡ.

Source: Watson 2002 (emphasis), as the broad allophone choice of the specification.

### short vowels

Use [ə] for the reflex of short *i and *u in closed and final closed syllables: [ˈktəb], [ˈʃʃəms]. Keep [a] as it is. Write [i] and [u] for word-final short vowels ([ˈbeːti] 'my house'), in open syllables the dialect keeps, and in loans. Write long vowels as [iː uː aː eː oː] with ː.

Source: Pereira 2010 (vowel system, /ə/); Benkato 2020 p.200.

### symbols for other consonants

Write خ as [x], غ as [ɣ], ح as [ħ], ع as [ʕ], ش as [ʃ], ر as [r]. Write the glottal stop [ʔ] only where it is pronounced, mostly in literary borrowings. Do not add a glottal stop before word-initial vowels: [ˈaːna]. Italian-loan [p v tʃ] are written as heard.

Source: Pereira 2011 EALL; Benkato 2020 p.201 for loan phonemes.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
