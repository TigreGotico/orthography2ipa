# Transcription conventions: Lebanese Arabic (Beiruti) (apc-LB)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark one primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every word, including monosyllabic content words. The only unmarked words are the unstressed function words listed under function_words. Secondary stress is never marked. Syllabify first: a geminate splits across two syllables (ʕal.lam), and an intervocalic single consonant starts the next syllable. Place stress by syllable weight, counting from the end of the word as transcribed (after case endings are dropped and clitics are attached). (1) Stress a final superheavy syllable, CVːC or CVCC: kiˈtaːb → kˈteːb, ʃaˈfit, katabˈt (rare), daˈraʒt. (2) Otherwise stress the penult if it is heavy, CVː or CVC: ˈbeːti, ˈkaːtib, mʕalˈlme, ʕalˈlam. (3) Otherwise stress the antepenult: ˈmadrase, ˈkatabit, ˈʔahwe. Unlike Cairene, Levantine never stresses a light penult in CVCVCV(C) words, so write ˈmadrase, not madˈrase. A geminate makes the syllable before it heavy, so it pulls stress: ħamˈmaːm, mʕalˈlim. Long vowels: an underlying word-final long vowel is shortened and unstressed (ˈʔana, ˈkaːnu → ˈkeːnu, ˈmaʃi). It becomes long and stressed again only when a suffix follows, including the silent object pronoun -h: ʃaˈfuː 'they saw him', ʔaˈriːha. Case endings and tanwīn are never pronounced, so they never count for stress (see pausal_forms). The fixed adverbs in -an keep the syllable: ˈʔabadan, taˈmaːman. Monosyllables: ˈbeːt, ˈʔaːl, ˈʃu (when used as an interrogative content word).

Source: Levantine (Syro-Lebanese) weight-based stress as in Cowell 1964 (Damascus), cited only as reported in Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 §1.3 since the primary text was not obtained; Watson 2002 (stress chapter: the Levantine/Cairene contrast in CVCVCV words). Stress marking with ˈ follows the spec notes.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and written without ˈ when they stand as separate words. Prepositions: fi, ʕala (and ʕa when the text writes it), min (and mn), maʕ, la, ʕan, baʕd when used as a preposition. Conjunctions: w/u, bas, ʔaw, ʔiza, law, ʔinnu, la-ʔinnu, ħatta. Relative: ʔilli/jalli. Negators: ma, mij/miʃ. Aspect and future particles: ʕam, raħ (rɑħ is not written; see other). Vocative: ja. Definite article: it is always part of its host word in Arabic script, so it is never a separate word and never stressed (ilˈbeːt). The proclitics b-, l-, w-, ka- and the bi- aspect prefix are written inside their host, as are the suffixed pronouns -i, -ak, -ik, -o, -a, -na, -kun, -un, -ni. They are never stressed themselves, but they can shift the host's stress by the rules above (ˈbeːto, beːtˈna → stressed ˈbeːtna by rule 2; ʃaˈfuː). These carry stress like content words: independent pronouns (ˈʔana, ˈʔinte, ˈhuwwe, ˈhiyye, ˈnəħna is not written, use ˈnaħna, ˈhinne), demonstratives (ˈheːda, ˈheːdi, ˈhaʔ), interrogatives (ˈʃu, ˈmiːn, ˈweːn, ˈleːʃ, ˈkiːf, ˈʔaddeːʃ), kaːn/keːn used as an auxiliary (ˈkeːn ʕam jiktob), the existential ˈfiː (fiː 'there is', stressed, as against the preposition fi), and the negative ˈmaː when it stands alone as an answer. Example: ma ˈbaʕrif ˈweːn ˈkeːn.

Source: Cowell 1964 (clitic and particle inventory of Syrian Arabic, as reported in Almbark & Hellmuth 2015) and general Levantine descriptions. The list of unstressed particles is a project decision for consistency; the descriptions do not tabulate stress per particle.

## Pausal forms

Beiruti has no case system, so there is no separate pausal/contextual alternation. The same colloquial form is written at the end of a sentence, at a comma and in the middle of a sentence. In every position, drop the Classical final short case vowels (-u -a -i) and nunation (-un -an -in) even if the diacritized input writes them: كِتابٌ → kˈteːb, البيتِ → ilˈbeːt. Exception: lexicalized adverbs in -an keep it everywhere (ˈʔabadan, taˈmaːman, ˈʃukran). Tāʾ marbūṭa is never read as -t or -h in absolute position. It is written -e, or -a after blockers (see other: imāla), in every position: ˈmadrase, ˈsaːʕa. Before a following genitive noun in construct (iḍāfa) or before a pronoun suffix, it is -it, or -at after blockers: ˈmadrasit ilˈbalad, ˈsaːʕta → write ˈsaːʕit when unsuffixed in construct and ˈsaːʕto with the suffix -o. Word-final underlying long vowels are written short in every position (ˈʔana, ˈkeːnu). They are not lengthened at pause.

Source: Fadda 2016 pp.27-30 and Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 (urban Levantine vowel and word structure, with no case inflection). The -e/-a/-it treatment of tāʾ marbūṭa follows the spec notes on final imāla.

## Connected speech

Write only two cross-boundary processes. Every word stays in its own slot and no words are joined. (1) Prothetic vowel of the article and of initial clusters. A word beginning with the article is written il- (or with an assimilated article, iʃ-, it-, etc.: iʃˈʃams, inˈnaːs) when it follows a pause or a consonant-final word. It is written l-, ʃʃ-, nn- without the vowel when it follows a vowel-final word. Examples: ˈʃift ilˈbeːt, but fi lˈbeːt and ʕala nˈnaːs. The sun-letter assimilation of the article (to t d s z ʃ ʒ r l n and their emphatics) is always written because it is word-internal: irˈraʒʒaːl, idˈdeni. Before ʒ the article stays l: ilʒˈʒaːr is not used, write ilˈʒaːr. (2) The bound pronoun -h after a vowel is silent and lengthens and stresses that vowel (ʃaˈfuː); this is word-internal. Do not write the following: voicing or place assimilation across word boundaries (mn ˈbeːrut, not mm ˈbeːrut); elision of word-final vowels before vowel-initial words; epenthetic vowels between words; gemination across words. Do not write resyllabification; syllabify each word on its own.

Source: Standard Levantine article allomorphy (il-/l-, sun-letter assimilation) as in Cowell 1964 (as reported in Almbark & Hellmuth 2015) and the urban Levantine description in Fadda 2016. Limiting cross-word marking to the article vowel is a broad-transcription decision of this project.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the glides as consonants j and w, never y, i̯ or u̯. Native /aj aw/ monophthongize to eː oː in a closed or final syllable: ˈbeːt, ˈjoːm, ˈloːn, ˈʔeːʃ. Keep aj aw, written with j w, (a) before a vowel or a glide (ˈʔawwal, ˈħajje → ˈħajje, ˈsajjaːra, ˈʔawaːm is not used) and (b) in learned Classical words that keep the diphthong (ˈdawle → ˈdawle, ˈʒawz is kept only if the text is clearly MSA). Geminate glides are written jj ww: ˈhiyye → ˈhijje, ˈhuwwe. Word-final -i and -u stay vowels and get no glide: ˈkursi, ˈʔabu. Word-initial j w before a vowel are consonants: ˈwalad, ˈjalla.

Source: Fadda 2016 p.30 (/aj aw/→[eː oː] except before a glide: bajt→beːt, sˤawt→sˤoːtˤ); Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 §1.1 (mid long vowels from coalescence). Symbols j/w follow broad IPA as in the WikiPron apc_arab_broad gold.

## Other choices

### imāla (raising of aː and final -a)

Following the spec notes, write long /aː/ as eː unless an emphatic (tˤ sˤ dˤ zˤ), a guttural (ħ ʕ x ɣ, and q in MSA words) or r is immediately adjacent: ˈbeːb, ˈkeːn, ˈʃeːf, kˈteːb, but ˈsˤaːr, ˈtˤaːwle, ˈħaːl, ˈraːħ, ˈxaːl. Write final tāʾ marbūṭa (and other final /-a/) as -e: ˈmadrase, ˈkbiːre. Write -a after emphatics, gutturals and h (ˈsaːʕa, ˈʔasˤsˤa, ˈwaʔfa is written ˈwaʔfe since ʔ does not block) and after r not preceded by i/iː/eː (sajˈjaːra). Published descriptions report that Beirut proper has weaker medial imāla ([aː] to [ɛː]) than Mount Lebanon [eː]. This transcription follows the spec's strong-imāla eː uniformly, and that choice departs from the Beiruti norm for medial aː.

Source: Spec notes (LB_IMALA_* rules). The final -e/-a split is the Syro-Lebanese pattern described by Cowell 1964 (as reported). On regional /aː/ height: Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 p.2.

### emphatics and emphasis spread

Write emphatic consonants with ˤ: tˤ sˤ dˤ zˤ. Do not write backed vowel allophones; next to emphatics write a/aː, not ɑ/ɑː: ˈsˤaːr, ˈtˤawiːl. Do not write emphasis spread onto neighbouring consonants.

Source: Watson 2002 (emphasis spreading, treated here as allophonic and not written in broad transcription).

### consonant reflexes (qāf, jīm, interdentals, gāf)

Write ق as ʔ in colloquial words (ˈʔaːl, ˈʔalb) and as q only in clearly MSA/learned words (ilˈqurʔaːn). Write ج as ʒ (ˈʒamal). Write ث ذ ظ as t d dˤ in core vocabulary (tˈleːte, ˈdahab, ˈdˤahr). Published descriptions and normal usage have s z zˤ in learned Classical borrowings, so write ħaˈdiːs, ˈzˤarf; this departs from the spec note, which merges only to t d dˤ. Write گ as ɡ, in loans only (ˈtfinɡe, ɡraˈvaːt).

Source: Fadda 2016 pp.27-28 (urban Levantine [ʔ t d dˤ ʒ]); WikiPron apc_arab_broad (seven گ rows read as ɡ); spec notes.

### short vowels and syncope

Write short vowels as i a u, never e o ə ɪ ʊ, except final -e from imāla. Mid-vowel realizations are allophones of /i u/. Lebanese drops the short vowel of the first open syllable in nouns and adjectives of the pattern CaCiːC, CiCaːC, CaCaːC, CuCuːC, even when the input diacritizes it: kˈbiːr, kˈtiːr, ʒˈdiːd, kˈteːb, ʒˈmiːl. Otherwise write exactly the short vowels the diacritics give.

Source: Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 §3-4 (three short phonemes /i a u/, with mid vowels as allophones); Levantine syncope as reflected in the WikiPron apc_arab_broad gold (e.g. tfinge).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
