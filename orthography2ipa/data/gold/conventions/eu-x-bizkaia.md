# Transcription conventions: Biscayan Basque (Bizkaiera) (eu-x-bizkaia)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark one primary stress ˈ before the stressed syllable of every content word, monosyllables included. Default rule (Markina type): disyllables take stress on the first syllable, words of three or more syllables on the antepenult, counting case endings and the article as syllables of the word: [ˈbaso] baso, [ˈetʃie] etxea, [ˈɡiʃona] gizona, [ˈɡiʃonaɾi] gizonari. Plural inflection is pre-accenting. Stress falls on the syllable just before the plural morph (-ak/-ek, -ei, -en, -etan, -etara, -etatik, -ekin): [ɡiˈʃonak] 'the men' against singular ergative [ˈɡiʃonak] 'the man'. Read number from context; when it stays ambiguous, treat the form as singular. Basque has no phonemic long vowels and no geminates, so neither affects the count. Vowel sequences made by the article count as two syllables: [ˈkalie]. Put the stress mark at the syllable onset: a single consonant goes with the following vowel, and obstruent plus liquid is an onset. This is a stress-mark rendering of a pitch-accent system; no tones are written.

Source: Hualde (2003) in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina, A Grammar of Basque, accentuation section (Markina-type and western Bizkaian systems); Bedialauneta Txurruka & Hualde (2023), JIPA 53(3), prosody section. The default and plural rules are a normalised reading of these descriptions, chosen for consistency.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ: conjunctions and particles eta/ta [eta], edo [eɾo], ze [s̺e], baina [baina], the indefinite bat [bat] after a noun, the complementiser and question particles, and every inflected auxiliary standing as its own word: dot [dot], dau [dau̯], da [da], dabe [dabe], eban [eban], ditu/dittu [ditʃu]. Stressed, with ˈ: personal pronouns [ˈni], [ˈs̺u], [ˈbeɾa], demonstratives [ˈau̯], [ˈoɾi], [ˈa], the negator ez [ˈes̺], bai [ˈbai̯], numerals other than indefinite bat, interrogatives, and the lexical (participle) verb. Articles and case markers are suffixes, so they are never separate words.

Source: Hualde (2003), A Grammar of Basque, accentuation section (auxiliaries and particles are deaccented in the verbal complex); Bedialauneta Txurruka & Hualde (2023).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Basque has no pausal forms and no nunation. Final vowels and case endings are written the same at a comma, at sentence end and inside the sentence: [ˈetʃie] in every position.

Source: Hualde (2003), A Grammar of Basque, segmental phonology, pp. 15-65 (no pausal alternations described).

## Connected speech

Write three cross-word processes, each on the affected word only. (1) Spirantisation: a word-initial /b d ɡ/ becomes [β ð ɣ] when the preceding word in the same sentence ends in a vowel or glide with no comma between. It stays a stop at sentence start, after a comma, after a nasal, and for /d/ after /l/: [ˈetʃie ðau̯], but [ˈ ɡis̺on bat]-type contexts keep the stop. Word-internal intervocalic /b d ɡ/ are always [β ð ɣ]: [ˈaβeɾe]. (2) A word-final nasal takes the place of articulation of a following word-initial consonant: [ˈɡiʃom ˈbat] → write [m] in the first word, and [ŋ] before /k ɡ/. (3) After ez, a following auxiliary's initial /d/ devoices: ez dot → [ˈes̺ tot]. Do not write vowel elision, liaison or resyllabification marks. Palatalisation after /i/ applies only inside a word. Each word keeps its own IPA string.

Source: Bedialauneta Txurruka & Hualde (2023), p. 1099 (approximants), p. 1103 (nasal assimilation); Hualde (2003), A Grammar of Basque, segmental phonology (ez + auxiliary devoicing, phrase-level spirantisation).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write falling diphthongs with a non-syllabic offglide: ai̯, ei̯, oi̯, ui̯, au̯, eu̯, as in [ˈbai̯], [ˈau̯], [ai̯ˈʃie] haizea. Write vowel sequences created by suffixation, including the raised article, as full vowels in hiatus with no glide and no epenthetic [j]: [ˈkalie], [ˈbas̺ue], [ˈmendie]. A palatal glide in syllable onset is written [j], but never for orthographic j (see other).

Source: Bedialauneta Txurruka & Hualde (2023), vowels and diphthongs section; pp. 1107-1108 for article sequences.

## Other choices

### Article vowel raising (departs from the specification note)

Apply it, although the note leaves it unmodelled. Singular article -a after a stem-final vowel: e+a → ie, i+a → ie, o+a → ue, u+a → ue, a+a → a. Examples: kalea [ˈkalie], mendia [ˈmendie], basoa [ˈbas̺ue], burua [ˈbuɾue], alaba [ˈalaβa]. The same applies to -ak in the singular ergative: [ˈbas̺uek].

Source: Bedialauneta Txurruka & Hualde (2023), pp. 1107-1108.

### Sibilants and palatals

Orthographic s and z → [s̺]. Orthographic ts and tz → [ts̻]. x → [ʃ]. tx and tt → [tʃ]. ll → [ʎ]. ñ → [ɲ]. Inside a word, after /i/ or offglide i̯: /s/ → [ʃ], /t/ → [tʃ], /n/ → [ɲ], /l/ → [ʎ]. Examples: [ˈɡiʃona], [ˈmaiʎa], [aˈtʃe].

Source: Bedialauneta Txurruka & Hualde (2023), p. 1098 (mergers), pp. 1102-1104 (palatalisation).

### Orthographic j

Write the velar fricative [x], following Markina usage: jan [ˈxan], jaun [ˈxau̯n]. Do not write [j] or [dʒ].

Source: Bedialauneta Txurruka & Hualde (2023), consonant inventory; Hualde (2003), segmental phonology (dialectal reflexes of j).

### h and aspiration

Orthographic h is silent, and no stop is aspirated: hau [ˈau̯], hemen [ˈemen].

Source: Hualde (2018), Aspiration in Basque; Bedialauneta Txurruka & Hualde (2023).

### Rhotics

Single intervocalic r → [ɾ]: [ˈoɾi]. Orthographic rr, and r at the end of a word or before a consonant → [r]: [ˈaɾi] against [ˈari].

Source: Hualde (2003), A Grammar of Basque, segmental phonology; Bedialauneta Txurruka & Hualde (2023).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
