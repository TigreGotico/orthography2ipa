# Transcription conventions: Belsetán Aragonese (Bielsa valley) (an-x-belsetan)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables. Do not mark monosyllables. Place the stress by the orthographic rule of Aragonese, which matches Spanish. A written acute accent fixes the stressed vowel: tenié [teˈnje], trayé [traˈje], río [ˈrio]. Without an accent, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s takes stress on the penult: casa [ˈkasa], capan·na [kaˈpanːa], casas [ˈkasas]. A word ending in any other consonant takes stress on the last syllable: nuet [ˈnwet], cantar [kanˈtaɾ], ciudat [θjuˈdat]. Diphthongs and triphthongs count as one syllable. There are no phonemic long vowels. The geminates [lː nː] are coda plus onset and never move the stress. Write the length mark on the consonant and keep ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable: bel·la [ˈbelːa], pen·na [ˈpenːa], payel·la [paˈjelːa]. Case endings: not applicable, since Aragonese has no nominal case.

Source: Aragonese orthographic stress rules as set out in Nagore Laín 2001 (Gramática de la Lengua Aragonesa, phonology and orthography chapters). These parallel Quilis 1993 (Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas) for Ibero-Romance. The geminate treatment follows Badía Margarit 1950, as reported in the secondary es.wikipedia 'Aragonés belsetano'.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following words are unstressed and are written without ˈ, even when they have two syllables. Articles: el, la, es, las, o, a, os, as, l. Prepositions: a, de, d, en, con, per, ta, enta, entre, dende, dica, sin, sobre. Conjunctions: e, y, u, que, pero, porque, si, cuan in its conjunction use, ni. Clitic object and adverbial pronouns: me, te, se, lo, la, los, las, le, les, nos, tos, vos, ne, en, i, bi, and their apostrophe forms 'm, 't, 's, 'l, 'n. Prenominal possessives. Auxiliary forms of aber in compound tenses: he, has, ha, em, tz, han. Examples: [de la ˈkasa], [ta ˈbjelsa], [me lo ˈdize]. The following words are stressed and take ˈ when they have two or more syllables. Subject and tonic pronouns: yo, tu, er, ers, nusaltros [nuˈsaltɾos], vusaltros [buˈsaltɾos]. Demonstratives, interrogatives and exclamatives (qué, quí, án, cómo), numerals, quantifiers such as bel·la [ˈbelːa], adverbs, negation, and all lexical verbs, including non-auxiliary uses of aber and estar. Stressed monosyllables are still left unmarked, as in the stress rule.

Source: Inventory of Aragonese articles, clitics and auxiliaries from Nagore Laín 2001. The Belsetán article allomorphs and the pronouns er/ers come from Badía Margarit 1950, via the lect notes and es.wikipedia. Treating these classes as atonic follows the Ibero-Romance tradition of Navarro Tomás 1918 (Manual de pronunciación española) and Quilis 1993.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Belsetán has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so every word is written in the same form at a comma, at sentence end and inside the sentence (e.g. nuet [ˈnwet] everywhere; no final devoicing or vowel loss is transcribed).

Source: Nagore Laín 2001; general Ibero-Romance phonology (Quilis 1993). No Aragonese description posits pausal alternants.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its own lexical, phonemic form. Do not transcribe cross-word assimilation: en Bielsa is [en ˈbjelsa], not [em …], and las bacas is [las ˈbakas], not [laz …]. Do not transcribe synalepha or resyllabification. Every vowel stays in its own word and no syllable dots or linking marks are used: la ama [la ˈama], de os [de os]. The only cross-word effects written are those the orthography spells. Apostrophe elision gives a consonant-only word for the elided article, preposition or clitic: l'agua [l ˈagwa], d'o [d o], me'n [me n]. Belsetán article allomorphs are written as spelled: ta o [ta o], en a [en a]. Inside words, keep the broad phonemic values too. ⟨n⟩ before a velar is [n]: cinco [ˈθinko]. ⟨b v d g⟩ are always [b d g], never [β ð ɣ].

Source: Broad phonemic practice of Nagore Laín 2001. The elision and article-allomorph forms are those of Badía Margarit 1950 as summarised in es.wikipedia 'Aragonés belsetano'. Allophonic spirantisation and assimilation, which Quilis 1993 describes for Ibero-Romance, are deliberately left out as narrow detail.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vocoids as [j] and [w] in all positions, never as i̯ u̯. This covers rising diphthongs from Ĕ/Ŏ and others, including Bielsa's uá: tierra [ˈtjera], bueno [ˈbweno], nuet [ˈnwet], puan [ˈpwan], agua [ˈagwa], ciudat [θjuˈdat]. It also covers falling diphthongs: aire [ˈajɾe], peixe [ˈpejʃe], auto [ˈawto]. Consonantal ⟨y⟩ is [j], as in the spec: payel·la [paˈjelːa], yo [jo]. Unstressed i/u next to another vowel is a glide. A written accent on í/ú marks hiatus, and both vowels are then written as vowels: río [ˈrio], fuí? not used. Conjunction y before a vowel is [j]. Elsewhere it is [i].

Source: Diphthongisation Ĕ/Ŏ > ie/ue and Bielsa inside the uá area follow Várvaro 1991, AFA XLVI-XLVII, p. 250. The j/w notation and ⟨y⟩ = [j] follow the spec notes and Nagore Laín 2001.

## Other choices

### Geminates and laterals

⟨l·l⟩ is [lː] and ⟨n·n⟩ is [nː]: bel·la [ˈbelːa], capan·na [kaˈpanːa]. Plain ⟨ll⟩ is [ʎ], with no yeísmo: chillo [ˈtʃiʎo]. ⟨ny⟩ and ⟨ñ⟩ are [ɲ]. Pronouns er and ers are [eɾ] and [eɾs].

Source: Badía Margarit 1950, via es.wikipedia 'Aragonés belsetano' (secondary); spec notes.

### Sibilants and affricates

⟨z⟩ and ⟨c⟩ before e/i are [θ], with distinción kept: zapato [θaˈpato]. ⟨s⟩ is [s]. ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ]: chen [tʃen]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ]. Postvocalic ⟨ix⟩ is [jʃ], keeping the Central/Eastern diphthong: peixe [ˈpejʃe]. ⟨tz⟩ is [θ]. ⟨f⟩ is [f], with F- preserved: fer [feɾ].

Source: Nagore Laín 2001; spec notes (Central profile, distinción, ⟨ch⟩ = [tʃ]).

### Rhotics

⟨rr⟩, word-initial ⟨r⟩, and ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s are the trill [r]: roda [ˈroda], tierra [ˈtjera]. Every other ⟨r⟩ is the tap [ɾ]: pero [ˈpeɾo]. Word-final ⟨r⟩, including in infinitives, is written [ɾ] as spelled: cantar [kanˈtaɾ]. Descriptions report that it is dropped in casual speech in many Aragonese varieties. This choice takes the conservative citation form, consistent with the spec.

Source: Nagore Laín 2001 (rhotic distribution and the variable loss of final -r).

### Lexical reflexes spelled in the text

Read -CT- and -ULT- outcomes exactly as spelled, with no feito-type diphthong inserted: feto [ˈfeto], muto [ˈmuto], nuet [ˈnwet].

Source: Badía Margarit 1950, via es.wikipedia (secondary); spec notes.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
