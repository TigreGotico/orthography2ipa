# Transcription conventions: Sãotomense (Forro / Santome) (cri)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Do not mark stress. Write no ˈ or ˌ in any word, and no tone marks. Long vowels written with a doubled vowel letter are transcribed as Vː with no stress mark, e.g. ⟨kaa⟩ /kaː/. Santome has no geminates and no case endings, so neither affects stress. This follows the spec note: ALUSTP spelling does not mark prosody, and ⟨ê ô⟩ mark vowel height, not stress. Examples: ⟨kafe⟩ /kafɛ/, ⟨mlaxka⟩ /mlaʃka/, ⟨bô⟩ /bo/.

Source: ALUSTP practice as in Araujo & Agostinho 2010, pp. 66-67, and Araujo & Hagemeijer 2013. This sets aside both the stress analysis of Ferraz 1979 and the tone analysis of Hagemeijer & Maurer 2013 (APiCS ch. 35), because the two disagree and neither can be recovered from the spelling.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Since nothing is marked for stress, function words differ from content words only in that they keep their full citation form. Write pronouns, aspect and tense markers, negators, prepositions and conjunctions exactly as spelled, with no reduced or weak forms. Examples: ⟨ami⟩ /ami/, ⟨bô⟩ /bo/, ⟨ê⟩ /e/, ⟨non⟩ /nõ/, ⟨inen⟩ /inẽ/, ⟨nansê⟩ /nãse/, ⟨ka⟩ /ka/, ⟨tava⟩ /tava/, ⟨sa⟩ /sa/, ⟨na⟩ /na/, ⟨fa⟩ /fa/, ⟨ku⟩ /ku/, ⟨pa⟩ /pa/. The preverbal 1sg pronoun ⟨n⟩ is always written /n̩/, with no place assimilation to the following verb, e.g. ⟨n ka bi⟩ /n̩ ka bi/.

Source: Citation forms of Araujo & Hagemeijer 2013. The pronoun and TMA inventory follows Hagemeijer & Maurer 2013 (APiCS ch. 35).

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Santome has no pausal forms, no case endings and no nunation, so sentence-final and comma-final words are written exactly as elsewhere, e.g. sentence-final ⟨fa⟩ /fa/.

Source: Hagemeijer & Maurer 2013 (APiCS ch. 35); Ferraz 1979. Neither describes pausal morphology.

## Connected speech

Do not apply sandhi that the spelling does not show. When a word is written in full, transcribe it in full, so ⟨mata ê⟩ is /mata e/ and not the elided form. When the orthography shows vowel elision with an apostrophe, the apostrophe splits the text into two words; transcribe each piece as spelled: ⟨mat'e⟩ is /mat e/. Do not write cross-word nasal place assimilation, liaison, linking or resyllabification. A word-final nasal vowel stays a nasal vowel before any following word: ⟨non bi⟩ /nõ bi/.

Source: The vowel-elision sandhi follows Hagemeijer & Maurer 2013 (APiCS ch. 35), and is written only where the ALUSTP spelling shows it (Araujo & Hagemeijer 2013).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write ⟨y⟩ as /j/ and ⟨w⟩ as /w/ in onset clusters and wherever else they occur, e.g. ⟨kwa⟩ /kwa/, ⟨fya⟩ /fja/. Write the vowel letters ⟨i u⟩ always as the full vowels /i u/, even next to another vowel, e.g. ⟨kai⟩ /kai/. Never use the non-syllabic diacritic (i̯ u̯).

Source: The ALUSTP grapheme table, Araujo & Agostinho 2010, pp. 66-67.

## Other choices

### Nasal vowels

A vowel followed by a coda ⟨n⟩ (or ⟨m⟩ before p/b) is written as a nasalized vowel, with no nasal consonant after it. Nasal vowels have no mid-height contrast, so en is /ẽ/ and on is /õ/. Examples: ⟨non⟩ /nõ/, ⟨inen⟩ /inẽ/, ⟨landji⟩ /lãdʒi/, ⟨ũa⟩ /ũa/. An ⟨n⟩ between vowels is an onset /n/: ⟨inen⟩ /i.nẽ/. This uses the five nasal vowel symbols of Ferraz 1979 for the surface form, while following the ALUSTP biphonemic spelling.

Source: Araujo & Agostinho 2010, p. 65 (biphonemic analysis); Ferraz 1979 (five nasal vowels).

### Prenasalized onsets

Follow the spelling. Word-initial ⟨mb nd ng mp⟩ are written as clusters /mb nd ŋɡ mp/, e.g. ⟨ngembu⟩ /ŋɡɛmbu/, but a word spelled without the nasal is written plain, e.g. ⟨gembu⟩ /ɡɛmbu/. Never use the unit prenasal symbols ᵐb ⁿd.

Source: Araujo & Agostinho 2010, pp. 68-69. This is not the unit-phoneme analysis of APiCS.

### Implosives

Never write /ɓ ɗ/. ⟨b d⟩ are always /b d/.

Source: ALUSTP, Araujo & Agostinho 2010. This rejects the implosives of Ferraz 1979 and APiCS.

### Palatalization

Do not write the allophonic palatalization of /t d s z/ before /i/: ⟨ti⟩ is /ti/. Write /tʃ dʒ ʃ ʒ/ only for the spellings ⟨tx dj x j⟩.

Source: ALUSTP, Araujo & Agostinho 2010. APiCS (Hagemeijer & Maurer 2013) records the allophony, but this transcription is broad.

### Vowel height and harmony

Take vowel height from the spelling only: ⟨ê ô⟩ are /e o/ and ⟨e o⟩ are /ɛ ɔ/. Do not apply the vowel harmony of Ferraz 1979. Example: ⟨gbêgbê⟩ /ɡ͡beɡ͡be/.

Source: Araujo & Agostinho 2010, p. 65. This sets aside the vowel harmony of Ferraz 1979, p. 25.

### Consonant symbols

Use these symbols: ⟨r⟩ /ɾ/ (never r or l, despite the variation reported by Fontes 2007), ⟨nh⟩ /ɲ/, ⟨lh⟩ /ʎ/, ⟨gb⟩ /ɡ͡b/ (never k͡p), and coda ⟨x⟩ /ʃ/. Examples: ⟨mlaxka⟩ /mlaʃka/, ⟨ope⟩ /ɔpɛ/.

Source: Araujo & Agostinho 2010, pp. 66-67.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
