# Transcription conventions: Castilian (es-ES-x-medieval)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables. Leave monosyllables unmarked, and mark no secondary stress. Stress falls on the syllable that carries it in the cognate modern Castilian word, which is the reflex of the Latin accent. Words ending in a vowel, -n or -s are stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkaza], cantan [ˈkantan]. Words ending in another consonant are stressed on the last syllable: señor [seˈɲoɾ], cibdat [tsibˈdat]. Lexical exceptions keep their inherited stress: árbol [ˈaɾbol], también [tamˈbjen]. Apocopated forms keep stress where the full form had it: noch [notʃ], dix [diʃ], fizieron [fiˈdzjeɾon]. The imperfect and conditional ending -ía is two syllables: tenía [teˈnia]. The variant -ié/-íe is a rising diphthong with stress on the e: tenié [teˈnje]. Clitics attached in writing (dél, nol, quel) take the stress of their host. The lect has no phonemic vowel length, so no length mark is written. It has no geminates: ⟨ss⟩, ⟨rr⟩ and ⟨nn⟩ are single phonemes [s], [r] and [ɲ], and ⟨ll⟩ is [ʎ]. It has no case endings, so that part of the topic does not apply.

Source: Penny (2002), ch. 2 on Old Spanish phonology and the continuity of the Latin accent. Lapesa (1981), §§52–56. Hualde (2005), ch. 13 on stress placement, which is carried back to the medieval lexicon.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed proclitics or enclitics and never carry ˈ. Definite articles: el, la, lo, los, las, and the medieval ell/ela before a vowel. Prenominal possessives mio, to, so, mi, tu, su: mio Çid [mjo tsid]. Prepositions: a, de, en, con, por, pora, para, sin, sobre, entre, contra, fasta, fata, desde, cabo; so pora [ˈpoɾa] is written [poɾa]. Conjunctions and complementizers: e, et and ⟨&⟩, all written [e]; y, o, u, que, ca, si, mas, nin, ni, pero; como and quando when used as conjunctions. Relative que, quien, qual and do. Object and reflexive clitics: me, te, se, le, lo, la, les, los, nos, vos, ge, gelo. The negator non/no. Forms of aver used as auxiliaries, including the detached future and conditional: dar vos he [daɾ βos e]. The following carry stress, and ˈ when polysyllabic: subject and prepositional pronouns (yo, tú, él, ella [ˈeʎa], nós, vós, mí, ti, sí); demonstratives (este [ˈeste], aquel [aˈkel]); numerals; quantifiers (todos [ˈtodos], muchos); interrogatives (quándo [ˈkwando], cómo [ˈkomo]); lexical verbs, including aver/seer as main verbs; adverbs.

Source: Penny (2002), ch. 3 on clitic pronouns, articles and possessives. Lapesa (1981), §§52–56. Hualde (2005), ch. 13 on unstressed function words, applied to the medieval forms.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Old Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so every word has the same form at a comma, at sentence end and inside the sentence. Two orthographic phenomena are transcribed exactly as spelled and never added or removed by the transcriber. Apocope is one: noch [notʃ], fiz [fidz]. Epic paragogic -e at a verse end is the other: Valençiae [baˈlentsiae] is not expected, so write the edition's form, e.g. señore [seˈɲoɾe] and Valençia [baˈlentsja].

Source: Penny (2002), ch. 2 on apocope. Lapesa (1981), §§52–56. The paragogic -e of the Cantar follows the Menéndez Pidal edition, as reflected in Lapesa.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form; no cross-boundary process is transcribed. Synalepha and vowel elision across words are not written: de otro [de ˈotɾo]. Voicing of /s/ before a voiced consonant is not written: los dos [los dos]. Nasal place assimilation across words is not written: en Burgos [en ˈbuɾɡos]. No resyllabification is written. Contractions and elisions that the manuscript or edition spells are transcribed as the words the tool gives. Single-word spellings are written as one IPA word: del [del], dell [del], dest [dest], nol [nol], quel [kel], gelo [ˈʒelo]. When an apostrophe splits a form, each piece is its own word in its own place: d'aquel gives [d] and [aˈkel].

Source: Penny (2002), ch. 2. Lapesa (1981), §§52–56. The convention of writing medieval elisions only where spelled follows editorial practice reflected in both.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vocoids as [j] and [w]; do not use i̯ or u̯. Rising diphthongs: tierra [ˈtjera], bueno [ˈbweno], Cid-era buono/cuemo [ˈbwono]/[ˈkwemo], quando [ˈkwando], agua [ˈaɡwa]. Falling diphthongs: rey [rej], reina [ˈrejna], muy [muj], auto [ˈawto]. ⟨y⟩ or ⟨i⟩ between vowels, or word-initially before a vowel, is [j]: mayor [maˈjoɾ], yo [jo]. In hiatus, a stressed i or u is a full vowel: día [ˈdia], tenía [teˈnia], oír [oˈiɾ]. Medieval ⟨u⟩/⟨v⟩ and ⟨i⟩/⟨j⟩/⟨y⟩ are read by value, not by letter. Consonantal ⟨u⟩/⟨v⟩ is [β]: cauallo [kaˈβaʎo]. Consonantal ⟨i⟩/⟨j⟩ before a vowel is [ʒ]: iudío [ʒuˈdio].

Source: Penny (2002), ch. 2 on diphthongization of Latin Ĕ, Ŏ and on the uo/ue variation. Hualde (2005), ch. 9 on glides and diphthongs.

## Other choices

### Sibilants

Apply the six-way system by spelling. ⟨c⟩ before e/i and ⟨ç⟩ are [ts]: plaça [ˈplatsa], Çid [tsid]. ⟨z⟩ is [dz]: fazer [faˈdzeɾ]. ⟨ss⟩ is [s]: passar [paˈsaɾ]. ⟨s⟩ is [s] word-initially, word-finally and next to a consonant: siete [ˈsjete], mesnada [mesˈnada]. ⟨s⟩ between vowels is [z]: casa [ˈkaza], rosa [ˈroza]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ]: dixo [ˈdiʃo]. ⟨j⟩, ⟨i⟩ consonantal, and ⟨g⟩ before e/i are [ʒ]: fijo [ˈfiʒo], muger [muˈʒeɾ]. Write [ʒ] also word-initially, not [dʒ]: gente [ˈʒente]. Write the affricates as plain digraphs ts dz, without a tie bar. The specification note calls /s z/ lamino-alveolar. Penny and Lapesa describe them as apico-alveolar, opposed to dental or predorsal /ts dz/. The broad symbols s z are the same under either reading. There is no [θ] and no [x].

Source: Penny (2002), ch. 2 on Old Spanish sibilants. Lapesa (1981), §§52–56 (apico-alveolar s, dental affricates, early [dʒ] variant not adopted).

### b and v

⟨b⟩ is [b] in all positions: cabeça [kaˈbetsa], bien [bjen]. ⟨v⟩ and consonantal ⟨u⟩ are [β] in all positions: aver [aˈβeɾ], vino [ˈβino]. The note allows /v~β/; this convention follows the bilabial fricative reading for Castile. [v] is never written. Spirantization of /b d ɡ/ is not written, so ⟨b d g⟩ are [b d ɡ] everywhere: lado [ˈlado].

Source: Penny (2002), ch. 2 on the /b/~/β/ opposition and its loss. Lapesa (1981), §§52–56, which doubts labiodental [v] in Castile.

### f and h

⟨f⟩ is [f] everywhere, following the written standard: fazer [faˈdzeɾ], fijo [ˈfiʒo]. ⟨h⟩ is silent where it is etymological or merely graphic: honor [oˈnoɾ], aver/haver [aˈβeɾ]. ⟨h⟩ is [h] only where it spells Latin F-, in late-15th-century spellings: hazer [haˈdzeɾ].

Source: Penny (2002), ch. 2 on Latin F- > [h] and the standard's retention of [f]. Lapesa (1981), §§52–56.

### Other consonant values

⟨ll⟩ is [ʎ]: castiello [kasˈtjeʎo]. ⟨ñ⟩ and ⟨nn⟩ are [ɲ]: senna/seña [ˈseɲa]. ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ]: noche [ˈnotʃe]. ⟨rr⟩, word-initial ⟨r⟩, and ⟨r⟩ after l, n or s are [r]: tierra [ˈtjera], rey [rej], onra [ˈonra]. Other ⟨r⟩ is [ɾ]. ⟨qu⟩ before e/i is [k]; ⟨qu⟩ before a/o is [kw]. ⟨gu⟩ before e/i is [ɡ]; elsewhere ⟨g⟩ before a/o/u is [ɡ]. Final ⟨t⟩ and ⟨d⟩, learned clusters, and nasals before labials are written as spelled: grant [ɡrant], cibdat [tsibˈdat], campo [ˈkampo], enbiar [enˈbjaɾ].

Source: Penny (2002), ch. 2. Lapesa (1981), §§52–56 (yeísmo post-medieval).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
