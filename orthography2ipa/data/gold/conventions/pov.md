# Transcription conventions: Guinea-Bissau Creole (Kriol) (pov)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical. Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable on every word of two or more syllables. It usually sits where the Portuguese etymon had it. Where the etymon's final unstressed vowel was lost, the stress becomes word-final. Examples: kasa [ˈkasa], mininu [miˈninu], fala [ˈfala], mindjer [minˈdʒɛr], kume [kuˈme], djintis [ˈdʒintis], papia [paˈpia]. Never mark monosyllables: bai [baj], pis [pis]. There is no phonemic vowel length, so write no ː. There are no geminates; write doubled spellings as one consonant. There are no case endings. The plural suffix -s never moves stress: mininus [miˈninus]. Unstressed vowels keep full quality, with no Portuguese-style reduction: tene [ˈtene], not [ˈtenɨ]. Kihm (1994) discusses possible tonal or pitch-accent properties. This transcription marks stress only and writes no tone.

Source: Kihm 1994, ch. 1 (phonology and prosody); stress placement as listed in Scantamburlo 1999 entries

## Stressed and unstressed words

Monosyllabic function words are unstressed and never take ˈ. They keep full vowels. This covers: subject pronouns N, bu [bu], i [i], no [no], bo [bɔ], e [e]; object clitics such as l, n, u; TMA and negation markers ta [ta], na [na], ba [ba], dja [dʒa], ka [ka]; prepositions di [di], na [na], pa [pa], ku [ku]; conjunctions ku [ku], ma [ma], o [o]; and the indefinite un [ũ]. Polysyllabic function words keep their lexical stress mark: pabia [paˈbia], dimas [diˈmas]. Emphatic pronouns are always stressed: ami [aˈmi], abo [aˈbɔ], anos [aˈnɔs], abos [aˈbɔs], elis [ˈɛlis]. The monosyllabic emphatic el [ɛl] gets no mark, under the monosyllable rule.

Source: Kihm 1994, chs. 2–4 (pronouns, TMA particles); Intumbo, Inverno & Holm 2007

## Pausal forms

Kriol has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so none apply. Every word keeps its citation form at a comma and at the end of the sentence. The note says final consonants may be reduced or deleted in fast speech. This transcription does not write that: always keep final -r, -l, -s as given in the dictionary form. Examples: mindjer [minˈdʒɛr], el [ɛl], mas [mas].

Source: Citation forms of Scantamburlo 1999; Kihm 1994, ch. 1

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form. The one cross-boundary process written is that 1sg N is a syllabic nasal homorganic with the first consonant of the next word. Write [m̩] before labials, [n̩] before alveolars, [ɲ̍] before palatals and [ŋ̍] before velars and vowels or at pause. Examples: N bai [m̩ baj], N tene [n̩ ˈtene], N djuda [ɲ̍ ˈdʒuda], N kume [ŋ̍ kuˈme]. Do not write elision, vowel coalescence, linking or resyllabification. Each word stays separate and unchanged.

Source: Kihm 1994, ch. 1 and ch. 2 (pronoun N); Intumbo, Inverno & Holm 2007

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vowels as [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. This applies in falling diphthongs, e.g. bai [baj], pai [paj], mau [maw], and to onset y, e.g. yagu [ˈjagu]. An orthographic i or u between a consonant and a following vowel is written as a syllabic vowel in hiatus, e.g. papia [paˈpia], kriol [kriˈɔl].

Source: Scantamburlo 1999 (orthography, y and i/u spellings); Kihm 1994, ch. 1

## Other choices

### vowel inventory

Write seven oral vowels /i e ɛ a ɔ o u/, not the five-vowel system of the note's spelling. Choose e/ɛ and o/ɔ by the dictionary pronunciation: tene [ˈtene], bo [bɔ], mindjer [minˈdʒɛr], kosta [ˈkɔsta]. Never write ɨ.

Source: Kihm 1994, ch. 1; Scantamburlo 1999 (introduction); departs from the note's 'five-vowel system'

### nasal vowels and VN sequences

A word-final vowel+n is written as a nasal vowel: un [ũ], nen [nẽ], bin [bĩ]. A vowel + nasal before a consonant inside a word is written as an oral vowel plus a homorganic nasal consonant: kansa [ˈkansa], mindjer [minˈdʒɛr], tempu [ˈtempu].

Source: Kihm 1994, ch. 1; Scantamburlo 1999

### prenasalized stops

Write mb, nd, ŋg, ɲdʒ as sequences, with no tie bar and no superscript: mbanha [mˈbaɲa], ndjanu [ɲˈdʒanu].

Source: Kihm 1994, ch. 1

### affricates and palatals

Write dj and j as [dʒ]: djintis [ˈdʒintis]. Write tx, tch and c as [tʃ]: txon [tʃõ]. Write ñ and nh as [ɲ]: ña [ɲa].

Source: Scantamburlo 1999 (orthography); Kihm 1994

### rhotic

Write every r as [r], following the note, whether it is realized as a tap or a trill: mindjer [minˈdʒɛr], riba [ˈriba].

Source: Kihm 1994, ch. 1 (single alveolar rhotic)

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
