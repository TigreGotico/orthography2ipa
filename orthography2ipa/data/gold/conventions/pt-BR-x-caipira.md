# Transcription conventions: Caipira Portuguese (pt-BR-x-caipira)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every lexical word, including stressed monosyllables: casa [ˈkazɐ], café [kaˈfɛ], mar [ˈmaɻ], pé [ˈpɛ]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Do not mark secondary stress. Placement follows the orthography. A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed syllable: médico [ˈmɛdʒiku], você [voˈse]. With no written accent, words ending in -a, -e, -o (optionally followed by -s), -am or -em are stressed on the penult: porta [ˈpɔɻtɐ], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], homem [ˈɔmẽj̃]. All other words are stressed on the final syllable: falar [faˈla], azul [aˈzuw], aqui [aˈki], irmão [iɻˈmɐ̃w̃]. Elision of final /r/ does not move the stress: andar [ɐ̃ˈda]. Portuguese has no phonemic vowel length, no geminates and no case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 (word stress); Silva (2002), on stress and accent marks.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed: definite articles o a os as [u ɐ us ɐs]; indefinite articles um uma [ũ ˈumɐ → write unstressed ũ, umɐ]; prepositions de [dʒi], em [ẽj̃], a [a], com [kõ], sem [sẽj̃], por [poɻ], para [pɾɐ]; contractions do da no na pelo pela [du dɐ nu nɐ pelu pelɐ]; conjunctions e [i], ou [o], que [ki], se [si], mas [mas], nem [nẽj̃]; the relative que [ki]; clitic pronouns me te se lhe nos [mi tʃi si ʎi→ji nus]; enclitic or proclitic o a [u ɐ]. They get no stress mark and their vowels reduce, so final e becomes [i] and o becomes [u]. These carry stress and are marked: subject and prepositional pronouns eu [ˈew], ele [ˈeli], nós [ˈnɔs], você [voˈse], mim [ˈmĩ]; demonstratives and possessives isso [ˈisu], meu [ˈmew]; all verb forms, including auxiliaries and copulas é [ˈɛ], está [isˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], vai [ˈvaj], foi [ˈfoj]; adverbs and negation não [ˈnɐ̃w̃]. Do not use colloquial clipped spellings (tá, cê) unless the text writes them. When the text writes them, transcribe what is written: tá [ˈta], cê [ˈse], pra [pɾɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6–7 (clitics and prosodic words); Castilho (2010), on clitic pronouns and prepositions in Brazilian Portuguese.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply in the Arabic or Classical sense. Portuguese has no pausal forms, no case endings and no nunation. Every word has the same form at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside a phrase. Final unstressed vowels are never dropped at a pause: casa [ˈkazɐ], leite [ˈlejtʃi]. The only boundary-sensitive choice is the voicing of final /s/, which is written [s] before a pause: os livros. [us ˈlivɾus].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 7 (no pausal morphology); Silva (2002), on sibilant voicing.

## Connected speech

Write one cross-word process only: word-final /s/ (also spelled z) becomes [z] when the next word in the same phrase begins with a vowel or a voiced consonant. The change is written on the first word only: os amigos [uz aˈmigus], mas não [maz ˈnɐ̃w̃], três dias [ˈtɾez ˈdʒiɐs]. Before a voiceless consonant or a pause it stays [s]: os pés [us ˈpɛs]. Do not write vowel sandhi across words. No elision, degemination or gliding: casa amarela [ˈkazɐ amaˈɾɛlɐ], de ouro [dʒi ˈoɾu], que eu [ki ˈew]. Do not write resyllabification. Final coda [ɻ] and [w] keep their coda form before a vowel: mar azul [ˈmaɻ aˈzuw], mal ouvido [ˈmaw oˈvidu]. A final /r/ that is elided stays elided: falar isso [faˈla ˈisu]. Palatalization of /t d/ applies only inside a word, so it is never caused by the next word. There are no syllable dots and no linking ties. Words stay separated by single spaces.

Source: Silva (2002), on sibilant voicing in sandhi; Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 7 (external sandhi); vowel sandhi is left unwritten as optional and rate-dependent, per Callou & Leite (2001).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vocoids as [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. Nasal glides are [j̃ w̃]. Falling diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], meu [ˈmew], céu [ˈsɛw], foi [ˈfoj]. Vocalized coda /l/ also gives [w]: sol [ˈsɔw]. Nasal diphthongs: mão [ˈmɐ̃w̃], mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃], põe [ˈpõj̃]. Rising sequences: write qu and gu followed by a pronounced u as [kw ɡw]: quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. Write post-tonic unstressed i or u before a vowel as a glide: história [isˈtɔɾjɐ], sério [ˈsɛɾju]. Write other vowel sequences as hiatus: dia [ˈdʒiɐ], rua [ˈhuɐ], saúde [saˈudʒi]. Monophthongization as in caipira speech: ou is always [o], as in outro [ˈotɾu] and falou [faˈlo]. Write ei as [e] before r, x, j or g+e/i: primeiro [pɾiˈmeɾu], beijo [ˈbeʒu]. Elsewhere ei stays [ej], as in leite [ˈlejtʃi]. Write ai as [a] before x: caixa [ˈkaʃɐ]. Do not insert an epenthetic glide before final /s/: nós [ˈnɔs], faz [ˈfas]; the lexical mais is [ˈmajs]. Write /ʎ/ (lh) as [j] (see other).

Source: Silva (2002), on diphthongs, monophthongization and glide notation; Amaral (1920) §25 for lh → i; Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 3 (glides).

## Other choices

### rhotics

Coda /r/, inside a word or at its end, is the retroflex approximant [ɻ]: porta [ˈpɔɻtɐ], carne [ˈkaɻni], mar [ˈmaɻ], amor [aˈmoɻ]. In verb infinitives and other verb forms ending in -r (andar, comer, sair, future subjunctive), the final /r/ is elided: andar [ɐ̃ˈda], comer [koˈme], quiser [kiˈzɛ]. Nouns, adjectives and other non-verbs keep it as [ɻ]. Single intervocalic r and r in onset clusters are the tap [ɾ]: arara [aˈɾaɾɐ], prato [ˈpɾatu], três [ˈtɾes]. This follows modern descriptions rather than Amaral §6b, which extends the retroflex to intervocalic r, and rather than the spec note, which extends it to clusters. Strong r (word-initial, rr, and after n, l or s) is [h]: rato [ˈhatu], carro [ˈkahu], honra [ˈõhɐ].

Source: Amaral (1920) §6b, §23a for retroflex coda and final-r elision; Silva (2002) and Callou & Leite (2001) for tap and strong-r distribution and for the restriction of elision to verbs.

### coda /l/

Coda /l/ is [w], following the spec's primary modern realization: mal [ˈmaw], papel [paˈpɛw], alto [ˈawtu], Brasil [bɾaˈziw]. Do not write Amaral's rhotacized [ɻ] variant (§22a, papér, arma) unless the text spells it with r.

Source: Spec note; Silva (2002) on /l/ vocalization; Amaral (1920) §22a for the traditional variant.

### coda /s/

Coda /s/ is alveolar [s] or [z] and never post-alveolar [ʃ ʒ]: festa [ˈfɛstɐ], mesmo [ˈmezmu], dois [ˈdojs]. It is voiced before voiced consonants inside a word too.

Source: Amaral (1920) §6a.

### palatalization and lh, nh

/t d/ before [i] become [tʃ dʒ]. This applies whether the [i] comes from i or from reduced final e: tia [ˈtʃiɐ], dia [ˈdʒiɐ], noite [ˈnojtʃi], cidade [siˈdadʒi]. lh becomes [j]: mulher [muˈjɛ], filho [ˈfiju], trabalho [tɾaˈbaju]. nh is [ɲ]: banho [ˈbɐ̃ɲu].

Source: Silva (2002) for palatalization and nh; Amaral (1920) §25 and §6e for lh.

### unstressed vowels

Word-final unstressed vowels are [ɐ i u], following the modern raising that the spec inherits: casa [ˈkazɐ], leite [ˈlejtʃi], gato [ˈɡatu], gatos [ˈɡatus]. Amaral §8 records the absence of raising in early-20th-century speech; that is not modelled here. Pretonic e and o are close-mid [e o], never [ɛ ɔ]: pequeno [peˈkenu], morar [moˈɾa]. Word-initial unstressed e before s, and es- generally, is [i]: estar [isˈta], esquecer [iskeˈse]. Pretonic a is [a]: falar [faˈla].

Source: Silva (2002) and Callou & Leite (2001) for the vowel system; Amaral (1920) §8 for the historical divergence.

### stressed mid vowels

Write lexical open-mid [ɛ ɔ] in stressed syllables according to the word's standard pronunciation, even where the spelling gives no clue: porta [ˈpɔɻtɐ], pedra [ˈpɛdɾɐ], avó [aˈvɔ]. Write close-mid [e o] where the word has them: avô [aˈvo], medo [ˈmedu]. This departs from the spec note, which says the engine derives [o] in porta.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2–3; Silva (2002).

### nasal vowels

Write vowel nasality with a tilde: ɐ̃ ẽ ĩ õ ũ. Do not write the nasal consonant in a coda: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], tempo [ˈtẽpu], mundo [ˈmũdu], falando [falˈɐ̃du → faˈlɐ̃du]. Stressed vowels before a nasal consonant in the next syllable are nasalized, and stressed a becomes [ɐ̃]: cama [ˈkɐ̃mɐ], banana [baˈnɐ̃nɐ], cimo [ˈsĩmu]. Do not reduce the gerund -ndo to -no unless the text spells it so.

Source: Silva (2002) and Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 3 (nasality).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
