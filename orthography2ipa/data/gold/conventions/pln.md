# Transcription conventions: Palenquero (Lengua ri Palenge) (pln)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

No stress mark and no tone mark are written. This follows the specification note: the acute accent is mapped to its base vowel, and no lexical stress or tone is modelled. For reference, Palenquero keeps the Spanish-inherited stress position. Vowel-final words and words ending in n or s are penultimate: ⟨suto⟩ [suto], ⟨jende⟩ [hende]. Other consonant-final words are final. Verbs from Spanish infinitives lose -r and are oxytone, and the written acute shows this: ⟨kumé⟩ [kume], ⟨ndá⟩ [nda], ⟨asé⟩ [ase]. Hualde & Schwegler describe stress here as realized mainly by high tone. Unstressed vowels are never reduced, so the unmarked transcription loses no vowel quality. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not occur in this lect, so nothing is written for them.

Source: Specification note 7; Schwegler (2013, APiCS ch. 48) for the inherited Spanish stress and accent orthography; Hualde, J. I. & Schwegler, A. (2008), 'Intonation in Palenquero', Journal of Pidgin and Creole Languages 23(1), for stress realized as high tone; Lipski (2012, pp. 24-36) for the oxytone verb forms.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Every function word is written in its full citation form, with full vowels and no reduced or contracted variant, and with no stress mark (see stress). In speech, these monosyllabic particles are unstressed proclitics: the TAM markers ⟨ta⟩ [ta] and ⟨a⟩ [a], plural ⟨ma⟩ [ma], ⟨ri⟩ [ɾi] 'of', ⟨ku⟩ [ku] 'with', ⟨pa⟩ [pa], ⟨lo ke⟩ [lo ke], and the pronouns ⟨i⟩ [i] and ⟨bo⟩ [bo]. These are stressed: the disyllabic particles ⟨taba⟩ [taba] and ⟨asé⟩ [ase], the pronouns ⟨ele⟩ [ele], ⟨suto⟩ [suto], ⟨enú⟩ [enu] and ⟨ané⟩ [ane], the copula ⟨jue⟩ [hwe], and clause-final negation ⟨nu⟩ [nu]. None of these differences is written. The past suffix -ba stays inside its host word: ⟨kumeba⟩ [kumeba].

Source: Lipski (2012, pp. 24-36) for the inventory of particles and pronouns; Hualde & Schwegler (2008) for the prosodic status of particles; Schwegler (2013, APiCS ch. 48).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Palenquero has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so every word has the same form at a comma, at sentence end and inside the phrase: ⟨nu⟩ is always [nu].

Source: Schwegler (2013, APiCS ch. 48): no case morphology; Lipski (2012).

## Connected speech

No process across a word boundary is written, and each word keeps its citation form. Vowel sinalefa is not written: ⟨ta asé⟩ [ta ase], not merged. Resyllabification is not written. Cross-word nasal place assimilation is not written: ⟨un poko⟩ [un poko], not [um poko]. Word-initial prenasalized stops keep their nasal after any preceding word, including a vowel-final one: ⟨i ndá⟩ [i nda], ⟨ma ngombe⟩ [ma ŋɡombe]. Word-final /s/ is never voiced or aspirated before the next word.

Source: Broad convention of the specification. Schwegler & Morton (2003) and Schwegler (2013) treat the prenasalized onset as a stable lexical property of the word.

## Glides and diphthongs

This follows the Hualde-style Spanish convention. Before a vowel in the same syllable, unaccented i and u are written [j w]: ⟨jue⟩ [hwe], ⟨bueno⟩ [bweno], ⟨tiene⟩ [tjene], ⟨hue-⟩ [we]. After a vowel in the same syllable, i (including word-final ⟨y⟩) and u are written [i̯ u̯]: ⟨ai⟩ [ai̯], ⟨oi⟩ [oi̯], ⟨hoy⟩ [oi̯], ⟨au⟩ [au̯]. Consonantal ⟨y ll⟩ before a vowel is [j]: ⟨yo⟩ [jo], ⟨calle⟩ [kaje]. Lone conjunction ⟨y⟩ is [i]. An accented ⟨í ú⟩ next to another vowel is a full vowel in hiatus: ⟨día⟩ [dia]. ⟨gü⟩ is [ɡw]. ⟨gue gui⟩ is [ɡe ɡi], with u silent. ⟨que qui⟩ is [ke ki].

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on glides and diphthongs, as the Spanish-inherited default; specification note 4 for yeísmo [j] (APiCS /ʝ/, written [j] here).

## Other choices

### Letter-to-sound core

⟨c qu k⟩ → [k]; ⟨c⟩ before e/i, and ⟨z s⟩ → [s] (seseo); ⟨v b⟩ → [b]; ⟨j⟩, and etymological ⟨g⟩ before e/i, → [h] (spec note 5; APiCS lists /x/, the spec writes [h]): ⟨jende⟩ [hende], ⟨jambre⟩ [hambɾe]; ⟨h⟩ is silent; ⟨ch⟩ → [tʃ]; ⟨ñ⟩ → [ɲ]; single intervocalic ⟨r⟩ → [ɾ]; ⟨rr⟩ and word-initial ⟨r⟩ → [r]; ⟨g⟩ elsewhere, and always in the k-based school orthography → [ɡ].

Source: Specification notes 3-6; Schwegler (2013, APiCS ch. 48) consonant inventory.

### Prenasalized stops

Word-initial ⟨mb nd ng⟩ are written as the clusters [mb nd ŋɡ], with no superscript nasal and no syllabic nasal: ⟨ndo⟩ [ndo], ⟨mbala⟩ [mbala], ⟨ngombe⟩ [ŋɡombe]. Inside a word, ⟨ng⟩ and ⟨nk⟩ are [ŋɡ] and [ŋk], with ⟨g⟩ staying [ɡ] even before e/i: ⟨Palenge⟩ [paleŋɡe]. Spanish ⟨mb mp⟩ stay [mb mp].

Source: Schwegler (2013, APiCS ch. 48); Schwegler & Morton (2003).

### Lenition of /b d ɡ/

Write [b d ɡ] in every position and never [β ð ɣ]: ⟨kaballo⟩ [kabajo]. Schwegler & Morton report intervocalic spirantization, but this broad transcription omits it.

Source: Schwegler & Morton (2003) describe the allophony; it is omitted per the broad convention.

### Liquids and /d/

Write l, r and d as spelled; no l/ɾ/d neutralization is applied: ⟨ri⟩ [ɾi], ⟨ele⟩ [ele], ⟨ndulo⟩ [ndulo]. This follows the spec's 'not modelled' flag, although the literature reports variable alternation.

Source: Specification open-issues note; Schwegler (2013, APiCS ch. 48).

### Word-final n and coda s

Word-final ⟨n⟩ → [n], not velar [ŋ]. Coda ⟨s⟩ → [s], with no aspiration or deletion beyond what the spelling already shows. Lipski's spellings such as ⟨suto⟩ already encode the loss of s.

Source: Specification open-issues note (Spanish-inherited defaults); Lipski (2012) spellings.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
