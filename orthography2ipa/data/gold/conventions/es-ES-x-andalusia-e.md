# Transcription conventions: Eastern Andalusian Spanish (es-ES-x-andalusia-e)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical, as in all Spanish, and is recovered from the spelling. A written accent marks the stressed vowel. Without one, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s takes penultimate stress, and any other word takes final stress. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every stressed word, monosyllables included: casa [ˈkasa], tres [ˈtɾɛ], canción [kanˈθjon]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Secondary stress is not written, except one ˈ on the adjective stem of -mente adverbs: rápidamente [ˈrapiðaˈmente]. Deleting a final consonant never moves the stress: perros [ˈpɛrɔ], verdad [beɾˈðæ], árbol [ˈaɾβɔ]. Enclitics written solid in the spelling keep the verb's stress: dámelo [ˈdamelo]. Spanish has no phonemic vowel length, no phonemic geminates and no case endings, so those parts of the question do not apply. Vowel opening is shown by vowel quality (see other), never by a length mark.

Source: Hualde 2005, The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 'Stress' (lexical stress and orthographic accent rules); Quilis 1993, Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, stress chapter.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, no ˈ: definite articles el, la, lo, los, las [el la lo lɔ læ]; all simple prepositions except según (a, de, en, con, por, para, sin, sobre, hasta, desde…); conjunctions y, e, o, u, ni, que, pero, si, como, cuando, donde when not interrogative; object and reflexive clitics me, te, se, lo, la, le, nos, os, les; possessive determiners mi, tu, su, mis, tus, sus [mi tu su mɪ tʊ sʊ]; relative que, quien, cual, cuyo. Stressed, with ˈ: indefinite un, una, unos [ˈun ˈuna ˈunɔ]; subject pronouns and pronouns after a preposition (yo, tú, él, mí, ti, nosotros…); interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent (qué, cómo, dónde); demonstratives; full possessives (mío, suyo); numerals; según; negative no; and every verb form, including auxiliary haber and ser: ha venido [ˈa βeˈnio]. Unstressed words still undergo final-consonant loss and vowel opening (see other): los perros [lɔ ˈpɛrɔ].

Source: Hualde 2005, ch. 'Stress', section on stressed versus unstressed words; Navarro Tomás 1918/1977, Manual de pronunciación española, §§ on palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas.

## Pausal forms

This topic does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word has the same form before a pause, a comma or the end of the sentence as before a consonant-initial word. Final /s θ/ and final /ɾ l d/ are deleted and the vowel is opened: ¿vienes? [ˈbjenɛ], adiós [aˈðjɔ].

Source: Zamora Vicente 1960, Dialectología española, Andaluz, sections on final consonants; Hualde 2005, ch. on /s/ weakening.

## Connected speech

Write each word as it is pronounced in place. Do not merge words. Do not write synalepha: lo hizo stays [lo ˈiθo], with both words complete. Only one cross-word process is written, and it is set by the first sound of the next word. (1) Before a vowel-initial word, final /s/ or /θ/ is written [h] at the end of the first word, and that word's vowels are not opened: los otros [loh ˈɔtɾɔ], dos años [ˈdoh ˈaɲɔ], luz azul [ˈluh aˈθu]. Resyllabification is not marked. (2) Before a consonant-initial word or a pause, final /s θ/ is deleted and the vowels are opened: las casas [læ ˈkæsæ]. No gemination is written across the boundary. Every other word-boundary effect is written inside the word it affects. Voiced stops spirantize at the start of a word after a vowel or continuant: la boca [la ˈβoka], el dedo [el ˈdeðo]. They stay stops after a pause or nasal: un beso [ˈun ˈbeso]. Nasal place assimilation is not written: un perro [ˈun ˈpero].

Source: Hualde 2005, chs. on spirantization and on /s/ aspiration in Southern Peninsular Spanish; Zamora Vicente 1960, Andaluz, aspiración de -s.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the non-syllabic high vowels of diphthongs as the consonant-like symbols [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. Rising diphthongs: tiempo [ˈtjempo], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [θjuˈðæ]. Falling diphthongs: aire [ˈajɾe], causa [ˈkawsa], hoy [ˈɔj], muy [ˈmuj]. Diphthong versus hiatus follows the spelling: an accented í or ú, or an unaccented i or u next to a vowel, decides (día [ˈdia], país [paˈi]). Word-initial hi- or y before a vowel is the fricative [ʝ], not a glide: hielo [ˈʝelo], yo [ˈʝo]. Word-initial hu- before a vowel is [w]: hueso [ˈweso]. Conjunction y is always [i].

Source: Hualde 2005, ch. 'Glides and diphthongs' (uses j, w); RFE/Navarro Tomás tradition for the hiatus criterion.

## Other choices

### Vowel opening and harmony after final consonant loss

When a word-final /s/ or /θ/ is deleted (before a consonant or pause), open the final vowel and spread the opening to the stressed vowel and any posttonic vowels: /e/→[ɛ], /o/→[ɔ], /a/→[æ]. /i u/ stay [i u] in stressed position; a final unstressed /i u/ becomes [ɪ ʊ]. Examples: perros [ˈpɛrɔ] versus perro [ˈpero]; casas [ˈkæsæ]; lobos [ˈlɔβɔ]; libros [ˈliβɾɔ]; tienes [ˈtjɛnɛ]; crisis [ˈkɾisɪ]. When final /ɾ l d/ is deleted, open only the final vowel and add no harmony: comer [koˈmɛ], verdad [beɾˈðæ], papel [paˈpɛ], árbol [ˈaɾβɔ]. The article el keeps its [l]: [el]. The spec note writes [ɛ ɔ] for e/o; [æ] for /a/ follows the published descriptions of the eastern variety.

Source: Navarro Tomás, Espinosa & Rodríguez-Castellano 1933 / Alonso, Zamora Vicente & Canellada 1950 (vocales andaluzas), summarized in Zamora Vicente 1960, Andaluz; Hualde 2005, ch. on /s/ weakening (Eastern Andalusian vowel opening).

### Word-internal coda /s θ/

Before a consonant inside a word, /s/ and /θ/ are written [h], with no vowel opening and no gemination: mismo [ˈmihmo], esto [ˈehto], pescado [pehˈkao], hazlo [ˈahlo]. This is the broad pan-Andalusian aspiration. Narrow eastern renderings with gemination, such as [ˈmimmo], are not used.

Source: Hualde 2005, ch. on /s/ aspiration; Zamora Vicente 1960, Andaluz.

### Distinción and sibilants

Keep distinción, as the spec note says. Spelling z and c before e or i is [θ]; spelling s is [s]: caza [ˈkaθa], casa [ˈkasa], cielo [ˈθjelo]. There is no seseo or ceceo.

Source: Zamora Vicente 1960, Andaluz, map of seseo/ceceo/distinción; Penny 2002, ch. on Andalusian.

### Velar fricative /x/

Write spelling j, and g before e or i, as [h]: jamón [haˈmon], gente [ˈhente], ojo [ˈoho].

Source: Zamora Vicente 1960, Andaluz; Hualde 2005, ch. on /x/.

### Palatals and affricate

Write ll and y as [ʝ], following yeísmo: calle [ˈkaʝe]. Write ch as [tʃ], as the spec note says: there is no deaffrication, so mucho [ˈmutʃo].

Source: Penny 2002, ch. on yeísmo; spec note; Zamora Vicente 1960.

### Intervocalic /d/ and voiced obstruents

Delete /d/ only in the endings -ado and -ados: cansado [kanˈsao], cansados [kanˈsæɔ]. Elsewhere, write /b d g/ as [β ð ɣ] after vowels and continuants, and as [b d g] after a pause or nasal, and /d/ also after /l/: nada [ˈnaða], todo [ˈtoðo], ganga [ˈganga], caldo [ˈkaldo].

Source: Hualde 2005, chs. on spirantization and /d/ deletion.

### Rhotics, laterals and final nasal

Spelling r between vowels and in codas is [ɾ]; rr, and r at the start of a word or after n, l or s, is [r]. Preconsonantal /l/ and /ɾ/ keep their spelled identity, with no rhotacism or lambdacism: alto [ˈalto], carta [ˈkaɾta]. Word-final n is written [n], not [ŋ]: pan [ˈpan].

Source: Hualde 2005, chs. on rhotics and nasals; standard-pronunciation choice over the variable Andalusian neutralizations noted in Zamora Vicente 1960.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
