# Transcription conventions: Cuyano Spanish (es-AR-x-cuyo)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: sol [ˈsol], no [ˈno], es [ˈes]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark: la casa [la ˈkasa]. Placement follows the spelling. (1) A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel: café [kaˈfe], árbol [ˈaɾbol], tenés [teˈnes], cantá [kanˈta]. (2) With no written accent, a word ending in a vowel, n or s takes penultimate stress: casa [ˈkasa], cantan [ˈkantan]. (3) Any other unaccented word takes final stress: verdad [beɾˈdad], comer [koˈmeɾ]. In the sequences ai, au, ei, eu, oi, ou, ia, ie, io, iu, ua, ue, uo, ui, the unaccented i and u are glides when counting syllables: historia [isˈtoɾja], ciudad [sjuˈdad]. Enclitics written on the verb do not move its stress: dámelo [ˈdamelo], decime [deˈsime]. Adverbs in -mente take primary stress on -mente and secondary stress ˌ on the adjective's stressed syllable: claramente [ˌklaɾaˈmente]. Mark no other secondary stress. Spanish has no phonemic vowel length and no case endings, so these do not apply. Geminates occur only as a spelled double consonant, which is written double: innato [inˈnato], acción [akˈsjon].

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP, chapter on word stress; Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, chapter on the accent; RAE/ASALE (2011), Nueva gramática: Fonética y fonología, ch. 8.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed and written without ˈ: definite articles el la los las lo (el perro [el ˈpero]); all simple prepositions except según (a, ante, con, de, en, para, por, sin, sobre, hasta, desde): de Mendoza [de menˈdosa]; conjunctions y, e, o, u, ni, pero, que, si, porque, aunque: pan y vino [ˈpan i ˈbino]; clitic pronouns me te se lo la le nos los las les, written separately or joined to the verb (me dijo [me ˈdixo]); prenominal possessives mi tu su mis tus sus nuestro (mi casa [mi ˈkasa]); and the non-interrogative relatives and adverbs que, quien, cual, donde, cuando, como (el que viene [el ke ˈbjene], cuando llegue [kwando ˈʝeɡe]). These are stressed and written with ˈ: indefinite articles un una unos unas (un día [ˈun ˈdia]); subject and prepositional pronouns yo, vos, él, ella, ustedes, mí, ti, sí (para vos [paɾa ˈbos]); demonstratives; postnominal possessives (mío, tuyo); interrogatives and exclamatives qué, quién, cómo, dónde, cuándo, cuál; según; the negation no; numerals; and all verbs, including the auxiliaries haber, ser, estar and ir (ha venido [ˈa beˈnido], va a ir [ˈba a ˈiɾ]). Where sources disagree on auxiliaries, we follow Quilis and treat them as stressed.

Source: Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, list of 'palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas'; Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1957), Manual de pronunciación española, chapter on stressed and unstressed words; Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP, stress chapter.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so a word at the end of the sentence or before a comma is written exactly as elsewhere: ¿Qué querés? [ˈke keˈɾes].

Source: Not applicable to Spanish; cf. Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP.

## Connected speech

Write every word in its citation form and do not write any process across a word boundary. Synalepha and vowel merging are not written: la otra [la ˈotɾa], de esa [de ˈesa], va a ir [ˈba a ˈiɾ]. Resyllabification is not written and no syllable dots are used: los otros [los ˈotɾos]. Nasal place assimilation is not written: un beso [ˈun ˈbeso], con gusto [kon ˈɡusto]. Voicing of /s/ before a voiced consonant is not written: mismo [ˈmismo], las manos [las ˈmanos]. Coda /s/ aspiration or loss is not written (see other). Within the word, apply the same phonemic level: n is written [n] before every consonant (tengo [ˈtenɡo], enviar [enˈbjaɾ]), with no [ŋ] or [ɱ].

Source: Broad, phonemic-level convention following Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, which describes these processes as allophonic; Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP, chapters on nasals and on vowel sequences across words.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write unstressed i or u next to another vowel in the same syllable as [j] or [w], never as i̯ or u̯: tierra [ˈtjera], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [sjuˈdad], auto [ˈawto], rey [ˈrej], hay [ˈaj], muy [ˈmuj], hoy [ˈoj]. A written accent on i or u marks hiatus: write two full vowels, with stress on the accented one: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis], baúl [baˈul]. Unaccented ui and iu are rising diphthongs: muy is the exception above; elsewhere write cuidar [kwiˈdaɾ], ciudad [sjuˈdad]. Initial hi before a vowel and the prevocalic consonants y and ll are the consonant /ʝ/, not [j]: yo [ˈʝo], hielo [ˈʝelo], calle [ˈkaʝe], ayer [aˈʝeɾ]. Initial hu before a vowel is [w], with no epenthetic [ɡ]: hueso [ˈweso]. Before a consonant or at the end of the sentence, the conjunction y is [i]: pan y vino [ˈpan i ˈbino]. Before a vowel it is also written [i], since no linking is written.

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP, chapter on glides and diphthongs; Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, chapter on vowel sequences.

## Other choices

### ll/y (yeísmo)

ll and prevocalic y are always [ʝ], following the specification note: llave [ˈʝabe], mayo [ˈmaʝo]. Do not use the Buenos Aires [ʒ] or [ʃ] (zheísmo/sheísmo). Traditional Cuyo descriptions report [ʝ] for this region, and the note agrees with them. Spreading of [ʒ] among younger urban speakers is not written.

Source: Vidal de Battini, B. E. (1964), El español de la Argentina, Consejo Nacional de Educación, section on the Cuyo region; Fontanella de Weinberg, M. B. (coord.) (2000), El español de la Argentina y sus variedades regionales, Edicial, chapter on Cuyo; specification note (Lipski 1994).

### seseo

c before e or i, z, and s are all [s]; [θ] is never used: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato], hacer [aˈseɾ].

Source: Lipski, J. M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Longman; specification note.

### rhotics and assibilation

Write the trill [r] for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after l, n or s: perro [ˈpero], rojo [ˈroxo], honra [ˈonra]. Write the tap [ɾ] elsewhere: pero [ˈpeɾo], comer [koˈmeɾ]. The assibilated, fricative realizations of /r/ and of the cluster tr ([r̝], [tɹ̝]) are described as typical of Cuyo but are allophonic, so the broad transcription does not write them: tres [ˈtɾes], carro [ˈkaro]. This departs from the regional pronunciation, but the spec's broad level keeps the phonemes.

Source: Vidal de Battini, B. E. (1964), El español de la Argentina, Cuyo region section (assibilated rr and tr); Fontanella de Weinberg (2000), El español de la Argentina y sus variedades regionales, Cuyo chapter.

### syllable-final /s/

Always write [s], although Cuyano speech commonly aspirates coda /s/ to [h] or deletes it: estos [ˈestos], mismo [ˈmismo], vamos [ˈbamos]. The weakening is variable and socially conditioned, so the broad transcription does not write it.

Source: Lipski, J. M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Longman, chapter on Argentina; Fontanella de Weinberg (2000), El español de la Argentina y sus variedades regionales.

### voiced obstruents b d g

Write /b d ɡ/ as [b d ɡ] in every position, never [β ð ɣ], since spirantization is predictable. b and v are both [b]: vino [ˈbino], lobo [ˈlobo]. Intervocalic and final d are written even where weakened or dropped: cansado [kanˈsado], verdad [beɾˈdad]. Use the IPA symbol ɡ throughout.

Source: Phonemic broad convention, Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos; Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP, chapter on voiced obstruents.

### velar fricative and other spellings

j, and g before e or i, are [x], with no fronting to [ç]: jugo [ˈxuɡo], gente [ˈxente], México [ˈmexiko]. ch is [tʃ]: chico [ˈtʃiko]. h is silent: hora [ˈoɾa]. qu and gu before e or i are [k] and [ɡ], and gü is [ɡw]: queso [ˈkeso], guerra [ˈɡera], pingüino [pinˈɡwino]. Elsewhere x is [ks]: taxi [ˈtaksi]. ñ is [ɲ]: año [ˈaɲo].

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP; Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
