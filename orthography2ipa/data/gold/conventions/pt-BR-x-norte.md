# Transcription conventions: Nortista/Amazônico Portuguese (pt-BR-x-norte)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ at the start of the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables, and on stressed monosyllables (content words). Unstressed clitics carry no mark (see function_words). Secondary stress is never marked. Placement: a written acute or circumflex marks the stressed syllable: café [kaˈfɛ], você [voˈse], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃pada]. Without an accent mark, words ending in -a, -e, -o (optionally + s), -am, -em or -ens are stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkaza], homem [ˈɔmẽj̃], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. All other words are stressed on the final syllable, including those ending in -i, -u, -l, -r, -z, -x, -im, -um, -ã or -ão: Belém [bɛˈlẽj̃], Manaus [maˈnawʃ], falar [faˈlah], azul [aˈzuw], irmão [ihˈmɐ̃w̃]. A glide is not a syllable, so stress falls on the vowel before it: água [ˈagwa], história [iʃˈtɔɾja]. Stressed mid-vowel quality is lexical: é and ó are [ɛ ɔ], ê and ô are [e o], and unmarked words follow the lexicon: pedra [ˈpɛdɾa], boca [ˈboka]. Vowel length, geminates and case endings do not apply to this lect: there is no phonemic length, written ss and rr are single consonants [s] and [h], and there are no case endings.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 (word stress: penultimate default for vowel-final words, final stress for consonant-final words); Silva (2002), syllable and stress chapters.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed, carry no ˈ, and take unstressed-vowel reduction (final /e o/ become [i u]). They do not take the open pretonic vowels. Articles: o a os as [u a uʃ aʃ], um uma [ũ uma]. Prepositions and their contractions: de do da [dʒi du da], em no na [ẽj̃ nu na], a ao à [a aw a], com [kõ], sem [sẽj̃], por [poh], pelo pela [pɛlu pɛla], para pra [paɾa pɾa]. Conjunctions: e [i], que [ki], se [si], mas [majʃ], ou [o], nem [nẽj̃], como (conjunction) [komu]. Clitic object and reflexive pronouns, including hyphenated ones: me te se lhe [mi tʃi si ʎi], nos [nuʃ], o a os as [u a uʃ aʃ]. Relative que [ki]. Everything else is stressed and marked. This covers subject and prepositional pronouns: eu [ˈew], tu [ˈtu], ele [ˈeli], ela [ˈɛla], nós [ˈnɔjʃ], você [voˈse], mim [ˈmĩ]. It covers demonstratives, possessives, quantifiers and negation: não [ˈnɐ̃w̃]. It covers all verb forms including auxiliaries and copulas: é [ˈɛ], está [iʃˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], vai [ˈvaj], foi [ˈfoj]. It also covers stressed quê and porquê: [ˈke], [poɦˈke].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 and ch. 7 on clitics and the prosodic word; Silva (2002) on unstressed monosyllables in Brazilian Portuguese.

## Pausal forms

There is no pausal morphology. Final short vowels, case endings and nunation (tanwīn) do not apply to Portuguese. A pause occurs at the end of the sentence and at any comma, colon, semicolon or dash. A pause only blocks the cross-word sandhi in connected_speech. Before a pause, each word keeps its isolation form: coda /S/ is [ʃ] (dias, [ˈdʒiaʃ]), final r is [h] (falar. [faˈlah]), and final unstressed /e o/ stay [i u] (noite, [ˈnojtʃi]). Vowels are not devoiced or deleted before a pause.

Source: Silva (2014, ALFAL #4814), citing Noll (2008) and Mota, Jesus & Evangelista (2010): the palatal [ʃ] variant predominates for Belém/Manaus in absolute-final coda position.

## Connected speech

Each word stays a separate IPA word in its own place. Only the boundary segment of the word before the boundary changes, and only when no pause intervenes. (1) Word-final /S/ depends on the next sound. Before a vowel it is [z]: os olhos [uz ˈɔʎuʃ], mas é [majz ˈɛ]. Before a voiced consonant it is [ʒ]: os dias [uʒ ˈdʒiaʃ], mesmas mãos [ˈmeʒmaʒ ˈmɐ̃w̃ʃ]. Before a voiceless consonant it is [ʃ]: as casas [aʃ ˈkazaʃ]. Before s- the [ʃ] is kept and not degeminated: as sete [aʃ ˈsɛtʃi]. (2) Word-final r before a vowel-initial word is the tap [ɾ]: falar alto [faˈlaɾ ˈawtu]; otherwise it is [h]. (3) Vowel sandhi is not written. There is no elision, crasis or glide formation across the boundary: de uma [dʒi uma], casa amarela [ˈkaza amaˈɾɛla], que eu [ki ˈew]. (4) Nasal place assimilation, final-l vocalisation and t/d palatalisation are word-internal and fixed, and are not changed by the next word: sem pai [sẽj̃ ˈpaj], mal amado [ˈmaw aˈmadu].

Source: Silva (2002), sections on coda /S/ voicing and resyllabification; Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 7 (sandhi of /S/ and /r/); Silva (2014, ALFAL #4814) for the palatal coda realisation in Belém.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vowels as [j w], never as i̯ u̯. Oral diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], meu [ˈmew], céu [ˈsɛw], foi [ˈfoj], mal [ˈmaw]. Coda l is always [w]: Brasil [bɾaˈziw]. Nasal diphthongs carry the tilde on both parts: mão [ˈmɐ̃w̃], tem [ˈtẽj̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], muito [ˈmũj̃tu]. Post-tonic unstressed i or u before a word-final vowel is a glide: história [iʃˈtɔɾja], água [ˈagwa], série [ˈsɛɾji]. Stressed í or ú, and pretonic i or u, stay syllabic: saída [saˈida], dia [ˈdʒia], piano [piˈɐ̃nu]. Monophthongisation is written. ou is always [o]: outro [ˈotɾu], sou [ˈso]. ei before r, x or j is [e]: primeiro [pɾiˈmeɾu], beijo [ˈbeʒu]. ai before x is [a]: caixa [ˈkaʃa]. A glide [j] is inserted between a stressed word-final vowel and final s or z: mas [majʃ], três [ˈtɾejʃ], nós [ˈnɔjʃ], voz [ˈvɔjʃ], arroz [aˈhojʃ].

Source: Silva (2002), chapters on diphthongs and monophthongisation; Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 3 (glides as non-syllabic high vowels); Silva (2014, ALFAL #4814) on diphthongisation before /S/.

## Other choices

### pretonic mid vowels

In an oral pretonic syllable, /e o/ are open [ɛ ɔ]: Belém [bɛˈlẽj̃], morena [mɔˈɾena], pequeno [pɛˈkenu], colégio [kɔˈlɛʒju]. No vowel-harmony raising is written. Before a nasal they are closed nasal [ẽ õ]: sentido [sẽˈtʃidu], contar [kõˈtah]. Word-initial es- and ex- + consonant are [iʃ iz]: estar [iʃˈtah], esmola [iʒˈmɔla]. Pretonic /a/ is [a]. This follows the specification note. Some sociolinguistic studies of Belém and Manaus report variable or closed pretonics; this convention does not follow them.

Source: Nascentes (1953:25), North/South division by pretonic openness; Cardoso et al. (2014, ALiB), pretonic-vowel isoglosses.

### final unstressed vowels

Word-final unstressed /e o a/ are [i u a]: leite [ˈlejtʃi], livro [ˈlivɾu], casa [ˈkaza]. The symbol ɐ is used only for the nasal vowel [ɐ̃]: cama [ˈkɐ̃ma], irmã [ihˈmɐ̃]. A stressed vowel before a nasal consonant is written nasal, and a coda nasal consonant is not written: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], ponte [ˈpõtʃi].

Source: Silva (2002), vowel chapter; Callou & Leite (2001).

### t/d palatalisation

/t d/ before [i], including [i] from final /e/ and from epenthesis, are [tʃ dʒ]: tia [ˈtʃia], cidade [siˈdadʒi], noite [ˈnojtʃi], de [dʒi]. Before other vowels they are [t d].

Source: Cardoso et al. (2014, ALiB): the North is the region of strongest palatalisation; specification note.

### rhotics

Strong r is the glottal fricative [h]. This covers initial r, rr, r after n, l or s, and coda r: rio [ˈhiu], carro [ˈkahu], porta [ˈpɔhta], mar [ˈmah]. Final r of infinitives is written [h], not deleted: comer [koˈmeh]. The exception is the sandhi tap in connected_speech. Intervocalic r and r in onset clusters are [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], prato [ˈpɾatu].

Source: Callou & Leite (2001), rhotic variants; Cardoso et al. (2014, ALiB), posterior fricative r in the Northern capitals.

### coda /S/

Word-internal coda /S/ is [ʃ] before a voiceless consonant and [ʒ] before a voiced one: festa [ˈfɛʃta], mesmo [ˈmeʒmu]. Word-final /S/ follows pausal_forms and connected_speech. nh is written [ɲ] and lh is written [ʎ]: ninho [ˈniɲu], filho [ˈfiʎu].

Source: Silva (2014, ALFAL #4814), citing Noll (2008) and Mota, Jesus & Evangelista (2010).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
