# Transcription conventions: Madeiran Portuguese (pt-PT-x-madeira)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is lexical, and each word keeps the stress of its citation form. Write ˈ immediately before the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables that is stressed in connected speech. Do not mark monosyllables, and do not mark secondary stress. Placement follows the orthography. A written acute or circumflex accent marks the stressed vowel: café [kɐˈfɛ], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃pɐdɐ], quilómetro [kiˈʎɔmɨtɾu]. A tilde vowel in the last syllable takes the stress: limão [liˈmɐ̃w], limões [liˈmõns]. Without an accent, words ending in -a, -e, -o (plus -s), -am or -em(s) take penultimate stress: casa [ˈkazɐ], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w], homem [ˈɔmɐ̃j]. Words ending in -i, -u, -l, -r, -z, other consonants or an oral diphthong take final stress: aqui [ɐˈki], papel [pɐˈpɛɫ], falar [fɐˈlaɾ], comeu [kuˈmew]. Syllable division before ˈ: put a single intervocalic consonant in the onset, and keep obstruent+liquid clusters together (outro [ˈotɾu]). Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not exist in this lect. Double rr and ss are single consonants [ʁ] and [s], and there is no vowel length or case morphology to mark.

Source: Mateus, M.H.M. & d'Andrade, E. (2000), The Phonology of Portuguese, OUP, ch. 6 (word stress). This is the standard European Portuguese stress system, which Madeiran shares (Segura 2013, Gramática do Português I, cap. 3, pp. 85-142, describes no stress differences for Madeira).

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed: write them without ˈ and with reduced vowels. Definite articles: o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ]. Prepositions and their contractions: de [dɨ], em [ɐ̃j], a [ɐ], à [a], ao [aw], do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], num [nũ], pelo [pɨlu], pela [pɨlɐ], para [pɐɾɐ], por [puɾ], com [kõ], sem [sɐ̃j]. Conjunctions and complementisers: e [i], que [kɨ], se [sɨ], mas [mɐʃ], ou [o], nem [nɐ̃j], como as a conjunction [kumu]. Relative que [kɨ]. Atonic clitic pronouns, whether free or hyphenated: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [sɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], lhes [ʎɨʃ], nos [nuʃ], vos [vuʃ], o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ], lo [lu], la [lɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ]. Example: dá-me [da mɨ], vê-lo [ve lu]. The following are stressed and keep full vowels, with ˈ if polysyllabic: lexical words, the indefinite article um [ũ] / uma [ˈumɐ], tonic and strong pronouns (eu [ew], tu [tu], ele [ˈelɨ], ela [ˈɛlɐ], nós [nɔʃ], você [voˈse], mim [mĩ], si [si]), demonstratives (este [ˈeʃtɨ], isso [ˈisu]), possessives (meu [mew], nossa [ˈnɔsɐ]), numerals, adverbs including não [nɐ̃w], interrogatives (quem [kɐ̃j], onde [ˈõdɨ]), and all forms of the auxiliaries and copulas ser, estar, ter, haver and ir (é [ɛ], está [ɨʃˈta], tem [tɐ̃j], tinha [ˈtiɲɐ], foi [foj]). Always write the final [ɨ] of de, que, me and similar words; never delete it.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), chs. 2 and 7 (unstressed vowel reduction and clitics), Lisbon-standard citation forms of atonic words. Madeiran forms are taken to be the same, as in Segura (2013), cap. 3.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Write every word in the same form at the end of a sentence, at a comma and sentence-internally. Final unstressed vowels are always the reduced [ɐ ɨ u] (casa [ˈkazɐ], noite [ˈnojtɨ], novo [ˈnovu]). Final /s/ before a pause is [ʃ] (casas [ˈkazɐʃ]); in the plural nasal endings it is [s] ([kɐ̃ns]).

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), chs. 2-3 (no pausal morphology; coda sibilant [ʃ] before pause). This is a non-Arabic lect, so the category is inapplicable.

## Connected speech

Only word-final sibilant voicing across a boundary is written; it changes the final segment of the first word. A word-final ordinary /s z/ (spelled s or z) is written as follows: [ʃ] before a pause or a voiceless consonant (os pés [uʃ pɛʃ]); [ʒ] before a voiced consonant (os dias [uʒ ˈdiɐʃ], mais nada [majʒ ˈnadɐ]); [z] before a vowel-initial word (os amigos [uz ɐˈmiɡuʃ], dez anos [dɛz ˈɐnuʃ]). The sibilant of the Madeiran nasal endings [õns] and [ɐ̃ns] is written [s] before a pause or a voiceless consonant and [z] before a vowel or a voiced consonant (cães e gatos [kɐ̃nz i ˈɡatuʃ]). Nothing else is written across word boundaries: no vowel elision, crasis, gliding or merging of vowels in hiatus (de amigos [dɨ ɐˈmiɡuʃ], not [dɐˈmiɡuʃ]; que ele [kɨ ˈelɨ]), and no nasal place assimilation. Final /ɾ/ stays [ɾ] in all positions (falar alto [fɐˈlaɾ ˈaɫtu]). Final /l/ stays [ɫ] in all positions (mal [maɫ]). Each word stays in its own slot.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 3 (coda /s/ realisation and resyllabification of final /s/ before vowels), following the Lisbon-standard pattern that Madeira shares (Segura 2013, cap. 3). Vowel sandhi is described as optional and gradient, so it is left unwritten for consistency.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the non-syllabic parts of diphthongs as [j] and [w], never i̯ or u̯. Oral examples: pai [paj], mau [maw], muito [ˈmũjtu], foi [foj], céu [sɛw], ou as [o] (outro [ˈotɾu]), and ei as [ɐj] (primeiro [pɾiˈmɐjɾu]). Nasal diphthongs carry the tilde on the nucleus only: mãe [mɐ̃j], são [sɐ̃w], põe [põj], bem [bɐ̃j], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w]. The Madeiran plural nasal endings are not written as diphthongs, but as nasal vowel + [n] + sibilant: -ões [õns] (leões [liˈõns]), -ães [ɐ̃ns] (cães [kɐ̃ns]), -ãos [ɐ̃ns] (mãos [mɐ̃ns]). Singular -ão keeps [ɐ̃w] (verão [vɨˈɾɐ̃w]), even though some Madeiran speech has [õns] there. Unstressed i or u before a vowel inside a word is a glide (série [ˈsɛɾjɐ], água [ˈaɡwɐ], quase [ˈkwazɨ]). Hiatus with a stressed vowel is kept (dia [ˈdiɐ], saúde [sɐˈudɨ], país [pɐˈiʃ]). The reported Madeiran diphthongisation of stressed /i/ to [ɐj] is not written; dia is [ˈdiɐ] and ilha is [ˈiʎɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 (glides and diphthongs of European Portuguese). The nasal + N plural endings follow Segura (2013), cap. 3, on Atlantic-island nasal diphthongs, as confirmed by Portuguese With Leo (2021) ('leôns', 'câns'). The singular -ão and stressed /i/ choices follow the specification notes, because published descriptions present these as sporadic or lexically restricted.

## Other choices

### /l/ palatalisation after /i/

Word-internal intervocalic /l/ after oral /i/, stressed or unstressed, is written [ʎ]: vila [ˈviʎɐ], quilo [ˈkiʎu], mochila [muˈʃiʎɐ], quilómetro [kiˈʎɔmɨtɾu]. Elsewhere, /l/ is [l] in the onset (bola [ˈbɔlɐ]) and [ɫ] in the coda (Brasil [bɾɐˈziɫ], alto [ˈaɫtu]). The rule does not apply across word boundaries.

Source: Segura (2013), Gramática do Português I, cap. 3, pp. 85-142 (Madeiran /l/ to [ʎ]), and Portuguese With Leo (2021) ('quilhómetro'). Coda [ɫ] follows Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2.

### /v/ vs /b/

There is no betacism, so /v/ is always [v] and /b/ is always [b]: visitar [viziˈtaɾ], levada [lɨˈvadɐ], novo [ˈnovu], vinho [ˈviɲu]. The isolated [ˈbiɲu] is a lexical item and is not written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 (/v/~/b/ contrast in the standard). Segura (2013), cap. 3, restricts betacism to northern mainland dialects.

### Stops /b d ɡ/

Write /b d ɡ/ as the stops [b d ɡ] in all positions, and never write the fricative or approximant allophones [β ð ɣ]: cada [ˈkadɐ], lago [ˈlaɡu]. Do not write aspiration or final devoicing either.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2, treat the spirants as predictable allophones that a broad transcription omits.

### Unstressed vowel reduction

Use the standard European Portuguese reduction. Unstressed /a/ is [ɐ], unstressed /e/ is [ɨ], and unstressed /o/ is [u]: pequeno [pɨˈkenu], menino [mɨˈninu], morar [muˈɾaɾ]. Initial unstressed es- and ex- before a consonant are [ɨʃ]: escola [ɨʃˈkɔlɐ]. Final unstressed -e is always written [ɨ] (sentimento [sẽtiˈmẽtu], noite [ˈnojtɨ]). The extreme Madeiran final-vowel deletion and mutation ('sentmente') and -oi- syncope (depois [dɨˈpojʃ], not [dɨˈpoʃ]) are not written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 (vowel reduction). Portuguese With Leo (2021) attests the Madeiran gradient reductions, which are treated as non-categorical.

### Stressed /e/ before palatals

Stressed /e/ before [ʎ ɲ ʒ ʃ] is written [ɐ], as in Lisbon: coelho [kuˈɐʎu], espelho [ɨʃˈpɐʎu], venho [ˈvɐɲu], igreja [iˈɡɾɐʒɐ], fecho [ˈfɐʃu]. Madeira's 'coâlho' is reported to pattern with Lisbon and against São Miguel 'coêlho' [e]. This departs from the specification notes, which leave this unmodelled.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 (pre-palatal centralisation in the standard). For Madeira: Segura (2013), cap. 3, and the Madeiran 'coâlho' reported in the eight-accents overview cited in the notes.

### Rhotics

Word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s are written [ʁ]: rua [ˈʁuɐ], carro [ˈkaʁu], honra [ˈõʁɐ]. Elsewhere, including the coda, r is the tap [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], mar [maɾ]. The lexical r-diphthongisation (correr as [kuˈʁweɾ]) is not written: correr [kuˈʁeɾ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 (European Portuguese rhotics, uvular strong r). For Madeira, see Segura (2013), cap. 3.

### Coda sibilants

Within the word, coda s, z and x are written [ʃ] before a voiceless consonant and [ʒ] before a voiced consonant: festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ], mesmo [ˈmeʒmu]. For word-final sibilants, follow the connected_speech convention.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 3 (Lisbon-type palatal coda sibilants, shared by Madeira).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
