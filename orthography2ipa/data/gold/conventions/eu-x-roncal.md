# Transcription conventions: Roncalese Basque (Erronkariera) (eu-x-roncal)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the penultimate syllable of every polysyllabic stressed word. Count syllables over the whole inflected form, so the article, plural and case endings count: [ˈeɡun] egun, [ɡiˈs̻ona] gizona, [eˈtʃea] etxea, [etʃeˈetan] etxeetan, [ˈtenpɾa] tenpra, [ˈbarts̻e] bartze. A falling diphthong is one syllable and its nucleus: [ɡau̯r] gaur (monosyllable, unmarked), [ˈɡau̯ɾe] gaure. Do not mark monosyllables. Vowel length is not phonemic, so a sequence of identical vowels is two syllables, and length is never written. Basque has no geminates, so a double letter is a single segment: rr = [r], tt = [c], dd = [ɟ], ll = [ʎ]. Words listed as unstressed under function_words get no mark. Final stress that some sources report for individual lexical items is not marked; the penultimate rule applies everywhere.

Source: Penultimate stress for Roncalese and Salazarese, as for Souletin, as reported from Michelena by Hualde, 'Accentuation', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (2003), A Grammar of Basque, phonology chapter (pages approximate, c. 65-72); Michelena 1977 (FHV).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Basque has no free articles or prepositions. The article (-a, -ak), case endings and postpositional suffixes belong to the host word and count for its stress. Leave the following without a stress mark: finite auxiliaries and copula forms that follow a lexical verb or predicate (da, du, dut, dira, zira, [s̻ɾa] zra, naiz, nau, dau, zen, zuen), the conjunctions eta / ta, edo, ala, the particles ere, al, ote, and any clitic split off by a hyphen. Examples: [ˈjan du] jan du, [aiˈtoɾ eta ˈmiren], [ni eɾe]. Mark stress on personal pronouns of two or more syllables ([ˈhaɾek] harek), on demonstratives ([ˈhoɾi]), on interrogatives ([ˈnoɾ], monosyllable unmarked), on the negator in initial position, and on auxiliaries that stand first in the clause after ez (stress the polysyllabic negated auxiliary, [ˈdiɾa]). Monosyllables are never marked.

Source: Hualde 2003, 'Accentuation', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque, on unstressed auxiliaries and clitic particles in phrasal accent; applied here to the penultimate system.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Basque has no pausal forms, case-ending loss or nunation. Every word is written in the same form at a comma, at sentence end and inside the sentence, and final vowels are kept: [ˈetʃe], [eˈtʃea].

Source: Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina 2003, 'Segmental phonology' (pp. 15-65): no pausal alternations are described for Basque.

## Connected speech

Write no cross-word processes. Each word keeps its citation transcription in its own place, so there is no resyllabification marking, no sibilant voicing, no nasal place assimilation and no elision across the space: [ˈhamar ˈetʃe], [ˈbas̻ ˈda] is written [ˈbas̻ da] with only the auxiliary rule applied. Word-final r is always [r] even before a vowel. Inside a word, write only what the spelling gives. The historical syncope and /i/ > /u/ metaphony appear only where the source text already spells them: [ˈtupla] tupla, [ˈulun] ulun, [ˈbarts̻e] bartze, [s̻ɾa] zra. They are not reconstructed from standard spellings such as tipula or ilun.

Source: Convention of this set, following the specification note that syncope and metaphony are not generated. The processes themselves are described in Egurtzegi 2013: 131-136 (citing Michelena FHV: 79, 160).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the second element of a falling diphthong as a non-syllabic vowel: [ai̯ ei̯ oi̯ ui̯ au̯ eu̯], e.g. [ɡau̯r], [ˈhaɪ̯] is not used, write [ˈlai̯ɾu]. Write a syllable-initial glide spelled j, y or i between vowels as [j], e.g. [ˈjan] is written [jan], [ˈbaja] baia (only where the text spells the glide). Write w only where the text spells u in syllable onset before a vowel. Other vowel sequences are hiatus: [eˈtʃea], [ˈmendi.a] is written [ˈmendia]. Epenthetic glides (mendija) are not inserted unless they are spelled. Roncalese has no front rounded /y/, unlike Souletin, so u is always [u].

Source: Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina 2003, 'Segmental phonology' (diphthongs and <j> realisations, pp. 15-65), with Navarrese [j] for <j>.

## Other choices

### aspiration

Write [h] wherever the text spells h: [ˈhamar], [ˈheri] herri. Write [pʰ tʰ kʰ] only where the text spells ph, th, kh, and plain [p t k] everywhere else. Do not use the variable h ~ zero; h is always written as [h].

Source: Hualde 2018, 'Aspiration in Basque', pp. 2-3, on eastern aspiration receding in Roncalese. One variant is chosen here for consistency, against the variable model in the specification note.

### nasal vowels

Do not mark vowel nasality. Michelena and Egurtzegi report a contrastive nasal-vowel series, but the orthography does not mark it and the specification does not emit it: [ˈaʰ...] is not used, write oral vowels.

Source: Egurtzegi 2013: 127; Michelena 1977 FHV: 48-50. Omitted per the specification note.

### sibilants and affricates

z = [s̻], s = [s̺], x = [ʃ], tz = [ts̻], ts = [ts̺], tx = [tʃ]: [ɡiˈs̻ona], [ˈbes̺o], [ˈetʃe], [ˈbarts̻e].

Source: Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina 2003, 'Segmental phonology'.

### rhotics and stops

Write [r] for rr and for any r in coda or word-final position. Write [ɾ] for single r between vowels and after a consonant in an onset: [ˈheri], [ˈtenpɾa], [ˈɡau̯ɾe]. Write voiced stops as [b d ɡ] in all positions, with no spirant allophones. Write ñ [ɲ], ll [ʎ], tt [c], dd [ɟ] only where spelled.

Source: Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina 2003, 'Segmental phonology' (rhotic neutralisation, broad stop transcription).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
