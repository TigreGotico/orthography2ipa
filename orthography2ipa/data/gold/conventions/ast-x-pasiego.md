# Transcription conventions: Pasiegu (Pasiego, Montes de Pas) (ast-x-pasiego)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable. Glides and clusters belong to that onset: [rɐˈbjosʊ], [ˈxwɪɣʊ], [ˈkasa]. Stress placement is lexical and Spanish/Asturian-type. Follow the written accent where there is one (⟨á é í ó ú⟩). Without one, stress the penult of words ending in a vowel, ⟨-n⟩ or ⟨-s⟩, and the last syllable of words ending in any other consonant. ⟨ui⟩/⟨iu⟩ count as one syllable. Mark every polysyllable and every stressed monosyllable (content words, tonic pronouns: [ˈsal], [ˈjo]). Do not mark the unstressed function words listed under function_words. No secondary stress. Metaphony, centralisation and final-vowel reduction never move stress. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not exist in the lect; not applicable.

Source: Spanish/Asturleonese lexical stress as assumed in Walker 2014 (§15-21 transcriptions, e.g. rᴀˈbjʊsʊ, ˈistə) and Penny 2009 §3.1.5 (stressed vowel as metaphony target).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, so no ˈ:
- articles: el, la, lu/lo, los/lus, las
- the neuter article lo
- all prepositions (a, de, en, con, pa, por, sin…)
- conjunctions: y, o, que, si, pero, ni
- clitic object pronouns: me, te, se, lu, la, le, nos, vos, los, las, les
- possessive determiners before a noun: mi, tu, su
- relative que
- forms of haber used as auxiliary

These words still get the final-vowel rules: lu [lʊ], de/di [də], que [kə]. They get no metaphony and no stress mark.

Stressed, so ˈ: subject and tonic pronouns ([ˈjo], [ˈel], [ˈmi] after a preposition), demonstratives, numerals, interrogatives and exclamatives, negative no/non, and all full verbs including ser/estar as main verbs. Penny's spread of metaphony onto proclitics is not written. Proclitics keep their own non-metaphonic vowels: lu [lʊ], not [lʉ].

Source: Spanish/Asturian clitic prosody as in Walker 2014 §15-21, which applies harmony per stressed word. Penny 2009 §3.1.5 p.115 (proclitics as targets) is noted and not followed.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Pasiegu has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word is written the same at a comma, at the end of the sentence and inside the phrase. Final vowels always follow the final-vowel rule under 'other'.

Source: None of Penny 2009, Walker 2014 or the UPF thesis (2019) describes any pausal alternation.

## Connected speech

Each word is transcribed in its own slot. Only these cross-boundary processes are written:
(1) Spirantisation: a word-initial ⟨b/v d g⟩ is written [β ð ɣ] when the preceding word in the same phrase ends in a vowel, glide or non-nasal consonant other than l (for d). Write [b d ɡ] after a pause, after a word ending in a nasal, and for d after l: la vaca [la ˈβaka], un bou [um ˈbow]. Inside words the same rule applies: [ˈxwɪɣʊ].
(2) Nasal place assimilation: a word-final ⟨n⟩ becomes [m] before [p b m] and [ŋ] before [k ɡ x]: un gatu [uŋ ˈɡɐtʊ], con pan [kom ˈpan].
Not written: synalepha, vowel elision, glide formation across words, s-voicing and resyllabification. Write la ama [la ˈama], with both vowels kept.

Source: Standard Castilian/Asturleonese allophony as written in the specification's notes (ḥuegu with [ɣ]). Walker 2014 transcribes each word separately.

## Glides and diphthongs

Non-syllabic high vowels are written with [j] and [w], never i̯ u̯. This holds in rising diphthongs ([ˈbjen], [ˈxwɪɣʊ], [rɐˈbjosʊ]) and in falling diphthongs ([ˈrej], [ˈbow], [ˈajɾe]). ⟨y⟩ as consonant onset is [ʝ]: [ˈʝeɣwa]. Glides are not targets of metaphony or centralisation. In ue/ie the vowel after the glide is the stressed vowel and is the one that changes: bueno-type count [ˈɡwɪnʊ], fem. [ˈɡwena]. Written hiatus (accented í/ú) stays two syllables: [ˈri.ʊ] → write [ˈriʊ].

Source: Walker 2014 §15-21 (rᴀˈbjʊsʊ with j). Penny 2009 §5.1 (bueno with the diphthong nucleus raised).

## Other choices

### Final unstressed vowels

Spelled final ⟨-o⟩ and ⟨-u⟩ (also before -s) → [ʊ]. Spelled final ⟨-i⟩ and ⟨-e⟩ → [ə]. Final ⟨-a⟩ → [a]. Examples: esti [ˈistə], casa [ˈkasa], gatos [ˈɡatʊs]. This follows Walker's surface notation rather than Penny's /ɨ ʉ/ or the thesis's [-u -i -ɘ]. It also differs from the note's [ˈxweɣu]: final -u is written [ʊ].

Source: Walker 2014 §18 and fn.4 (/-i/→[ə], /-u/,/-o/→[ʊ], /-a/→[a]).

### Metaphony (written, against the note that leaves it unmapped)

Use the published pronunciation. In a singular word whose final vowel is a high trigger, raise the stressed vowel one step: e→i, o→u, a→ɐ. Already high i and u stay.

Triggers:
- final -u of a countable masculine singular noun, or of an adjective, pronoun or determiner agreeing with one
- spelled final -i (esti, isi, aquelli)

No metaphony:
- feminines
- plurals
- words spelled -o
- mass or neuter forms agreeing with uncountable nouns or with lo/esto (the neuter of matter)

Decide count versus mass from the sentence context, not the spelling: pelu count [ˈpɪlʊ] vs mass [ˈpelʊ]; rabiosu count [rɐˈbjʊsʊ] vs mass [rɐˈbjosʊ].

Source: Walker 2014 §15-18 (trigger /u/, /-i/, not [ʊ] < /-o/; count/mass pairs). Penny 2009 §3.1.5-3.1.6 p.115 (a→ɐ in set 2a) and §5.1. UPF thesis 2019 fn.2 p.3 (neuter of matter).

### Centralisation harmony

In any word whose last vowel is [ʊ] (with or without -s), write i→ɪ, u→ʊ, a→ɐ in all its vowels; e and o stay. This applies after metaphony: /pelu/ count → pilu → [ˈpɪlʊ]; mass [ˈpelʊ]; [rɐˈbjosʊ]; gatos [ˈɡɐtʊs]. Words ending in [a] or [ə] are untouched: [ˈistə], [ˈkasa]. Pretonic raising before a stressed high vowel is variable and is not written.

Source: Walker 2014 §19-21 (centralisation as a separate harmony triggered by final [ʊ]; ᴀ written here as IPA [ɐ]).

### Aspirated F- and jota

⟨ḥ⟩ (⟨h.⟩, from Latin F-) → [x]: ḥuegu count [ˈxwɪɣʊ]. ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩+e/i → [h], keeping the conservative contrast: juegu [ˈhwɪɣʊ]. Plain ⟨h⟩ is silent.

Source: UPF thesis 2019 fn.5 p.5 and p.27 (ḥuegu/juegu x/h contrast, conservative Pas speakers), as given in the specification's notes.

### Palatal lateral and other consonants

⟨ll⟩ → [ʎ] (no yeísmo): llumiacu [ʎuˈmjakʊ] → with harmony [ʎʊˈmjɐkʊ]. ⟨y⟩ → [ʝ]. ⟨z⟩, ⟨c⟩+e/i → [θ] (distinción). ⟨ch⟩ → [tʃ]. ⟨ñ⟩ → [ɲ]. ⟨r⟩ → [ɾ], and → [r] word-initially, after n/l/s and for ⟨rr⟩. ⟨qu⟩/⟨c⟩ → [k]. ⟨x⟩ → [ʃ] in vernacular words.

Source: UPF thesis 2019 p.27 (ʎ < L-, -LL-). Cantabrian/Asturleonese consonant frame of the specification.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
