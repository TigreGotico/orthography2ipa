# Transcription conventions: Litoraleño (Guaranític) Spanish (es-AR-x-litoral)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is lexical and falls where standard Spanish spelling puts it. A written accent marks the stressed syllable. Otherwise words ending in a vowel, n or s stress the penultimate syllable, and words ending in any other consonant stress the last syllable. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every stressed word, monosyllables included: casa ˈkasa, árbol ˈaɾbol, ciudad sjuˈdad, Corrientes koˈrjentes, sol ˈsol. Voseo verb forms keep their final stress: tenés teˈnes, vení beˈni, sabés saˈbes. Guaraní-origin names and loans take stress where the spelling marks it, usually the last syllable: Itatí itaˈti, ñandú ɲanˈdu, Paraná paɾaˈna. Adverbs in -mente take ˈ on -mente and ˌ on the adjective's stressed syllable: rápidamente ˌrapidaˈmente. Do not mark syllable boundaries. Spanish has no phonemic vowel length, no geminates and no case endings. Write rr as a single trill r, and doubled vowels as two vowels, each written: leer leˈeɾ, creer kɾeˈeɾ.

Source: Standard Spanish lexical stress as in Quilis, A. (1993) Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, ch. on accent; Hualde, J.I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 13 (including the double stress of -mente). Voseo stress follows Fontanella de Weinberg (ed.) (2000) El español de la Argentina y sus variedades regionales.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Leave these words unstressed, with no ˈ mark. Definite articles: el la los las lo, as el /el/ and los /los/. Object and reflexive clitics: me te se nos os le les lo la los las. Prenominal possessives: mi tu su mis tus sus. All simple prepositions except según: a de en con por para sin sobre entre hasta desde hacia. Conjunctions and complementizers: y e ni o u que pero si mas aunque porque. Relative (non-interrogative) que quien donde cuando como cual. The forms of address don doña. Give these words ˈ: indefinite articles (un ˈun, una ˈuna); subject and prepositional pronouns (yo ˈʝo, vos ˈbos, él ˈel, ella ˈeʎa, mí ˈmi, ustedes usˈtedes); según seˈɣun, written seˈɡun in this broad style; demonstratives; stressed possessives (mío ˈmio); all verbs, including the auxiliaries haber, ser and estar (ha ˈa, es ˈes, está esˈta); interrogatives and exclamatives with a written accent (qué ˈke, dónde ˈdonde); and negative no ˈno.

Source: Quilis, A. (1993) Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, the list of accented and unaccented words (palabras acentuadas / inacentuadas); Hualde, J.I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 13.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so a word is written the same at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside a phrase.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish; no description of Spanish posits pausal morphophonology.

## Connected speech

Write every word in its own citation form and mark no cross-word process. Do not write synalepha or vowel merger: la abuela la aˈbwela, de usted de usˈted. Do not write resyllabification or linking of final consonants: los otros los ˈotɾos. Do not write nasal place assimilation: un poco ˈun ˈpoko, not ˈum. Do not write /s/ voicing or aspiration across words. Do not write the glottal stop [ʔ] that Guaranític Spanish often inserts between words in hiatus: la otra la ˈotɾa. This lect is described as resisting cross-word linking more than other Spanish varieties, so the unlinked citation form is also closest to its usual pronunciation.

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994) Latin American Spanish, ch. on Paraguay (and NE Argentina), on reduced synalepha, weak cross-word resyllabification and glottal stop insertion under Guaraní influence; Abadía de Quant, I. (2000) 'El español del nordeste', in Fontanella de Weinberg (ed.) El español de la Argentina y sus variedades regionales.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic i and u as j and w in every position, both before the vowel (rising diphthongs) and after it (falling diphthongs). Examples: bien ˈbjen, bueno ˈbweno, aire ˈajɾe, auto ˈawto, hoy ˈoj, muy ˈmuj, ciudad sjuˈdad. Do not use i̯ or u̯. Write the consonant spelled y, or hi before a vowel, as ʝ: yo ˈʝo, mayo ˈmaʝo, hielo ˈʝelo. The conjunction y is i, and is unstressed. Write vowels in hiatus as full vowels: día ˈdia, país paˈis, río ˈrio.

Source: The j/w notation of Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1957) Manual de pronunciación española, as adapted in Hualde, J.I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 9 on diphthongs; this deliberately simplifies Hualde's use of i̯ and u̯ for falling diphthongs.

## Other choices

### ll versus y (lleísmo)

Write ll as ʎ and y as ʝ: calle ˈkaʎe, lluvia ˈʎubja, ella ˈeʎa, versus mayo ˈmaʝo, yo ˈʝo. This departs from the specification note, which models yeísmo (ll -> ʝ). The published descriptions give the traditional Corrientes–Misiones norm as keeping /ʎ/ distinct, under Paraguayan and Guaraní influence. Do not write the Rioplatense ʒ or ʃ.

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994) Latin American Spanish, chs. on Argentina and Paraguay; Vidal de Battini, B. (1964) El español de la Argentina; Abadía de Quant, I. (2000) 'El español del nordeste'.

### Seseo

Write c before e or i, and z, as s: cinco ˈsinko, zapato saˈpato, hacer aˈseɾ.

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994) Latin American Spanish; the specification note.

### Rhotics

Write the tap ɾ for single r between vowels and in coda and clusters: pero ˈpeɾo, árbol ˈaɾbol, tres ˈtɾes. Write the trill r for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l or s: perro ˈpero, rojo ˈroxo, Enrique enˈrike. Do not write the regional assibilated pronunciation of the trill as a separate symbol.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on rhotics; the assibilated trill of NE Argentina and Paraguay is described in Lipski (1994) and is treated here as an allophone.

### Voiced stops and approximants

Write b d ɡ in all positions, never β ð ɣ: lado ˈlado, agua ˈaɡwa, abuela aˈbwela. Write both b and v as b: vaca ˈbaka.

Source: Broad phonemic practice of Hualde, J.I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 7 (where [β ð ɣ] are allophones).

### Coda /s/

Write s in every coda, even though it is variably aspirated or elided in this region: los ˈlos is written los (unstressed), mismo ˈmismo, estás esˈtas. Do not write h or delete the s.

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994) Latin American Spanish, on variable /s/ weakening in Paraguay and NE Argentina; this transcription keeps the phonemic form.

### Velar fricative

Write j, and g before e or i, as x: jugo ˈxuɡo, gente ˈxente. Do not write the weaker regional h.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish; Lipski, J.M. (1994) on the weak /x/ of the Paraguayan zone, treated here as allophonic.

### Other consonant letters

Write ñ as ɲ (año ˈaɲo), ch as tʃ (che ˈtʃe), silent h as nothing (hoy ˈoj), and qu or hard c as k (que ke).

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
