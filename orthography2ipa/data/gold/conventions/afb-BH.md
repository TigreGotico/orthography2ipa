# Transcription conventions: Bahraini Arabic (afb-BH)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable. Every content word gets exactly one ˈ, including monosyllables (ˈbeːt 'house'). Unstressed function words (see function_words) get none. Count from the right of the whole word as written, including attached clitics and pronoun suffixes. (1) A final superheavy syllable (CVVC, CVCC) takes stress: kiˈtaːb, ʃiˈfitʰ is wrong, write ˈʃifit; dʒaːˈboːh 'they brought him'. (2) Otherwise a heavy penult (CVV or CVC) takes stress: ˈbaːba, muˈdarris, ˈmaktab. (3) Otherwise the antepenult takes stress: ˈʃirbat is CVC.CVC so rule 2 applies; ˈɡalami 'my pen' takes the antepenult. (4) A word shorter than three syllables with no heavy syllable is stressed on its first syllable: ˈɡalam, ˈwalad. A long vowel always makes its syllable heavy. A geminate closes the preceding syllable, so that syllable is heavy and attracts stress by rule 2: ˈʕaddal, muˈʕallim. The dialect has no case endings. Never write or count case vowels, even when the diacritised input shows them; stress the dialect form. Word-final long vowels are pronounced short and do not attract stress (ˈʃaːfu 'they saw'). The underlying long vowel reappears and is stressed when a suffix follows (ʃaːˈfuːh 'they saw him').

Source: Holes, C. (1990) Gulf Arabic (Routledge Descriptive Grammars), phonology chapter, section on stress; Holes, C. (2016) Dialect, Culture, and Society in Eastern Arabia, Vol. III (Brill), phonology chapter. The specification gives no stress note, so this follows the standard Eastern Arabian/Gulf rule as Holes describes it.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ: the article (il-/l-), one- and two-syllable prepositions (b(i)-, l(i)-, fi, min, ʕala, ʕan, ma(ʕ), wiyya), conjunctions (w(i)/u, fa, aw, bas 'but' when used as a conjunction, loːn/law 'if'), the relative illi, the future/progressive prefixes (b(i)-, ɡaʕad as an auxiliary) and pronoun suffixes. Example: [w ilˈwalad] (only when these are separate words in the split), [fi lˈbeːt], [ʕala ṭˈṭaːwla] → write [ʕala tˤˈtˤaːwla]. Stressed, with ˈ, as content words: independent pronouns (ˈaːna, ˈinta, ˈhuwa), demonstratives (ˈhaːða, ˈhaːði), negators (ˈmaː, ˈmuː, ˈlaː), interrogatives (ˈwiːn, ˈʃinhu, ˈmnu), and kaːn even when it is used as an auxiliary (ˈkaːn). Clitics and suffixes that the split leaves inside a word only shift that word's stress by the stress rule. A clitic split off by a hyphen is written as its own unstressed word.

Source: Holes, C. (1990) Gulf Arabic, chapters on phonology and on pronouns, particles and the verb phrase; Holes (2016) Vol. III, morphology chapters.

## Pausal forms

No separate pausal form applies anywhere: not at the end of the sentence, not at a comma. Dialect words already have the shape that Classical Arabic gives only in pause, and they keep it in every position. Drop final short case vowels and tanwīn everywhere, even if the diacritised input marks them: كتابٌ → kiˈtaːb. Ta marbuta is -a in every position (ˈmadrasa). In a construct or before a suffix it is -at (madrasat ilˈwalad written as [madˈrasat ilˈwalad], madˈrasti). Word-final long vowels are short in every position. The one exception is tanwīn -an lexicalised in adverbs and formulas, which keeps [an] in every position: ˈʃukran, ˈabadan, ˈtaɡriːban.

Source: Holes, C. (1990) Gulf Arabic, phonology chapter (final vowels) and nominal morphology; Holes (2016) Vol. III.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its own place and never join words. Only these processes are written. (1) Sun-letter assimilation of the article inside a word: ʃˈʃams, tˤˈtˤaːwla, nˈnaːs; before moon letters it stays l: lˈbeːt. (2) Elision of the article's vowel: the article is written il- after a pause or after a consonant-final word ([ˈʃifit ilˈbeːt]). It is written l- (or the assimilated consonant) after a vowel-final word ([fi lˈbeːt], [ˈʃaːfu ʃˈʃams]). The vowel is removed from the second word only. (3) The prepositions b(i)-, l(i)- and the conjunction w(i)/u take their vowel before a consonant cluster or after a pause, and lose it after a vowel, inside their own word. Assimilation of place across word boundaries (n→m before b, voicing) is not written. Resyllabification and linking are not written, and no syllable dots or linking marks are used. The ˈ stays in the word it belongs to.

Source: Holes, C. (1990) Gulf Arabic, phonology chapter (article assimilation, helping vowels); Holes (2016) Vol. III, phonology chapter.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as the consonants j and w, never i̯ or u̯. Geminates are jj, ww: ˈjoːm, ˈwalad, ˈʃajjib is written [ˈʃajjib], ˈɡawwa. Old *ay and *aw are monophthongised in the dialect, so write eː and oː: ˈbeːt, ˈloːn, ˈjoːm. Write a sequence aj or aw only where the dialect keeps a diphthong, mainly before a geminate glide or in learned words: ˈhawa, ˈʕajn only if the item is learned. Never write a tie or non-syllabic diacritic.

Source: Holes, C. (1990) Gulf Arabic, phonology chapter (vowels and diphthongs); Johnstone, T. M. (1967) Eastern Arabian Dialect Studies (OUP), Bahrain chapter.

## Other choices

### Communal variety

Transcribe the Sunni ʕArab (Bedouin-type, mainstream Gulf) variety throughout, as the specification models it. Do not write the Baḥārna features (retained q in some items, distinct jiim reflex, 2fs -iʃ) even where a sentence might suit them.

Source: Holes, C. (1987) Language Variation and Change in a Modernising Arab State: The Case of Bahrain (Kegan Paul); Al-Balushi (2016) Macrolinguistics 4(1), p.87; specification note.

### Reflex of *q

Write ɡ in native vocabulary: ˈɡalam, ˈɡaːl. Write q in learned or Standard Arabic vocabulary, where speakers keep it: qaːˈnuːn, qurˈʔaːn. This departs from the specification's blanket *q→ɡ, following Holes.

Source: Holes (1990) Gulf Arabic, phonology chapter; Holes (1987); Alshammari (2026) JLTR 17(4), p.1335.

### Velar affrication

Write k→tʃ and ɡ→dʒ next to a front vowel only in native vocabulary where Holes reports it: ˈtʃalb 'dog', ˈtʃiːs, ˈdʒidʒ? not affricated → use only attested items; 2fs suffix -itʃ (ˈbeːtitʃ). Do not affricate in learned or Standard Arabic words (kiˈtaːb stays k). Holes treats the process as lexically conditioned, not automatic; this narrows the specification's rule.

Source: Holes (1990) Gulf Arabic, phonology chapter; Johnstone (1967); Alshammari (2026), p.1335.

### Jiim

Write dʒ: ˈdʒiːt, ˈridʒʒaːl. Do not write the [j] variant used by some speakers.

Source: Holes (1990); Holes (1987).

### Emphatics and interdentals

Keep the interdentals θ ð. Write emphatics with ˤ: sˤ, tˤ. Write both ض and ظ as ðˤ, since they have merged: ˈðˤahar, ˈʔarðˤ written [ˈarðˤ].

Source: Holes (1990) Gulf Arabic, phonology chapter.

### Glottal stop

Write ʔ only where the dialect pronounces it. Initial hamza before a vowel and most medial or final hamza are not written: ˈaːna, ˈraːs, ˈmaːj. Keep ʔ in learned words: qurˈʔaːn.

Source: Holes (1990) Gulf Arabic, phonology chapter.

### Persian p and loans

Keep p in Persian and English loans where it is pronounced: ˈpanka 'fan', ˈpeːp? write [ˈpaːjp] only if attested; otherwise b.

Source: Holes (2016) Vol. III, phonology chapter (Persian adstrate); specification note.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
