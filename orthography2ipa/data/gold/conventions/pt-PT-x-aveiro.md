# Transcription conventions: Beira Litoral Portuguese (pt-PT-x-aveiro)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every lexical word of two or more syllables and in every stressed monosyllable (content words, stressed pronouns, the negator não, finite forms of ser/estar/ter/haver). Do not mark secondary stress. Placement is lexical and falls on one of the last three syllables. A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed vowel: café [kɐˈfɛ], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃pɐdɐ]. With no accent mark, words ending in -a, -e or -o (plus -s), -am or -em(-ens) take penultimate stress: casa [ˈkas̺ɐ], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], homem [ˈɔmɐ̃j̃]. Words ending in -i, -u, -im, -um, -ão, a nasal or oral diphthong, or a consonant (l, r, z, x) take final stress: comer [kuˈmeɾ], papel [pɐˈpɛɫ], feliz [fɨˈliʃ], irmão [iɾˈmɐ̃w̃]. Long vowels and geminates do not apply: Portuguese has no length contrast. ⟨ss⟩ and ⟨rr⟩ are digraphs for one consonant: passo [ˈpas̺u], carro [ˈkaru]. Case endings do not apply: Portuguese has no case inflection. Stressed vowel quality (ɛ/e, ɔ/o) follows the standard lexical forms: avó [ɐˈbɔ], avô [ɐˈbo].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (word stress); Cunha & Cintra 1984, ch. on accentuation and the orthographic accent rules.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words are unstressed in connected speech, take no ˈ, and have reduced vowels. Articles: o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ], um [ũ], uma [umɐ]. Prepositions: de [dɨ], em [ɐ̃j̃], com [kõ], por [puɾ], sem [s̺ɐ̃j̃], para [pɐɾɐ], a [ɐ]. Contractions: do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], ao [aw], à [a], pelo [pɨlu], num [nũ]. Conjunctions: e [i], que [kɨ], se [s̺ɨ], mas [mɐʃ], ou [o], nem [nɐ̃j̃], porque [puɾkɨ]. Relatives: que [kɨ], quem [kɐ̃j̃], onde [õdɨ], quando [kwɐ̃du]. Clitic pronouns, including those split off by a hyphen: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [s̺ɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], lhes [ʎɨʃ], nos [nuʃ], vos [buʃ], o/a/os/as [u ɐ uʃ ɐʃ], lo/la [lu lɐ]. Example: dá-me [ˈda mɨ]. These words carry stress: interrogatives (quem? [ˈkɐ̃j̃], quê [ˈke], porquê [puɾˈke]), strong pronouns (eu [ˈew], tu [ˈtu], ele [ˈelɨ], nós [ˈnɔʃ], mim [ˈmĩ]), possessives (meu [ˈmew]), demonstratives (este [ˈeʃtɨ]), não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], and all verb forms, auxiliaries included (é [ˈɛ], está [ɨʃˈta], tem [ˈtɐ̃j̃], há [ˈa], foi [ˈfoj]).

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (clitics and prosodic words) and ch. 2 (vowel reduction); Cunha & Cintra 1984, chapters on articles, pronouns and prepositions.

## Pausal forms

Pausal forms in the Arabic sense do not apply: Portuguese has no case endings, no nunation and no pausal vowel loss. At the end of a sentence, at a comma or other punctuation, and at the end of the text, every word takes its isolation form. Final unstressed vowels are kept as reduced vowels and are never deleted in writing: noite [ˈnojtɨ], bolo [ˈbolu]. Final coda ⟨s, z, x⟩ is [ʃ]: luz, [ˈluʃ]. No cross-word sandhi applies across punctuation.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 and ch. 3 (word-final unstressed vowels; coda fricative at pause).

## Connected speech

Only one cross-word process is written: voicing and placement of a word-final sibilant (⟨s, z, x⟩), judged by the first sound of the next word in the same phrase with no punctuation between. Before a vowel it resyllabifies as apical [z̺]: os amigos [uz̺ ɐˈmiɡuʃ]. Before a voiced consonant it is [ʒ]: os dias [uʒ ˈdiɐʃ]. Before a voiceless consonant or a pause it is [ʃ]: os pés [uʃ ˈpɛʃ]. The same voicing rule applies inside words: mesmo [ˈmeʒmu], gosto [ˈɡoʃtu]. Each word keeps its own place, and the change shows only in the final segment of the first word. Final r stays [ɾ] before a vowel: comer a [kuˈmeɾ ɐ]. Final l stays [ɫ]: mal a [ˈmaɫ ɐ]. Vowel elision, coalescence and glide formation across word boundaries are not written. Each word keeps its own vowels: de amor [dɨ ɐˈmoɾ], not [dɐˈmoɾ]; que é [kɨ ˈɛ]. Nasal assimilation and other cross-word assimilations are not written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 (coda /S/ and its sandhi realisations) and ch. 6 (resyllabification); apical value of the prevocalic sibilant per Cintra 1971 p. 93.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vocoids as [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. In nasal diphthongs, nasalize both members: [ɐ̃w̃], [ɐ̃j̃], [õj̃], [ũj̃]. Examples: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], foi [ˈfoj], pão [ˈpɐ̃w̃], mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], muito [ˈmũj̃tu], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. ⟨ei⟩ is [ej]: primeiro [pɾiˈmejɾu]. ⟨ou⟩ is [o]: outro [ˈotɾu]. Unstressed ⟨i, u⟩ before a vowel in the same word is a glide: piano [ˈpjɐnu]. A stressed or accented ⟨i, u⟩ is a vowel in hiatus: dia [ˈdiɐ], saúde [s̺ɐˈudɨ]. Word-final -em/-ém and -am are [ɐ̃j̃] and [ɐ̃w̃]: bem [ˈbɐ̃j̃], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 (glides and diphthongs). [ej] for ⟨ei⟩ follows Cintra 1971 and Cunha & Cintra 1984, who describe [ɐj] as a Lisbon-area feature; this departs from the Lisbon-based standard (and likely from the pt-PT base the spec inherits). [o] for ⟨ou⟩ follows the general EP standard (Cunha & Cintra 1984); the conservative northern [ow] is not written.

## Other choices

### sibilants

⟨s⟩ and ⟨ss⟩ in onset, and ⟨c⟩ (before e, i), ⟨ç⟩ and onset ⟨z⟩, all write apico-alveolar [s̺] and [z̺]. Examples: sapo [ˈs̺apu], passo [ˈpas̺u], caça [ˈkas̺ɐ], cinco [ˈs̺ĩku], casa [ˈkas̺ɐ], zero [ˈz̺ɛɾu]. The diacritic is written every time. Coda sibilants are [ʃ] and [ʒ], as under connected_speech. ⟨ch⟩ is [ʃ], not [tʃ]: chave [ˈʃabɨ]. ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩ before e, i are [ʒ]: gente [ˈʒẽtɨ].

Source: Cintra 1971 pp. 92-93; Álvarez Pérez 2014 pp. 37-38 (the group kept only the apico-alveolar branch).

### betacism

⟨v⟩ and ⟨b⟩ both write /b/ as broad [b] in every position. The approximant [β] is a predictable allophone and is not written. Examples: vinho [ˈbiɲu], vaca [ˈbakɐ], novo [ˈnobu], cabo [ˈkabu]. Likewise, d and ɡ are always written [d ɡ], never [ð ɣ].

Source: Cintra 1971 p. 88; Álvarez Pérez 2014 pp. 35-37.

### rhotics

Strong r (word-initial ⟨r⟩, ⟨rr⟩, and ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s) is the alveolar trill [r]: rato [ˈratu], carro [ˈkaru], honra [ˈõrɐ]. Single intervocalic ⟨r⟩ and coda ⟨r⟩ are the tap [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], mar [ˈmaɾ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2, which gives [r] as the traditional variant beside the urban uvular [ʁ]. The trill is chosen as the conservative northern-coastal value; this departs from the Lisbon-based standard (and likely from the pt-PT base the spec inherits), which favours [ʁ].

### lateral

Onset /l/ is [l]: lado [ˈladu]. Coda /l/ (word-final or before a consonant) is velarized [ɫ]: sal [ˈs̺aɫ], alto [ˈaɫtu]. Coda [ɫ] is kept before a following vowel. The retroflex [ɭ] reported locally is not written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 (velarized coda lateral); the retroflex variant is set aside per the spec note (not in Cintra 1971 or Álvarez Pérez 2014).

### unstressed vowels

Unstressed ⟨a⟩ is [ɐ], unstressed ⟨e⟩ is [ɨ], and unstressed ⟨o⟩ is [u]: pequeno [pɨˈkenu], morar [muˈɾaɾ]. Word-initial unstressed ⟨e⟩ before a coda s is [ɨ]: estar [ɨʃˈtaɾ]. Word-initial unstressed ⟨e⟩ alone is [i]: ermida [iɾˈmidɐ]. Final -e is always written [ɨ], never deleted. Stressed ⟨e⟩ before a palatal stays [e], not Lisbon [ɐ]: telha [ˈteʎɐ]. Stressed ⟨a⟩ before a nasal consonant is [ɐ]: cama [ˈkɐmɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 (vowel reduction); Cintra 1971 and Cunha & Cintra 1984 for [e] before palatals outside Lisbon.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
