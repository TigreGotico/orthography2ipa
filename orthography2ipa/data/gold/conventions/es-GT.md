# Transcription conventions: Guatemalan Spanish (es-GT)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ immediately before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, including stressed monosyllables (ˈsol, ˈdos, ˈmas 'más', ˈfue). Unstressed function words carry no mark (see function_words). Placement follows the spelling. A written accent marks the stressed vowel (kaˈfe, ˈarbol, ˈmusika, paˈis). With no written accent, a word ending in a vowel, n or s takes stress on the penultimate syllable (ˈkasa, ˈkomen, ˈlibɾos). Any other word takes final stress (aˈmoɾ, sjuˈdad, reˈloj). Final y counts as a consonant, so esˈtoi̯, ˈrei̯ and uˈɾuɡwai̯ take final stress. Words with enclitics written as one word keep the verb's stress (ˈdamelo, diˈɣamelo is wrong: write ˈdiɡamelo, komiˈendolo is wrong: write komjenˈdolo). Adverbs in -mente keep two stresses: ˌ on the stressed syllable of the adjective base and ˈ on men (ˌrapidaˈmente, ˌfasilˈmente). Spanish has no phonemic vowel length, so long vowels are never written. That includes the vowel lengthening attributed to Mayan-substrate bilingual speech in the spec note, which the standard description does not include. There are no geminates. Double letters are written as two segments only where two phonemes occur (innato inˈnato, akˈsjon 'acción'), and ll and rr are single phonemes (ʝ, r). Case endings do not apply: Spanish has no morphological case.

Source: Hualde 2005, The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on word stress (stress rules, -mente compounds).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ mark: definite articles (el la los las lo); all prepositions except según (a de en con por para sin sobre entre hasta desde hacia); conjunctions (y o u e ni que si pero sino porque aunque como 'as/like'); object and reflexive clitics (me te se lo la los las le les nos); prenominal possessives (mi tu su mis tus sus); and relative/subordinating que quien donde cuando como without a written accent. Examples: el ˈpeɾo, de la ˈkasa, me lo ˈdio, mi ˈmadɾe, ˈkɾeo ke ˈbjene. Stressed, with the ˈ mark: indefinite articles (ˈun ˈuna ˈunos); subject and prepositional pronouns (ˈʝo ˈtu ˈel ˈeʝa noˈsotɾos ˈmi 'mí' ˈti ˈsi 'sí'); según (seˈɡun); nuestro and vuestro (ˈnwestɾo); demonstratives (ˈeste ˈese ˈakel); interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent (ˈke ˈkjen ˈdonde ˈkomo ˈkwando); numerals; muy (ˈmui̯); tan (ˈtan); and every verb form, auxiliaries included (ˈa beˈnido, ˈestaba koˈmjendo, ˈboi̯ a saˈliɾ).

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on word stress, section on stressed vs. unstressed words (following the Navarro Tomás / Quilis classification).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written in the same form at a comma, at the end of a sentence and medially, and final vowels are never dropped or altered (ˈkasa, ˈtodo, ˈfue in any position).

Source: Hualde 2005 (no pause-conditioned morphophonology in Spanish).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form. Do not transcribe any cross-word process. Resyllabification (los otros, phonetically [lo.ˈso.tɾos]) is not shown: write los ˈotɾos. Vowel sinalefa and fusion are not shown, so both vowels stay (la aˈmiɡa, de ˈeʝos, ˈkasa ˈɡɾande). Nasal place assimilation is not shown, so written n and m stay n and m (un ˈbeso, en ˈkasa, and within words ˈtenɡo, enˈbio). Voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants is not shown (ˈmismo, los ˈdos). Word-final r before a vowel is ɾ (seɾ aˈmiɡos). Coda /s/ is always s, never h or zero, word-finally and before vowels (los ˈniɲos, ˈmas ˈaltos), in line with the spec note and the conservative Guatemalan norm. The conjunction y is i before consonants and before vowels alike (pan i ˈaɡwa, ˈtu i ˈel).

Source: Hualde 2005 (resyllabification, sinalefa, nasal assimilation, described there as phonetic); Lipski 1994, Guatemala chapter (retention of syllable-final /s/).

## Glides and diphthongs

Follow Hualde's broad notation. Rising diphthongs use the onglides j and w (ˈbjen, ˈbweno, ˈtjene, kwiˈdado, sjuˈdad). Falling diphthongs use the offglides i̯ and u̯ (ˈai̯ɾe, ˈau̯to, ˈrei̯, ˈmui̯, ˈboi̯, ˈdeu̯da). Triphthongs combine both (ˈbwei̯, uˈɾuɡwai̯). An unaccented i or u next to another vowel is a glide. An accented high vowel forms a hiatus (ˈdia, paˈis, ˈrio, baˈul). Lexical hiatus words without an accent are written with a glide, following the spelling (ˈkljente, ˈpjano). Between vowels, y and ll are the consonant ʝ (ˈmaʝo, ˈkaʝe). Initial hie- is ʝe (ˈʝelo) and initial hue- is we (ˈweso). The diaeresis spelling gü is ɡw (ˈpinɡwino, ˈbeɡwensa is wrong: write berˈɡwensa).

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on vowels and glides (j w vs. i̯ u̯ convention; hie-/hue-; hiatus).

## Other choices

### seseo

Write c before e/i and z as s. There is no θ (ˈsjelo, ˈsapato, ˈkasa 'caza').

Source: Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 (general American seseo); Lipski 1994, Guatemala chapter.

### yeísmo and palatal

Write ll and consonantal y as ʝ in all positions (ˈʝamo, ˈkaʝe, ˈʝo). The weakening or loss of intervocalic /ʝ/ next to front vowels that Lipski reports for Guatemala (ɡaˈina for gallina) is not transcribed. Write ɡaˈʝina.

Source: Hualde 2005 (ʝ symbol); Lipski 1994, Guatemala chapter (weak /ʝ/, not encoded).

### velar fricative

Write j, and g before e/i, as x, per the spec note (ˈxente, ˈoxo, ˈmexiko is wrong: write ˈmexiko only for México, see the x-letter topic). Lipski reports a weak, sometimes [h]-like realisation in Central America; it is not transcribed.

Source: Spec note; Lipski 1994, Guatemala chapter.

### rhotics

Write r for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l or s (ˈpero 'perro', ˈrosa, onˈrado). Write ɾ elsewhere, including codas (ˈpeɾo, ˈmaɾ, ˈpwerta is wrong: write ˈpweɾta). The assibilated Guatemalan/Costa Rican realisation of /r/ and of final /ɾ/ is not transcribed.

Source: Hualde 2005 (r/ɾ distribution, coda neutralisation written ɾ); Lipski 1994 and Lipski 2011 zone Guatemala–Yucatán–Costa Rica (assibilation, not encoded).

### voiced stops

Write b, d and ɡ for b/v, d and g (hard) in all positions. Do not write the spirants β ð ɣ (ˈlobo, ˈkada, ˈaɡua is wrong: write ˈaɡwa, ˈdesde, ˈbaka 'vaca'). Final d is written (sjuˈdad, uˈsted).

Source: Broad phonemic practice in Hualde 2005; Lipski 1994 notes Central American occlusive realisations after consonants, which is another reason not to encode spirantisation.

### word-final n

Write n. The Central American velarisation to ŋ is not transcribed (ˈpan, kanˈsjon).

Source: Lipski 1994, Central America chapters (velar /n/, not encoded).

### letter x and Mayan loans

Write x as ks in general words (ekˈsamen, ˈtaksi). Write it as x in México, mexicano and Oaxaca (ˈmexiko). Write it as ʃ in Mayan-origin names and words (ˈʃela, ˈmiʃko). In Mayan-origin words, tz is ts (ketsalteˈnanɡo). The glottalisation in the spec note is not written, apostrophes in Mayan spellings split words per the tool, and no ʔ is written.

Source: Lipski 1994, Guatemala chapter (Mayan loans); spec note (glottalisation, not part of the standard description).

### other spellings

Write ch as tʃ (ˈtʃiko), ñ as ɲ (ˈniɲo), qu and hard c as k (ˈkeso, ˈkasa), and gu before e/i as ɡ (ˈɡera). Letter h is silent (ˈoɾa). Write the five vowels a e i o u with no reduction.

Source: Hualde 2005.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
