# Transcription conventions: Pacific Colombian Spanish (es-CO-x-pacifico)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable, taking the largest legal Spanish onset (pl, bl, tɾ, dɾ, kɾ, ɡɾ and so on). Stress is placed from the standard spelling. A written accent mark gives the stressed syllable. With no accent mark, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the penult, and any other word on the final syllable. Apply this rule to the spelling even when final -s and -n surface as [h] and [ŋ]: hablan [ˈablaŋ], casas [ˈkasah], hablar [aˈblaɾ], canción [kanˈsjoŋ], árbol [ˈaɾbol], teléfono [teˈlefono]. Stressed monosyllables take ˈ: sol [ˈsol], mí [ˈmi]. Unstressed function words take no mark (see function_words). Adverbs in -mente keep the stress of the adjective as ˈ and add ˌ on -men-: rápidamente [ˈrapidaˌmente]. Spanish has no phonemic vowel length, no geminates and no case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply. Double rr is the single trill [r]: perro [ˈpero].

Source: Quilis, A. (1993) Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, chapter on accent; Hualde, J. I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish, CUP, ch. 12 (stress, -mente adverbs). The Pacific variety does not differ from general Spanish here (Lipski 1994, Colombia chapter).

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and written without ˈ: definite articles el la los las lo; prepositions a ante bajo con contra de desde en entre hacia hasta para por sin sobre tras (según is stressed); conjunctions y e o u ni pero mas que si porque aunque, and como or cuando used as conjunctions; relatives que quien donde cual when they carry no accent mark; object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos les los las; prenominal possessives mi tu su nuestro and the others; and the titles don and doña before a name. Everything else is stressed and takes ˈ. This includes the indefinite articles un una unos unas; subject and prepositional pronouns yo tú él ella usted nosotros mí ti sí; demonstratives; interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent mark; numerals; no; all verbs, including the auxiliaries haber, ser and estar. Examples: la casa [la ˈkasa], de mi padre [de mi ˈpadɾe], se lo dijo [se lo ˈdiho], un hombre [ˈuŋ ˈombɾe], ha comido [ˈa koˈmido], no sé [ˈno ˈse].

Source: Quilis, A. (1993) Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, list of accented and unaccented words; Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1977) Manual de pronunciación española, ch. on the accent of words in the phrase; Hualde (2005) ch. 12.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply in the Arabic sense: Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word is written the same at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside a phrase. The coda rules below (final -s → [h], final -n → [ŋ], final -d → Ø) apply in every position, not only before a pause. Examples: ¿Vienes? [ˈbjeneh], son dos [ˈsoŋ ˈdoh], la verdad [la beɾˈda].

Source: Lipski, J. M. (1994) Latin American Spanish, Colombia chapter (Pacific coast: final /s/ and /n/ weakened in all positions, including prevocalically and prepausally).

## Connected speech

Each word keeps its own segments and is written separately. Resyllabification, synalepha (vowel merger across words) and cross-word nasal place assimilation are not written. Final -s stays [h] before a vowel: los amigos [loh aˈmiɡoh]. Final -n stays [ŋ] before vowels and consonants alike: en el agua [eŋ el ˈaɡwa], un poco [ˈuŋ ˈpoko], con Pedro [koŋ ˈpedɾo]. Vowels meeting at a word boundary are all kept: la escuela [la ehˈkwela], de usted [de uhˈte]. Inside a word, a nasal before a consonant is homorganic: campo [ˈkampo], tango [ˈtaŋɡo], enfermo [eɱˈfeɾmo]. No other cross-word process is written.

Source: Lipski (1994) Colombia chapter, on Pacific velar /n/ and aspirated /s/ before vowels and consonants. Hualde (2005) chs. 9 and 10 for word-internal nasal assimilation.

## Glides and diphthongs

An unstressed i or u next to another vowel in the same syllable is written as the consonantal glide j or w, never as i̯ or u̯. Rising diphthongs: bien [ˈbjeŋ], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [sjuˈda]. Falling diphthongs: aire [ˈajɾe], auto [ˈawto], hoy [ˈoj], muy [ˈmuj], rey [ˈrej]. Orthographic y or ll in a syllable onset is the obstruent [ʝ], not j: calle [ˈkaʝe], yo [ˈʝo], ayer [aˈʝeɾ]. Word-initial hie- is [ʝe]: hielo [ˈʝelo]. Word-initial hue- is [we]: huevo [ˈwebo]. The conjunction y is [i] in every context. An accented í or ú forms hiatus and stays a vowel: día [ˈdia], país [paˈih], río [ˈrio]. The silent u in gue, gui, que and qui is not written: guerra [ˈɡera], quien [ˈkjeŋ]. Written ü is [w]: pingüino [piŋˈɡwino].

Source: Hualde (2005) chs. 4 and 9 (glides and the ʝ–j contrast); Quilis (1993) chapter on diphthongs. The ʝ follows the specification's yeísmo note (Lipski 1994).

## Other choices

### Seseo and yeísmo

c before e or i, z, and s are all [s]: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato], casa [ˈkasa]. ll and y in an onset are both [ʝ]: llave [ˈʝabe], haya [ˈaʝa]. h is silent: hombre [ˈombɾe].

Source: Specification note (pan-Latin-American baseline), Lipski (1994), Lipski (2011) pp. 1-26.

### Coda /s/

Every syllable-final /s/ is written [h], both inside the word and at its end: este [ˈehte], mismo [ˈmihmo], las casas [lah ˈkasah]. Coda x before a consonant is [h]: extraño [ehˈtɾaɲo]. Intervocalic x is [ks]: taxi [ˈtaksi]. Total loss of /s/ is frequent but variable, so it is not written. This goes beyond the stub note, which models only seseo and yeísmo; published descriptions of the Pacific coast report aspiration or loss.

Source: Lipski (1994) Colombia chapter, Pacific coastal zone; Montes Giraldo, J. J. (1982) 'El español de Colombia: propuesta de clasificación dialectal', Thesaurus 37.

### Word-final /n/

Word-final n is written [ŋ] in all contexts: pan [ˈpaŋ], también [tamˈbjeŋ], en eso [eŋ ˈeso]. This departs from the stub note, which does not model it; it follows the published descriptions.

Source: Lipski (1994) Colombia chapter, Pacific coast velarization of /n/.

### Velar fricative

j, and g before e or i, are written [h], the Colombian glottal realization: ojo [ˈoho], gente [ˈhente], México [ˈmehiko].

Source: Lipski (1994) Colombia chapter; Montes Giraldo (1982).

### Voiced stops

b and v are both [b]; d is [d]; g is [ɡ]. Write them as stops in all positions, never as spirant [β ð ɣ]: lobo [ˈlobo], vida [ˈbida], lago [ˈlaɡo]. This is a broad phonemic choice, and the Pacific coast also has frequent occlusive realizations. The variable Chocoan change of intervocalic /d/ to [ɾ] (todo as [ˈtoɾo]) is not written.

Source: Lipski (1994) Colombia chapter, on occlusive /b d g/ and /d/ > [ɾ] in the Pacific zone, which is treated as variable and so not transcribed.

### Word-final /d/

Word-final d is deleted: verdad [beɾˈda], usted [uhˈte], Madrid [maˈdɾi]. Word-internal d is kept: cansado [kanˈsado].

Source: Lipski (1994) Colombia chapter (general Latin American and Pacific loss of final /d/).

### Coda liquids

Coda r is written [ɾ] and coda l is written [l], with no neutralization and no deletion: hablar [aˈblaɾ], mar [ˈmaɾ], sol [ˈsol], puerta [ˈpweɾta]. The loss of infinitival -r reported for the Pacific coast is variable, so it is not written. The trill [r] is used word-initially, after n, l or s, and for rr: rosa [ˈrosa], honra [ˈonra], perro [ˈpero].

Source: Lipski (1994) Colombia chapter (Pacific /r/ weakening noted as variable); Hualde (2005) ch. 10 for tap and trill distribution.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
