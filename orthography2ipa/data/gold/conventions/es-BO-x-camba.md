# Transcription conventions: Camba (Lowland) Bolivian Spanish (es-BO-x-camba)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ placed before the stressed syllable, so that it comes before that syllable's whole onset: [ˈka.sa] is written [ˈkasa], and ciudad is [sjuˈða]. Syllable dots are not written.

Where the stress falls follows standard Spanish orthography. A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel: [ˈaɾβol], [kanˈsjoŋ]. Without an accent, a word ending in a vowel, n or s is stressed on the penult: [ˈkasa], [ˈkantaŋ]. Any other word is stressed on the final syllable: [paˈpel], [koˈmeɾ].

Voseo forms carry final stress as written, and this is common in Camba speech: [teˈneh] tenés, [beˈni] vení.

Every stressed word is marked, including monosyllables: [ˈsol], [ˈbjeŋ]. Unstressed function words get no mark (see function_words).

Words in -mente get ˌ on the adjective's stressed syllable and ˈ on -mente: [ˌrapiðaˈmente].

Enclitic pronouns written joined to the verb do not move the stress: [ˈdamelo] dámelo, [deˈsiɾle] decirle.

Secondary stress is otherwise not marked. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply.

Source: Hualde 2005, ch. 13 (word stress and the orthographic accent rules). Lipski 1994, ch. on Bolivia, for voseo in the eastern lowlands.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following words are unstressed and carry no ˈ:
- definite articles: el, la, los, las, lo [el la loh lah lo];
- all simple prepositions except según: a, de, en, con, por, para, sin, sobre, hasta, desde [a de eŋ kom poɾ paɾa];
- conjunctions: y, e, o, u, ni, que, pero, si, porque, como (as a conjunction) [i e o ni ke peɾo si];
- object and reflexive clitics: me, te, se, lo, la, le, nos, les, los, las;
- preposed possessives: mi, tu, su, mis, tus, sus [mi tu su];
- relative que, quien, donde, cuando, como (without written accent).

The following words are stressed and carry ˈ:
- indefinite articles un, una, unos [ˈuŋ ˈuna];
- subject and prepositional pronouns yo, vos, él, ella, usted, nosotros, mí, ti [ˈʝo ˈboh ˈel];
- demonstratives: [ˈehte];
- tonic possessives mío, suyo;
- accented interrogatives and exclamatives qué, cómo, dónde, cuándo, quién [ˈke ˈkomo];
- numerals, según, and negative no [ˈno];
- all verb forms, including the auxiliaries haber, ser and estar: [ˈa ˈβeniðo] ha venido, [ˈehta ˈaki] está aquí.

Source: Hualde 2005, ch. 13 (§ on stressed and unstressed words). This follows the standard Navarro Tomás and Quilis classification that Hualde adopts.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Spanish has no pausal forms, final short vowels, case endings or tanwīn. Words before a pause or at the end of a sentence follow the word-final rules given under connected_speech and other: final /s/ becomes [h], final /n/ becomes [ŋ], and final /d/ is dropped.

Source: Hualde 2005 (no pausal morphology in Spanish).

## Connected speech

Each word stays in its own place, and only its edge segments change according to the neighbouring word.

(1) Word-final /s/:
- [h] before a consonant-initial word and before a pause: [loh ˈperoh] los perros;
- [s] before a vowel-initial word: [los aˈmiɣoh] los amigos.
The resyllabification itself is not marked.

(2) Word-final /n/:
- [m] before p, b, m: [um ˈpeso];
- [n] before dental, alveolar and palatal consonants: [un ˈdia];
- [ŋ] before velars, [h], a vowel or a pause: [uŋ ˈkaso], [uŋ ˈaɲo], [ˈbjeŋ].

(3) Voiced stops /b d ɡ/ are written [b d ɡ] after a pause or a nasal, and /d/ also after [l]. Everywhere else they are written [β ð ɣ], including across word boundaries: [la ˈβaka] la vaca against [um ˈbaso] un vaso; [loh ˈðeðoh] los dedos.

(4) Word-initial r is a trill [r] in every position: [la ˈrosa].

(5) Vowel contact across words (synalepha) is not written. No vowel is elided and no glide is created: [la iˈɣlesja], [de ˈeso], [mi ˈamiɣo].

Source: Hualde 2005: chs. on obstruents (spirantization), nasals (place assimilation), rhotics, and vowel sequences across words. Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (eastern lowlands): /s/ aspiration preconsonantally and prepausally with prevocalic retention, and velarization of final /n/. Note: the specification's stub models only seseo and yeísmo; these Camba features are added from the published descriptions.

## Glides and diphthongs

Prenuclear glides are written [j w]: [ˈbjeŋ] bien, [ˈkwando] cuando, [sjuˈða] ciudad, [ˈfweɣo] fuego.

Postnuclear glides are written [i̯ u̯]: [ˈai̯] hay, [ˈrei̯] rey, [ˈau̯to] auto, [ˈbei̯nte] veinte.

The palatal consonant written y, ll, or word-initial hi + vowel is [ʝ], not a glide: [ˈʝa] ya, [ˈkaʝe] calle, [ˈʝelo] hielo. Word-initial hu + vowel is [w]: [ˈweso] hueso.

Sequences of non-high vowels stay in hiatus as two vowels: [teˈatɾo], [ˈpoeta]. An accented í or ú is a syllable nucleus, not a glide: [ˈdia], [ˈrio].

Source: Hualde 2005, ch. 9 (diphthongs: on-glides [j w] against off-glides [i̯ u̯]) and the chapter on palatals ([ʝ]).

## Other choices

### seseo and yeísmo

The letters c before e/i, z, and s are all written [s]: [ˈsjelo] cielo, [ˈkasa] caza and casa. Both ll and y are [ʝ], so there is no /ʎ/: [ˈʝama] llama. Unlike the Bolivian highlands, Camba Spanish does not keep /ʎ/.

Source: Specification note. Lipski 1994 and Lipski 2011 (Bolivia: /ʎ/ is retained in the highlands but lost in the eastern lowlands).

### /x/ (j, g before e/i)

Written as glottal [h]: [ˈhoβeŋ] joven, [ˈhente] gente, [ˈmehiko] México.

Source: Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (eastern lowland /x/ is a weak aspirate). The specification's notes are silent on this.

### /d/ weakening

Word-final /d/ is dropped: [beɾˈða] verdad, [uhˈte] usted. Intervocalic /d/, including in the -ado ending, is kept as [ð]: [kanˈsaðo]. This conservative choice is applied uniformly, even though casual elision is reported.

Source: Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (eastern lowlands). Hualde 2005 (spirantization).

### rhotics

Write the tap [ɾ] for single intervocalic or syllable-final r: [ˈpeɾo], [koˈmeɾ]. Write the trill [r] for rr, word-initial r, and r after n, l or s: [ˈpero], [onˈrado]. No assibilated rhotic is written: that is a highland feature, absent in Camba.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on rhotics. Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter (assibilation is restricted to the highlands).

### word-internal codas

Word-internal coda /s/ is [h]: [ˈehte] este, [ˈmihmo] mismo.

Word-internal coda /n/ assimilates in place:
- [m] before p, b, m: [ˈkampo];
- [ŋ] before k, ɡ, h: [ˈteŋɡo], [ˈaŋhel];
- [n] elsewhere, including before f: [enˈfeɾmo].

The letters ch are [tʃ] and ñ is [ɲ].

Source: Lipski 1994, Bolivia chapter. Hualde 2005, chapter on nasals.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
