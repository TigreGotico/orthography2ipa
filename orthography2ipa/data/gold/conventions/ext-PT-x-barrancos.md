# Transcription conventions: Barranquenho (ext-PT-x-barrancos)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable on every polysyllabic word. Divide syllables by onset maximisation: [ˈkazɐ], [ˈpɔɾtɐ], [ˈlibɾi]. Do not mark monosyllables: [tẽ], [bõ], [kẽ]. Do not write secondary stress. Placement follows Portuguese-type lexical stress as the Barranquenho orthography shows it. (1) A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed vowel: [kɐ̃ˈta] cantá, [sɨˈɲo] senhô, [bɾɐˈzi] Brasí, [ɐbɨˈgɐ̃w̃] abegãu. (2) A word ending in an unaccented vowel ⟨a e i o u⟩, or in ⟨-am -em -en -ens⟩, is stressed on the penult: [ˈpɐ̃tu] pãtu, [pɨˈkenu] pequenu, [ˈtẽpu] tempu. (3) A word ending in a nasal diphthong ⟨-ão -ãu -âu -õi -ôi -ãi⟩ is stressed on the final syllable: [ɐbitɐˈsõj] abitaçõi. (4) Portuguese-spelled input: a final ⟨r⟩, ⟨l⟩ or ⟨z⟩ draws stress to the last syllable even though the consonant is deleted (cantar [kɐ̃ˈta], Natal [nɐˈta]). Plural or final ⟨s⟩ does not move stress: casas [ˈkazɐ]. (5) Lexical exception: aqui/aquí is oxytone, [ɐˈki]. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply. The lect has no vowel length and no geminates; ⟨rr⟩ is a single trill [r]. It has no case endings.

Source: Gramática Básica de Barranquenho (Gonçalves, Navas & Correia 2025), stress rules. Convenção Ortográfica do Barranquenho (2025), accentuation and final-consonant sections pp. 26–32. Navas Sánchez-Élez 2011, p. 52 for aqui.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed proclitics or enclitics. Write them without ˈ and with reduced vowels.
- Definite articles: u/o [u], a [ɐ]. The plural article loses its final -s: us/os [u], as [ɐ].
- Monosyllabic prepositions and their contractions: de [dɨ], du [du], da [dɐ], em/en [ẽ], nu [nu], na [nɐ], cum [kũ], pur [puɾ], pa [pɐ], a [ɐ]. Crasis à [a].
- Conjunctions and complementisers: i/e 'and' [i], que [kɨ], se [sɨ], mah/mas [mɐ].
- Object and reflexive clitic pronouns: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [sɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], nu/nos [nu], u/a.
A hyphenated enclitic is its own word and stays unstressed: dá-me → [da] [mɨ].
The following keep their lexical stress and full vowels, and take ˈ when polysyllabic:
- Subject and tonic pronouns: eu [ew], ele [ˈelɨ], nós [nɔ].
- Demonstratives: este [ˈehtɨ].
- Possessives.
- Polysyllabic prepositions and conjunctions: sobre [ˈsobɾɨ], entre [ˈẽtɾɨ], porque [ˈpuɾkɨ].
- Adverbs.
- All verbs, including auxiliaries: está [ɨhˈta], tinha [ˈtiɲɐ], é [ɛ].
The monosyllables mal, tal, cual are full words: [mal], [tal], [kwal].

Source: Standard European-Portuguese clitic/proclitic treatment inherited by Barranquenho (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, The Phonology of Portuguese, ch. 7). Barranquenho forms per Convenção 2025 §2.6 (pur, mal, tal, cual) and Gramática 2025.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Barranquenho has no distinct pausal forms, no case endings and no nunation. Every word is written in the same form at a sentence end, at a comma and sentence-internally. Final unstressed vowels are never dropped or devoiced in the transcription: [ˈnojtɨ], [ˈkazɐ], [ˈaltu]. Word-final /s r l/ deletion is a lexical, word-level fact, not a pausal one, so it applies in every position.

Source: Convenção Ortográfica do Barranquenho 2025, pp. 31–32 (final -s, -r, -l not written in any position). Clements, Amaral & Luís 2008.

## Connected speech

Write every word in its citation form. No cross-word process is written.
- No liaison or resyllabification of a deleted final consonant before a vowel: os otros → [u] [ˈotɾu], not *[uˈzotɾu]; cantar alto → [kɐ̃ˈta] [ˈaltu].
- No EP sandhi [z] or [ʒ].
- No vowel elision, crasis or glide formation between words: de ela → [dɨ] [ˈɛlɐ].
- No nasal place assimilation or coda voicing across the boundary.
Word-internal processes are always written, because they belong to the word itself:
- Coda /s/ → [h]: mehmu [ˈmehmu], Lihboa [lihˈboɐ], bihtu [ˈbihtu]. This applies before voiced consonants too, with [h], never [ɦ].
- Coda nasal absorbed into a nasal vowel: campu [ˈkɐ̃pu].

Source: Navas Sánchez-Élez 2011 and Clements, Amaral & Luís 2008 for coda aspiration and final deletion. Convenção 2025, pp. 26, 28–31 for the orthographic encoding of these as word-level forms.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vowels with [j] and [w], never i̯ u̯. Oral falling diphthongs: ⟨ei⟩ [ej] feira [ˈfejɾɐ]; ⟨oi⟩ [oj] noite [ˈnojtɨ]; ⟨eu⟩ [ew] euru [ˈewɾu]; ⟨au⟩ [aw]; ⟨éi⟩ [ɛj]; ⟨ói⟩ [ɔj] herói [eˈɾɔj]; ⟨ui⟩ [uj]. ⟨ou⟩ is the monophthong [o]: poucu [ˈpoku]. Nasal diphthongs: ⟨ão ãu âu⟩ [ɐ̃w̃]; ⟨ãi âi ãe⟩ [ɐ̃j]; ⟨õi ôi õe⟩ [õj]. Only the back nasal offglide carries the tilde. ⟨em en⟩ is a plain nasal vowel, never a diphthong: tempu [ˈtẽpu], quen [kẽ]. Rising diphthongs:
- Write ⟨qu gu⟩ + ⟨a o⟩ with [w]: quatru [ˈkwatɾu], água [ˈagwɐ].
- Write unstressed ⟨i u⟩ after the stressed syllable and before a vowel as a glide: históriu [ihˈtɔɾju].
- Elsewhere a written ⟨i u⟩ next to a vowel is a separate syllable.

Source: Convenção Ortográfica do Barranquenho 2025, pp. 26–27 (nasal diphthongs, ⟨ou⟩), following southern European-Portuguese diphthong analysis (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2) for glide notation.

## Other choices

### unstressed vowels

Use the European-Portuguese reduction pattern, with these values:
- Unstressed ⟨a⟩ is [ɐ]: cama [ˈkamɐ].
- Unstressed ⟨e⟩ is [ɨ]: pequenu [pɨˈkenu]. Word-initial unstressed ⟨e⟩ before a coda sibilant is also [ɨ], realised [ɨh]: está [ɨhˈta].
- Unstressed ⟨o⟩ and written ⟨u⟩ are [u]: altu [ˈaltu].
- Written final ⟨-i⟩ is [i]: libri [ˈlibɾi], sociedadi [susjɨˈdadi].
- Portuguese-spelled final ⟨-e⟩ stays [ɨ].
Stressed vowels are /a ɛ e i ɔ o u/. Navas reports less consistent raising, including final [i] for ⟨-e⟩ 'a veces'. That variant is not written.

Source: Convenção 2025, pp. 20–21. Spec decision against Navas Sánchez-Élez 2011, p. 52.

### word-final consonants

Delete word-final /s r l/ in every word, whatever the stress. Exceptions are the monosyllables mal [mal], tal [tal], cual [kwal] and pur [puɾ]. Examples: senhor/senhô [sɨˈɲo], Isabel [izɐˈbɛ], casas [ˈkazɐ]. Word-internal coda ⟨l⟩ is clear [l], not [ɫ]: altu [ˈaltu]. Word-internal coda ⟨r⟩ is [ɾ]: porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ].

Source: Convenção 2025, pp. 31–32 and §2.6 Nota. Gramática 2025, pp. 19–20. Clements, Amaral & Luís 2008.

### rhotics

Write the alveolar trill [r] word-initially, after a nasal or ⟨l s⟩, and for ⟨rr⟩: rio [ˈriu], terra [ˈtɛrɐ]. Never write the uvular [ʁ]. Write the tap [ɾ] intervocalically, in clusters and in internal coda: caru [ˈkaɾu], libri [ˈlibɾi].

Source: Convenção 2025. Navas Sánchez-Élez 2011.

### labials, stops and ⟨h⟩

Write betacism as [b] for both ⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩; there is no [v]. Write /b d g/ as [b d g] in all positions; spirant allophones [β ð ɣ] are not written in this broad transcription. Write ⟨h⟩ as [h] everywhere: mehmu [ˈmehmu]. The [x] that the Gramática notes in some Spanish loans is not written. ⟨lh⟩ is [ʎ], ⟨nh⟩ is [ɲ] (banhu [ˈbaɲu]), ⟨ch⟩ is [ʃ], and ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩ before e/i are [ʒ].

Source: Gramática 2025, pp. 17–18. Convenção 2025.

### sibilants and ⟨x⟩

Seseo applies: there is no [θ]. ⟨c⟩ before e/i, ⟨ç⟩, ⟨ss⟩ and initial ⟨s⟩ are [s]. Intervocalic ⟨s⟩ and ⟨z⟩ are [z]: casa [ˈkazɐ]. A coda sibilant is never [ʃ ʒ]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ] word-initially (xá [ʃa]) and [ks] in learned words (crucifixu [kɾusiˈfiksu]). Elsewhere ⟨x⟩ follows the Convenção listing, [z] or [s].

Source: Convenção 2025, pp. 28, 31. Gramática 2025, pp. 18–19.

### nasal vowels

A vowel before a coda ⟨m n⟩ is nasalised and the nasal consonant is not written. ⟨am an⟩ is [ɐ̃], ⟨em en⟩ is [ẽ], ⟨im in⟩ is [ĩ], ⟨om on⟩ is [õ], ⟨um un⟩ is [ũ]: campu [ˈkɐ̃pu], lindu [ˈlĩdu], bon [bõ], mundu [ˈmũdu]. An onset ⟨m n⟩ leaves the vowel oral: cama [ˈkamɐ].

Source: Convenção 2025, p. 26, §1.8.1.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
