# Transcription conventions: Upper Navarrese Basque (eu-x-nafarra-garaia)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark one primary stress (ˈ) per content word of two or more syllables, placed before the onset of the stressed syllable. Leave monosyllables and unstressed function words unmarked. Do not mark pitch accent, secondary stress or phrase-level prominence. Rule (Central/High Navarrese 'second-syllable' type): (1) Count syllables from the left edge of the whole inflected word. Case endings, articles and other suffixes are part of the word and count as syllables. (2) In words of three or more syllables, stress the second syllable: gizona [ɡiˈs̻ona], emakume [eˈmakume], lagunak [laˈɣunak], euskara [eu̯s̺ˈkaɾa]. (3) In words of exactly two syllables, stress the first syllable, because final stress is avoided: etxe [ˈetʃe], gizon [ˈɡis̻on]. (4) Stress moves when a suffix adds a syllable: etxe [ˈetʃe] but etxean [eˈtʃean]. (5) A diphthong counts as one syllable. (6) Lexically marked words, mostly recent Spanish loans, take initial stress whatever their length: [ˈmakina] 'machine'. (7) Absolutive plural article -ak/-ek forms are marked and take initial stress: gizonak 'the men' [ˈɡis̻onak], but ergative singular gizonak [ɡiˈs̻onak]. Use the plural reading only where the syntax makes it clear. Basque has no phonemic long vowels and no geminates, so neither ever affects stress. Write identical adjacent vowels across a morpheme boundary as two syllables, not as a long vowel.

Source: Hualde (2003) in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina, A Grammar of Basque, ch. 1 'Segmental phonology' and accentuation section (Central/Western second-syllable type and lexically marked initial-accent words); Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri (2010: 113, 123) for the Goizueta accentual system, simplified here to a stress mark without pitch-accent notation.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Leave unmarked (unstressed): (a) inflected auxiliaries (forms of izan, *edun, *edin, *ezan) when they follow a lexical verb or participle: etorri da [eˈtori ða], ikusi dut [iˈkus̺i ðut]; (b) the conjunctions eta/ta [eta]/[ta], edo [eðo], ere [eɾe] and baina [bai̯na]; (c) the particles ba, al and omen; (d) the complementiser and relative endings, which are suffixes on the auxiliary anyway. Mark as stressed: the negator ez [ˈes̻]; an auxiliary that follows ez or begins its clause, which takes the word stress rule; personal pronouns (ni, zu, gu, hura [ˈura]); demonstratives; all nouns, adjectives, adverbs and lexical verbs. Articles and postpositions are bound suffixes in Basque, not separate words, so they never stand alone. Apply this rule to a separate-word form only if the segmenter delivers one, for example after a hyphen.

Source: Hualde (2003) in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina, A Grammar of Basque, accentuation section (auxiliaries phrase with the preceding verb; focus/negation on ez); Saltarelli (1988), phonology chapter.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Basque has no pausal forms. Final vowels, case endings and articles are written the same at a comma, at sentence end and inside the sentence, and there is no nunation.

Source: Hualde (2003) in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina, A Grammar of Basque, ch. 1 (no pausal alternations described); Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri (2010).

## Connected speech

Write these processes inside the affected word only; never join words. (1) Sandhi devoicing: a word-initial /b d ɡ/ becomes [p t k] after a word ending in a sibilant or affricate: ez da [ˈes̻ ta], ez dut [ˈes̻ tut], ez gara [ˈes̻ kaɾa], ez banaiz [ˈes̻ panai̯s̻]. Write the sibilant of ez in full; do not apply the colloquial loss of /s̻/ before n or l. (2) Spirantisation across the boundary: a word-initial /b d ɡ/ is written [β ð ɣ] after a word ending in a vowel, glide or rhotic, inside the same phrase: etorri da [eˈtori ða], ura dago [ˈuɾa ðaɣo]. Write it as a stop [b d ɡ] at the start of the sentence, after a comma, after a nasal, and (for /d/) after /l/: ni naiz, gizon bat [ˈɡis̻om bat]. (3) Nasal place assimilation: word-final /n/ is written [m] before a labial and [ŋ] before a velar: gizon bat [ˈɡis̻om bat], egin genuen [eˈɣiŋ ɡenuen]. (4) No vowel elision, no liaison and no written resyllabification across word boundaries; vowels in hiatus stay as they are.

Source: Hualde (2003) in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina, A Grammar of Basque, ch. 1, sections on obstruent voicing assimilation/sandhi and nasal assimilation; Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri (2010: 116) for intervocalic [β ð ɣ].

## Glides and diphthongs

Write falling diphthongs with the non-syllabic diacritic: ai [ai̯], ei [ei̯], oi [oi̯], ui [ui̯], au [au̯], eu [eu̯]: baina [bai̯na], gaur [ɡau̯r], euskara [eu̯s̺ˈkaɾa]. Do not use j or w for these offglides. A prevocalic high vowel inside a word stays a full vowel in hiatus: ia [ia], etxean [eˈtʃean]. Use [j] only where a consonantal palatal approximant is needed, for example in Spanish loans with y. Orthographic j is not a glide (see 'other').

Source: Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri (2010: 120–121), vowels and diphthongs; Hualde (2003) in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina, A Grammar of Basque, ch. 1.

## Other choices

### sibilants and affricates

Keep the full three-way contrast. z = laminal [s̻], s = apical [s̺], x = [ʃ]; tz = [ts̻], ts = [ts̺], tx = [tʃ]. Examples: zu [ˈs̻u], su [ˈs̺u], etxe [ˈetʃe], hotz [ˈots̻], hots [ˈots̺]. Never merge these to plain [s].

Source: Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri (2010: 119), which agrees with the lect notes.

### h

Never write [h]. Orthographic h is silent: hura [ˈuɾa], hemen [ˈemen].

Source: Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri (2010: 113 fn. 1); Hualde (2018).

### orthographic j

Write j as the velar fricative [x]: jan [ˈxan], jende [ˈxende].

Source: Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri (2010) consonant inventory; Hualde (2003) in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina, ch. 1 (dialectal realisations of j; [x] in Gipuzkoan and High Navarrese).

### palatal lateral (yeísmo)

Write orthographic ll and il-derived palatal laterals as [ʎ]: [ˈoʎo] 'hen'. Do not write the delateralised [ʝ]/[j], which the notes describe as only incipient and variable. This conservative choice is deliberate.

Source: Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri (2010: 120).

### palatalisation after i

Write /n l/ as [ɲ ʎ] when they follow /i/ or an i̯ offglide and precede a vowel in the same word: mutila [muˈtiʎa], baina [ˈbaɲa], with the glide absorbed. Do not apply this across word boundaries.

Source: Hualde (2003) in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina, A Grammar of Basque, ch. 1, automatic palatalisation in central dialects.

### rhotics

Intervocalic r = tap [ɾ], rr = trill [r]: ura [ˈuɾa], urra [ˈura]. Word-final r and preconsonantal r are always written [r]: gaur [ɡau̯r], ur [ˈur].

Source: Hualde (2003) in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina, ch. 1; Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri (2010: 117).

### word-internal lenition

Inside words, write /b d ɡ/ as [β ð ɣ] after a vowel, glide or rhotic: alaba [aˈlaβa], lagun [ˈlaɣun]. Write them as stops after a nasal and word-initially after pause: ondo [ˈondo].

Source: Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri (2010: 116).

### vowel raising before the article

Write the stem-final mid vowel raised before the singular article -a: e+a → [ia], o+a → [ua]. Examples: etxea [eˈtʃia], basoa [baˈs̺ua]. Keep the two vowels as separate syllables.

Source: Hualde (2003) in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina, A Grammar of Basque, ch. 1, vowel assimilation in Gipuzkoan and High Navarrese. Varieties differ on this; it is applied uniformly here.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
