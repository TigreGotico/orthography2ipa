# Transcription conventions: Ansotano Aragonese (Ansó valley) (an-x-ansotano)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every lexical word of two or more syllables. Write no mark on monosyllables and no secondary stress. Stress is lexical and is read from the spelling by the Aragonese default rule. A word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the penult: 'casa' [ˈkasa], 'fuegos' [ˈfweɡos], 'cantan' [ˈkantan]. A word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the last syllable: 'animal' [aniˈmal]. A written accent overrides both rules: 'cantará' [kantaˈɾa], 'árbol' [ˈaɾbol]. Stress is placed on the spelled form before final-r deletion, so the syllable stays stressed after the r is lost: 'cantar' [kanˈta], 'muller' [muˈʎe], 'fer' [fe]. Proparoxytones are written only where the spelling marks them. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Aragonese has no vowel length, no geminates and no case endings.

Source: Default stress rule and accentuation as in Nagore Laín (1989) Gramática de la lengua aragonesa (Zaragoza: Mira), accentuation chapter, and the Academia de l'Aragonés orthography (2010). Ansó stress patterns as in Kuhn (1935) and Barcos (2007), via the Spanish-Wikipedia 'Aragonés ansotano' summary. Section and page numbers not verified.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and never take ˈ, even when they have two syllables. Articles: o, a, os, as, lo, la, los, las, un, una, unos, unas, so 'una' [una], 'o fillo' [o ˈfiʎo]. Prepositions: a, de, en, con, per, por, ta, enta, dende, entre, sin, so 'enta casa' [enta ˈkasa]. Conjunctions: e, y, u, que, si, pero, porque, como (as conjunction). Clitic pronouns: me, te, se, nos, vos, le, les, lo, la, los, las, li, lis, en/ne, bi/i. Relative que, and the monosyllabic auxiliary forms of haber and ser. The following are stressed and marked when polysyllabic: tonic pronouns (yo, tu, el, ella, nusatros [nuˈsatɾos]), demonstratives, tonic possessives, numerals, interrogatives, polysyllabic auxiliaries ('heba' [ˈeba]) and the negation no. As monosyllables these are unmarked under the stress rule.

Source: Ibero-Romance treatment of unstressed words after Navarro Tomás (1918) Manual de pronunciación española, chapter on 'palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas', applied to Aragonese. Aragonese clitic and article inventory after Nagore Laín (1989) and Alvar (1953) El dialecto aragonés. Ansó article and clitic forms after Barcos (2007), via es.wikipedia.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Ansotano has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written in the same form at a sentence end, at a comma and inside a phrase. Final vowels are never dropped or reduced in pause, and final-r deletion applies in every position: 'ir a cantar.' [i a kanˈta].

Source: Aragonese descriptions (Nagore Laín 1989; Alvar 1953) describe no pause-conditioned word forms. Final-r deletion in Ansó is positional, not pausal: Barcos (2007), via es.wikipedia 'Aragonés ansotano'.

## Connected speech

Apply only nasal place assimilation across word boundaries, and write it in the word that changes. A word-final n becomes [m] before a word beginning with p, b or m: 'en Pamplona' [em pamˈplona]. It becomes [ŋ] before k or ɡ: 'en casa' [eŋ ˈkasa]. Apply the same rule inside words: 'campo' [ˈkampo], 'banco' [ˈbaŋko]. Do not write any other process. Vowel sinalefa and elision are not written: each word keeps all its vowels, 'de a casa' [de a ˈkasa]. Linking and resyllabification are not marked, there is no liaison, and there are no tie bars. Final-r deletion is not undone before a vowel or an enclitic: 'fer-lo' [fe lo], 'cantar a' [kanˈta a]. Voicing of s before voiced consonants and spirantisation of b d ɡ are left out of the broad transcription: write s, b, d, ɡ, as in 'os dias' [os ˈdias].

Source: Nasal assimilation and the broad omission of approximant allophones follow the Ibero-Romance treatment of Navarro Tomás (1918) Manual de pronunciación española, §§ on nasal consonants, as applied to Aragonese by Nagore Laín (1989). Final-r deletion in Ansó after Barcos (2007), via es.wikipedia.

## Glides and diphthongs

In rising diphthongs, write a non-syllabic i or u before the nucleus as j or w. This covers the diphthongs from Ĕ and Ŏ: 'tierra' [ˈtjera], 'fuego' [ˈfweɡo], 'puen' [pwen], 'agua' [ˈaɡwa]. Spelled ⟨y⟩ before a vowel is also j: 'yo' [jo], 'ye' [je]. In falling diphthongs, write a non-syllabic i or u after the nucleus as i̯ or u̯: 'reino' [ˈrei̯no], 'causa' [ˈkau̯sa], 'muito' [ˈmui̯to]. A written accent on i or u marks hiatus, with no glide: 'dia' [ˈdia]. Unaccented i or u before or after another vowel is a glide.

Source: Diphthong inventory after Nagore Laín (1989) and Alvar (1953). Ansó diphthongisation of Ĕ/Ŏ to ie/ue after Várvaro (1991), Archivo de Filología Aragonesa XLVI-XLVII, p. 250. The j w versus i̯ u̯ split follows common Ibero-Romance broad IPA practice.

## Other choices

### word-final r

Word-final spelled ⟨r⟩ is not pronounced and is written as nothing, in infinitives and in nouns alike: 'cantar' [kanˈta], 'muller' [muˈʎe], 'calor' [kaˈlo]. Ansó does not keep final r, unlike neighbouring Fago and Cheso. Non-final r is kept: initial r and ⟨rr⟩ are [r] ('rio' [ˈrio], 'tierra' [ˈtjera]), r after n, l or s is [r], and other r is [ɾ] ('caro' [ˈkaɾo], 'parte' [ˈpaɾte]).

Source: Barcos (2007) El aragonés ansotano, via es.wikipedia 'Aragonés ansotano' ('la -r final no se pronuncia en Ansó pero sí en Fago'). Primary pages not verified.

### consonant symbols

Map the following spellings to the following symbols. ⟨z⟩ and ⟨c⟩ before e or i are [θ] (distinción): 'zaga' [ˈθaɡa]. ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ]: 'chen' [tʃen]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ]: 'baxo' [ˈbaʃo]. ⟨ll⟩ is [ʎ]: 'fillo' [ˈfiʎo]. ⟨ny⟩ and ⟨ñ⟩ are [ɲ]. ⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩ are both [b]. ⟨h⟩ is silent. ⟨f⟩ is kept as [f]: 'fer' [fe]. ⟨j⟩, which occurs only in Castilian loans, is [x]. ⟨s⟩ is written s.

Source: Western High-Aragonese profile after Kuhn (1935) and Alvar (1953). Spelling-to-sound mapping after the Academia de l'Aragonés orthography (2010) and Nagore Laín (1989).

### intervocalic d

Write intervocalic d wherever it is spelled, since Ansotano keeps it where Castilian loses it: 'odir' [oˈdi], 'cantau' [kanˈtau̯] versus 'cantado' [kanˈtado] as spelled. Do not restore or drop d against the spelling.

Source: Barcos (2007) and Kuhn (1935), via es.wikipedia 'Aragonés ansotano'.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
