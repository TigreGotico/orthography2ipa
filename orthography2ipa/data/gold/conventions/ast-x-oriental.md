# Transcription conventions: Eastern Asturian-Leonese (ast-x-oriental)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write one primary stress mark ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. There is no secondary stress. Stress is lexical and follows the spelling: a written accent (á é í ó ú) marks the stressed vowel. With no accent, a word ending in a vowel, ⟨-n⟩ or ⟨-s⟩ is stressed on the penult and any other word on the final syllable: casa /ˈkasa/, home /ˈome/, cantar /kanˈtaɾ/, verdá /beɾˈda/, árbol /ˈaɾbol/, nós /ˈnos/. Syllabify before applying the rule. Rising diphthongs count as one nucleus: tierra /ˈtjera/, fuente /ˈfwente/. Accented ⟨í ú⟩ next to a vowel is a separate syllable: país /paˈis/, tíu /ˈtiu/. A hyphenated enclitic is its own word and never moves the host's stress: da-y /ˈda i/. The lect has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so none of these is marked.

Source: Academia de la Llingua Asturiana, Normes ortográfiques (7th ed., 2012), chapter on accentuation. García Arias (2003) on Asturian word stress. The rules match Morala & Egido (2009) for Leonese.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ: definite articles el la lo los les /el la lo los les/; contractions del al pel col na nel /del al pel kol na nel/; unstressed object and reflexive clitics me te se lu la lo nos vos los les y /me te se lu la lo nos bos los les i/; prepositions a de en con por pa ente ensin dende hasta /a de en kon poɾ pa ente enˈsin/ (ensin keeps its lexical stress mark because it is disyllabic and contrastive in speech; all the others are unmarked); conjunctions y o que si pero porque nin /i o ke si peɾo poɾke nin/; relative que; prenominal possessives mio to so /mjo to so/; auxiliary forms of haber and ser in periphrases (he ha hai when auxiliary). Stressed, with ˈ: tonic pronouns yo tu él ella nós vós ellos /ˈʝo ˈtu ˈel ˈeʎa ˈnos ˈbos/; demonstratives esti esta eso aquel /ˈesti ˈesta ˈeso aˈkel/; interrogatives and exclamatives qué cuándo ónde /ˈke ˈkwando ˈonde/; numerals; adverbs; postnominal possessives; and all nouns, adjectives and lexical verbs, including existential hai /ˈaj/.

Source: Follows the Spanish/Ibero-Romance treatment of atonic words (Navarro Tomás, Manual de pronunciación española, §§ on palabras inacentuadas; Quilis 1993, ch. 9), which matches the clitic inventory in Academia de la Llingua Asturiana, Gramática de la llingua asturiana (3rd ed., 2001), chapters on articles and pronouns.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: the lect has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written in the same form at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside a phrase.

Source: Academia de la Llingua Asturiana, Gramática de la llingua asturiana (2001); Morala & Egido (2009).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form in its own place, with no linking marks. Do not write any of the following: resyllabification of a final consonant onto a following vowel, vowel synalepha or sinaloepha, nasal place assimilation (en casa stays /en ˈkasa/, not [eŋ]), voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants, or loss of final /d/ beyond what the spelling shows. The only elision written is the one the spelling marks with an apostrophe. Each apostrophe piece is its own word and is transcribed as written: d'agua → /d ˈaɡwa/, m'alcuerdo → /m alˈkweɾdo/, qu'él → /k ˈel/. An elided fragment never carries stress.

Source: Broad phonemic practice of the Academia de la Llingua Asturiana, Normes ortográfiques (2012), section on apostrophe and contractions. Allophonic sandhi is treated as phonetic detail, as in García Arias (2003), phonology chapters.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vowels as the consonant symbols j and w in every position, both rising and falling. Never use i̯ or u̯. Examples: tierra /ˈtjera/, bueno /ˈbweno/, güei /ˈɡwej/, hai /ˈaj/, reina /ˈrejna/, autu /ˈawtu/, ciudá /θjuˈda/. Diphthongs written ⟨ei ou⟩ are transcribed as spelled (/ej ow/) wherever they appear. The eastern lect has monophthongised the etymological ones, so the text normally writes queso /ˈkeso/, poco /ˈpoko/, not queisu, poucu. A high vowel bearing a written accent is syllabic and in hiatus: río /ˈrio/. Conjunction y and enclitic -y are syllabic /i/ when they stand alone or after a consonant. Keep ⟨y⟩ = /ʝ/ before a vowel (yerba /ˈʝeɾba/), which is distinct from the glide j.

Source: Morala & Egido (2009), p. 8, on the absence of ei/ou in eastern Leonese. The j/w notation follows the IPA phonemic convention used for Asturian in García Arias (2003).

## Other choices

### Final -o vs -u

Transcribe the final vowel exactly as the text spells it: ⟨-o⟩ /o/, ⟨-u⟩ /u/. Eastern Leonese texts mostly write -o: llobo /ˈʎobo/, poco /ˈpoko/. Words spelled with -u keep /u/: fechu /ˈfetʃu/. Do not raise or lower vowels against the spelling, and do not write metaphony.

Source: Propuesta de Norma Ortográfica para el Leonés (c. 2010), §2.4; Morala & Egido (2009), p. 8.

### ⟨ll⟩ and yeísmo

⟨ll⟩ is always /ʎ/ and ⟨y⟩ before a vowel is always /ʝ/. Do not write the incipient yeísta merger: llave /ˈʎabe/, fiyo /ˈfiʝo/, caballo /kaˈbaʎo/.

Source: Morala & Egido (2009). The variation with [ʝ] is noted in Cano González (2009) but is not transcribed.

### Obstruents and fricatives

⟨b v⟩ are /b/, ⟨d⟩ is /d/ and ⟨g⟩ is /ɡ/, written as stops in every position, with no [β ð ɣ]. ⟨c⟩ before e/i and ⟨z⟩ are /θ/ (distinction, no seseo): cielo /ˈθjelo/. ⟨x⟩ is /ʃ/: xente /ˈʃente/. ⟨ch⟩ is /tʃ/, including the -KT- outcome when the text spells it ch: noche /ˈnotʃe/, muchu /ˈmutʃu/. ⟨ñ⟩ is /ɲ/. ⟨j⟩ (Castilianisms) is /x/. Plain ⟨h⟩ is silent. ⟨ḥ⟩ or ⟨h.⟩, the eastern aspiration of Latin F-, is /h/: ḥacer /haˈθeɾ/. Write /h/ only where the spelling marks it.

Source: Academia de la Llingua Asturiana, Normes ortográfiques (2012), sections on the alphabet and on ⟨ḥ⟩. García Arias (2003), on the aspiration of F- in eastern Asturian.

### Rhotics and nasals

Write /r/ for word-initial ⟨r⟩, for ⟨rr⟩, and for ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s: rueda /ˈrweda/, tierra /ˈtjera/, honra /ˈonra/. Write /ɾ/ everywhere else, including coda and word-final position: cantar /kanˈtaɾ/. Nasals are written as spelled, with no assimilation: ⟨n⟩ /n/, ⟨m⟩ /m/.

Source: Academia de la Llingua Asturiana, Gramática de la llingua asturiana (2001), phonology chapter; García Arias (2003).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
