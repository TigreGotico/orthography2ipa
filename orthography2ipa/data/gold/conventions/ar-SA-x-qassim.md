# Transcription conventions: Qassimi Arabic (ar-SA-x-qassim)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write primary stress as ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable. Mark it on every content word of two or more syllables, and on no monosyllable. Count syllables on the form actually transcribed, after the elision and glottal-stop rules under 'other' have applied. Weights: CV is light. CVː and CVC are heavy. CVːC and CVCC are superheavy. A geminate closes its first syllable, so muˈdarris has a heavy penult; write geminates as doubled consonants, never with ː. A single word-final consonant does not count toward weight. The rule: (1) stress a final superheavy syllable: maˈtˤaːr, ʕaˈleːh. (2) Otherwise stress a heavy penult: maˈdiːnah, sajˈjaːrah, muˈdarris. (3) Otherwise stress the antepenult: ˈmadrasah, ˈkatabat. (4) A disyllable with two light syllables takes initial stress: ˈkatab. Unstressed long vowels stay long: sajˈjaːrah, not sajˈjarah. Case endings never take part, because the dialect has none (see pausal_forms). Clitics and suffixes count as part of the word: ˈbeːtah but beːˈteːn. Intonation is not transcribed: the boundary tones belong to the statement/question tags, not the IPA.

Source: Ingham 1994 (Najdi Arabic, phonology chapter on syllable structure and stress), which describes the Classical/Levantine-type weight-sensitive rule for Najdi; typology as in Watson 2002 ch. 4. Intonation: Aljamaan & Aljutaily 2026.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed and never take ˈ, even when they have two syllables: the article al-/l- (always inside its host word); the conjunctions w(a) and f(a); the prepositions b(i), l(i), fi/fiː, min, ʕala, ʕan, maʕ, ila; the relative illi; the particles ʔinn/ʔann/ʔin, ja (vocative) and gid/ɡad; the verbal negator ma and the prohibitive la; bound pronoun suffixes. Examples: fi lbeːt, ʕala ttˤaːwilah, illi ˈʃifta. These are stressed as content words, with the usual rule: independent pronouns (ˈʔana, ˈʔinta, ˈhumm), demonstratives (ˈhaːða, ˈhaːði), interrogatives (weːʃ, ˈwineːn), the negator muː, and auxiliary or modal verbs (ˈkaːn, jaˈbiː). A function word written as a separate word stays a separate word.

Source: Ingham 1994 (Najdi pronouns, particles and clitics); general Arabic clitic prosody as in Watson 2002 ch. 4.

## Pausal forms

Pause applies at sentence end (before . ? ! or end of text) and before a comma, semicolon or colon; nowhere else. The dialect has no case inflection, so short case vowels written on nouns (ُ َ ِ on the final consonant) are dropped everywhere, not only in pause: كتابٌ → ktaːb. Tanwīn ٌ/ٍ: dropped in pause and in most positions. The one exception is phrase-internal: when an indefinite noun is followed directly by an adjective or a relative clause and the text writes the tanwīn, write it as -in: beːtin kibiːr, but beːt in pause. Tanwīn ً: written as -an only on lexicalised adverbs, in every position (ˈʃukran, ˈʔabadan); elsewhere dropped. Tāʾ marbūṭa: -ah in pause (ˈmadrasah.), -a phrase-internally when unannexed (ˈmadrasa ˈkibiːrah), and -at in construct or before a suffix (madrasat ilˈbanaːt, madˈrasti). Short final vowels that belong to the stem or a suffix are kept in pause: ˈʔana, ˈhaːða, ˈʔinta.

Source: Ingham 1994 (Najdi: no case; -in nunation linking an indefinite noun to its modifier; feminine -ah/-at); feminine -ah as transcribed in Alhoody 2019:41 (/qaːrrah/).

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its own slot; nothing is joined across a space. Only one cross-word process is written: hamzat al-waṣl. After a vowel-final word, the article and other waṣl-initial words lose their initial vowel and take no ʔ: fi lbeːt, ʕala ʃʃams. After a consonant-final word, or after a pause, the article is al- with no ʔ: albeːt, aʃʃams. Sun-letter assimilation of the article to t d θ ð tˤ ðˤ s z sˤ ʃ r n l is word-internal and always written: aʃʃams, arraːdʒil, annaːs. True hamza (أ إ ؤ ئ) is written ʔ in every position, initial included: ˈʔakil. These are not written across word boundaries: nasal place assimilation, voicing assimilation, gemination of identical consonants, vowel coalescence, resyllabification, and linking marks (‿). Velar affrication is decided inside the word only. Elision and medial-ʔ loss are word-internal (see 'other').

Source: Ingham 1994 (Najdi article al-/l- and sun-letter assimilation); waṣl behaviour as general Arabic, Watson 2002 ch. 3 (syllable structure).

## Glides and diphthongs

Glides are consonants, written j and w; never use i̯ or u̯. The Classical diphthongs /aj aw/ are monophthongised to eː oː wherever they are tautosyllabic, in stems and before suffixes. This holds even when the vocalisation writes the sukūn, because the parent Najdi spec's retention of [aj aw] is a gap, not the lect's pronunciation: beːt, loːn, ʕaleːh, joːm, ˈweːʃ. Before a vowel or as a geminate, the glide stays consonantal: sajˈjaːrah, ˈʔawwal, ˈbajaːn. Long vowels are written iː uː aː eː oː. /aː/ is never raised or fronted (no imāla). The emphatic-adjacent backing [ɑ] is allophonic and not written: ðˤaːʕ, not ðˤɑːʕ.

Source: Alhoody 2019:42 (/bajt/→[beːt], /lawn/→[loːn]; 8-vowel system); Ingham 1994:15; Algethami 2023 (pan-Najdi eː oː, no imāla of aː).

## Other choices

### velar affrication

/k/ is written t͡s, and /ɡ/ (from qāf or gāf) d͡z, when adjacent to a high front vowel /i iː/, before or after it, in native words: ˈʔat͡sil 'food', briːd͡z 'teapot', the 2f.sg suffix -it͡s (ʕaleːt͡s). /k/ stays k before or after a aː; this follows the receding Qaṣīm profile, not the Northern Najdi t͡salb. /ɡ/ stays ɡ before e eː and all non-high-front vowels: ɡumar, not d͡zumar. This is narrower than the engine's /e eː/ trigger for ɡ, following the literature. No affrication when the vowel has been elided (ktaːb), or in MSA/loan words, which also keep q: qurˈʔaːn. Use tie bars.

Source: Al-Rojaie 2013:43; Alhoody 2019:42; Alshammari 2026:1335-1337 (ɡ restricted to high front vowels); Mahzari 2023:800-804.

### short-vowel elision

A short high vowel /i u/ in a word-initial open syllable that is followed by CV or CVː is deleted, giving an initial cluster: كِتاب → ktaːb, قُطَع → ɡtˤaʕ. Short /a/ is kept as vocalised. This departs from the note's statement that the engine does not apply elision; it follows the published pronunciation.

Source: Alhoody 2019:43 (/ktaːb/, /ɡtˤaʕ/, /ktabat/); Ingham 1994.

### medial glottal stop

A post-vocalic medial ʔ before a consonant is dropped and the preceding vowel lengthened: رأس → raːs, ذئب → ðiːb. Word-initial ʔ is kept, and so is ʔ in MSA/Qurʾānic borrowings. This applies the lexical fact the note leaves to a lexicon.

Source: Alhoody 2019:41.

### gahawa syndrome

In CaGC, where G is one of x ɣ ħ ʕ h, insert a after the guttural, as the parent spec does: ˈɡahawah, ˈnaxalah. Stress then follows the stress rule on the epenthesised form. The further elision to ɡhawah is not applied.

Source: Ingham 1994 (gahawa syndrome); inherited from the ar-SA-x-najd spec.

### consonant symbols

ق is ɡ, except q in MSA borrowings. ض and ظ are both ðˤ. ث is θ and ذ is ð. Emphatics are written tˤ sˤ ðˤ. ج is d͡ʒ. Loan letters: پ is b, ڤ is f, and چ is ʃ in English loans but t͡s in native words such as t͡saːn.

Source: Alhoody 2019:41, 62-66; Ingham 1994:13.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
