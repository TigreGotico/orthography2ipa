# Transcription conventions: Cape Verdean Portuguese (pt-CV)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: mar [ˈmaɾ], casa [ˈkaza], café [kaˈfɛ], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃pada]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Secondary stress is not marked. Stress is lexical and falls on one of the last three syllables. A written acute or circumflex accent fixes it: médico [ˈmɛdiku], você [voˈse]. Without an accent, words ending in -a, -e, -o (with or without -s), -am, -em or -ens are stressed on the penult: falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], jovens [ˈʒɔvẽj̃s]. All other unaccented words are stressed on the final syllable: falar [faˈlaɾ], papel [paˈpɛl], aqui [aˈki], comum [koˈmũ]. A tilde alone does not move stress: irmã [iɾˈmɐ̃], órgão [ˈɔɾɡɐ̃w̃]. Long vowels, geminates and case endings: not applicable. Portuguese has no vowel length and no case endings, and double letters are written as a single sound: carro [ˈkaru], passo [ˈpasu].

Source: Standard Portuguese stress rules as in Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), The Phonology of Portuguese, ch. 6 (stress), and Cunha & Cintra (1984), Nova Gramática do Português Contemporâneo, chapter on accentuation. Stress placement is the same in European and African Portuguese, so there is no CV-specific divergence.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, no ˈ mark, with vowels raised as in final unstressed position:
- definite articles: o [u], a [a], os [us], as [as]
- simple prepositions: de [di], em [ẽj̃], a [a], por [poɾ], para [paɾa], com [kõ], sem [sẽj̃]
- preposition+article contractions: do [du], da [da], no [nu], na [na], ao [aw], pelo [pelu], num [nũ]
- conjunctions: e [i], ou [ow], mas [mas], nem [nẽj̃], se [si], que [ki], porque [poɾki]
- relative que [ki]
- clitic object pronouns, whether proclitic or hyphen-separated enclitic: me [mi], te [ti], se [si], lhe [ʎi], lhes [ʎis], o [u], a [a], nos [nus], vos [vus]
Example: disse-lhe [ˈdisi ʎi].

Stressed, marked ˈ:
- indefinite articles: um [ˈũ], uma [ˈuma]
- subject and prepositional pronouns: eu [ˈew], tu [ˈtu], ele [ˈeli], ela [ˈɛla], nós [ˈnɔs], mim [ˈmĩ], você [voˈse]
- possessives, demonstratives, quantifiers and numerals
- interrogatives: quê [ˈke], quem [ˈkẽj̃], onde [ˈõdi]
- adverbs
- all verb forms, including auxiliaries and copulas: é [ˈɛ], foi [ˈfoj], tem [ˈtẽj̃], está [isˈta], há [ˈa], vai [ˈvaj]
- não [ˈnɐ̃w̃]

Source: Clitic and function-word status follows Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 on clitics and prosodic words; Cunha & Cintra (1984), chapter on atonic forms. The raised vowels [i u] in atonic function words follow the African-Portuguese, non-reduced vocalism adopted below (Undolo 2014:183-184, by analogy). This differs from European Portuguese [dɨ kɨ mɨ], which is not used here.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable as a distinct form: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written the same at a sentence end, at a comma and sentence-internally. Final vowels are never dropped or devoiced in the transcription. The only position-dependent detail is word-final s/z/x, which is written [s] before a pause: casas. [ˈkazas]. Word-final r stays [ɾ] before a pause: falar. [faˈlaɾ].

Source: No pausal morphology exists in Portuguese (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000). Final-/s/ realisation follows the African-Portuguese alveolar coda reported for Cape Verde (spec feature 2; secondary Wikipedia description, flagged).

## Connected speech

Each word keeps its own IPA form in its own place; only the final segment of a word may change, as follows.

Word-final sibilant (s, z, x):
- [z] before a word starting with a vowel or a voiced consonant: os amigos [uz aˈmiɡus], as mesas [az ˈmezas], dez anos [ˈdɛz ˈanus]
- [s] before a voiceless consonant or a pause: os carros [us ˈkarus]
- never [ʃ ʒ]

No other cross-word process is written:
- no vowel elision or merger across words: de água [di ˈaɡwa], que é [ki ˈɛ], a amiga [a aˈmiɡa]
- no nasal assimilation
- final r stays [ɾ] before vowels and consonants: por isso [poɾ ˈisu]
- final l stays [l]: mal estar [ˈmal isˈtaɾ]

A word left by an apostrophe split is written as its bare sound: the d of d'água is [d], água is [ˈaɡwa].

Source: Sibilant voicing sandhi as described for Portuguese generally in Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 3 and ch. 7 (fricative coda). The alveolar, non-palatalised outcome follows the Lusophone-African pattern (spec feature 2; Wikipedia, flagged; no read CV-Portuguese primary). Absence of written vowel sandhi follows the citation-form-per-word convention of this transcription set.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as the consonant-like symbols [j w], never [i̯ u̯]:
- falling diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], sei [ˈsej], dói [ˈdɔj], mau [ˈmaw], céu [ˈsɛw], ouro [ˈowɾu]
- rising sequences after a consonant: água [ˈaɡwa], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], qual [ˈkwal]

Nasal diphthongs carry tildes on both parts:
- ão [ɐ̃w̃]: mão [ˈmɐ̃w̃], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]
- ãe/ães [ɐ̃j̃]: mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃]
- õe [õj̃]: põe [ˈpõj̃]
- final -em/-ens [ẽj̃]: bem [ˈbẽj̃], também [tɐ̃ˈbẽj̃]

Unstressed i/u before another vowel inside a word are written as full vowels, not glides: dia [ˈdia], piano [piˈanu], rua [ˈrua], história [isˈtɔɾia].

Orthographic ou is written [ow].
Orthographic ei is written [ej]: primeiro [pɾiˈmejɾu].

Source: Glide inventory and nasal diphthongs after Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2-3. Final -em as [ẽj̃] rather than European [ɐ̃j̃], and ou/ei kept as diphthongs rather than monophthongised, follow the spelling-close African-Portuguese norm (Undolo 2014 by analogy; flagged, no read CV-Portuguese source).

## Other choices

### Unstressed vowels (provisional)

No European-style reduction.

Pretonic vowels are written by spelling: a [a], e [e], o [o], i [i], u [u]. Examples: pequeno [peˈkenu], morar [moˈɾaɾ], cidade [siˈdadi], felicidade [felisiˈdadi]. Pretonic orthographic e is never [ɨ] or deleted.

Post-tonic and final unstressed vowels:
- -a [a]: casa [ˈkaza]
- -e [i]: nome [ˈnomi]
- -o [u]: livro [ˈlivɾu]

Word-initial unstressed es-/ex- is written [is]: estar [isˈtaɾ], escola [isˈkɔla].

Source: Spec feature 1, modelled on Undolo (2014:183-184) for Angolan cultured Portuguese by analogy. The final -e [i] and -o [u] raising matches the Creole-influenced pattern noted by Freitas (2017:154) on unstressed vowels. European descriptions (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000) give [ɐ ɨ u] with deletion; that tradition is not followed. Provisional: no read CV-Portuguese source.

### Stressed mid vowels and stressed a

Stressed e/o take the lexical open or closed value of standard Portuguese: [ɛ ɔ] vs [e o]. Examples: pé [ˈpɛ], avó [aˈvɔ], ele [ˈeli], avô [aˈvo], porta [ˈpɔɾta], todo [ˈtodu]. Stressed a is [a]: falar [faˈlaɾ]. Before a nasal consonant or written with a circumflex it is [ɐ]: cama [ˈkɐma], câmara [ˈkɐmaɾa].

Source: Lexical mid-vowel distribution of standard Portuguese per Cunha & Cintra (1984) and Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2; adopted as the CV-Portuguese norm since no variety-specific source was read.

### Nasal vowels

A vowel followed by m/n in the same syllable is written as a nasal vowel with no following consonant: [ɐ̃ ẽ ĩ õ ũ]. Examples: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], tempo [ˈtẽpu], fim [ˈfĩ], ponte [ˈpõti], mundo [ˈmũdu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2, nasal vowels.

### Rhotics (provisional)

Two rhotics, both alveolar; never uvular [ʁ].
- Trill [r] for word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s: rua [ˈrua], carro [ˈkaru], honra [ˈõra].
- Tap [ɾ] everywhere else, including codas and word-final position: caro [ˈkaɾu], porta [ˈpɔɾta], mar [ˈmaɾ].

Source: Spec feature 3; Lusophone-African alveolar rhotic (Wikipedia, flagged). Contrasts with the uvular strong-r of Lisbon described in Mateus & d'Andrade (2000).

### Coda s word-internally

Word-internal coda s/z/x is written by the following sound:
- [s] before a voiceless consonant: festa [ˈfɛsta], escola [isˈkɔla]
- [z] before a voiced consonant: mesmo [ˈmezmu]
- never [ʃ ʒ]

Source: Spec feature 2 (Wikipedia, flagged); differs from European [ʃ ʒ] coda.

### Lateral

l is [l] in all positions; it is never velarised [ɫ] and never vocalised [w]. Examples: sol [ˈsɔl], Brasil [bɾaˈzil], alto [ˈaltu]. lh is [ʎ]: filho [ˈfiʎu].

Source: Spec feature 5 (flagged); contrasts with European [ɫ] and Brazilian [w] (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 1-3).

### Voiced stops and t/d before i

b d g are plosives [b d ɡ] in every position, with no spirantisation: cidade [siˈdadi], lago [ˈlaɡu], sábado [ˈsabadu]. t and d are never palatalised before [i]: tia [ˈtia], dia [ˈdia], noite [ˈnojti].

Source: Spec feature 4 (flagged), against European [β ð ɣ] allophony noted in Mateus & d'Andrade (2000). The absence of Brazilian [tʃ dʒ] follows standard non-Brazilian norms.

### Other consonant letters

- ch [ʃ]: chave [ˈʃavi]
- j and g before e/i [ʒ]: jogo [ˈʒoɡu], gente [ˈʒẽti]
- nh [ɲ]: vinho [ˈviɲu]
- c and g before a/o/u: [k ɡ]
- c and ç before e/i: [s]
- intervocalic s: [z]: casa [ˈkaza]
- x follows the standard lexicon: [ʃ] in xadrez [ʃaˈdɾes], [ks] in táxi [ˈtaksi], [z] in exame [eˈzɐmi], [s] in próximo [ˈpɾɔsimu]
- h is silent: hora [ˈɔɾa]

Source: Standard Portuguese grapheme-phoneme correspondences, Cunha & Cintra (1984), chapter on orthography and phonetics.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
