# Transcription conventions: Souletin Basque (Zuberera) (eu-x-zuberera)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable on every content word of two or more syllables. Leave monosyllables unmarked. The default rule is penultimate stress, counted on the full inflected word form including its case and determiner suffixes: [ɡiˈs̻on] → gizona [ɡiˈs̻ona]; etxia 'the house' [eˈtʃia]; gizonari [ɡis̻oˈnari]. Final stress applies only where a syllable has been lost by contraction or apocope. This covers lexically oxytone words such as ardu 'wine' [arˈdũ]. It also covers absolutive plural -ak forms, which descriptions give as final-stressed against the penultimate ergative singular, e.g. alhabák 'the daughters' [alhaˈbak] vs alhábak 'the daughter (erg.)' [alˈhabak]. Basque has no phonemic long vowels and no geminates, so neither affects the count. Sequences of identical vowels that the spelling keeps are two syllables. Diphthongs (ai, au, ei, eu, oi, üi) count as one syllable.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2003). 'Accentuation', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque, §2 (Souletin penultimate system with final stress from contraction). Lafon (1958, repr. in Lafon 1999) on the Souletin of Larrau.

## Stressed and unstressed words

There are no separate articles: the determiner is a suffix and is stressed as part of its host. The following are written without ˈ even when polysyllabic: finite auxiliaries and synthetic auxiliary forms after a participle or after ez (da, dü, zen, dira, nüzü, dütüzü → [da], [dy], [s̻en], [diɾa], [nys̻y], [dytys̻y]); the conjunctions eta, edo, ala, ere (e.g. [eta], [edo]); and the particle ba- when written separately. The following carry stress by the normal rule: personal and demonstrative pronouns (ni [ni] unmarked as a monosyllable, hori [ˈhori], hura [ˈhuɾa]); the negator ez and the affirmative bai, which are monosyllables and so unmarked; postpositions written as separate words (gabe [ˈɡabe], artian [ˈartian]); and main verbs.

Source: Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (2003), A Grammar of Basque, chs. on accentuation and the auxiliary (clitic status of the inflected auxiliary).

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Souletin has no pausal forms and no nunation. Every word is written in the same form at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside a phrase. Final vowels and case endings are never dropped or changed at a pause, e.g. etxia [eˈtʃia] in all positions.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), pp. 15-65 (no pausal alternations described).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form. Write no cross-word vowel elision, liaison or resyllabification, and write no voicing or place assimilation across words. The single written sandhi is after the negator ez: a following word-initial b d ɡ is written devoiced as unaspirated p t k in the second word, while ez itself stays [es̻]: ez da [es̻ ta], ez dü [es̻ ty], ez gira [es̻ kiɾa]. Other initial consonants after ez are unchanged: ez naiz [es̻ nai̯s̻].

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque (sandhi with the negative particle ez).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write falling diphthongs with non-syllabic offglides: ai [ai̯], ei [ei̯], oi [oi̯], ui [ui̯], üi [yi̯], au [au̯], eu [eu̯]; e.g. bai [bai̯], gau [ɡau̯]. Write [j] only for a glide that is a syllable onset, i.e. prevocalic i or y at the start of a syllable in words where the spelling shows it. Souletin <j> is the fricative [ʒ], not a glide: jan [ʒan]. Vowels in hiatus are written as two syllabic vowels with no inserted glide: etxia [eˈtʃia].

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), pp. 15-65 (diphthongs; northern [ʒ] for j).

## Other choices

### Front rounded vowel

ü → [y], kept distinct from u → [u]: ükhen [ˈykʰen], hur [hur]. Do not write [ø] even though the vowel is phonetically between French y and ø.

Source: Egurtzegi (2013), pp. 127-128; Hualde (1993).

### Aspirated stops and h

Follow the Souletin spelling: ph th kh → [pʰ tʰ kʰ]; plain p t k → unaspirated [p t k]; b d ɡ always [b d ɡ] (lenition to β ð ɣ is not written in broad transcription). For example khatiña [kʰaˈtiɲa] and thipi [ˈtʰipi]. Orthographic h → [h]. Sonorant + h (lh, nh, rh) → [lh], [nh], [rh]. Write the nasal aspirate [h̃] only in words where a published source lists it; otherwise write [h].

Source: Hualde (2018), 'Aspiration in Basque', p. 2; Lafon (1958/1999).

### Nasal vowels (departs from the spec note)

The spec emits no nasal vowels. Following the published descriptions, write the contrastive word-final nasal vowels that result from loss of old -n where the word is known to have them: ardu [arˈdũ]. Do not write the predictable nasalisation of vowels next to a nasal consonant: ama [ˈama], gazna [ˈɡas̻na]. Never write [õ].

Source: Egurtzegi (2013), p. 127.

### Sibilants and palatals

Map the spelling as follows: s → apical [s̺]; z → laminal [s̻]; x → [ʃ]; ts → [ts̺]; tz → [ts̻]; tx → [tʃ]; j → [ʒ]; ñ and il+V → [ɲ]; ll → [ʎ]; tt → [c]; dd → [ɟ]. Sibilant voicing is not written, because the spelling does not show it.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), pp. 15-65.

### Rhotics

Write single intervocalic r as [ɾ] and rr as [r]. Write r before a consonant or word-finally as [r]: ür [yr], ardu [arˈdũ]. Do not write the uvular realisation used by some speakers under French influence.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
