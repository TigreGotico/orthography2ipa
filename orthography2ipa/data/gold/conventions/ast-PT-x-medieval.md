# Transcription conventions: Medieval Asturian-Leonese of Portugal (ast-PT-x-medieval)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every word of two or more syllables. Do not mark monosyllables. Do not mark secondary stress. Stress keeps its Latin position. A word ending in a vowel, or in -s or -n that marks a plural or a verb ending, is stressed on the penult: ⟨casa⟩ [ˈkaz̺a], ⟨fazen⟩ [ˈfad͡zen], ⟨lobos⟩ [ˈlobos̺]. A word ending in any other consonant, after loss of Latin final -e, is stressed on the last syllable: ⟨salir⟩ [s̺aˈliɾ], ⟨cidad⟩ [t͡siˈdad], ⟨sal⟩ is a monosyllable [s̺al]. A syllable with ⟨ie⟩ or ⟨uo⟩ is always the stressed one, since these come only from stressed Latin Ĕ and Ŏ: ⟨tierra⟩ [ˈtjera], ⟨fuogo⟩ [ˈfwoɡo]. Learned proparoxytones keep their Latin stress: ⟨ánima⟩ [ˈanima]. The lect has no phonemic long vowels and no geminates. ⟨ll⟩ is /ʎ/, ⟨ss⟩ and ⟨rr⟩ are single consonants /s̺/ and /r/, and none is written double. It has no case endings, so that part of the topic does not apply.

Source: Latin stress retention as in Penny 2002, ch. 2 (stress and vowel system); Leonese accentuation and the diphthongs as stress markers as in Menéndez Pidal 1906, El dialecto leonés, §§3–4; Mirandese accent as in Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, Estudos de philologia mirandesa, vol. I

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and never get ˈ, even when they have two syllables. Definite articles: ⟨el, l', la, los, las⟩ give [el], [l], [la], [los̺], [las̺]. Prepositions: ⟨de, a, en, con, por, pa, para, sobre, entre⟩ give [de], [a], [en], [kon], [poɾ], [pa], [paɾa], [s̺obɾe], [entɾe]. Conjunctions and complementizers: ⟨e/i, ou, que, se, mas, porque⟩ give [e]/[i], [ow], [ke], [s̺e], [mas̺], [poɾke]. Object and reflexive clitics, whether free or joined by a hyphen: ⟨me, te, se, le, lo, la, nos, vos, les⟩ give [me], [te], [s̺e], [le], [lo], [la], [nos̺], [vos̺], [les̺]. Prenominal possessive determiners are also unstressed. The following carry their lexical stress: stressed subject and prepositional pronouns (⟨you⟩ [ˈjow], ⟨nós⟩ [nos̺]), demonstratives (⟨aquel⟩ [aˈkel]), interrogatives, adverbs, numerals, and every finite verb form, including auxiliary haber and ser (⟨habie⟩ [aˈbie]).

Source: Clitic and article system of Leonese as in Menéndez Pidal 1906, El dialecto leonés, §§18–20; for Mirandese, Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, morphology chapter on the article and pronouns

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. This Romance lect has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so every word is written the same at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside the sentence: ⟨casa⟩ is always [ˈkaz̺a].

Source: No pausal alternation is described for Asturleonese or Galaico-Portuguese: Menéndez Pidal 1906; Maia 1986

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form. Assimilation across word boundaries is not written: no sibilant voicing before a voiced consonant, no place assimilation of a final nasal, no spirantization of b d g. So ⟨los dias⟩ is [los̺ ˈdias̺], not [loz̺ ˈdias̺], and ⟨con boca⟩ is [kon ˈboka]. No resyllabification or linking marks are used, and there is no liaison. Elision is written only where the orthography writes it with an apostrophe. The elided form is then its own word, made of the remaining consonant: ⟨l'home⟩ is [l ˈome] and ⟨d'aquel⟩ is [d aˈkel]. When two vowels meet across a word boundary with no apostrophe, each vowel stays in its own word in full, and no synalepha is written.

Source: Broad citation-form practice of the spec; elision with apostrophe as in the regional orthographic tradition codified in Ferreira 1999, Convenção Ortográfica da Língua Mirandesa; the absence of vowel reduction as in the spec, note 10

## Glides and diphthongs

Write every non-syllabic high vowel as j or w, both before a vowel (rising) and after one (falling). Do not use i̯ or u̯. Leonese diphthongs: ⟨ie⟩ [je] as in ⟨tierra⟩ [ˈtjera], ⟨uo⟩ [wo] as in ⟨puorta⟩ [ˈpwoɾta]. Other rising diphthongs: ⟨agua⟩ [ˈaɡwa]. Falling diphthongs: ⟨pai⟩ [paj], ⟨cousa⟩ [ˈkows̺a], ⟨muito⟩ [ˈmujto]. Unstressed i or u next to a vowel is a glide. Stressed i or u is syllabic and stands in hiatus: ⟨dia⟩ [ˈdia], ⟨rio⟩ [ˈrio]. In ⟨qu, gu⟩ before e or i, u is silent: ⟨que⟩ [ke].

Source: Leonese diphthongization of Ĕ and Ŏ as in Menéndez Pidal 1906, El dialecto leonés, §3; Galaico-Portuguese falling diphthongs as in Maia 1986; glide notation j w as in Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2

## Other choices

### unstressed vowels

Unstressed vowels are written in full as the five vowels [a e i o u] according to spelling: ⟨lhana⟩ [ˈʎana], ⟨fazer⟩ [faˈd͡zeɾ]. This follows the spec, note 10. Modern Mirandese reduces them to [ɐ ɨ u], but that reduction is not written.

Source: Spec note 10; the modern reduction is described in Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §24, p. 200

### stressed mid vowels

Stressed mid vowels are written as e and o. No ɛ/ɔ contrast is written, because Ĕ and Ŏ became the diphthongs je and wo, which leaves a five-vowel stressed system: ⟨pena⟩ [ˈpena], ⟨boca⟩ [ˈboka].

Source: Five-vowel outcome of the Ĕ/Ŏ diphthongizing systems as in Penny 2002, ch. 2; Menéndez Pidal 1906, §3; the Galaico-Portuguese seven-vowel system of Maia 1986 is not followed

### sibilants

There are four sibilants. ⟨ss⟩ and initial ⟨s-⟩ are [s̺]. Intervocalic ⟨-s-⟩ is [z̺]. ⟨ç⟩ and ⟨c⟩ before e or i are [t͡s]. ⟨z⟩ is [d͡z]. Examples: ⟨passo⟩ [ˈpas̺o], ⟨casa⟩ [ˈkaz̺a], ⟨cinco⟩ [ˈt͡sinko], ⟨fazer⟩ [faˈd͡zeɾ]. Final ⟨-s⟩ is [s̺] and final ⟨-z⟩ is [t͡s]: ⟨luz⟩ [lut͡s]. Other values: ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ], ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ], and ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩ before e or i are [ʒ].

Source: Medieval sibilant system as in Penny 2002, ch. 2 (Old Spanish); Maia 1986 (Galaico-Portuguese); spec notes 1, 6 and 7

### b/v

⟨v⟩ is written [v] and ⟨b⟩ is written [b], with no spirantization in the broad transcription: ⟨vaca⟩ [ˈvaka], ⟨lobo⟩ [ˈlobo]. This follows the spec, note 8. Modern Mirandese merges the two as [b], but that merger is not applied.

Source: Spec note 8; the modern merger and the older distinction are described in Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §14, p. 193

### nasals

Before a consonant and at the end of a word, a vowel followed by ⟨n⟩ or ⟨m⟩, and a vowel written with a tilde, are written as nasal vowels. Before a vowel, n and m stay consonants. Examples: ⟨pan⟩ [pɐ̃], ⟨mão⟩ [mɐ̃w̃], ⟨son⟩ [s̺õ], ⟨lhana⟩ [ˈʎana].

Source: Spec note 4; Galaico-Portuguese nasalization as in Maia 1986

### laterals, palatals, rhotics, h

⟨lh⟩ and ⟨ll⟩ are [ʎ]. ⟨nh⟩ and ⟨ñ⟩ are [ɲ]. Plain ⟨l⟩, including word-initial ⟨l⟩, is [l], since initial palatalization is written only where the spelling shows ⟨lh⟩. ⟨rr⟩, initial ⟨r-⟩ and ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s are [r]. Other ⟨r⟩ is [ɾ]. ⟨h⟩ is silent. Examples: ⟨lhéngua⟩ [ˈʎeŋɡwa], ⟨ome⟩ [ˈome], ⟨terra⟩ [ˈtera], ⟨caro⟩ [ˈkaɾo].

Source: Spec note 5; Leonese palatalization as in Menéndez Pidal 1906, §§7–8

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
