# Transcription conventions: Bahian Portuguese (pt-BR-x-bahia)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included ([ˈmah] mar, [ˈpɛ] pé). Do not mark secondary stress or other syllable boundaries. (1) A written acute or circumflex accent marks the stressed syllable ([ˈlɐ̃padɐ] lâmpada, [kaˈfɛ] café, [vɔˈse] você). A tilde marks stress only when no other accent appears in the word ([ihˈmɐ̃w̃] irmão, but [ˈɔɾfɐ̃] órfã). (2) Without an accent, stress falls on the penult when the word ends in -a, -e, -o (with or without -s), -am, -em or -ens ([ˈkazɐ] casa, [ˈfalɐ̃w̃] falam, [ˈʒɔvẽj̃] jovem). (3) Without an accent, stress falls on the final syllable when the word ends in -i, -u, -im, -um, -l, -r, -z, -x, or a consonant other than s/m ([bɾaˈziw] Brasil, [faˈlah] falar, [aˈhojs] arroz, [aˈki] aqui). Vowel length is not phonemic, so no ː is written. Written rr and ss are single consonants, not geminates ([ˈkahʊ] carro, [ˈpasʊ] passo). Portuguese has no case endings.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 (word stress); Silva (2002), section on the accent of Brazilian Portuguese.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed and get no ˈ: (a) definite articles: o [ʊ], a [ɐ], os [ʊs], as [ɐs]; (b) monosyllabic prepositions and their contractions: de [d͡ʒɪ], do [dʊ], da [dɐ], em [ẽj̃], no [nʊ], na [nɐ], com [kõ], por [poh], sem [sẽj̃], a [ɐ], à [a], ao [aw]; (c) para [paɾɐ], pra [pɾɐ], and pelo/pela(s) [pelʊ pelɐ]; (d) monosyllabic conjunctions and complementisers: e [ɪ], ou [o], que [kɪ], se [sɪ], nem [nẽj̃], mas [mas]; (e) clitic object and reflexive pronouns, including hyphenated ones: me [mɪ], te [t͡ʃɪ], se [sɪ], lhe [ʎɪ], nos [nʊs], vos [vʊs], o/a/lo/la/no/na [ʊ ɐ lʊ lɐ nʊ nɐ]. Their final vowels take the unstressed values [ɪ ʊ ɐ], and the open-pretonic rule does not apply to them. The following carry stress: indefinite articles ([ˈũ] um, [ˈumɐ] uma); subject and prepositional pronouns ([ˈew], [ˈelɪ], [vɔˈse], [ˈnɔjs], [ˈmĩ]); possessives and demonstratives ([ˈmew], [ˈesɪ]); contractions with pronouns ([ˈdelɪ], [ˈnesɐ]); disyllabic prepositions and conjunctions other than para/pelo ([aˈtɛ] até, [ˈkomʊ] como, [pohˈke] porque, [kɐ̃ˈdʊ] is wrong, write [ˈkwɐ̃dʊ] quando); negation [ˈnɐ̃w̃]; stressed quê [ˈke]; all forms of ser, estar, ter, haver, ir, including auxiliaries ([ˈtẽj̃], [isˈta], [ˈvaj], [ˈfoj]).

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 on clitics and unstressed words; Castilho (2010), chapters on clitic pronouns and prepositions.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Portuguese has no case endings, no nunation and no pausal word forms. Every word is written the same at a comma, at the end of a sentence and phrase-internally. Before a pause, final /S/ is [s] and final /r/ is [h]. Final unstressed vowels keep [ɪ ʊ ɐ] and are never deleted or devoiced ([ˈnojt͡ʃɪ] noite, [ˈfalʊ] falo).

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6–7; nothing in the cited descriptions sets up pausal forms for Portuguese.

## Connected speech

Write only these consonantal sandhi processes, each changing the edge of its own word. (1) Word-final /S/ is [z] before a word beginning with a vowel or a voiced consonant ([ʊz aˈmigʊs] os amigos, [ˈmajz ˈbõ] mais bom). It is [s] before a voiceless consonant or a pause ([ɐs ˈkazɐs] as casas). (2) Word-final /r/ is [ɾ] before a word beginning with a vowel ([poɾ ˈisʊ] por isso, [ˈmaɾ aˈzuw] mar azul). Elsewhere it is [h]; voicing to [ɦ] is not written. (3) Word-final /l/ stays [w] even before a vowel ([ˈmaw isˈtah] mal estar). Do not write vowel sandhi: elision, crasis and gliding of a final vowel before a following vowel. Each word keeps its full vowel ([d͡ʒɪ aˈmoh] de amor, not [d͡ʒjaˈmoh]). Do not write a linking mark (‿), and do not resyllabify across the space. Palatalisation of /t d/ applies only inside a word, triggered by its own [i ɪ j].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 7 on external sandhi of /S/ and /r/; Callou & Leite (2001), chapters on coda /S/ and /r/.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vowels as [j w], never i̯ u̯. Nasal glides are [j̃ w̃]. Falling diphthongs: [ˈpaj] pai, [ˈmew] meu, [ˈsɛw] céu, [ˈfoj] foi; nasal ones: [ˈmɐ̃w̃] mão, [ˈmɐ̃j̃] mãe, [ˈpõj̃s] põe(s), [ˈtẽj̃] tem, [tɐ̃ˈbẽj̃] também. Coda /l/ is [w] ([ˈsaw] sal). An unstressed i/u next to a vowel is also a glide in post-tonic rising sequences ([isˈtɔɾjɐ] história, [ˈsɛɾjɪ] série, [ˈagwɐ] água). An accented or stressed i/u is a syllable nucleus ([paˈis] país, [baˈiɐ] Bahia, [saˈudʒɪ] is wrong, write [saˈud͡ʒɪ] saúde). Monophthongise in three contexts: ou → [o] ([ˈotɾʊ] outro, [ˈpoku] is wrong, write [ˈpokʊ] pouco); ei → [e] before [ɾ ʒ ʃ] ([pɾiˈmeɾʊ] primeiro, [ˈbeʒʊ] beijo); ai → [a] before [ʃ] ([ˈkaʃɐ] caixa).

Source: Silva (2002), sections on diphthongs and monophthongisation; Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 3–4 on glides and syllable structure.

## Other choices

### pretonic mid vowels

Oral pretonic e/o are open [ɛ ɔ] in every pretonic syllable, with no harmony-conditioned raising: [kɔˈlɛʒʊ] colégio, [mɛˈʎɔh] melhor, [tɔˈmat͡ʃɪ] tomate, [dɔhˈmih] dormir, [fɛˈlis] feliz, [vɔˈse] você. There are four exceptions. (1) Before a nasal consonant or in a nasal vowel, write closed nasalised [ẽ õ] ([sẽˈt͡ʃih] sentir). (2) Word-initial es-/ex- + consonant is [is] ([isˈkɔlɐ] escola), and des- is [d͡ʒis]/[d͡ʒiz] ([d͡ʒisˈkuwpɐ] desculpa). (3) Pretonic e/o in hiatus are [i u] ([t͡ʃiˈatɾʊ] teatro, [puˈɛtɐ] poeta). (4) Unstressed function words follow function_words. This is the note's Northeastern open-pretonic rule, as the atlas maps it for Salvador.

Source: Cardoso et al. (2014), ALiB vol. 2, pretonic-vowel maps for Salvador; Silva (2002) on Northeastern pretonic vowels.

### unstressed final vowels

Word-final unstressed -e/-es → [ɪ ɪs], -o/-os → [ʊ ʊs], and -a/-as → [ɐ ɐs]: [ˈlejt͡ʃɪ] leite, [ˈlivɾʊs] livros, [ˈkazɐ] casa. Non-final post-tonic vowels keep their plain values ([ˈpasaɾʊ] pássaro). Stressed vowels before a nasal consonant are nasalised, with mid vowels closed as the spelling shows: [ˈkɐ̃mɐ] cama, [ˈfõmɪ] fome.

Source: Callou & Leite (2001), chapter on unstressed vowels; Silva (2002).

### palatalisation of /t d/

/t d/ → [t͡ʃ d͡ʒ] before [i ɪ j], including final -te/-de: [ˈt͡ʃiɐ] tia, [ˈd͡ʒiɐ] dia, [siˈdad͡ʒɪ] cidade, [ˈnojt͡ʃɪ] noite, [ˈpat͡ʃjʊ] pátio. There is no palatalisation after a preceding glide when no high vowel follows: [ˈmũjtʊ] muito, [ˈojtʊ] oito, [ˈdojdʊ] doido. This follows the note's resolution.

Source: Cardoso et al. (2014), ALiB palatalisation maps (Salvador).

### coda /S/ and yod insertion

Coda /S/ is alveolar everywhere, never the chiado [ʃ ʒ]: [ˈfɛstɐ] festa, [ˈmezmʊ] mesmo, [ˈdojs] dois. In a stressed word-final syllable ending in written s/z after a vowel, insert [j]: [ˈtɾejs] três, [ˈnɔjs] nós, [ˈlujs] luz, [aˈhojs] arroz, [ˈmajs] mais. Do not insert [j] in unstressed syllables ([ˈlapis] lápis) or in the unstressed word mas [mas].

Source: Cardoso et al. (2014), ALiB coda-/S/ maps; Callou & Leite (2001) on coda /S/.

### rhotics

Strong r is glottal [h]. This covers word-initial r, rr, and r after n/l/s ([ˈhuɐ] rua, [ˈkahʊ] carro, [ˈõhɐ] honra). Coda r is also [h], including word-final r in infinitives, which is kept and not deleted ([ˈpɔhtɐ] porta, [faˈlah] falar). Intervocalic r and r in onset clusters are [ɾ] ([ˈkaɾʊ] caro, [ˈpɾatʊ] prato). Before a vowel-initial word, final r is [ɾ] (see connected_speech).

Source: Callou & Leite (2001), chapter on /r/; Silva (2002).

### /ʎ/

Write [ʎ] in all words ([ˈfiʎʊ] filho, [ˈmuʎɛh] mulher). The variable weakening to [j] mentioned in the note is not written.

Source: Silva (2002) on the palatal lateral; the note treats [j] as a variable allophone.

### consonant clusters and epenthesis

Do not write the epenthetic [i] in heterosyllabic clusters. Keep the consonants adjacent: [advɔˈgadʊ] advogado, [ˈhitmʊ] ritmo, [ˈpnew] pneu.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 4, which treats epenthesis as phonetic; Silva (2002) describes the epenthetic variant.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
