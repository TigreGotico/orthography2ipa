# Transcription conventions: Santandereano Spanish (es-CO-x-santande)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Spanish lexical stress follows the orthography. A written accent marks the stressed vowel. With no accent mark, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the penultimate syllable, and any other word on the final syllable. Write the primary stress mark ˈ immediately before the onset of the stressed syllable. In a written cluster such as tr, pl, bl or tl, the mark goes before the whole cluster. Syllable dots are never written. Every stressed word carries ˈ, including stressed monosyllables. Unstressed function words (see function_words) carry no mark. There is no secondary stress, except that an adverb in -mente gets two marks: [ˈklaɾaˈmente]. Examples: casa [ˈkasa], ciudad [sjuˈdad], árbol [ˈaɾbol], teléfono [teˈlefono], sol [ˈsol], tenés [teˈnes]. Voseo forms keep their final stress as spelled. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so stress never depends on them. Orthographic double vowels are two syllables, as in creer [kɾeˈeɾ] and leer [leˈeɾ]. Orthographic cc is two consonants, as in acción [akˈsjon].

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, Cambridge UP, chapter on stress; RAE & ASALE (2011), Nueva gramática de la lengua española: Fonética y fonología, ch. 9 (acento). This is the pan-Hispanic system, unchanged in Colombian interior speech (Lipski 1994, ch. on Colombia).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed words, written with no ˈ:
- definite articles: el, la, los, las, lo;
- simple prepositions: a, ante, bajo, cabe, con, contra, de, desde, en, entre, hacia, hasta, para, por, sin, so, sobre, tras;
- conjunctions: y/e, o/u, ni, que, si, pero, mas, aunque, porque, pues;
- relatives without an accent mark: que, quien, cual, donde, cuando, como, cuanto;
- clitic pronouns: me, te, se, nos, os, lo, la, los, las, le, les;
- prenominal possessives: mi, tu, su, mis, tus, sus;
- titles before a name: don, doña, san, santo, fray.
Stressed words, written with ˈ:
- indefinite articles: un, una, unos, unas;
- según;
- subject and prepositional pronouns: yo, tú, vos, él, ella, usted, nosotros, ustedes, mí, ti, sí, conmigo;
- nuestro and vuestro;
- demonstratives;
- interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent mark: qué, quién, dónde, cómo, cuándo;
- all verb forms, including the auxiliaries haber, ser and estar: ha, es, está, había;
- negative no;
- all nouns, adjectives and adverbs.
An enclitic is written in its own word slot when the tool splits it off. Otherwise it stays inside the host word, unstressed, and the host keeps its own stress: dámelo [ˈdamelo]. Examples: el libro de usted [el ˈlibɾo de usˈted]; un amigo [ˈun aˈmigo]; lo que vos querés [lo ke ˈbos keˈɾes]; se lo ha dado [se lo ˈa ˈdado].

Source: Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, chapter on accent (palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas); Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1977), Manual de pronunciación española, §§ on palabras inacentuadas. Where these two differ (nuestro, vuestro, auxiliaries), this follows Hualde (2005), which treats all verb forms and nuestro as stressed.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written in the same form at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside a phrase. Final vowels are never dropped or devoiced in the transcription.

Source: Hualde (2005); Quilis (1993). No description of Colombian Spanish reports pausal morphology.

## Connected speech

No cross-word process is written. Each word is transcribed in its citation form, in its own slot:
- Synalepha and vowel fusion are not written: lo hace [lo ˈase], de ella [de ˈeʝa].
- Resyllabification of a final consonant onto the next word is not written: los otros [los ˈotɾos].
- Nasal place assimilation is not written across words: en Bogotá [en bogoˈta], un poco [ˈun ˈpoko].
- Voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants is not written: mismo [ˈmismo], las manos [las ˈmanos].
- Merger of identical consonants is not written: el lado [el ˈlado].
Inside a word, write only what the spelling's phonemes give. Nasals are m, n, ɲ as spelled, with no [ŋ] or [ɱ]: tengo [ˈtengo], invierno [inˈbjeɾno].

Source: Broad-transcription practice of Hualde (2005), which treats synalepha, voicing and assimilation as postlexical allophony. For interior Colombian /s/ and nasal retention, see Lipski (1994, ch. on Colombia) and Flórez, L. (1964), 'El español hablado en Colombia y su atlas lingüístico', Thesaurus 19.

## Glides and diphthongs

A non-syllabic high vowel before the nucleus is written j or w: tierra [ˈtjera], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [sjuˈdad], cuidado [kwiˈdado], ruido [ˈrwido]. A non-syllabic high vowel after the nucleus is written i̯ or u̯: aire [ˈai̯ɾe], causa [ˈkau̯sa], hoy [ˈoi̯], muy [ˈmui̯], rey [ˈrei̯], deuda [ˈdeu̯da]. The sequences ui and iu are always rising (j, w), except word-final -uy, which is [ui̯]. An accent mark on i or u marks hiatus: país [paˈis], día [ˈdia], baúl [baˈul]. Sequences of mid and low vowels are always written in hiatus: teatro [teˈatɾo], poeta [poˈeta]. Popular gliding such as [ˈtjatɾo] is not written. The conjunction y is [i]. Orthographic y or ll before a vowel is the consonant [ʝ]: yo [ˈʝo], calle [ˈkaʝe]. Word-initial hie- and hue- are [ʝe] and [we]: hielo [ˈʝelo], hueso [ˈweso].

Source: Hualde (2005), chapters on vowels, glides and diphthongs (j w versus i̯ u̯ convention); RAE & ASALE (2011, Fonética y fonología §7–8) for diphthong and hiatus. The yeísmo symbol ʝ follows the specification note (Lipski 1994).

## Other choices

### Stops versus spirants for /b d g/

Write b, d and ɡ in all positions, never β, ð or ɣ. v and b are both [b]: vaca [ˈbaka], lado [ˈlado], agua [ˈagwa], verdad [beɾˈdad]. Word-final and intervocalic d are written as d, not deleted: cansado [kanˈsado], usted [usˈted]. This keeps one symbol per phoneme. Interior Colombian speech keeps stops after non-nasal consonants, where other dialects have spirants, as in algo and desde, and a broad transcription would otherwise have to choose between the two.

Source: Lipski (1994), ch. on Colombia (occlusive /b d g/ after consonants in highland Colombia); Hualde (2005), chapter on obstruents. The broad choice follows the specification's phonemic practice.

### Velar fricative j/ge/gi

Write [h] for orthographic j, and for g before e or i: hijo [ˈiho], gente [ˈhente], México [ˈmehiko]. Orthographic h is silent: hacer [aˈseɾ]. This departs from the inherited es-ES/es-419 /x/ of the stub, following the published description of Colombian Spanish.

Source: Lipski (1994), ch. on Colombia (/x/ as weak glottal [h] throughout most of Colombia); Flórez (1964).

### Coronal fricative /s/ and seseo

Write s for s, z, and c before e or i in every position. Coda /s/ is retained as [s], never aspirated or deleted: los cazadores [los kasaˈdoɾes], después [desˈpwes]. The letter x is [ks] between vowels and at the end of a word: examen [ekˈsamen]. Before a consonant, x is [s] in the broad standard: texto [ˈtesto]. Hmm, see the correction below. In fact, follow the RAE orthoepic norm and write texto [ˈteksto] for consistency.

Source: Specification note (seseo; Lipski 1994); Lipski (1994, ch. on Colombia) and Montes Giraldo, J. J. (1982), 'El español de Colombia: propuesta de clasificación dialectal', Thesaurus 37 (interior 'andino' zone retains coda /s/); RAE & ASALE (2011, Fonética y fonología) for x [ks].

### Rhotics

Write the trill r for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l or s: perro [ˈpero], rosa [ˈrosa], honra [ˈonra]. Write the tap ɾ elsewhere, including word-finally and in clusters: pero [ˈpeɾo], comer [koˈmeɾ], tres [ˈtɾes]. The assibilated [r̝] or [ʐ] reported for parts of highland Colombia is not written.

Source: Hualde (2005), chapter on rhotics; Lipski (1994), ch. on Colombia (assibilation noted as regional and variable, so it is excluded from a broad transcription).

### Other consonant letters

ch is [tʃ]: mucho [ˈmutʃo]. ñ is [ɲ]: año [ˈaɲo]. ll and y are [ʝ]. qu and c before a, o or u are [k]. gu before e or i is [g]: guerra [ˈgera]. gü is [gw]: pingüino [pinˈgwino]. Word-final n is alveolar [n]; velarization is a coastal feature and is not written.

Source: Hualde (2005); Lipski (1994, ch. on Colombia) (alveolar final /n/ in the interior).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
