# Transcription conventions: Alfena Portuguese (vowel-breaking) (pt-PT-x-alfena)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable on every lexically stressed word, monosyllables included: casa [ˈkaz̺ɐ], café [kɐˈfɛ], pai [ˈpaj]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Secondary stress is never marked. Placement follows the orthography. A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed vowel: médico [ˈmɛdiku], você [boˈs̺e]. Without an accent mark, words ending in -a, -e, -o (plus optional -s), -am or -em(-ns) take penultimate stress: falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], homem [ˈɔmẽj̃]. All other unaccented words take final stress: falar [fɐˈlaɾ], azul [ɐˈz̺uɫ], ruim [ʁuˈĩ] is an exception written as spoken. Stressed vowels are full: [a ɛ e ɔ o i u]. Unstressed vowels reduce: /a/→[ɐ], /e/→[ɨ], /o/→[u]: pequeno [pɨˈkenu]. Vowel-breaking of stressed mid vowels ([ɛ͜ɐ] etc.) is not written, and Porto je/wo diphthongisation is not written: pé [ˈpɛ], porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ]. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (word stress) and ch. 2 (vowel reduction); Cunha & Cintra 1984, ch. on accentuation. Vowel-breaking is omitted as the spec notes direct.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, unmarked and with reduced vowels: definite articles o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ]. Monosyllabic prepositions: de [dɨ], em [ẽj̃], com [kõ], sem [s̺ẽj̃], por [puɾ]. Preposition+article contractions: do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], ao [aw], à [a]. Also para [pɐɾɐ], pelo [pɨlu], pela [pɨlɐ]. Conjunctions e [i], que [kɨ], se [s̺ɨ], mas [mɐʃ], ou [ow], nem [nẽj̃]. Clitic object pronouns me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [s̺ɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], o [u], a [ɐ], nos [nuʃ], vos [buʃ], lhes [ʎɨʃ]; a hyphenated clitic is unstressed in its own word slot: dá-me [ˈda mɨ]. Relative que [kɨ]. Stressed and marked: indefinite articles um [ˈũ], uma [ˈumɐ]. Subject and strong pronouns eu [ˈew], tu [ˈtu], ele [ˈelɨ], mim [ˈmĩ]. Possessives, demonstratives, polysyllabic prepositions (sobre [ˈs̺obɾɨ], entre [ˈẽtɾɨ]), adverbs, negation não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], and all verb forms, auxiliaries included: é [ˈɛ], tem [ˈtẽj̃], foi [ˈfoj], está [ɨʃˈta].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (clitics and prosodic words); Cunha & Cintra 1984, sections on átonos (articles, prepositions, oblique pronouns).

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or tanwīn. The only position-sensitive alternation is the coda sibilant (see connected_speech): before a pause, at a sentence end or a comma, final ⟨s/z⟩ is [ʃ], e.g. as casas [ɐʃ ˈkaz̺ɐʃ]. Final unstressed -e stays [ɨ] and is never deleted in writing: noite [ˈnojtɨ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 and 7 (coda /S/ and final vowels).

## Connected speech

Only word-final sibilant sandhi is written; every word stays in its own slot. Final ⟨s/z/x⟩ depends on the next word in the same phrase. Before a vowel-initial word it is [z̺]: os amigos [uz̺ ɐˈmiɡuʃ], dez anos [ˈdɛz̺ ˈɐnuʃ]. Before a voiced consonant it is [ʒ]: os dias [uʒ ˈdiɐʃ], mesmo [ˈmeʒmu]. Before a voiceless consonant or a pause it is [ʃ]: as casas [ɐʃ ˈkaz̺ɐʃ]. A comma or sentence end counts as a pause. Vowel sandhi is not written: no elision of [ɨ], no crasis and no cross-word glide formation. de outro is [dɨ ˈotɾu], not *[ˈdotɾu]. Final ⟨r⟩ stays [ɾ] before a vowel: comer agora [kuˈmeɾ ɐˈɡɔɾɐ]. No other cross-word assimilation is written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 and 7 (coda /S/ voicing and place). Apical [z̺] in the linking position follows the apical-only system of Cintra 1971 p.93 and Álvarez Pérez 2014 pp.37-38.

## Glides and diphthongs

Glides in falling diphthongs are written as the consonantal symbols j and w, never i̯ u̯: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], rei [ˈʁej], dois [ˈdojʃ], céu [ˈsɛw]. Nasal diphthongs take nasalised glides: mão [ˈmɐ̃w̃], mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃]. Following northern EP, ⟨ei⟩ is [ej], not Lisbon [ɐj]: primeiro [pɾiˈmejɾu]. ⟨ou⟩ is [ow], not Lisbon [o]: outro [ˈowtɾu]. ⟨em/ens⟩ is [ẽj̃], not Lisbon [ɐ̃j̃]: também [tɐ̃ˈbẽj̃]. The labial element of ⟨qu, gu⟩ before a pronounced vowel is [w]: quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. Unstressed ⟨i, u⟩ before another vowel are written as full vowels in hiatus: dia [ˈdiɐ], história [iʃˈtɔɾiɐ], suar [s̺uˈaɾ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 (diphthongs, glides as non-nuclear vocoids). Northern [ej]/[ow]/[ẽj̃] follow Cintra 1971 and Cunha & Cintra 1984 (dialect chapter); here the pronunciation departs from the Lisbon-based pt-PT values.

## Other choices

### Sibilants

Every onset sibilant is apico-alveolar, whether spelled ⟨s, ss, c(e,i), ç, z⟩ or intervocalic ⟨s⟩. The voiceless one is [s̺]: sapato [s̺ɐˈpatu], passo [ˈpas̺u], cinco [ˈs̺ĩku], caça [ˈkas̺ɐ]. The voiced one is [z̺]: casa [ˈkaz̺ɐ], fazer [fɐˈz̺eɾ]. Coda ⟨s/z⟩ is [ʃ]/[ʒ] (chiado) as in connected_speech. ⟨ch, x⟩ are [ʃ] (no affricate [tʃ]): chave [ˈʃabɨ]. ⟨j, g(e,i)⟩ are [ʒ].

Source: Cintra 1971 p.93; Álvarez Pérez 2014 pp.37-38 (Durienses kept only the apico-alveolar branch).

### Betacism

⟨v⟩ and ⟨b⟩ are both written /b/ as [b] in every position: vinho [ˈbiɲu], novo [ˈnobu], você [boˈs̺e], cabo [ˈkabu]. The spirant [β], like [ð ɣ], is allophonic and is not written in this broad transcription.

Source: Cintra 1971 p.88; Álvarez Pérez 2014 pp.35-37.

### Rhotics

⟨rr⟩, word-initial ⟨r⟩, and ⟨r⟩ after ⟨n, l, s⟩ are written [ʁ], keeping the pt-PT base symbol: rato [ˈʁatu], carro [ˈkaʁu]. Older northern speech has an apical trill [r], but it is not written. Single ⟨r⟩ elsewhere, codas included, is [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], mar [ˈmaɾ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 (rhotic variation [r]~[ʁ]); the uvular symbol follows the pt-PT base for consistency.

### Laterals and unstressed initial e

Onset ⟨l⟩ is [l] and coda ⟨l⟩ is [ɫ]: lado [ˈladu], sal [ˈs̺aɫ]. ⟨lh⟩ is [ʎ] and ⟨nh⟩ is [ɲ]. Unstressed word-initial ⟨e⟩ before a coda sibilant is [ɨ]: estar [ɨʃˈtaɾ], escola [ɨʃˈkɔlɐ]. Before a palatal, stressed ⟨e⟩ keeps northern [e], not Lisbon [ɐ]: telha [ˈteʎɐ], venho [ˈbeɲu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2; northern [e] before palatals per Cunha & Cintra 1984 (dialect chapter).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
