# Transcription conventions: Northwest Argentine Spanish (es-AR-x-norte)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word of two or more syllables. Leave monosyllables unmarked whether or not they are stressed. Stress follows the standard Spanish lexical position: written accents are decisive. Otherwise a word ending in a vowel, n or s is stressed on the penult: casa /ˈkasa/, joven /ˈxoben/. Any other word is stressed on the final syllable: ciudad /sjuˈdad/, papel /paˈpel/. Voseo verb forms keep their final stress: tenés /teˈnes/, sentate /senˈtate/, cantá /kanˈta/. Enclitics do not move the stress: dámelo /ˈdamelo/, poniéndose /poˈnjendose/. Adverbs in -mente carry two stresses, written ˌ on the adjective and ˈ on -mente: rápidamente /ˌrapidaˈmente/. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so none is marked. Doubled letters are not geminates: carro /ˈkaro/ has the trill, and acción is /akˈsjon/.

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 13 (stress) and ch. 15 (stressed and unstressed words). Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, ch. on accent. Voseo stress follows Vidal de Battini, B. E. (1964), El español de la Argentina, and Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Argentina.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Words that are unstressed in connected speech never get a stress mark, even when they have two syllables. These are: definite articles (el, la, lo, los, las); prepositions other than según (para /paɾa/, desde /desde/, hacia /asja/, con, de, en, por); conjunctions (y /i/, o, pero /peɾo/, que, si, aunque /au̯nke/, porque /poɾke/); object, reflexive and clitic pronouns (me, te, se, lo, la, le, nos, les); prenominal possessives (mi, tu, su, nuestro /nwestɾo/ before a noun); and relative or conjunctive que, quien, donde /donde/, cuando /kwando/, como /komo/. Stressed words are marked when they have two or more syllables: indefinite articles (una /ˈuna/, unos /ˈunos/); subject and prepositional pronouns (ella /ˈeʝa/, nosotros /noˈsotɾos/; vos stays unmarked as a monosyllable); demonstratives (este /ˈeste/); post-nominal possessives (mío /ˈmio/); interrogatives and exclamatives (¿cuándo? /ˈkwando/, ¿dónde? /ˈdonde/); numerals; adverbs; según /seˈɡun/; and all verb forms, including auxiliaries (había /aˈβia/ is written /aˈbia/; estaba /esˈtaba/).

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 15 (classification of stressed and unstressed words). Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Words are written identically at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside a phrase: final vowels are kept, and final /s/, /n/ and /d/ are written as in the citation form, e.g. verdad /beɾˈdad/, más /mas/.

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish (no pausal morphology in Spanish). Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Argentina (no prepausal alternations relevant to a broad transcription).

## Connected speech

No cross-word process is written. Every word keeps its citation form in its own place. Not written are: synalepha or vowel merger (la amiga /la aˈmiɡa/, de esa /de ˈesa/); resyllabification (los otros /los ˈotɾos/, with no linking mark or syllable dot); nasal place assimilation (un perro /un ˈpero/, en casa /en ˈkasa/); voicing of /s/ before a voiced consonant (las manos /las ˈmanos/, mismo /ˈmismo/); and spirantization of /b d ɡ/ (la boca /la ˈboka/, lado /ˈlado/). The same broad phonemic choices hold inside words: written m and n stay as written (tango /ˈtanɡo/, campo /ˈkampo/).

Source: Broad phonemic practice as in Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 7 and 8 (these processes are described as allophonic, so they are left out of a broad transcription). Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas.

## Glides and diphthongs

Prevocalic (rising) glides are written j and w: tierra /ˈtjera/, cielo /ˈsjelo/, bueno /ˈbweno/, cuatro /ˈkwatɾo/. Postvocalic (falling) glides are written i̯ and u̯: aire /ˈai̯ɾe/, peine /ˈpei̯ne/, causa /ˈkau̯sa/, muy /mui̯/, hoy /oi̯/, rey /ˈrei̯/ is written /rei̯/. In ui and iu the second vowel is the syllable nucleus and the first is the glide: ciudad /sjuˈdad/, cuidado /kwiˈdado/. Written-accent hiatus stays as two vowels: día /ˈdia/, país /paˈis/, baúl /baˈul/. Word-initial hie- and orthographic y are /ʝ/: hielo /ˈʝelo/, yo /ʝo/. Hue- is /w/: hueso /ˈweso/. The conjunction y is always /i/.

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 10 (glides and diphthongs; j w versus i̯ u̯ notation). Initial ʝ follows the spec's yeísmo note (Lipski 1994).

## Other choices

### ll and y (yeísmo)

Following the spec note, ll and y before a vowel are both written /ʝ/: calle /ˈkaʝe/, llover /ʝoˈβeɾ/ is written /ʝoˈbeɾ/, mayo /ˈmaʝo/. The Buenos Aires sounds [ʒ] and [ʃ] are not used, because the northwest has a non-strident palatal. Rural lateral /ʎ/ retention is not written.

Source: Spec note (yeísmo, Lipski 1994). Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Argentina (non-rehilado palatal in the northwest). Vidal de Battini, B. E. (1964), El español de la Argentina (pockets of /ʎ/ retention noted but not followed).

### c, z and s (seseo)

c before e or i, z and s are all written /s/: cielo /ˈsjelo/, zapato /saˈpato/, caza /ˈkasa/.

Source: Spec note (seseo, Lipski 1994).

### Coda /s/

Syllable-final and word-final s is always written /s/: los pastos /los ˈpastos/, este /ˈeste/. This follows the conservative highland (Jujuy, Salta) pronunciation. The aspiration or loss heard further south and east in the northwest is not written.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Argentina (retention of /s/ in the Andean northwest). Fontanella de Weinberg, M. B. (1992), El español de América.

### r and rr

The tap is /ɾ/: pero /ˈpeɾo/, flor /floɾ/. Write /r/ for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l and s: perro /ˈpero/, rato /ˈrato/, honra /ˈonra/. The northwest's assibilated realisation of /r/ and of the cluster tr is published as allophonic and is not written, so tres is /tɾes/.

Source: Vidal de Battini, B. E. (1964), El español de la Argentina (assibilated rr and tr in the northwest). Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Argentina. Notation follows Hualde (2005), ch. 9.

### j and g before e or i

Write the velar fricative /x/: gente /ˈxente/, ajo /ˈaxo/, mujer /muˈxeɾ/.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 8. Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Argentina.

### Unstressed vowels

Write the five vowels /a e i o u/ in full in every position. The Andean-adstrate weakening or reduction of unstressed vowels is not written: pues /pwes/, entonces /enˈtonses/.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Andean-zone discussion (unstressed vowel reduction described as a variable phonetic feature).

### Intervocalic d in -ado

Always write /d/: cansado /kanˈsado/, todo /ˈtodo/. Casual deletion is not written.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 8. Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Argentina.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
