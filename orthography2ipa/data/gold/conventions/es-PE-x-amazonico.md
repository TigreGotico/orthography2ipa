# Transcription conventions: Amazonic Peruvian Spanish (es-PE-x-amazonico)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: casa /ˈkasa/, café /kaˈfe/, sol /ˈsol/, árbol /ˈaɾbol/, rápido /ˈrapido/. Placement follows Spanish lexical stress as the orthography shows it. A written accent marks the stressed syllable. With no accent, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s stresses the penultimate syllable (joven /ˈxoben/); any other word stresses the final syllable (ciudad /sjuˈdad/, papel /paˈpel/). Enclitics attached in the spelling stay in the host word and leave its stress in place: dámelo /ˈdamelo/. Adverbs in -mente take two stresses, and both are marked: rápidamente /ˈrapidaˈmente/. Write no secondary stress anywhere else. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so these do not apply.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP, ch. 13 (stress); Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, ch. on accent. Amazonic Peruvian descriptions (Escobar 1978; Caravedo 1992) report no difference in stress placement from general Spanish.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and carry no ˈ mark: definite articles el la lo los las (/el/, /la/); prenominal possessives mi tu su nuestro (/mi/, /su/); object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos les (/me/, /se/); all prepositions except según (a /a/, de /de/, en /en/, con /kon/, por /poɾ/, para /paɾa/); conjunctions y e o u pero que si porque (/i/, /o/, /peɾo/, /ke/); relative que quien donde cuando como (/ke/, /donde/); the vocative/title forms don doña. The following are stressed and carry ˈ: nouns, adjectives, adverbs, numerals, demonstratives (este /ˈeste/), subject and prepositional pronouns (yo /ˈʝo/, él /ˈel/, mí /ˈmi/), stressed possessives (mío /ˈmio/), indefinite articles un una (/ˈun/, /ˈuna/), interrogatives and exclamatives with written accent (qué /ˈke/, dónde /ˈdonde/), según /seˈɣun/ written /seˈɡun/, and all verb forms including auxiliaries and ser/estar (ha /ˈa/, es /ˈes/, está /esˈta/).

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, §13.2 (stressed vs. unstressed words); Quilis (1993) for the class lists. The analysis is general Spanish; Amazonic descriptions report no deviation.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, and every word is written in the same form at a comma, at sentence end and sentence-internally.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish (no pausal morphology in Spanish).

## Connected speech

Write every word in its citation form, with no cross-word process. Not written: resyllabification of final consonants (los otros /los ˈotɾos/); vowel synalepha (la_obra /la ˈobɾa/); nasal place assimilation (un poco /ˈun ˈpoko/, en casa /en ˈkasa/); voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants (mismo /ˈmismo/, las manos /las ˈmanos/); /s/ weakening or aspiration. The same broad treatment applies inside words. Write /b d ɡ/ in all positions, never [β ð ɣ] (cada /ˈkada/, lago /ˈlaɡo/). Write n for the spelled letter before velars (tengo /ˈtenɡo/), and write m only where the spelling has m (campo /ˈkampo/).

Source: Broad phonemic practice of Hualde (2005), chs. 7–8 (allophony treated as predictable), and Quilis (1993). The aspiration and weakening of coda /s/ reported for Amazonic speech by Escobar (1978) and Caravedo (1992) are allophonic and variable, so they are not written.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write prevocalic glides in rising diphthongs as j w: bien /ˈbjen/, ciudad /sjuˈdad/, bueno /ˈbweno/, huevo /ˈwebo/. Write postvocalic glides in falling diphthongs as i̯ u̯: aire /ˈai̯ɾe/, hoy /ˈoi̯/, auto /ˈau̯to/, deuda /ˈdeu̯da/. Write muy as /ˈmui̯/. Hiatus with an accented or stressed high vowel is written as two full vowels: río /ˈrio/, país /paˈis/. The conjunction y is /i/ in every context. Consonantal y and ll are the fricative /ʝ/, never j: yo /ˈʝo/, calle /ˈkaʝe/.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 6 (glides and diphthongs) and §10 (palatal /ʝ/); Navarro Tomás, T. (1918), Manual de pronunciación española. Yeísmo follows Lipski (1994).

## Other choices

### seseo and yeísmo

Write c before e/i and z as /s/: cielo /ˈsjelo/, zapato /saˈpato/. Write ll and y as /ʝ/: lluvia /ˈʝubja/. There is no /θ/ and no /ʎ/.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish; Lipski (2011), pp. 1–26.

### rhotics

Write the trill /r/ word-initially, after n l s, and for rr: rosa /ˈrosa/, honra /ˈonra/, perro /ˈpero/. Write the tap /ɾ/ elsewhere, including word-finally: pero /ˈpeɾo/, mar /ˈmaɾ/. Assibilated realizations reported for Peruvian varieties are not written.

Source: Hualde (2005), ch. 10. Assibilation noted in Escobar (1978), Variaciones sociolingüísticas del castellano en el Perú, IEP, and in Caravedo (1992), but not written in this broad transcription.

### velar fricative and /f/

Write j and g before e/i as /x/: jefe /ˈxefe/, gente /ˈxente/. Write f as /f/: fuego /ˈfweɡo/. This is the standard pronunciation of the lect, and it agrees with the note's baseline. Amazonic descriptions report glottal [h] for /x/ and variable bilabial [ɸ] or [xw] for /f/ (e.g. [ˈhwego]). These are allophonic or socially marked, so they are not written.

Source: Escobar (1978), section on castellano amazónico; Caravedo, R. (1992), 'Espacio geográfico y modalidades lingüísticas en el español del Perú', in C. Hernández Alonso (ed.), Historia y presente del español de América.

### other consonants

Write ch /tʃ/ (chico /ˈtʃiko/), ñ /ɲ/ (año /ˈaɲo/), and h as silent (hora /ˈoɾa/). Write x as /ks/ (examen /ekˈsamen/), except in México and its derivatives, where it is /x/. Write qu and gu before e/i as /k/ and /ɡ/; gü before e/i is /ɡw/. Indigenous place names and loanwords are written with Spanish phonemes as spelled: Iquitos /iˈkitos/, Ucayali /ukaˈʝali/.

Source: Hualde (2005), chs. 9–10; Real Academia Española (2011), Ortografía de la lengua española.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
