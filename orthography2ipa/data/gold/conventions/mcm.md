# Transcription conventions: Kristang (Papia Kristang, Malacca Creole Portuguese) (mcm)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable in every polysyllable and every stressed monosyllable (see function_words). Stress is phonemic. Apply these rules in order. (1) A written acute marks the stressed syllable: kazá /kaˈza/ 'marry' vs kaza /ˈkaza/ 'house'; falá /faˈla/; kumí /kuˈmi/; papiá /papiˈa/. (2) An unaccented word ending in a vowel takes stress on the penultimate syllable: jenti /ˈdʒenti/, nubu /ˈnubu/, kaza /ˈkaza/. (3) An unaccented word ending in a consonant takes final stress when it continues a Portuguese oxytone. These are mostly words in -ng from Portuguese -ão, -ã, -om, and words in -l, -r or -s from final-stressed etyma: Kristang /krisˈtaŋ/, kurasang /kuraˈsaŋ/, irmang /irˈmaŋ/, ingles /inˈɡles/. Consonant-final Malay and English loans stay penultimate: kampong /ˈkampoŋ/. This rule refines the spec note, which says 'penultimate unless accented'. Rule (2) follows that note. Rule (3) follows Baxter's statement that the inherited stress of Portuguese etyma is kept. (4) A syllabic nasal counts as a syllable: ngua /ŋ̍ˈua/, nsentu /n̩ˈsentu/. Place ˈ before the whole onset, but put the nasal of a homorganic NC cluster in the preceding coda: kanggrezu /kaŋˈɡrezu/, kambrang /kamˈbraŋ/. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply to this lect: it has no length contrast, no gemination and no case morphology.

Source: Baxter 1988, §2.6 (cited via the APiCS survey because printed pp. 33-48 are missing from the scan), contrastive stress p. 29; Baxter 2013, APiCS ch. 42, orthography and stress rule; Baxter & de Silva 2004, dictionary headword accents.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Write function words in their full citation vowels; never reduce them to ə. Leave these monosyllabic grammatical words without ˈ: the preverbal TMA markers ja /dʒa/ (perfective), ta /ta/ (progressive) and lo /lo/ (irrealis); the prepositions na /na/, di /di/, pa /pa/ and ku /ku/ (also 'and'); the possessive sa /sa/; the complementizer and relativizer ki /ki/; the conjunctions i /i/ and ma /ma/; the monosyllabic personal pronouns yo /jo/, bos /bos/ and nus /nus/. Example: yo ja bai /jo dʒa ˈbaj/. Give ˈ to these: polysyllabic pronouns and determiners (eli /ˈeli/, olotu /oˈlotu/, isti /ˈisti/, akeli /aˈkeli/), negators (nang /ˈnaŋ/, nadi /ˈnadi/), the existential and possessive verb teng /ˈteŋ/, question words, the numeral or indefinite ngua /ŋ̍ˈua/, and all content words. A monosyllabic pronoun takes ˈ only when the sentence marks it as contrastive or emphatic, for example when it is fronted or isolated by punctuation.

Source: Baxter 1988, ch. 3-4 (preverbal markers and pronouns as unstressed particles), §2.3 pp. 29-30 (reduction is confined to unstressed vowels and is not written in broad transcription).

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Kristang has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Final vowels are written in full in every position, at commas and at the end of the sentence alike: kaza /ˈkaza/.

Source: Baxter 1988, ch. 2; Baxter 2013, APiCS ch. 42 (no pausal alternations described).

## Connected speech

Do not transcribe any cross-word process. Write each word in its citation form in its own place: no nasal place assimilation across the boundary (mang ku /ˈmaŋ ku/), no vowel elision or coalescence at vowel-vowel boundaries (ja anda /dʒa ˈanda/), no linking consonants and no resyllabification. Coda /r/ instability, as in pertu ~ petru ~ petu, is lexical variation within a word: write the form the spelling gives.

Source: Baxter 1988, §2.2-2.3, pp. 23-30 (fast-speech variation described as optional; not part of the phonemic norm); Baxter & de Silva 2004 spellings.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vowels as /j w/, never i̯ u̯. ⟨i u⟩ after a vowel in the same syllable become /j w/, giving the diphthongs /aj ej oj uj aw ew ow/: pai /ˈpaj/, mai /ˈmaj/. ⟨i⟩ between vowels becomes /j/: oiu /ˈoju/, praia /ˈpraja/. ⟨u⟩ after k or g and before a vowel, from Portuguese qu and gu, becomes /w/: kuatru /ˈkwatru/, kuantu /ˈkwantu/. In all other cases ⟨i u⟩ before a vowel are syllabic in hiatus: dia /ˈdia/, papiá /papiˈa/, ngua /ŋ̍ˈua/. Any ⟨y⟩ or ⟨w⟩ in the text is written /j w/.

Source: Baxter 1988, Table 2.2 and §2.2, pp. 23-28 (glides as non-syllabic realisations of /i u/).

## Other choices

### ⟨e⟩ and ⟨o⟩ vowel quality

In stressed syllables, write /ɛ ɔ/ where the Portuguese etymon has open é and ó, and /e o/ where it has close ê and ô: festa /ˈfɛsta/, porta /ˈpɔrta/, kumeh-type close vowels /e/. Unstressed syllables, word-initial syllables and word-final vowels are neutralized: write /e o/ there. Write /ə/ only in Malay loans where the Malay source has schwa. The eight-vowel inventory is /i e ɛ a ɔ o u ə/.

Source: Baxter 1988, pp. 23-27 and p. 219.

### Rhotic

Always write /r/. Tap and trill are in free variation and are not distinguished: rosa /ˈrosa/, karu /ˈkaru/.

Source: Baxter 1988, p. 23.

### Consonant letter values

ch → /tʃ/, j → /dʒ/, ny → /ɲ/, ng → /ŋ/, ngg → /ŋɡ/ (Malay-style spelling), z → /z/, v → /v/ (marginal: novi /ˈnovi/), and b stays /b/: nubu /ˈnubu/. There is no /ʃ ʒ ʎ h/. Final consonants are limited to /s r l m n ŋ/.

Source: Baxter 1988, Table 2.1 p. 20, pp. 22-23; Baxter 2013, APiCS ch. 42.

### Nasal vowels

Write no nasal vowels. A historic nasal vowel is written as an oral vowel plus the nasal written in the spelling: mang /ˈmaŋ/, bong /ˈboŋ/.

Source: Baxter 1988, p. 219.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
