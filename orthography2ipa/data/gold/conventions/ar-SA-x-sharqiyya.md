# Transcription conventions: Eastern Saudi (Sharqiyya) Arabic (ar-SA-x-sharqiyya)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable on every word of two or more syllables, except the unstressed function words listed under function_words. Monosyllables are not marked. Syllable weight: CV is light. CVV, CVC, CVːC and CVCC are heavy. A long vowel (ː) makes its syllable heavy. A geminate is written as a double consonant and is split across the syllable boundary (sab.bah), so it closes, and makes heavy, the syllable before it. Rule, applied from the right edge of the word: (1) a final superheavy syllable (CVːC, CVCC) takes the stress: [kiˈtaːb], [ʃaˈribt]. (2) Otherwise a heavy penult takes it: [ˈmadrasa]? no, see rule 3; [mudarˈrisa], [ˈbeːti], [ˈɡaːlat]. (3) Otherwise the stress goes back to the antepenult: [ˈmadrasa], [ˈkatabat]. In a disyllable with two light syllables, the stress goes on the first: [ˈkatab], [ˈɡalam]. Count suffixed pronouns and the feminine -a as part of the word: [ˈbeːtah], [biˈteːhum]? no, write [beːˈtuhum] only when that is the vocalised form; otherwise apply the same rule to the form as written. Classical case endings and tanwīn are not pronounced (see pausal_forms), so they never count toward stress. The article il- never carries stress: [ilˈbeːt], [ilˈmadrasa].

Source: Gulf/Eastern Arabian stress rule (final superheavy > heavy penult > antepenult) as given in Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic (Routledge Descriptive Grammars), §2 phonology, and for Bahraini/Baḥārna in Holes 2016, Language, Variation and Change in Bahrain; consistent with Johnstone 1967 ch. on Eastern Arabian. The specification notes do not state a stress rule; ˈ marking is this convention's choice.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ mark: the article il-/l- (part of its host word); the proclitics w-/u- 'and', b-/bi- 'with, in' and future bi-, l-/li- 'to, for', ʕa- 'on'; the monosyllabic prepositions fi [fi], min [min], ʕan [ʕan]; the negators ma [ma], la [la], mu/mub [mu]/[mub]; the subordinators/complementizers in [in] and illi [illi]; the conjunctions aw [aw] 'or' and bas [bas] 'but'; and all pronoun suffixes (-ah/-a, -ha, -hum, -ik/-ak 2ms, -itʃ/-tʃ 2fs, -i/-ni, -na, -kum). Stressed, with stress placed by the stress rule: independent pronouns [ˈana], [ˈinta], [ˈinti], [ˈhuwa], [ˈhiya], [ˈiħna], [ˈhum]; disyllabic prepositions and adverbs such as [ˈʕala], [ˈʕind]? (a monosyllable, so unmarked [ʕind]), [wiˈjjaː]→ write [ˈwijja] 'with', [ɡidˈdaːm] 'in front of'; auxiliaries and aspect verbs kaːn [kaːn] (monosyllable, unmarked), [ˈɡaːʕid] (progressive), [ˈbaːtʃir]; and demonstratives [ˈhaːða], [ˈhaːði], [ˈðaːk]. Proclitic و: write [w] before a vowel, including the article ([wilˈbeːt]), and [u] before a consonant ([uˈmħammad] → [umˈħammad], [ubiˈnt] → [uˈbint]).

Source: Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic, chapters on pronouns, prepositions and particles; Holes 2016 for Bahraini/Baḥārna forms; the 2fs suffix in -tʃ follows Johnstone 1967 and Al-Taisan 2022 p.81.

## Pausal forms

Write no separate pausal form anywhere, at the end of a sentence, at a comma or inside the sentence. The dialect has no case inflection, so every word is written in its one dialect form in all positions. Classical case vowels (-u, -a, -i) and tanwīn (-un, -an, -in) are dropped even when the input carries the harakat: كتابٌ → [kiˈtaːb], ولداً → [ˈwalad]. Lexical final short vowels that are part of the dialect word are kept everywhere: [ˈana], [ˈhuwa], [ˈinti]. Tāʾ marbūṭa is [a] when not in construct: [ˈmadrasa]. It is [at] in construct and before suffixes: [madraˈsat ilˈwalad] (write [ˈmadrasat] by the stress rule), [madˈrasathum]. There is no final lengthening, devoicing or added vowel at pause.

Source: Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic, morphology of nouns (no case, -a/-at feminine); Johnstone 1967 on Eastern Arabian; matches the specification's input contract (harakat are read, but case endings are a Classical feature that is not realised in the dialect).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its own form and place. Only two cross-boundary changes are written. (1) After a word that ends in a vowel, the initial vowel of the article il- and of any word-initial epenthetic or hamzat-al-waṣl i- is elided, and the word after the boundary loses that vowel: [fi lˈbeːt], [ˈʕala lˈmeːz], [ma ʃtiˈɣal]. After a consonant or at the start of an utterance the i- stays: [min ilˈbeːt]. (2) Sun-letter assimilation of the article is written inside the host word in every position: [iʃˈʃams] → [iʃˈʃams], [is ˈsuːɡ] written [isˈsuːɡ], [itˈtamr]; after a vowel, [fi ssuːɡ] is written [fi sˈsuːɡ]. Do not write any other process across boundaries: no voicing or place assimilation (min ˈbeːt stays [min], not [mim]), no gemination across words, no added linking vowels, and no moving a consonant into the next word.

Source: Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic, phonology section on the article and elision of helping vowels; Holes 2016 for Bahraini usage.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides only as the consonants [j] and [w], never as [i̯] or [u̯]. A geminate glide is written doubled: [ˈɡawwi], [ˈwijja]. Classical *ay and *aw are written as the Gulf monophthongs [eː] and [oː] in colloquial words: بيت [beːt], يوم [joːm], لون [loːn]. Write [aj]/[aw] only in learned or MSA lexemes where the diphthong is kept, for example [ˈdawla]. The diphthong is never written as V+[i̯]. Long vowels are [aː iː uː eː oː]. Do not write the allophonic backing or rounding of /aː/ to [ɑː]/[ɒː]; write it as [aː].

Source: Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic, vowel phonology (monophthongisation); Johnstone 1967; [ɒː] rounding treated as sub-phonemic per Alaodini 2019 pp.52-83.

## Other choices

### jīm ج

Always write [dʒ]: [dʒa] 'he came', [ˈdʒiːraːn]? write as [dʒiːˈraːn], [riˈdʒaːl], [fiˈriːdʒ]. Never write [j] for jīm. The [j] reflex belongs to the Sunni ʕArab/Dawāsir variety and is outside this lect. No [ʒ].

Source: Al-Taisan 2022 p.12, p.20, p.166; Holes 1980/2016 via Alaodini 2019 p.94; follows the specification note (primary reflex [dʒ]).

### qāf ق

In colloquial core vocabulary, write [ɡ]: [ɡaːl], [ɡalb], [ˈɡahwa], [ɡidˈdaːm]. Write [q] in religious, learned and MSA-register words: [qurˈʔaːn], [qaːˈnuːn], [θaˈqaːfa]. Write [q] also in a word that the transcriber knows belongs to the stable local q-stratum. Before or after /i iː/ in a colloquial word, the [ɡ] reflex is written [dʒ]: رفيق [riˈfiːdʒ], قِدر [dʒidir]? write [ˈdʒidir]. Never write [dz].

Source: Al-Taisan 2022 pp.13, 79, 81 (Example 4-3b), 166; Prochazka 1988; Johnstone 1967. The specification notes allow this, though the conditioning is lexical.

### kāf ك

In colloquial words, write [tʃ] for k in contact with a front vowel (i, iː, eː), especially before it: [tʃalb]? write [tʃalb] (a lexicalised affricate), [tʃiˈbiːr], [tʃeːf], [ˈsamatʃ]. The 2fs suffix is always -itʃ/-tʃ: [ˈbeːtitʃ], [ˈʕaliːtʃ]? write [ʕaˈleːtʃ]. Keep [k] next to back vowels and in learned words, and for the 2ms suffix -ak/-ik: [kitaːb] → [kiˈtaːb] (learned), [ˈbeːtak]. Never write [ts].

Source: Johnstone 1967 (Eastern Arabian kaškaša); Al-Taisan 2022 p.81; specification rule GULF_K_AFFRICATION.

### interdentals and ḍād/ẓāʾ

Write ث as [θ] and ذ as [ð]. Write both ض and ظ as [ðˤ]: [ˈðˤahar], [ʔarðˤ] → [arðˤ]. The other emphatics are [tˤ] and [sˤ].

Source: Al-Taisan 2022 p.13; Alaodini 2019 p.42.

### back fricatives

Write غ [ɣ], خ [x], ح [ħ], ع [ʕ] and ه [h]. Do not write uvular [ʁ]/[χ], and do not model the lexical q~ʁ alternation.

Source: Holes 1990 symbol conventions; Al-Taisan 2022's q~ʁ alternation is lexically conditioned and is left out, per the specification note.

### hamza

Never write a word-initial glottal stop: [ˈana], [akl]. In colloquial words, medial or final hamza is lost, with a long vowel where the dialect has one: رأس [raːs], بئر [biːr], ماء [maːj]. In learned words, write medial or final [ʔ]: [qurˈʔaːn], [masˈʔala].

Source: Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic, phonology (loss of hamza).

### short vowels and vocalisation

Write short vowels as the harakat give them. Where the input is unvocalised, write only long vowels and the consonants, and do not supply short vowels by guesswork. This follows the specification's input contract, although published descriptions do give the dialect's short-vowel patterns, such as raising and elision in open syllables.

Source: Specification note (input contract). This differs from the full vocalisation given in Holes 1990 and 2016.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
