# Transcription conventions: Benasquese (patués, Benasque valley) (an-x-benasque)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every polysyllabic word. Leave monosyllables unmarked, whether stressed or not. Placement, applied in this order: (1) A written accent (à, è, é, í, ò, ó, ú) marks the stressed syllable: chemecà [tʃemeˈka], chirà [tʃiˈɾa], Benás [beˈnas]. (2) Without an accent, a word ending in a vowel, a vowel plus s, or a vowel plus n is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkasa], feito [ˈfei̯to], vaques [ˈbakes]. (3) Without an accent, a word ending in any other consonant or in a falling diphthong is stressed on the last syllable: perdiu [perˈdiu̯], fort [ˈfort]? no: fort is a monosyllable, so [fort]; llagó [ʎaˈɡo]. When counting syllables, ie, ue, ua and the falling diphthongs ai, ei, oi, au, eu, iu each count as one syllable. Because of apocope of -e/-o, many words are oxytone; do not restore the lost vowel. Stressed mid vowels take the seven-vowel values (see 'other'). The lect has no long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply.

Source: Saura Rami (2003), pp. 43-71 (stress and the seven-vowel tonic system), as reported in Giralt Latorre (2006), AFA LXI-LXII, pp. 419-420. Placement follows the Catalan-type graphic accentuation of Benasquese spelling (grave à/è/ò, acute é/ó), as in Ballarín Cornel, Vocabulario de Benasque (2nd ed. 1978).

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed and never carry ˈ: definite and indefinite articles (la casa [la ˈkasa]); object and reflexive clitics, including the adverbial clitics corresponding to en/ne and i/bi, whether standing alone or joined by a hyphen (me, te, se, mos, tos, li, les); monosyllabic prepositions (a, de, en, con, per, ta: de Benás [de beˈnas]); monosyllabic conjunctions (i, u, que, se); and monosyllabic auxiliary forms of aber and of the copula. The following carry their lexical stress by the rule above: polysyllabic prepositions, conjunctions and adverbs (pero [ˈpeɾo], dende [ˈdende]); strong pronouns; possessives; polysyllabic auxiliary forms. Unstressed function words use only the five unstressed vowels a e i o u, never ɛ or ɔ.

Source: General Ibero-Romance prosodic practice for High Aragonese as described by Nagore Laín (2001), Gramática de la lengua aragonesa (clitics and articles), applied to the Benasquese forms given in Saura Rami (2003), morphosyntax chapters, via Giralt Latorre (2006).

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Benasquese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Each word is transcribed the same way at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside the sentence. Final vowels are kept or lost only as the lexical (spelled) form shows: casa [ˈkasa] stays [ˈkasa] at a pause.

Source: Saura Rami (2003), pp. 43-106, via Giralt Latorre (2006): no pausal alternations are described.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation (isolation) form. Do not mark processes across word boundaries:
- Do not write nasal place assimilation across a boundary: en Benás [en beˈnas], not [em].
- Do not write synalepha or vowel elision unless the spelling shows it with an apostrophe. In that case the elided item is its own word, written by its remaining sounds: l'aigua → [l ˈai̯ɡwa].
- Do not write resyllabification; there are no syllable marks.
- Do not write sibilant voicing in liaison, because the lect has no voiced sibilants: dos ombres [dos ˈombres], never [doz].
- Word-final obstruents are voiceless in every context.
Inside a word, write nasal assimilation before velars and labials: banco [ˈbaŋko], [mb] in ombres. This contrasts with Catalan, where liaison voicing [z] is written.

Source: Saura Rami (2003), pp. 71-106 (no /z ʒ dʒ/), via Giralt Latorre (2006), p. 421. Word-internal nasal assimilation follows the Hispanic broad tradition (Navarro Tomás / RFE, as used by Saura).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write rising diphthongs with the consonantal glides j and w before the vowel:
- ie → [je]
- ue → [we]
- ua → [wa]
- gü → [ɡw]
Examples: güeit [ɡwei̯t], aigua [ˈai̯ɡwa]. Write falling diphthongs with the non-syllabic vowels i̯ and u̯ after the vowel: feito [ˈfei̯to], nèu [nɛu̯], perdiu [perˈdiu̯]. A written i or u between vowels is [j] or [w]. ⟨y⟩ before a vowel is [j]. The i of ⟨ix⟩ is not a glide; ⟨ix⟩ is just [ʃ] (see 'other').

Source: Hispanic RFE/Quilis tradition (Quilis 1993, Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas), which Saura Rami (2003) follows. This choice is made over the Catalan IEC practice of using j/w in all positions. The diphthong inventory follows Saura pp. 43-59, via Giralt Latorre (2006).

## Other choices

### vowel qualities

Stressed vowels use seven qualities: /a e ɛ i o ɔ u/. Spelled è and ò are [ɛ] and [ɔ] (nèu [nɛu̯]). Spelled é and ó are [e] and [o]. Unaccented stressed e and o are [e] and [o] unless the source spelling marks them open. The e of ie and ue is [e]. Unstressed vowels use only /a e i o u/. There is no reduction to [ə] or [u], unlike Central Catalan: vaques [ˈbakes]. Zone-A final -a raising is written only when the spelling shows it.

Source: Saura Rami (2003), pp. 43-71 (seven vowels, four apertures in tonic position), via Giralt Latorre (2006), pp. 419-420.

### sibilants and palatals

Map spellings as follows:
- ⟨z⟩, and ⟨c⟩ before e/i, are [s] (seseo; there is no θ).
- ⟨j⟩, ⟨g⟩ before e/i, and ⟨ch⟩ are [tʃ]; there is no x.
- ⟨x⟩ and ⟨ix⟩ are [ʃ].
- ⟨ll⟩ is [ʎ] (llagó [ʎaˈɡo]); there is no yeísmo.
- ⟨ny⟩ and ⟨ñ⟩ are [ɲ].
- Spelled palatalised clusters keep [ʎ]: pllanre [ˈpʎanre], cllau [kʎau̯].
- Never write z, ʒ or dʒ.

Source: Saura Rami (2003), pp. 71-106, via Giralt Latorre (2006), pp. 420-421. This agrees with the specification notes.

### stops and rhotics

Write ⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩ as [b], and ⟨d⟩ and ⟨g⟩ as [d] and [ɡ], in all positions. Do not write the approximant allophones [β ð ɣ]. Spelled word-final b, d, g are [p t k]. Rhotics: write [r] for ⟨rr⟩, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l or s. Write [ɾ] elsewhere, including codas. Infinitives spelled without -r get no r (chirà [tʃiˈɾa]). ⟨h⟩ is silent.

Source: Broad Hispanic tradition (Navarro Tomás; Quilis 1993) applied to Saura Rami (2003), pp. 71-106. Final devoicing follows the Catalan/Ribagorzan neutralisation pattern that Saura describes for apocopated forms.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
