# Transcription conventions: East Timorese Portuguese (pt-TL)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Put one primary-stress mark ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable of every content word, including stressed monosyllables: [ˈpaj] pai, [ˈbɔ] bom? no, bom is [ˈbõ]. Do not write syllable dots or secondary stress. Find the stressed syllable with the standard Portuguese written-accent rules. (a) A written acute, circumflex or tilde marks the stressed syllable: café [kaˈfɛ], você [voˈse], irmã [iɾˈmã]. (b) With no accent, words ending in a, e, o (plus s), am or em take penultimate stress: casa [ˈkaza], falam [ˈfalãw̃]. (c) Words ending in r, l, z, i, u, im, um, om or ns take final stress: falar [faˈlaɾ], papel [paˈpɛl], aqui [aˈki]. One exception follows the paroxytone tendency of this variety: proparoxytones, which carry a written accent on the antepenult, move their stress to the penult. Examples: rápido [raˈpido], público [puˈbliko], lâmpada [lamˈpada]. Oxytones keep final stress, because final consonants are retained. Stressed mid vowels keep their standard lexical open or close quality: avó [aˈvɔ], avô [aˈvo]. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Portuguese has no phonemic length, no geminates and no case morphology.

Source: Albuquerque 2010:279–280 (paroxytone tendency, near-absence of proparoxytones in PTL); the lexical stress baseline is Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (EP), via Albuquerque 2010:276.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed: they get no ˈ but keep full vowels, with no reduction. Definite articles: o [o], a [a], os [os], as [as]. Simple prepositions and their contractions: de [de], em [ẽj̃], a [a], por [poɾ], com [kõ], sem [sẽj̃], para [paɾa], do [do], da [da], no [no], na [na], ao [aw], à [a], pelo [pelo], num [nũ]. Conjunctions and complementisers: e [e], ou [o], que [ke], se [se], mas [mas], nem [nẽj̃]. Clitic pronouns, including hyphenated ones as separate words: me [me], te [te], se [se], lhe [le], nos [nos], vos [vos], o [o], a [a], lo [lo], la [la]. The relative que [ke] is also unstressed. The following are stressed and get ˈ. All verbs, including auxiliaries and copulas: é [ˈɛ], está [esˈta], foi [ˈfoj], tem [ˈtẽj̃], há [ˈa]. Strong and subject pronouns: eu [ˈew], ele [ˈele], mim [ˈmĩ], você [voˈse]. Demonstratives, possessives, numerals and the indefinite articles um [ˈũ] and uma [ˈuma]. Negation não [ˈnãw̃]. Interrogatives. Disyllabic prepositions other than para: sobre [ˈsobɾe], entre [ˈentɾe], desde [ˈdesde], contra [ˈkontɾa].

Source: Standard Portuguese clitic/stress classes (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, baseline via Albuquerque 2010:276). The full unstressed vowels follow the no-reduction trait in Albuquerque 2010:275 fn.7.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so every word is written the same at a comma, at sentence end and sentence-internally. Final vowels are never deleted or devoiced, and final consonants are always written: falar [faˈlaɾ], not [faˈla]. Basilectal infinitive apocope is not transcribed.

Source: Albuquerque 2010:278–279 documents apocope (abraçar [aˈba.sa]) only for the basilectal 'português acrioulado' end; it is excluded here, following the acrolectal norm in the spec.

## Connected speech

Only one sandhi process is written: the voicing of word-final ⟨s⟩, ⟨z⟩ or ⟨x⟩ within a phrase, applied to the final consonant of the first word. Write [z] before a word beginning with a vowel or a voiced consonant: os amigos [oz aˈmiɡos], os dois [oz ˈdojs], mas eu [maz ˈew]. Write [s] before a voiceless consonant and before any punctuation or pause: os pais [os ˈpajs]. Do not apply this across punctuation. Final ⟨r⟩ stays [ɾ] before a vowel, unchanged: falar a [faˈlaɾ a]. The following are not written: vowel elision or crasis between words beyond the orthographic contractions (de amor [de aˈmoɾ]), resyllabification, and place assimilation of nasals. Each word keeps its own IPA form in its own slot.

Source: Standard Portuguese sibilant sandhi (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000). The alveolar rather than postalveolar outcome follows the alveolar coda /s/ norm in Albuquerque 2010:277.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vowels as [j] and [w], never i̯ or u̯; nasal glides are [j̃] and [w̃]. Falling diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], meu [ˈmew], noite [ˈnojte], primeiro [pɾiˈmejɾo], mãe [ˈmãj̃], pão [ˈpãw̃], põe [ˈpõj̃]. Rising sequences after ⟨q⟩ or ⟨g⟩: quando [ˈkwando], água [ˈaɡwa]. Post-tonic unstressed i or e before a final vowel becomes a glide: história [isˈtɔɾja], série [ˈsɛɾje]. A pretonic or stressed vowel sequence elsewhere stays in hiatus as full vowels: piano [piˈano], saúde [saˈude], dia [ˈdia]. ⟨ou⟩ is the monophthong [o]: outro [ˈotɾo], sou [ˈso].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (EP glide analysis, baseline via Albuquerque 2010:276). ⟨ei⟩ as [ej] rather than Lisbon [ɐj] follows the absence of [ɐ] in PTL (Albuquerque 2010:275 fn.7).

## Other choices

### unstressed vowels

There is no reduction. Unstressed a, e and o are written [a], [e] and [o] in every position, including final: bate [ˈbate], roda [ˈɾɔda], casa [ˈkaza], menino [meˈnino]. Stressed a is also [a] before nasals: cama [ˈkama]. Never write [ɐ], [ə] or [ɨ]. The one exception is word-initial unstressed ⟨es⟩ or ⟨ex⟩ before a consonant, written [is]: escola [isˈkɔla], estar [isˈtaɾ]. This rule overrides the earlier example esta [esˈta] given under function_words, which should read [isˈta]. Basilectal raising of e to i and o to u elsewhere is not written.

Source: Albuquerque 2010:275 fn.7 (bate, roda); p.277 (escola [isˈkɔ.la]); raising elsewhere is basilectal, pp.276–277.

### rhotics

Write [r] for word-initial ⟨r⟩, ⟨rr⟩, and ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s: rua [ˈrua], carro [ˈkaro], honra [ˈonra]. Write [ɾ] elsewhere, including clusters and codas: caro [ˈkaɾo], três [ˈtɾes], perguntar [peɾɡunˈtaɾ]. Never write [ʁ] or [ʀ].

Source: Albuquerque 2010:278.

### coda sibilants

Coda ⟨s⟩, ⟨z⟩ and ⟨x⟩ are alveolar: [s] before a voiceless consonant or a pause, [z] before a voiced consonant: escola [isˈkɔla], mesmo [ˈmezmo], luz [ˈlus]. Lisbon [ʃ]/[ʒ] is not used, even though Albuquerque records [iʃˈkɔla] as a variant. ⟨ch⟩ and ⟨x⟩ as onsets remain [ʃ]: chá [ˈʃa]. ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩ before e or i remain [ʒ]: já [ˈʒa]. ⟨v⟩ and ⟨f⟩ remain [v] and [f]; the basilectal substitutions ʃ→s, ʒ→z, v→b and f→p are not written.

Source: Albuquerque 2010:276–277.

### palatal laterals and nasals

⟨lh⟩ is written [l] and ⟨nh⟩ [n]: olho [ˈolo], vinho [ˈvino], senhor [seˈnoɾ]. This follows the spec's first-listed simplified variant. The palatals [ʎ] and [ɲ], which Albuquerque attributes to speakers nearest the European norm, are not written. Albuquerque's own examples carry an extra vocoid ([ˈo.liu]); that is not transcribed.

Source: Albuquerque 2010:276.

### voiced stops and laterals

⟨b⟩, ⟨d⟩ and ⟨g⟩ are always the stops [b d ɡ], with no EP spirantisation to β ð ɣ: cada [ˈkada], lago [ˈlaɡo]. ⟨l⟩ is [l] in all positions, never [ɫ] or [w]: mal [ˈmal]. ⟨t⟩ and ⟨d⟩ before i are not palatalised: dia [ˈdia]. ⟨h⟩ is silent.

Source: Albuquerque 2010:276 lists no such allophones for PTL, unlike the EP baseline of Mateus & d'Andrade 2000. The machine-generated gold's ð/ɣ/w are rejected per the spec note.

### nasal vowels

Nasal vowels are written [ã ẽ ĩ õ ũ] before a coda nasal letter or with a tilde: campo [ˈkampo], ponte [ˈponte]. Here a homorganic nasal consonant is written before a stop, and the vowel itself is left unmarked for nasality. Elsewhere use a nasal vowel with no consonant: sim [ˈsĩ], bom [ˈbõ], irmã [iɾˈmã]. Final -em/-ém is [ẽj̃] and -am/-ão is [ãw̃]: tem [ˈtẽj̃], falam [ˈfalãw̃]. Nasal diphthongs are not denasalised: educação [edukaˈsãw̃].

Source: Albuquerque 2010:278 (denasalisation is basilectal); baseline Mateus & d'Andrade 2000.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
