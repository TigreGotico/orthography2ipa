# Transcription conventions: Algarvio Portuguese (pt-PT-x-algarve)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, including stressed monosyllables: [ˈtydu] tudo, [ˈsyɫ] sul, [ˈɛ] é, [ˈpaj] pai. Unstressed clitics (see function_words) carry no mark. Place stress by the orthographic rule. A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed syllable: [kɐˈfɛ] café, [ˈlɐ̃pɐdɐ] lâmpada. With no accent mark, words ending in -a, -e, -o, -am, -em, -ens (with or without -s) stress the penult: [ˈkazɐ] casa, [ˈfalɐ̃w̃] falam. All other words stress the final syllable: [fɐˈlaɾ] falar, [ɐˈʒiɫ] ágil is an exception because of its written accent, [ˈaʒiɫ]. Do not mark secondary stress. Stress conditions the vowel rules, so it must be placed first. Only stressed syllables keep full [a ɛ e ɔ o i u/y]. Unstressed syllables reduce (see other). Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so these do not apply. Double rr is a single [r], and double ss is a single [s].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), The Phonology of Portuguese, ch. 6 (word stress); Cunha & Cintra (1984), Nova Gramática, ch. on accentuation and the orthographic accent.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, written without ˈ:
- definite articles o a os as [u ɐ uʃ ɐʃ];
- indefinite articles um uma uns umas [ũ umɐ ũʃ umɐʃ], with no fronting because they are unstressed;
- prepositions and contractions de do da dos das [dɨ du dɐ duʃ dɐʃ], em no na nos nas [ɐ̃j̃ nu nɐ nuʃ nɐʃ], a ao à aos [ɐ aw a awʃ], por pelo pela [puɾ pɨlu pɨlɐ], com [kõ], para [pɐɾɐ], sem [sɐ̃j̃];
- conjunctions e [i], ou [o], que [kɨ], se [sɨ], mas [mɐʃ], nem [nɐ̃j̃];
- object and reflexive clitics me te se lhe nos vos o a lhes [mɨ tɨ sɨ ʎɨ nuʃ vuʃ u ɐ ʎɨʃ], also when hyphenated as their own word: diz-me [ˈdiʒ mɨ], viu-o [ˈviw u];
- relative que [kɨ].
Stressed, with ˈ:
- all nouns, lexical verbs, adjectives and adverbs;
- auxiliaries and copulas é [ˈɛ], está [ɨʃˈta], tem [ˈtɐ̃j̃], foi [ˈfoj], vai [ˈvaj];
- negation não [ˈnɐ̃w̃];
- subject and prepositional pronouns eu [ˈew], tu [ˈty], ele [ˈelɨ], nós [ˈnɔʃ], mim [ˈmĩ];
- demonstratives and possessives;
- numerals, including um 'one' [ˈũ];
- interrogatives, including quê [ˈke].
The contractions o/os/no/nos/do/dos/ao/aos/pelo/pelos/um/uns never front, as the spec's proclitic guard requires.
Disagreement with the spec: it writes mas as [maʃ]/[maʒ] and the preposition a as stressed [ˈɐ]. This convention follows the standard reduced unstressed forms [mɐʃ] and [ɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 (clitics and prosodic words); Cunha & Cintra (1984), chapters on article, preposition and pronoun forms.

## Pausal forms

Portuguese has no pausal forms in the Arabic sense. It has no case endings and no nunation, and final vowels are not altered at pause. Write [ɐ ɨ u] in full and do not write the optional central-southern deletion of final -u/-e: [ˈsestu] cesto, not [ˈsest]. The one pause-sensitive alternation is coda /s, z/ at the end of a word. Before the end of the sentence and before a comma or other punctuation, it is [ʃ]: [ˈtydu ˈbɐ̃j̃ ˈmajʃ], [ˈlyʃ], [mɐʃ] at a comma.
Disagreement with the spec: it writes word-final [ʒ] categorically, even before pause (mas → [maʒ]). This convention follows the published description of final-sibilant voicing, which has [ʃ] at pause.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 (coda fricatives: voiceless before pause); Cintra (1971: 81–116) and Brissos (2014) on final-vowel deletion as a non-categorical central-southern feature (not transcribed).

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its own citation form. The one exception is the final sibilant, which is decided by the first sound of the next word.
- Word-final /s, z/ (spelled -s, -z) before a vowel-initial word is [ʒ]. This is the Algarve [ʒ] sandhi, where Lisbon and the North have [z]: [ɨʃˈtaʒ ɐ ˈveɾ] estás a ver, [ˈmũj̃tuʒ ɐˈmiɡuʃ] muitos amigos, [ˈɛʒ u ˈnumɨɾu ˈũ] és o número um.
- Before a voiced consonant it is [ʒ]: [uʒ ˈdiɐʃ] os dias.
- Before a voiceless consonant or at pause it is [ʃ]: [ˈmajʃ ˈtaɾdɨ] mais tarde, [ˈtɾeʃ] três.
Word-internal coda /s/ follows the same voicing rule: [ˈeʃtɨ] este, [ˈmeʒmu] mesmo.
Do not write any of the following:
- vowel elision or crasis across words: write [dɨ ɐˈmiɡuʃ], not [dɐˈmiɡuʃ];
- liaison of final /ɾ/ or /ɫ/ (keep [ɾ], [ɫ]: [fɐˈlaɾ ɐˈʃi], [ˈmaɫ ɐˈsĩ]);
- nasal place assimilation across words;
- resyllabification.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 (voicing assimilation of coda fricatives across word boundaries); Portuguese With Leo (2021), 'The 8 Portuguese accents of Portugal' (Algarve prevocalic [ʒ] for [z]; popular, non-academic source).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic glides as [j] and [w], never as [i̯ u̯].
- Oral diphthongs: [ˈpaj] pai, [ˈmaw] mau, [ˈsɛw] céu, [ˈew] eu, [ˈfoj] foi, [ˈmɔjɐ] mói-a, [ˈfuj] fui.
- Nasal diphthongs carry tilde on both parts: [ˈnɐ̃w̃] não, [ˈmɐ̃j̃] mãe, [ˈpõj̃ʃ] pões, [ˈmũj̃tu] muito, [ˈtɐ̃j̃] tem.
- The orthographic diphthongs ei and ou are monophthongised, the southern pattern, in all positions: [ˈletɨ] leite, [pɾiˈmeɾu] primeiro, [fɐˈle] falei, [ˈotɾu] outro, [ˈpoku] pouco. The ou monophthong also applies to unstressed conjunction ou [o].
- Disagreement with the spec: it writes -em/-ens as Lisbon [ɐ̃j̃]. Do not use Lisbon [ɐj] for ei.
- Vowels in hiatus stay syllabic, with no inserted glide: [ˈdiɐ] dia, [piˈɐnu] piano, [ˈsuɐ] sua (stressed u after s, so see the u-fronting rule: [ˈsyɐ]).

Source: Cintra (1971: 81–116) on ei > e and ou > o monophthongisation as a centro-meridional feature; Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 on glides and nasal diphthongs.

## Other choices

### Stressed /u/ fronting (Barlavento)

Write stressed oral /u/ as [y]: [ˈtydu] tudo, [ˈlymɨ] lume, [ˈsyɫ] sul, [ˈlyʃ] luz, [ˈty] tu, [ɐˈʃyʃ] (as written), [ˈsyɐ] sua.
Exceptions, which stay [u]:
- after a bilabial onset /p b m/: [ˈmudu] mudo, [ˈpuru] puro, [ˈbuʃu] bucho;
- unstressed /u/: [tyˈdu]-type forms never occur, so write [puˈdeɾ] poder;
- nasal /ũ/: [ˈmũdu] mundo, [ˈʒũtu] junto;
- /u/ as the first element of the diphthong /uj/: [ˈfuj] fui.
Do not write [ʉ].
Disagreement with the spec: its own example mudo → [ˈmydu] is superseded by its bilabial guard, and [ˈmudu] is used.

Source: Cintra (1971); Segura da Cruz (1989: 434–456); Brissos (2014, JPL 13(1): 63–115); Brissos (2014b, APL Textos Selecionados: 87–89) for the bilabial guard and [y] in the Barlavento; Brissos & Saramago (2014: 55–59, 65).

### Unstressed vowel reduction

Use the standard European reduction. Unstressed /a/ is [ɐ], unstressed /e/ is [ɨ], and unstressed /o/ is [u]: [ˈkazɐ] casa, [ˈlymɨ] lume, [ˈgatu] gato, [mɨˈninu] menino, [kuˈmeɾ] comer. Word-initial unstressed es-/ex- before a consonant is [ɨʃ]: [ɨʃˈtaʃ] estás. Do not write the spec's [e] in this position, and do not write schwa for final -o.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 (vowel reduction); against Brissos & Saramago (2014: 57), who record final <o> as schwa in parts of the Centro-Sul (not followed).

### Stressed mid vowels before palatals

Before the palatals /ʎ ɲ ʒ ʃ/ and before /j/, keep stressed e as [e]. Do not centralise it to the Lisbon [ɐ]: [ˈteʎɐ] telha, [ˈveɲu] venho, [ˈfeʃu] fecho. Stressed a before a nasal consonant is [ɐ]: [ˈkɐmɐ] cama. Elsewhere stressed a is [a], and the [a] → [ɛ] chain shift is not written. Write [ɛ ɔ] versus [e o] lexically: [ˈvɛʎu] velho is an exception by lexical [ɛ], [ˈpɔʃtɐ] posta, [ˈveɾ] ver.

Source: Cintra (1971: 81–116) on centralisation before palatals as a Lisbon-area feature; Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2.

### Rhotics

Write [r] for word-initial r, for rr, and for r after a consonant in a different syllable: [ˈrɔzɐ] rosa, [ˈkaru] carro, [ˈɔ̃ɾɐ] honra is an exception in practice, so write [ˈõrɐ]. Write [ɾ] elsewhere, including codas: [ˈkaɾu] caro, [fɐˈlaɾ] falar. Do not write uvular [ʁ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2 (alveolar trill versus uvular variant; the alveolar trill retained outside Lisbon).

### Laterals and sibilants

Write coda /l/ as velarised [ɫ]: [ˈsyɫ] sul, [ˈmaɫ] mal. Use [ɫ] only, never [ł]. Write onset /l/ as [l]. Write sibilants as [s z ʃ ʒ]. Do not write the apico-alveolar [s̺]. Keep /b/ and /v/ distinct: [ˈvɐ̃w̃] vão versus [ˈbɐ̃w̃].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2; Cintra (1971) on apical sibilants and the absence of betacism in the South.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
