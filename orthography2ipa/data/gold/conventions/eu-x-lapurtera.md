# Transcription conventions: Lapurdian Basque (Lapurtera) (eu-x-lapurtera)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the second syllable of every content word of two or more syllables, counting from the left edge of the whole written word. Disyllables are therefore stressed on the final syllable. A content monosyllable takes ˈ before its only syllable. A diphthong (ai̯, ei̯, oi̯, ui̯, au̯, eu̯) counts as one syllable. A vowel sequence in hiatus (i.a, e.a, u.a) counts as two. Examples: etxe [eˈtʃe], etxea [eˈtʃea], gizona [ɡiˈs̻ona], gizonarekin [ɡiˈs̻onaɾekin], mendia [menˈdia], gaitza [ɡai̯ˈts̻a] (two syllables, so final stress). Case endings, the article -a/-ak and other suffixes never move the stress, because it is counted from the left: etxe [eˈtʃe], etxetik [eˈtʃetik], etxeetarik [eˈtʃeetaɾik]. Basque has no phonemic vowel length and no geminates, so these play no part. A doubled vowel across a morpheme boundary is written as two vowels in hiatus: etxeetan [eˈtʃeetan]. Lexical exceptions are not marked; the rule applies to loanwords and names too. Ignore the singular/plural accent contrasts reported for some Western varieties.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2003), 'Accentuation', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque, ch. 1 (phonology); Hualde (1999), 'Basque accentuation', in van der Hulst (ed.), Word Prosodic Systems in the Languages of Europe. I follow the 'Western-Central' second-syllable type as the general Lapurdian/Navarro-Lapurdian pattern. I do not follow the penultimate system described for Souletin and eastern Low Navarrese, or the pitch-accent systems of Western Basque. The specification notes give no stress rule.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words carry no ˈ and are transcribed unstressed. (1) Inflected auxiliaries after a participle or other host: da, du, dut, naiz, zen, zuen, dira, ditu, dezake, nintzen, as in etorri da [eˈtori da] and ikusi dut [iˈkus̺i dut]. (2) The conjunctions eta [eta] and edo [edo], and the subordinating forms ere [eɾe] and ba- when written separately. (3) Monosyllabic or clitic-like particles written as separate words: ote [ote], omen [omen], bide [bide], ohi [oi], al [al]. (4) Words split off by a hyphen, as the tool segments them. These words carry stress under the stress rule: nouns, adjectives, adverbs, numerals, participles and synthetic (non-auxiliary) verbs (dago [daˈɡo], dakit [daˈkit], doa [doˈa]); personal pronouns (ni [ˈni], hura [uˈɾa], gu [ˈɡu], zuek [s̻uˈek]); demonstratives (hau [ˈau̯], hori [oˈɾi], hura [uˈɾa]); interrogatives (nor [ˈnor], zer [ˈs̻er], non [ˈnon], nola [noˈla]); the negator ez [ˈes̺] (with the sandhi of connected_speech); bai [ˈbai̯]; and ba when it means 'yes'. An auxiliary placed first after ez, or fronted in a question, still takes no ˈ: ez da [ˈes̺ ta]. Basque has no articles or prepositions as separate words; the article and the postpositions are suffixes inside the host word.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Accentuation', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque, ch. 1, on the deaccented status of auxiliaries and particles in the phrase. Morphological status of the article and case suffixes follows Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (2003).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Basque has no pausal forms. Final vowels, case endings and the article are written the same at sentence end, at commas and medially (etxea [eˈtʃea] everywhere), and there is no nunation.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque, pp. 15-65, which reports no pausal alternations.

## Connected speech

Write only the sandhi of the negator ez before a following auxiliary or verb. It changes only the words concerned, and each word keeps its own slot. (a) After ez, a word-initial voiced stop is devoiced: b→p, d→t, ɡ→k. Examples: ez da [ˈes̺ ta], ez du [ˈes̺ tu], ez gara [ˈes̺ kaɾa], ez baita [ˈes̺ pai̯ta]. (b) Before a word-initial nasal or lateral (n, m, l), the s̺ of ez is dropped: ez naiz [ˈe nai̯s̻], ez luke [ˈe luke]. (c) Before vowels and before other consonants, ez is unchanged: ez zen [ˈes̺ s̻en], ez al [ˈes̺ al]. Write nothing else across word boundaries. That excludes vowel elision or contraction in hiatus (ura edan [uˈɾa eˈdan]), nasal place assimilation (egin behar [eˈɡin beˈar], not [m]), voicing assimilation of sibilants, and resyllabification marks or linking glides. Word-internal alternations are written as pronounced within the word, for example affrication of a sibilant after l, n, r in suffixed forms. Spirantization of b d ɡ is not written, because the transcription is broad.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque, pp. 15-65, sections on voicing assimilation and the negative particle ez with following auxiliaries. Northern (Navarro-Lapurdian) usage follows Oyharçabal (2003), 'The dialects of the Basque language'. The other optional connected-speech processes are left out to keep rows consistent.

## Glides and diphthongs

Falling diphthongs are written with non-syllabic marks: ai [ai̯], ei [ei̯], oi [oi̯], ui [ui̯], au [au̯], eu [eu̯]. Examples: bai [ˈbai̯], gaur [ɡau̯ˈr] (one syllable, so ˈ goes before it: [ˈɡau̯r]), euskara [eu̯s̺ˈkaɾa]. Orthographic j is the palatal approximant [j], as in Northern Basque: jan [ˈjan], jaun [ˈjau̯n], ja [ˈja]. Orthographic y is also [j]. Orthographic i or u before a vowel within a word is written as a vowel in hiatus, not as a glide: mendia [menˈdia], buruan [buˈɾuan], lehoia [leˈoi̯a]. Write no epenthetic [j] or [β] between vowels (mendia, not [mendija]). The notes do not decide j; [j] rather than Gipuzkoan [x] or Souletin [ʒ] follows the Lapurdian norm.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque, pp. 15-65, on diphthongs and dialectal values of j. Oyharçabal (2003), 'The dialects of the Basque language', for the Navarro-Lapurdian [j] realization.

## Other choices

### aspiration /h/

Write no [h] anywhere. Orthographic h is silent, following modern coastal Lapurdian (Bayonne, Biarritz, Saint-Jean-de-Luz): hura [uˈɾa], hiru [iˈɾu], ahal [aˈal], behar [beˈar]. Do not write aspirated stops: ph, th, kh are [p t k], as in ukho [uˈko]. This departs from the notes' variable h→[h]~[∅] in order to give one consistent choice. It also departs from inland Lapurdian and the classical (Axular) norm, which keep /h/.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2018), 'Aspiration in Basque', Papers in Historical Phonology 3: 1-27, p. 2 (citing Michelena, FHV VI: 168, loss of aspiration on the French Basque coast by the 19th century) and p. 5 (classical Lapurdian aspiration).

### rhotics

Use the inherited alveolar rhotics, not uvular [ʁ]. Orthographic rr and r before a consonant or word-finally are the trill [r]: herri [eˈri], zer [ˈs̻er], ardo [arˈdo]. Single r between vowels, including before a vowel-initial suffix, is the tap [ɾ]: hiru [iˈɾu], zerua [s̻eˈɾua]. A word-initial r (loans only) is [r]. The contact uvular mentioned in the notes is not written.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque, pp. 15-65, on the rhotic contrast and its neutralization. The specification notes describe the uvular only as a French-contact variant.

### sibilants and affricates

Keep the full three-way contrast. s = apical [s̺], z = laminal [s̻], x = [ʃ]; ts = [ts̺], tz = [ts̻], tx = [tʃ]. Examples: su [ˈs̺u], zu [ˈs̻u], xori [ʃoˈɾi], hotz [ˈots̻], etxe [eˈtʃe]. After l, n or r inside a word, s and z become the affricate: zaldiz written as is; e.g. ontsa [onˈts̺a]. All sibilants are written voiceless.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque, pp. 15-65; specification notes, feature (2).

### palatals and u

tt = [c], dd = [ɟ], ñ = [ɲ], ll = [ʎ]: ttipi [ciˈpi], anddere [aɲˈɟeɾe] is not assumed, so write [anˈɟeɾe]. Write no automatic palatalization of n, l, t after i unless the spelling shows it: mutila [muˈtila]. Orthographic u is [u], never Souletin [y]. b d ɡ are written as stops in every position.

Source: Specification notes, feature (3); Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque; Oyharçabal (2003) for the absence of [y] outside Souletin.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
