# Transcription conventions: Balearic Catalan (ca-x-balear)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, including monosyllables (pa [ˈpa], molt [ˈmolt]); mark no secondary stress. Place it as follows: (1) a written accent ⟨à è é í ò ó ú⟩ always carries the stress: política [poˈlitikə], podrà [poˈðɾa]; (2) an unmarked word ending in a vowel, in vowel+⟨s⟩ or in ⟨-en/-in⟩ is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkazə], cases [ˈkazəs], canten [ˈkantən]; (3) any other unmarked ending, including consonant+⟨s⟩ plurals, is stressed on the last syllable: cantar [kənˈta], parets [pəˈɾəts]. The diaeresis ⟨ï ü⟩ marks hiatus, not stress: veïna [vəˈinə]. Catalan has no phonemic vowel length and no case endings, so neither is written. Do not write ⟨l·l⟩ as a geminate; it is a single [l] (col·legi [koˈlɛʒi]). Geminates arise only in sandhi (see connected_speech). Majorcan stressed-vowel quality is covered under other.

Source: IEC 2016, Gramàtica de la llengua catalana ch. 3 (l'accent); Wheeler 2005 §3.1; Badia i Margarit 1994 as cross-check.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The atonic words in the IEC 'mots àtons' list carry no stress mark and take reduced vowels. Articles, including the Majorcan salat set: es [əs], sa [sə], ses [səs], so [so], el [əl], la [lə], els [əls], les [ləs]; the personal articles en [ən] and na [nə]. Contractions: des [dəs], pes [pəs], as [əs], del [dəl], al [əl], pel [pəl]. Atonic prepositions: a [ə], de [də], en [ən], amb [əm], per [pəɾ] / [pə] before a consonant. Conjunctions: i [i], o [o], que [kə], si [si], ni [ni], però keeps its stress. Weak pronouns, including the Majorcan forms mos and vos: me [mə], te [tə], se [sə], mos [mos], vos [vos], li [li], los [los], hi [i], ho [o], en [ən]; elided forms such as m', t', s', l', n' are written as their consonant alone. Everything else keeps its lexical stress. This includes auxiliaries and the copula (ha [ˈa], han [ˈan], és [ˈəs], són [ˈson]), strong pronouns (jo [ˈʒo], ell [ˈeʎ], ella [ˈəʎə]), demonstratives, possessives, numerals, adverbs and the stressed prepositions and conjunctions (sense [ˈsənsə], perquè [pəɾˈkɛ]).

Source: IEC 2016 §6.2 and the 'mots àtons' list; Wheeler 2005 §3.1; Majorcan article and pronoun forms per Veny 1982 ch. 4.

## Pausal forms

Catalan has no pausal morphology: there are no final short vowels to drop, no case endings and no nunation (tanwīn), so pausal forms do not apply. The end of the sentence and any punctuation mark (comma, colon, semicolon, dash) count as a pause. Before a pause, a word-final obstruent is voiceless (verd [ˈvəɾt], peix [ˈpəʃ], maig [ˈmat͡ʃ]) and no cross-word sandhi applies across it. Final ⟨-r⟩ is deleted in every position, pausal or not: cantar [kənˈta].

Source: Wheeler 2005 §5.3 (final-obstruent devoicing) and §10.2 (final-rhotic loss); Veny 1982 ch. 4.

## Connected speech

Write these processes within a phonological phrase, never across punctuation, changing the edge segments of each word while each word keeps its own place. (a) Voicing: a word-final sibilant voices before a vowel or a voiced consonant: ses aigües [səz ˈajɣwəs], es dia [əz ˈdiə]. A final stop voices before a voiced consonant (cap dia [ˈkab ˈdiə]) but stays voiceless before a vowel (cap home [ˈkap ˈɔmo]). (b) Nasal place assimilation of final /n/: en Pere [əm ˈpəɾə], un gat [uŋ ˈɡat]. (c) Spirantization: word-initial /b d ɡ/ becomes [β ð ɣ] after a vowel or a non-nasal, non-lateral continuant in the same phrase: sa dona [sə ˈðonə], es gat [əz ˈɣat]. They stay stops after a pause, a nasal or (for /d/) a lateral. (d) A final stop after /n l m ŋ/ is kept: molt alta [ˈmolt ˈaltə], camp [ˈkamp]. (e) Majorcan sibilant affrication: when the left word ends in vowel+[s] or vowel+[ʃ] and the right word begins with /s/ or /ʃ/, write the left sibilant as [t] and the right onset as [t͡s] or [t͡ʃ]: és sa [ˈət t͡sə], més són [ˈmet ˈt͡son], cossos sense [ˈkɔsot ˈt͡sənsə]. Do not apply it after the sonorant-cluster codas /ns rs ʎs ws/, after the codas /ks t͡s t͡ʃ/, or across punctuation. (f) Do not write vowel elision, synalepha or gliding across word boundaries, beyond what the apostrophe already shows; both vowels stay: sa amiga [sə əˈmiɣə]. (g) Do not write the total assimilation of a coda stop to a following consonant: aquests vailets [əˈkəts vəjˈlɛts], not [əˈkev vəjˈlɛv].

Source: Wheeler 2005 §5.2, §5.3 and §10.2–§10.5; Veny 1982 ch. 4 (Majorcan consonant assimilation, s#s affrication as in the 4catac Balearic annotation); Bonet & Lloret 1998 ch. 5 on vowel contact. The elision and gliding they describe as optional are deliberately not written.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vocoids as the consonant symbols [j] and [w], never as [i̯ u̯]. Falling diphthongs: mai [ˈmaj], cauen [ˈkawən], remei [rəˈməj], vailet [vəjˈlɛt]. Rising diphthongs after ⟨q/g⟩ + ⟨ua/uo⟩ (qualitat [kwəliˈtat], aigua [ˈajɣwə]) and word-initial ⟨i⟩ + vowel (iogurt [joˈɣuɾt]). ⟨ï ü⟩ and the stress rules mark hiatus, which is written with two syllabic vowels: veïna [vəˈinə], diari [diˈaɾi], país [pəˈis]. Intervocalic ⟨y⟩ does not occur; ⟨ny⟩ is [ɲ].

Source: Wheeler 2005 §2.5 (diphthong inventory); IEC 2016 ch. 3 on the diaeresis.

## Other choices

### Unstressed vowels

Use the Majorcan four-vowel system [ə i o u]. Unstressed /a e ɛ/ become [ə]; unstressed /o ɔ/ become [o] and do not raise to [u]; /i u/ stay as they are: xocolata [ʃokoˈlatə], podrà [poˈðɾa], portam [poɾˈtam], cucut [kuˈkut]. This follows the Majorcan instrumental description, not the general Eastern [ə i u] system that Recasens 1996 gives for Balearic (unstressed ⟨o⟩ → [u]).

Source: Llompart & Simonet 2018; Wheeler 2005 §2.3; Veny 1982 ch. 4 (against Recasens 1996).

### Stressed mid vowels and the Majorcan stressed schwa

Use seven stressed qualities plus [ə]: [a ə e ɛ i o ɔ u]. For stressed unmarked ⟨e⟩, write [ə] by default (the reflex of Latin ē/ĭ): cadena [kəˈðənə], pera [ˈpəɾə], negre [ˈnəɣɾə]. Write [ɛ] where the DCVB gives the open vowel (Latin ĕ): terra [ˈtɛrə], cel [ˈsɛl]. Write [e] where it gives the close vowel: més [ˈmes], ell [ˈeʎ]. ⟨è⟩ is [ɛ] (cafè [kəˈfɛ]); ⟨é⟩ is [ə] or [e] per the DCVB. Stressed ⟨o⟩ is [o] or [ɔ] per the DCVB: gos, tot, dos, molt [o]; cos, pont, font [ɔ]. ⟨ò⟩ is [ɔ] and ⟨ó⟩ is [o].

Source: Wheeler 2005 §2.3; Recasens 1996; Veny 1982 ch. 4; DCVB (Alcover & Moll 1962) by headword.

### Labiodental /v/

Keep /v/ as a phoneme distinct from /b/: ⟨v⟩ is [v], ⟨b⟩ is [b]/[β]: vaca [ˈvakə], beure [ˈbəwɾə], lliure [ˈʎiwɾə]. Do not merge them as Central does.

Source: Veny 1982 ch. 4; Wheeler 2005 §5.1.

### Consonant symbols

Write ⟨r⟩ between vowels and after an onset consonant as the tap [ɾ]; write word-initial ⟨r⟩, ⟨rr⟩ and ⟨r⟩ after ⟨n l s⟩ as the trill [r]: roba [ˈrɔβə], carro [ˈkaro], cara [ˈkaɾə]. ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g+e,i⟩ are [ʒ]. ⟨x⟩ and ⟨ix⟩ are [ʃ]: xot [ˈʃot], peix [ˈpəʃ]. ⟨tx⟩ and final ⟨-ig⟩ are [t͡ʃ]; ⟨tj tg⟩ are [d͡ʒ]; ⟨tz⟩ is [d͡z]. Write affricates with the tie bar. ⟨ll⟩ is [ʎ], with no yeísmo. Write /l/ as [l]; the velarized [ɫ] is not written. Majorcan palatalized [c ɟ] for /k ɡ/ is not written in this broad transcription: write [k ɡ].

Source: Wheeler 2005 §5.1; Recasens 1996; Ladefoged & Maddieson 1996 for the symbols.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
