# Transcription conventions: Panamanian Spanish (es-PA)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word of two or more syllables. Place the mark at the syllable onset by Spanish syllabification, counting an aspirated coda as part of the preceding syllable: estado [ehˈtao], hombre [ˈombɾe], cansado [kanˈsao]. Stress placement is lexical and follows the spelling. A written accent marks the stressed vowel: café [kaˈfe], árbol [ˈaɾbol], dámelo [ˈdamelo]. Without an accent, a word ending in a vowel, n or s is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkasa], joven [ˈhobeŋ], martes [ˈmaɾteh]. Any other word is stressed on the last syllable: ciudad [sjuˈda], comer [koˈmeɾ]. Monosyllables never carry the mark, whether stressed or not: pan [paŋ], sé [se], el [el]. Unstressed polysyllabic function words (see function_words) are written with no mark: para [paɾa]. Secondary stress is not written, and -mente adverbs get one mark only: rápidamente [rapidaˈmente]. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so none are written. A double vowel letter is written as two vowels: leer [leˈeɾ], creemos [kɾeˈemoh].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on word stress (orthographic stress rules, stressed vs unstressed words); the same standard description as Navarro Tomás and Quilis.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and carry no ˈ even when polysyllabic. Definite articles: el, la, los, las, lo. Object and reflexive clitics: me, te, se, lo, la, le, nos, les, los, las. Prenominal possessives: mi, tu, su, nuestro, nuestra and their plurals. All prepositions except según: a, de, en, con, por, para [paɾa], desde [dehde], hasta [ahta], entre [entɾe], sobre [sobɾe], hacia [asja]. Conjunctions: y, e, o, u, ni, que, si, pero [peɾo], porque [poɾke], aunque [awŋke], mas. The relatives que, quien, cual, cuyo, donde [donde], cuando [kwando] and como [komo]. Treatment words don, doña. The following are stressed and carry ˈ when polysyllabic: indefinite articles una [ˈuna], unos [ˈunoh]; subject and prepositional pronouns ella [ˈeʝa], usted [uhˈte], nosotros [noˈsotɾoh]; demonstratives este [ˈehte]; postnominal possessives mío [ˈmio]; interrogatives and exclamatives qué, cómo [ˈkomo], dónde [ˈdonde]; según [seˈɣuŋ] → written [seˈguŋ]. All verb forms are stressed, including auxiliaries: había [aˈbia], están [ehˈtaŋ], ha [a] (monosyllable, unmarked). Enclitics written joined to the verb are part of one word, stressed per spelling: dígamelo [ˈdigamelo].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on stress (list of inherently unstressed words; indefinite articles and verbs stressed), following Navarro Tomás' classification of palabras átonas/tónicas.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written the same at a pause, a comma or mid-sentence; the Caribbean coda rules (/s/ → [h], final /n/ → [ŋ], final /d/ → zero) apply in all positions, not only before pause: más [mah], pan [paŋ], verdad [beɾˈda].

Source: Hualde 2005 (coda processes); Fontanella de Weinberg 1992, Caribbean/Panamanian section (aspiration, velar n and -d loss as general coda phenomena).

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its citation form under the coda rules. Resyllabification, synalepha, vowel fusion and cross-word nasal or voicing assimilation are not written, and no syllable dots are used. Word-final s stays [h] before a vowel-initial word: los otros [loh ˈotɾoh]. Word-final n stays [ŋ] before any following sound: un beso [uŋ ˈbeso], en eso [eŋ ˈeso]. Vowels meeting across a boundary are each written in their own word with no merging or glide: casa amarilla [ˈkasa amaˈɾiʝa]. The single exception is the conjunction y: it is written [j] before a vowel-initial word and [i] elsewhere. Examples: y otro [j ˈotɾo], pan y vino [paŋ i ˈbino]. A word-initial r is always the trill: la rosa [la ˈrosa].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on syllabification and on the conjunction y as glide; aspiration of prevocalic word-final /s/ in Panamanian speech per Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 and Lipski 1994 (Panama chapter).

## Glides and diphthongs

Glides are written with consonant symbols [j] and [w], both as on-glides and as off-glides. Do not use i̯ or u̯. Examples: bien [bjeŋ], bueno [ˈbweno], aire [ˈajɾe], auto [ˈawto], hoy [oj], muy [muj], ciudad [sjuˈda]. Unstressed i or u next to another vowel is a glide. A written accent on i or u, and the sequences of two non-high vowels, are hiatus and are written as two vowels: país [paˈis], día [ˈdia], río [ˈrio], teatro [teˈatɾo]. Word-initial hie- and hue- are written [je] and [we]: hielo [ˈjelo], huevo [ˈwebo]. Consonantal y and ll are [ʝ], not [j] (see other).

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on diphthongs and glides (uses [j w] for both rising and falling diphthongs).

## Other choices

### /s/ aspiration and seseo

s, z and c before e/i are all /s/ (seseo): cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato]. Syllable-final /s/ (from s, z, or x before a consonant) is written [h], never deleted. This applies word-internally and word-finally: este [ˈehte], luz [luh], extra [ˈehtɾa]. Intervocalic x is [ks]: éxito [ˈeksito]. Onset /s/ stays [s].

Source: Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 (Caribbean zone incl. Panama); Hualde 2005 (seseo, aspiration). Optional deletion, frequent in casual Panamanian speech per Lipski 1994, is not written.

### /x/

j, and g before e/i, are written [h]: jota [ˈhota], gente [ˈhente], México [ˈmehiko].

Source: Fontanella de Weinberg 1992; Hualde 2005 (glottal realization in Caribbean and Central American Spanish).

### nasals

Word-final n is always [ŋ]: pan [paŋ], joven [ˈhobeŋ]. A word-internal coda nasal takes the place of the following consonant: campo [ˈkampo], banco [ˈbaŋko], cansado [kanˈsao], enfermo [emˈfeɾmo]. Before other consonants it is written [n]: canto [ˈkanto].

Source: Hualde 2005 (nasal place assimilation; velarization in Caribbean dialects); Fontanella de Weinberg 1992.

### /d/ loss and b d g

b and v are both [b]. b, d, g are written [b d g] everywhere; spirant allophones [β ð ɣ] are not written: lobo [ˈlobo], nada [ˈnada], agua [ˈagwa]. Word-final d is deleted: verdad [beɾˈda], usted [uhˈte]. Intervocalic d in the ending -ado(s) is deleted: cansado [kanˈsao], pescado [pehˈkao]. Intervocalic d is kept elsewhere: nada [ˈnada], cansada [kanˈsada].

Source: Hualde 2005 (b/v merger, d weakening); Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 (-d and -ado loss in Panama). This departs from Hualde's narrow notation by not writing spirants, per the broad-transcription brief.

### rhotics

The trill is [r] word-initially, after n, l or s, and for rr: rosa [ˈrosa], honra [ˈonra], perro [ˈpero]. Elsewhere the rhotic is [ɾ], including in the coda and word-finally: pero [ˈpeɾo], comer [koˈmeɾ]. Lateralization, deletion of infinitival -r and assibilation are not written.

Source: Hualde 2005 (rhotic distribution). The Panamanian variable forms reported by Lipski 1994 are not adopted as the standard.

### palatals, ch and h

ll and consonantal y are written [ʝ] (yeísmo): calle [ˈkaʝe], yo [ʝo]. ch is written [tʃ]: mucho [ˈmutʃo]; the deaffricated Panamanian [ʃ] is not written. ñ is [ɲ]. Letter h is silent: hora [ˈoɾa].

Source: Hualde 2005 (yeísmo, [ʝ], [tʃ]). Lipski 1994 notes Panamanian [ʃ] as sociolinguistically marked, so it is not used here.

### English loans

Loanwords from Canal Zone English contact are transcribed as nativized into Spanish phonology under the same rules, not with English phonemes: okey [oˈkej], wachimán [watʃiˈmaŋ].

Source: Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 and Lipski 1994 on Panamanian anglicisms; Hualde 2005 for the phoneme inventory.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
