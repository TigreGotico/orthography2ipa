# Transcription conventions: Northern Andean Chilean Spanish (es-CL-x-andino)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and is predicted from the spelling. A written accent marks the stressed vowel. Without an accent, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s takes penultimate stress; any other word takes final stress. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable (Spanish onset rules: one consonant goes to the following vowel, and so do the clusters stop/f + l/ɾ). Examples: casa /ˈkasa/, comen /ˈkomen/, ciudad /sjuˈdad/, árbol /ˈaɾbol/, canción /kanˈsjon/, sílaba /ˈsilaba/, dámelo /ˈdamelo/. Mark ˈ on every stressed word of two or more syllables. Leave stressed monosyllables unmarked (sol /sol/, pan /pan/, es /es/), as well as all unstressed function words. Adverbs in -mente keep two stresses: write ˌ on the adjective's stress and ˈ on -men-, e.g. rápidamente /ˌrapidaˈmente/. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Spanish has no phonemic vowel length, no geminate consonants and no case endings. Double letters are written as the sounds they spell: cc = /ks/ (acción /akˈsjon/), rr = /r/ (perro /ˈpero/), nn = /nn/ (innato /inˈnato/).

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on word stress (the orthographic stress rules and the stressed/unstressed word classes). Northern Chilean and Andean Spanish do not differ from general Spanish in stress placement (Lipski 1994, chapters on Chile, Bolivia and Peru).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed function words get no ˈ, even when they have two syllables. These are: definite articles (el, la, los, las, lo); all prepositions except según (a, de, en, con, por, para /paɾa/, desde /desde/, hasta /asta/, sobre /sobɾe/); conjunctions (y /i/, o, pero /peɾo/, que /ke/, si /si/, aunque /au̯nke/, porque /poɾke/); object and reflexive clitics (me, te, se, nos, os, lo, la, le, les); prenominal possessives (mi, tu, su, nuestro /nwestɾo/, nuestra); and relatives used non-interrogatively (que, quien, donde /donde/, como /komo/, cuando /kwando/). Proclitic titles are also unstressed (don, doña /doɲa/, san). The following carry stress and take ˈ when they have two or more syllables: indefinite articles (una /ˈuna/, unos /ˈunos/); demonstratives (este /ˈeste/, esa /ˈesa/); subject and prepositional pronouns (ella /ˈeʝa/, nosotros /noˈsotɾos/, mí, ti); interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent (qué, cómo /ˈkomo/, dónde /ˈdonde/); numerals; según /seˈɡun/; and all verbs, including auxiliaries (estaba /esˈtaba/, había /aˈbia/, he /e/, ha /a/). Example: la casa de mi padre /la ˈkasa de mi ˈpadɾe/.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on stress: the lists of inherently stressed and unstressed words.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, no case endings and no nunation. Each word is written in the same form at a comma, at the end of the sentence and inside the sentence. Final vowels and final /s/ keep their full broad form before a pause. Phrase-final devoicing or weakening of unstressed vowels, reported for Andean speech, is not written. Example: …y vino. /i ˈbino/.

Source: Hualde (2005) for the absence of pausal morphology in Spanish. The Andean devoicing of unstressed vowels is reported in Lipski (1994, chapters on Bolivia and Peru, the Andean zone); it is left out as sub-phonemic.

## Connected speech

No cross-word process is written. Each word keeps its citation (isolation) form in the broad transcription. The following are therefore not shown: resyllabification of a final consonant (los otros /los ˈotɾos/, not [lo.ˈso.tɾos]); synalepha or vowel merger (la amiga /la aˈmiɡa/; de ese /de ˈese/); nasal place assimilation (un poco /un ˈpoko/, en Bolivia /en boˈlibja/); voicing of /s/ before a voiced consonant (los mismos /los ˈmismos/); and spirantization of /b d ɡ/ after a vowel (la boca /la ˈboka/). The conjunction y stays /i/ before a vowel (y eso /i ˈeso/). Elision is never written.

Source: Hualde (2005) describes resyllabification, synalepha, nasal assimilation and spirantization as automatic, predictable allophonic processes in all varieties. The broad transcription leaves them out, keeping the phonemic level at which they are predictable.

## Glides and diphthongs

In rising diphthongs, write the prenuclear glides as j and w: tierra /ˈtjera/, bueno /ˈbweno/, ciudad /sjuˈdad/, cuota /ˈkwota/. In falling diphthongs, write the postnuclear glides as i̯ and u̯: aire /ˈai̯ɾe/, rey /rei̯/, hoy /oi̯/, muy /mui̯/, causa /ˈkau̯sa/, Europa /eu̯ˈɾopa/. In a triphthong both glides appear: buey /bwei̯/, Uruguay /uɾuˈɡwai̯/. Unaccented i or u next to another vowel is always a glide. An accented í or ú is a full vowel in hiatus: día /ˈdia/, país /paˈis/, búho /ˈbuo/. Word-initial hie- and hue- are written with the consonant /ʝ/ and with /w/: hielo /ˈʝelo/, huevo /ˈwebo/.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapters on vowels and on diphthongs/hiatus (the j w vs i̯ u̯ notation).

## Other choices

### y/ll (yeísmo vs /ʎ/)

Following the specification's note, ll and y are both written /ʝ/: calle /ˈkaʝe/, yo /ʝo/, llama /ˈʝama/. The /ʎ/ kept by some Aymara- and Quechua-contact highland speakers is not written.

Source: Specification note (yeísmo, after Lipski 1994). Lipski (1994, chapters on Bolivia and Peru) reports /ʎ/ retention in parts of the Andean highlands, and his chapter on Chile reports yeísmo as general. The specification is followed.

### c/z/s (seseo)

c before e/i, z and s are all written /s/: cielo /ˈsjelo/, zapato /saˈpato/, casa /ˈkasa/.

Source: Specification note; Lipski (1994).

### Syllable-final /s/

Always write /s/, whatever its position: estos /ˈestos/, más /mas/. General Chilean aspiration or loss ([h], ∅) is not written. The Andean adstrate zone tends to keep the sibilant, and broad transcription stays phonemic.

Source: Lipski (1994): Chile chapter (aspiration) and Andean chapters (sibilant retention). The phonemic level follows Hualde (2005).

### Stops /b d ɡ/ and intervocalic /d/

Write b, d, ɡ (IPA script ɡ) in every position, both inside and across words: lobo /ˈlobo/, cada /ˈkada/, lago /ˈlaɡo/. Do not write [β ð ɣ]. Do not write the Chilean loss of /d/ in -ado: cansado /kanˈsado/. v is written /b/: vaca /ˈbaka/.

Source: Hualde (2005) for spirantization as allophonic. Lipski (1994, Chile chapter) for the loss of intervocalic /d/.

### Rhotics

Write ɾ for single r between vowels and in codas: pero /ˈpeɾo/, mar /maɾ/. Write r for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l or s: perro /ˈpero/, rosa /ˈrosa/, honra /ˈonra/. The assibilated [ř] reported for Andean speech is not written.

Source: Hualde (2005) for the phonemic contrast. Lipski (1994, chapters on Bolivia and Peru) for Andean assibilation.

### j/g and ch

Write /x/ for j and for g before e/i, including the front contexts that are fronted in Chile: gente /ˈxente/, mujer /muˈxeɾ/. Do not write [ç] or [h]. Write ch as /tʃ/: chico /ˈtʃiko/. Do not write the Chilean variant [ts]. Write ñ as /ɲ/: año /ˈaɲo/. h is silent: hora /ˈoɾa/.

Source: Lipski (1994, Chile chapter) for the fronted [ç] before front vowels and the [ts] variant of ch. The phonemic symbols follow Hualde (2005).

### Unstressed vowels

Always write the five full vowels /a e i o u/ in unstressed syllables. Do not write the Andean reduction, centralization or e/i–o/u confusions: noches /ˈnotʃes/, pues /pwes/.

Source: Lipski (1994, chapters on Bolivia and Peru) for Andean unstressed-vowel reduction. The full vowels follow the five-vowel system of Hualde (2005).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
