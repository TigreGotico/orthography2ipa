# Transcription conventions: Hejazi Arabic (ar-SA-x-hejaz)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables. Monosyllables get no mark: beːt, muː, laː. Syllabify first. A single consonant between vowels starts the next syllable. A cluster or geminate is split, and its first consonant closes the preceding syllable. Then apply, from the end of the word: (1) A final superheavy syllable (CVːC, CVCC, CVːCC) takes the stress: kiˈtaːb, maˈħall, ʕaˈʃaːn. So does a final long vowel, which occurs only where 3ms -h has been lost after a vowel: ʕaˈleː. (2) Otherwise a heavy penult (CVː or CVC) takes it: maˈdaːris, muˈʕallim, ˈbeːtak, ˈʃukran, ˈkatab. (3) Otherwise the antepenult takes it: ˈmadrasa, ˈmaktaba, ˈʃadʒara, ˈkatabu, ʃaˈdʒaratu. Stress never falls before the antepenult. Long vowels and geminates make a syllable heavy. A final CVC is not superheavy. Case endings do not exist in Hejazi speech, so they never count. Lexicalised adverbial -an is an ordinary final CVC: ˈmasalan, ˈtabʕan. Suffixes count as part of the word, and stress moves to fit: ˈbeːtu, ˈbeːtaha, ˈbeːtakum. The definite article is a syllable of its host like any other: al-mad-ra-sa gives almadˈrasa, al-beːt gives alˈbeːt. This is the urban, sedentary, quantity-sensitive Levantine-type system. It is not the iambic Bedouin Hijazi system.

Source: Al-Mohanna 1998, stress and metrification chapters (urban Hijazi). Kabrah 2004 (Makkan, sedentary metrics with the (H́L) foot). Contrast Al Solami 2023 p.8 (Bedouin Bani Sulaim is iambic, urban Hijazi trochaic). Background: Watson 2002, stress chapter.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed words get no ˈ even when they have two syllables. These are: all prepositions (fi, min, ʕala, maʕa, ʕan, ʕind, li-, bi-, ka-); the relative ʔilli; the complementiser ʔinn- / ʔinnu; the conjunctions w-, ʔaw, walla, bas; the verbal negator ma; the vocative ja; the preverbal particles ħa-, raħ, b-. Write them as ʕala ʔilli ʔinnu, never ˈʕala. Clitics written joined to the host (w-, bi-, li-, ka-, the article al-, pronoun suffixes) are syllables of the host word. The host's stress rule applies to the whole: wilˈbeːt, bilˈʕarabi, ˈbeːtaha. Words that carry their lexical stress, and are marked when polysyllabic, are: independent pronouns (ˈʔana, ˈʔinta, ˈʔinti, ˈhuwa, ˈhiya, ˈʔiħna, ˈʔintu, ˈhumma), demonstratives (ˈhaːda, ˈhaːdi, haːˈdoːl), interrogatives (feːn, meːn, leːʃ, ʔeːʃ, kiːf, ˈkam), the nominal negator muː, multi-syllable conjunctions and adverbs (ʕaˈʃaːn, ˈlaːkin, ˈlamma, ˈʔiza), and auxiliary kaːn and its forms (ˈkaːnu, ˈkunt).

Source: Omar 1975, pronunciation guide pp.xi-xvi, and the lesson transcriptions (urban Jiddan usage). Almalky 2020 p.29 for the independent pronoun forms. Stress placement as in the stress convention (Al-Mohanna 1998).

## Pausal forms

Hejazi has no separate pausal form. The same form of each word is written at the end of a sentence, at a comma and in the middle of a phrase. The dialect has no case endings and no tanwīn. If vocalised input writes a case vowel or tanwīn on an ordinary noun or verb, it is not pronounced and not written: كِتابٌ is kiˈtaːb, البَيْتِ is alˈbeːt. The only exception is lexicalised adverbs in -an, which keep /an/ in every position: ˈmasalan, ˈʃukran, ˈtabʕan, ˈʔabadan. Final ة is -a in every position: ˈmadrasa. It is -at only in a construct before its possessor or a pronoun suffix, and this is a grammatical rule, not a pausal one: madˈrasat alˈbanaːt, madˈrasatu.

Source: Omar 1975, pp.xv-xvi and lesson transcriptions (no case or tanwīn in urban Hijazi). Holes 2004 (loss of case and nunation in the modern dialects). Watson 2002 (ta marbuta and the construct state).

## Connected speech

Two processes are written. (1) Inside a word, the article assimilates to a following coronal ('sun') consonant. These are t d s z ʃ sˤ dˤ tˤ zˤ n l r, including the t d dˤ that come from ث ذ ظ. Examples: aʃˈʃams, atˈtaːdʒir, adˈdˤuhr, anˈnaːs. There is no assimilation before dʒ, k, g or q: aldʒaˈmiːl, alˈgamar. (2) Across a word boundary, the vowel of the article (hamzat al-waṣl) is dropped after a word ending in a vowel. The article then becomes a bare l- or a doubled sun consonant on the second word: fi lˈbeːt, ʕala ʃˈʃaːriʕ. After a consonant, or at the start of an utterance, it stays al-: min alˈbeːt, alˈbeːt. When the vowel is dropped, the stress of the second word is recalculated on its remaining syllables: fi lˈbeːt, ʕala lmadˈrasa. Nothing else across word boundaries is written. Voicing assimilation, n→m before labials, linking, resyllabification and epenthesis are not shown, and no ‿ or other linking mark is used. A true hamza (أ إ) keeps its /ʔ/ in every position: ˈʔana, ˈʔinta. Each word stays one IPA word in its own place.

Source: Omar 1975, pp.xi-xvi (article al- with sun-letter assimilation, elision of the article vowel after vowels). Watson 2002, chapters on syllable structure and assimilation.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as the consonants j and w, never i̯ or u̯. Classical /aj/ and /aw/ in a closed syllable or at the end of a word are written as the monophthongs eː and oː in all inherited words: beːt, feːn, xeːr, joːm, loːn, moːt, sˤoːt. A sequence stays vowel + glide when the glide is geminate (sajˈjaːra, ˈʔawwal, ʕaraˈbijja) or followed by a vowel (ħajaˈwaːn, ˈjawadd); the same holds after a long vowel (ʃaːj). ي or و after a consonant is read by lexical knowledge. It is iː or uː where the word has a historical long vowel (kiːf, fuːg 'wake up', biːdˤ 'whites'). It is eː or oː where the word has historical aj or aw (foːg 'above', beːdˤ 'eggs'). It is j or w where it is a consonant (ˈbujuːt). Never write two long vowels in a row: ˈbujuːt, not biːuːt.

Source: Omar 1975, p.xv (/ee oo/ from Classical /ay aw/) and pp.xii, xvi (xeer, beet, feen, Soot, loon, moot). Abdoh 2010 (8-vowel system). Wikipedia 'Hejazi Arabic', vowel table (fuːg/foːg, biːdˤ/beːdˤ).

## Other choices

### qaf ق

Write ɡ in inherited vocabulary: gaːl, guːl, ˈgissˤa, giˈtˤaːr. Write q only in learned, religious or MSA words: qurˈʔaːn, ʔiqtiˈsˤaːd. Never write ʔ for ق.

Source: Omar 1975 p.xiii.

### interdentals ث ذ ظ

In inherited words write ث as t, ذ as d, and ظ as dˤ: taˈlaːta, ˈhaːda, dˤahr. In learned words write ث as s, ذ as z, and ظ as zˤ: ˈmasalan, niˈzˤaːm. Never write θ ð ðˤ, which belong to Bedouin speech. The spec note gives dˤ for ظ. Omar gives ẓ (zˤ), which is kept here for learned words only.

Source: Omar 1975 p.xiv ('The TH Consonants'). Al Solami 2023 and Al-Rohili 2019 pp.9-10 for the Bedouin retention.

### jiim ج

Always write dʒ: dʒaˈmiːl, ˈdʒoː. Aloufi 2016 p.84 describes urban Hijazi /ʒ/ instead. That variant is not used.

Source: Omar 1975, pp.xi-xvi. Abdoh 2010. Contrast Aloufi 2016 p.84.

### word-final vowel length

A final vowel in an unstressed syllable of a word with two or more syllables is short, even when the spelling has a mater: كتبوا is ˈkatabu, إنتي is ˈʔinti, كرسي is ˈkursi. This differs from the spec's long reading of matres. The 3ms suffix is -u after a consonant (ˈbeːtu). After a vowel it lengthens that vowel, and the result is stressed: ʕaˈleː, ʃaːˈfuː. Content monosyllables keep their long vowel (muː, laː, diː). Unstressed function monosyllables are short (fi, ma).

Source: Omar 1975 pp.xv-xvi. Watson 2002, syllable structure chapter (final vowel shortening). Wikipedia 'Hejazi Arabic' vowel table (ʕaˈleː, ˈmuː).

### hamza in inherited words

In everyday words that have lost a Classical medial or final hamza, write the dialect form: raːs, biːr, ˈjaːkul. In learned words, and word-initially, write ʔ: ˈʔana, suˈʔaːl.

Source: Omar 1975, pp.xi-xvi and lessons. Watson 2002.

### emphasis, geminates, short vowels

Write only the four emphatics sˤ dˤ tˤ zˤ. Do not write the backing of neighbouring vowels: write a, not ɑ, in tˤaːlib. Write the velarised lateral ɫ only in the word Allah and its compounds: aɫˈɫaːh. Write geminates as doubled consonants, not with ː: muˈʕallim. Short vowels are a i u only. Write ج as dʒ, never ʒ. Write چ as ʃ and گ as ɡ.

Source: Omar 1975 p.xiv (emphatics, ḷ in aḷḷaah). Abdoh 2010. Aloufi 2016 pp.130-131 and Alahmadi 2015 p.39 for ʃ in loans.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
