# Transcription conventions: Cantabrian (ast-x-cantabrian)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: sol [ˈsol], casa [ˈkasa], verdad [beɾˈðað], árbol [ˈaɾβol], teléfono [teˈlefono]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Placement follows standard Spanish spelling. A written accent marks the stressed vowel. Otherwise, words ending in a vowel, -n or -s are stressed on the penult: hablan [ˈaβlan], lunes [ˈlunes]. All other words are stressed on the final syllable: comer [koˈmeɾ], reloj [reˈlox]. Enclitic pronouns written solid with the verb do not move the stress: dámelo [ˈdamelo], decirle [deˈθiɾle]. Spanish has no phonemic vowel length, so no length mark is written. Orthographic double consonants are written as two consonants: innato [inˈnato], acción [akˈθjon]. Case endings do not apply. The 'cantado' montañés intonation is not transcribed.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on stress; Alarcos Llorach (1994), Gramática de la Lengua Española, section on the accent.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed words, written without ˈ: definite articles el la los las lo; possessive determiners before the noun mi tu su mis tus sus nuestro (before a noun); object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos os los las les; prepositions a ante bajo con contra de desde en entre hacia hasta para por sin sobre tras; conjunctions y e ni o u que pero mas sino aunque porque si; relatives and non-interrogative que quien cual donde cuando como; and tan. Examples: el perro [el ˈpero], para mí [paɾa ˈmi], que viene [ke ˈβjene]. Stressed words, written with ˈ: all verb forms, including auxiliary haber and copulas (ha venido [ˈa βeˈniðo], es [ˈes]); indefinite articles un una unos unas ([ˈun], [ˈuna]); subject and prepositional pronouns yo tú él ella mí ti nosotros usted; demonstratives; post-nominal possessives mío tuyo suyo; interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent, qué cómo dónde cuándo cuál quién ([ˈke], [ˈkomo]); según [seˈɣun]; numerals, adverbs and nouns.

Source: Quilis (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, list of tonic and atonic words; Hualde (2005), chapter on stress (unstressed function words); Alarcos Llorach (1994), section on the accent.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Final vowels and consonants are written the same at a pause as inside the sentence: final /d/ stays [ð] (verdad [beɾˈðað]) and final /s/ stays [s]. The one pause effect is that /b d g/ after a pause, that is at the start of the text, are stops: Dame [ˈdame].

Source: Hualde (2005), chapters on obstruent allophones and word-final consonants.

## Connected speech

Each word stays in its own slot and gets its own IPA. Three processes are written on the word they affect. (1) Spirantization: /b d g/ are written [β ð ɣ] whenever the preceding sound, in the same word or at the end of the previous word, is a vowel or a consonant other than a nasal; /d/ also stays [d] after /l/. Examples: la boca [la ˈβoka], los dedos [los ˈðeðos], un dedo [un ˈdeðo], el dedo [el ˈdeðo], mucho gusto [ˈmutʃo ˈɣusto]. (2) Nasal place assimilation: a word-final /n/ takes the place of the following word-initial consonant: un beso [um ˈbeso], en casa [eŋ ˈkasa], con fuerza [koɱ ˈfweɾθa]. Word-internal nasals are treated the same way: campo [ˈkampo], tengo [ˈteŋgo]. (3) Trill choice: r is written [r] at the start of a word, after n l s, and for ⟨rr⟩: la rosa [la ˈrosa], honra [ˈonra]; it is [ɾ] elsewhere. A final [ɾ] stays [ɾ] before a vowel: mar azul [ˈmaɾ aˈθul]. The following are not written: synalepha, vowel merger or glide formation across word boundaries (la ama [la ˈama], mi hijo [mi ˈixo]); resyllabification; voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants (mismo [ˈmismo], los mismos [los ˈmismos]); lateral or dental assimilation of /l/. Liaison does not apply.

Source: Hualde (2005), chapters on the allophones of /b d g/, nasal assimilation, rhotics and synalepha; Alarcos Llorach (1994), phonology section.

## Glides and diphthongs

An unstressed i or u next to another vowel in the same word is written [j] or [w] before the vowel (rising diphthongs): tierra [ˈtjera], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [θjuˈðað], cuidado [kwiˈðaðo]. It is written [i̯] or [u̯] after the vowel (falling diphthongs), including word-final ⟨y⟩: aire [ˈai̯ɾe], causa [ˈkau̯sa], hoy [ˈoi̯], muy [ˈmui̯], rey [ˈrei̯]. A written accent on í or ú marks hiatus, with no glide: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis], río [ˈrio]. ⟨hie-⟩ and onset ⟨y⟩ or ⟨ll⟩ are written [ʝ]: hielo [ˈʝelo], yo [ˈʝo]. ⟨hue-⟩ is written [w]: hueso [ˈweso]. The conjunction y is written [i].

Source: Hualde (2005), chapter on vowels, glides and diphthongs; Alarcos Llorach (1994).

## Other choices

### Standard versus dialectal Cantabrian (montañés/pasiego) features

Follow the standard northern Peninsular Castilian pronunciation of Cantabria. The following dialectal features are not written: final -o raised to [u] (cántabro [ˈkantaβɾo], not [-u]); plural -os raised to -us; Pasiego metaphony; aspiration of coda /s/ as [h] (las casas [las ˈkasas]); Liébana /ʎ/. This departs from the note's mention of variable /ʎ/ and western aspiration.

Source: Penny (2000), Variation and Change in Spanish, on northern Peninsular Spanish; Hualde (2005), standard Peninsular norm.

### Distinción and /s/

⟨z⟩, and ⟨c⟩ before e or i, are written [θ]: cerca [ˈθeɾka], zapato [θaˈpato]. ⟨s⟩ is written plain [s]. Its apico-alveolar quality [s̺], which the note mentions, is a phonetic detail that broad transcription omits.

Source: Hualde (2005), chapter on fricatives (Peninsular apical /s/ and /θ/).

### Yeísmo

⟨ll⟩ and consonantal ⟨y⟩ are written [ʝ] in all positions, also after n and l: calle [ˈkaʝe], llamar [ʝaˈmaɾ], cónyuge [ˈkonʝuxe]. No [ʎ], no [ɟʝ] and no [j] are written for them.

Source: Hualde (2005), chapter on palatals and yeísmo.

### Word-final and intervocalic /d/

Word-final /d/ is written [ð]: Madrid [maˈðɾið], usted [usˈteð]. It is not written as zero, and not as the Castilian popular [θ]. The participle ending -ado is written in full: cansado [kanˈsaðo]. It is not reduced to [ao].

Source: Hualde (2005), section on /d/ weakening; Penny (2000) on regional weakening of /d/.

### Other segment values

The following values apply. ⟨j⟩, and ⟨g⟩ before e or i, are written [x] (not [χ]): jamás [xaˈmas], gente [ˈxente]. ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ]. ⟨ñ⟩ is [ɲ]. ⟨v⟩ is the same as ⟨b⟩. ⟨h⟩ is silent. ⟨qu⟩ and ⟨gu⟩ before e or i are [k] and [g]/[ɣ]; ⟨gü⟩ is [gw]/[ɣw]. ⟨x⟩ is [ks] between vowels and [s] before a consonant: examen [ekˈsamen], extraño [esˈtɾaɲo].

Source: Hualde (2005), chapters on consonants; Alarcos Llorach (1994).

### Adverbs in -mente

Two stresses are written: the adjective's stress as secondary ˌ and -mente as primary ˈ. Examples: rápidamente [ˌrapiðaˈmente], solamente [ˌsolaˈmente].

Source: Hualde (2005), chapter on stress (compounds and -mente adverbs).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
