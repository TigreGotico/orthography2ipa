# Transcription conventions: Western Asturian-Leonese (ast-x-occident)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: ⟨pan⟩ /ˈpan/, ⟨casa⟩ /ˈkasa/. Unstressed function words (see function_words) carry no mark. Placement follows the orthography. A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel: ⟨árbol⟩ /ˈaɾbol/, ⟨camín⟩ /kaˈmin/. Without an accent, a word ending in a vowel, ⟨-n⟩ or ⟨-s⟩ is stressed on the penult: ⟨Cabreira⟩ /kaˈbɾei̯ɾa/, ⟨poucu⟩ /ˈpou̯ku/, ⟨outros⟩ /ˈou̯tɾos/. Any other ending is stressed on the last syllable: ⟨chamar⟩ /t͡ʃaˈmaɾ/, ⟨tsabor⟩ /t͡saˈboɾ/. A word ending in an unaccented falling diphthong is stressed on that diphthong: ⟨rei⟩ /ˈrei̯/, ⟨cantei⟩ /kanˈtei̯/. Syllabify V.CV, with stop or /f/ plus liquid as an onset (/kaˈbɾei̯ɾa/). The mark goes before the whole onset, and a tied affricate stays whole after it: ⟨vatse⟩ /ˈbat͡se/, ⟨chover⟩ /t͡ʃoˈbeɾ/. Hyphenated enclitics are separate unstressed words. Pronominal suffixes written joined to the verb leave the stress where the spelling puts it. Not applicable to this lect: there is no vowel length, no phonemic geminates and no case endings. ⟨ḷḷ, l.l, ts⟩ is the single affricate /t͡s/, not a geminate. ⟨rr⟩ is /r/, not /ɾɾ/.

Source: Academia de la Llingua Asturiana (2021), Normes Ortográfiques, rules of accentuation (Hispanic-type written accent); ALLA (2001), Gramática de la Llingua Asturiana, chapter on stress; García Arias (2003) for the lexical, last-three-syllables stress of Asturian.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ:
- Definite articles el, la, lo, los, las, and the contractions al, del, pol, pola, nel, na, col, cola: ⟨la casa⟩ /la ˈkasa/, ⟨nel monte⟩ /nel ˈmonte/.
- Simple prepositions a, de, en, con, por, pa, pente, sin, so, fasta/hasta, dende.
- Conjunctions y/e /i/ /e/, o/u, ni, que, si, pero, mas, porque, and cuandu/comu/onde used as conjunctions or relatives.
- Object clitics me, te, se, nos, vos, lu/lo, la, los, las, y/-y, yos, whether proclitic or hyphenated: ⟨dio-y⟩ /ˈdjo i/ (dio /djo/ is a monosyllable written without accent).
- Prenominal possessives mio, to, so and their forms.
- Proclitic negator nun: ⟨nun vien⟩ /nun ˈbjen/.

Stressed:
- All verbs, including ser and haber as auxiliary or copula: ⟨ye⟩ /ˈʝe/, ⟨yera⟩ /ˈʝeɾa/.
- Indefinite articles un, una, unos, unas.
- Demonstratives, numerals, quantifiers and adverbs.
- Tonic pronouns yo, tu, él, ella, nós, vós, mí, ti.
- Interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent (qué, ónde, cómo).
- Stand-alone non.

A polysyllabic unstressed word such as pero, porque or pente also gets no mark: /ˈpeɾo/ is wrong, write /peɾo/.

Source: Hispanic tradition of palabras inacentuadas (Navarro Tomás 1918, Manual de pronunciación española, chapter on accent in the phrase; Quilis 1993, Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, chapter on accent), as applied to Asturian clitics, articles and possessives in ALLA (2001), Gramática de la Llingua Asturiana, chapters on the article, personal pronouns and possessives.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: there is no pausal form, no case ending and no nunation. Every word is written the same at a comma, at the end of the sentence and inside the phrase. Unstressed final vowels are kept in full everywhere: ⟨casu⟩ /ˈkasu/, ⟨peixe⟩ /ˈpei̯ʃe/.

Source: ALLA (2021), Normes Ortográfiques §1.4, p. 22 (unstressed final vowels described without pausal alternation).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form. Do not write synalepha, resyllabification, linking or cross-word assimilation:
- ⟨en casa⟩ is /en ˈkasa/, not [eŋ].
- ⟨los güeyos⟩ is /los ˈgweʝos/, with no [z].
- ⟨de outru⟩ is /de ˈou̯tɾu/.

The only elision written is the one the spelling marks with an apostrophe. The elided form is its own word, written as its consonant alone: ⟨l'home⟩ /l ˈome/, ⟨d'agua⟩ /d ˈagwa/. A contraction spelled as one word is one word: ⟨del⟩ /del/, ⟨pola⟩ /pola/. Word-internal allophony is not written either:
- No spirant [β ð ɣ]: ⟨cabana⟩ /kaˈbana/.
- No velar [ŋ] for final /n/.
- No voicing of /s/.

Source: ALLA (2021), Normes Ortográfiques, section on the apostrophe and contractions; broad-transcription practice of the specification's notes (phonemic symbols only).

## Glides and diphthongs

A glide before the nucleus is written j or w; a glide after the nucleus is written i̯ or u̯.
- Rising diphthongs: ⟨tierra⟩ /ˈtjera/, ⟨güerta⟩ /ˈgweɾta/, ⟨territoriu⟩ /teriˈtoɾju/.
- Falling diphthongs ⟨ei, ou, ai, oi, au, eu⟩ are always diphthongs, never monophthongs: ⟨Cabreira⟩ /kaˈbɾei̯ɾa/, ⟨roupa⟩ /ˈrou̯pa/, ⟨xeitu⟩ /ˈʃei̯tu/, ⟨teitu⟩ /ˈtei̯tu/.
- Triphthongs combine the two: ⟨bueis⟩ /ˈbwei̯s/.

An unstressed ⟨i, u⟩ next to another vowel is a glide. An accented ⟨í, ú⟩ marks a hiatus: ⟨día⟩ /ˈdia/. Adjacent ⟨e, a, o⟩ stay full vowels in hiatus: ⟨real⟩ /reˈal/. Unaccented ⟨ui, iu⟩ are rising: ⟨cuidar⟩ /kwiˈdaɾ/, ⟨ciudá⟩ /θjuˈda/. The exception is the -KT- word ⟨muitu⟩ /ˈmui̯tu/. Consonantal ⟨y⟩ is /ʝ/: ⟨güeyu⟩ /ˈgweʝu/. The conjunction ⟨y⟩ is /i/.

Source: Morala & Egido (2009), p. 8, and Propuesta de Norma Ortográfica (2010), §3.2 (ei/ou as the western marker), §2.1 and §6.3 (gü), §3.3 (-KT- > it); ALLA (2021), Normes Ortográfiques, section on diphthongs and hiatus.

## Other choices

### che vaqueira vs ch

Write ⟨ḷḷ⟩, ⟨l.l⟩ and ⟨ts⟩ as the tied affricate /t͡s/: ⟨l.levar⟩ /t͡seˈbaɾ/, ⟨botiel.los⟩ /boˈtjet͡sos/, ⟨vatse⟩ /ˈbat͡se/. Never use the apical, retroflex or ʈ/ɖ variants. Write ⟨ch⟩ as /t͡ʃ/: ⟨chegar⟩ /t͡ʃeˈgaɾ/. Never merge the two.

Source: ALLA (2021), Normes Ortográfiques §1.1.3, p. 14 (values [t̺s̺, t's, ʈʂ, ʈ, ɖ]; the first is written broadly as t͡s per the specification); Morala & Egido (2009), p. 9; Propuesta (2010), §3.1 and §3.5.

### final unstressed vowels

An unstressed singular final ⟨o⟩ is /u/ even where spelled -o: ⟨casu/caso⟩ /ˈkasu/. Stressed final o stays /o/. Plurals are written as spelled: masculine -os /os/, -e plurals -es /es/, and western feminine -as /as/ (not central -es): ⟨outras casas⟩ /ˈou̯tɾas ˈkasas/. Final -e stays /e/, never [i], including after /ʃ/: ⟨viaxe⟩ /ˈbjaʃe/, ⟨imaxe⟩ /iˈmaʃe/.

Source: ALLA (2021), Normes Ortográfiques §1.4, p. 22; Morala & Egido (2009), pp. 10 and 27; Propuesta (2010), §2.4 and §4.3.

### consonant symbols

Use these values:
- ⟨x⟩ /ʃ/
- ⟨y⟩ /ʝ/
- ⟨ll⟩ /ʎ/
- ⟨ñ⟩ /ɲ/
- ⟨z, ce, ci⟩ /θ/
- ⟨b, v⟩ /b/
- ⟨gu⟩ before e/i is /g/ (⟨guerra⟩ /ˈgera/); ⟨gü⟩ is /gw/
- ⟨h⟩ is silent
- ⟨r⟩ is /ɾ/; ⟨rr⟩ and word-initial ⟨r⟩ are /r/
- -NN- words have /n/: ⟨panu⟩ /ˈpanu/

Eastern aspirated ⟨ḥ⟩ does not apply.

Source: ALLA (2021), Normes Ortográfiques §1.1; Propuesta (2010), §2.1 and §3.6; Morala & Egido (2009), p. 9.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
