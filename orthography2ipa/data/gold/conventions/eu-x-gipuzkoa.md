# Transcription conventions: Gipuzkoan Basque (Gipuzkera) (eu-x-gipuzkoa)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Follow the Western-Central (standard) accent pattern. Primary stress falls on the second syllable from the left of the whole inflected word. Mark it with ˈ before that syllable. Divide syllables by maximal onset: a single C, or C+l/ɾ, begins the syllable. A falling diphthong (ai̯, ei̯, oi̯, ui̯, au̯, eu̯) counts as one syllable. Vowels in hiatus count as two. Disyllables are therefore stressed on the final syllable: etxe [eˈtʃe], mendi [menˈdi], gero [ɡeˈɾo]. Monosyllables get no mark: ur [ur], zu [s̻u]. Long vowels and geminates do not exist phonemically, so they never affect stress; identical vowels across a morpheme boundary that the spelling keeps are two syllables. Case and number endings are part of the word, and counting always starts from the left edge, so stress stays put as endings are added: gizon [ɡiˈs̻on], gizona [ɡiˈs̻ona], gizonarekin [ɡiˈs̻onaɾekin], euskara [eu̯s̺ˈkaɾa], etxea [eˈtʃea]. Lexical exceptions, pre-accenting plural -ak (a Bizkaian feature) and phrase-level prominence are not marked. Loanwords and names follow the same rule.

Source: Hualde 2003, 'Segmental phonology' and the accentuation section that follows it, in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque (second-syllable stress of the Western-Central/standard type). Pitch-accent systems such as Goizueta (Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri 2010: 113, 123-124) are explicitly not followed.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words get no stress mark: inflected auxiliaries and copula forms of izan/*edun when they follow another word (da, dira [diɾa], naiz [nai̯s̻], zara [s̻aɾa], gara [ɡaɾa], zen, ziren [s̻iɾen], du, dut, dugu [duɣu], dute [dute], zuen [s̻uen], dio [dio], zaio [s̻ai̯o]); the conjunctions eta [eta], edo [edo], ala [ala], baina [bai̯ɲa] and ere [eɾe]; the particles al, omen [omen], ote [ote] and ba; and the numeral/indefinite bat after its noun. These words carry stress by the normal rule: personal pronouns (zuek [s̻uˈek], haiek [aˈi̯ek]); demonstratives (hori [oˈɾi], hauek [aˈu̯ek]); separately written postpositions (gabe [ɡaˈβe], aurrean [au̯ˈrean], bezala [beˈs̻ala]); synthetic main verbs (dago [daˈɣo], daki [daˈki], dauka [dau̯ˈka]); nouns, adjectives, adverbs and participles. The negator ez is stressed, but as a monosyllable it gets no mark: ez dut [es̻ tut].

Source: Hualde 2003, accentuation section, in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque (auxiliaries and particles as unaccented elements grouped with the preceding accented word).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Basque has no pausal forms. Words are written identically at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside a phrase. Final vowels and case endings are never dropped or altered, and there is no nunation: etxean [eˈtʃean] everywhere. Phrase-final lengthening is not written.

Source: Hualde 2003, 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque (no pausal alternations described); Saltarelli 1988.

## Connected speech

Each word keeps its own IPA word. Only these cross-boundary processes are written, and only inside a stretch without punctuation; a comma or sentence start counts as a pause. (1) Spirantization: /b d ɡ/ are written [β ð ɣ] whenever the preceding sound is a vowel or glide. This holds inside the word and also across a word boundary when the previous word ends in a vowel or glide. Elsewhere they are stops: after a pause, after any consonant (including n, l, r), and word-initially after a consonant-final word. Examples: eta gero [eta ɣeˈɾo], ardo [arˈdo], zu bezala [s̻u βeˈs̻ala], ez bada [es̻ bada]. (2) Stop devoicing after the negator ez: a word-initial /b d ɡ/ of the word after ez is written [p t k]: ez da [es̻ ta], ez dut [es̻ tut], ez gara [es̻ kaɾa]. Not written: nasal place assimilation (egin behar [eˈɣin beˈaɾ]), vowel elision or contraction across words, sibilant fusion, and any linking or resyllabification symbol. Word-final consonants stay as they are before a vowel: ur asko [ur as̺ˈko].

Source: Hualde 2003, 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque (spirantization of voiced stops; devoicing after ez). The spec note restricts approximants to intervocalic position; here that is extended to any post-vocalic position, following Hualde's description.

## Glides and diphthongs

Falling diphthongs are written with non-syllabic i̯ u̯: ai̯, ei̯, oi̯, ui̯, au̯, eu̯. Examples: gauza [ɡau̯ˈs̻a], euskara [eu̯s̺ˈkaɾa], oihan [oi̯ˈan]. Never write j or w for these. Prevocalic i or u in native words is a syllabic vowel in hiatus, with no inserted glide: mendia [menˈdia], egia [eˈɣia], lua [luˈa]. Colloquial glide insertion such as mendiya is not written. In loanwords where C+u/i+V is one syllable, use [w] and [j]: kuadro [kwaˈdɾo]. Orthographic j is the consonant [x], not a glide: jan [xan], jaun [xau̯n].

Source: Hualde 2003, 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque (diphthong inventory; velar-fricative realization of j in Gipuzkoa).

## Other choices

### sibilants

Keep the three-way standard/eastern Gipuzkoan contrast, not the western merger. s is [s̺], z is [s̻], x is [ʃ], ts is [ts̺], tz is [ts̻], tx is [tʃ]. Examples: esan [eˈs̺an], zazpi [s̻as̻ˈpi], etxe [eˈtʃe], hitz [its̻], gatz [ɡats̻]. Sibilants are not voiced before voiced consonants: esne [es̺ˈne].

Source: Hualde 2003, 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque. Western neutralization noted in Bedialauneta & Hualde 2023: 1098 is not followed.

### palatals

Orthographic tt is [c], dd is [ɟ], ñ is [ɲ], ll is [ʎ]. These are kept distinct from [tʃ]: the Eibar-area merger of /c/ with /tʃ/ is not written. Following the Gipuzkoan norm, n and l are also written [ɲ] [ʎ] after a diphthong-final i̯ before a vowel: baina [bai̯ˈɲa], oilo [oi̯ˈʎo]. They are not palatalized after a syllabic i: mutila [muˈtila].

Source: Hualde 2003, 'Segmental phonology' (palatalization after /i/), in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque. The merger is described in Hualde, Lujanbio & Zubiri 2010: 118 but is not followed.

### rhotics

Single intervocalic r, and r after an obstruent in a cluster, is a tap [ɾ]: gara [ɡaˈɾa], bera [beˈɾa], obra [oˈβɾa]. rr is a trill [r]: ura [uˈɾa] vs hurra [uˈra]. Word-final and preconsonantal r is also written as a trill [r]: ur [ur], zer [s̻er], ardo [arˈdo].

Source: Hualde 2003, 'Segmental phonology' (neutralization of rhotics in final and preconsonantal position), in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque.

### h

Orthographic h is silent and is never written: hau [au̯], hori [oˈɾi], behar [beˈaɾ]. There are no aspirated stops.

Source: Hualde 2018: 2; Hualde 2003, in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque.

### vowel assimilation in hiatus

Transcribe vowel sequences as spelled. Colloquial Gipuzkoan raising and assimilation are not written: etxea [eˈtʃea], not etxia; mendia [menˈdia], not mendiye. The five vowels are /a e i o u/.

Source: Hualde 2003, 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque. Standard (Batua-aligned) Gipuzkoan pronunciation is followed rather than local colloquial variants.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
