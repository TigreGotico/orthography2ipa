# Transcription conventions: Pernambucano Portuguese (pt-BR-x-recife)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every lexical word, including monosyllables: pé [ˈpɛ], mar [ˈmah]. Secondary stress is not marked. Stress is lexical, and the orthography decides it. A written acute or circumflex accent marks the stressed syllable: café [kaˈfɛ], ônibus [ˈonibʊʃ]. Without an accent, stress falls on the penult in words ending in -a, -e, -o (plus -s), -em or -am: casa [ˈkazɐ], homem [ˈɔmẽj̃], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. Otherwise it falls on the final syllable: in words ending in -i, -u, -im, -um, -om, -l, -r, -z, -x, -ão or -ões, or in a stressed oral diphthong. Examples: aqui [aˈki], papel [paˈpɛw], rapaz [haˈpaʃ]. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so those parts do not apply.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (word stress); Silva 2002, stress section.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed (no ˈ, reduced vowels) are: definite articles o [ʊ], a [ɐ], os [ʊʃ], as [ɐʃ]; the prepositions de [dɪ], em [ẽj̃], com [kõ], por [pɔh], para [paɾɐ] and pra [pɾɐ]; the contractions do [dʊ], da [dɐ], no [nʊ], na [nɐ], ao [aw], pelo [pɛlʊ], num [nũ]; the conjunctions e [ɪ], que [kɪ], se [sɪ], mas [maʃ], nem [nẽj̃], ou [ow]; relative que [kɪ]; clitic pronouns me [mɪ], te [tɪ], se [sɪ], lhe [ʎɪ], nos [nʊʃ], o [ʊ], a [ɐ]. Stressed (ˈ) are: indefinite articles um [ˈũ], uma [ˈumɐ]; subject and prepositional pronouns eu [ˈew], ele [ˈelɪ], você [voˈse], nós [ˈnɔʃ], mim [ˈmĩ]; demonstratives; possessives; quantifiers; negation não [ˈnɐ̃w̃]; and all verbs, auxiliaries included: é [ˈɛ], está [iʃˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], vai [ˈvaj], foi [ˈfoj]. A hyphenated clitic is its own unstressed word: deu-me [ˈdew mɪ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (clitics and prosodic words); Silva 2002 (unstressed monosyllables).

## Pausal forms

There are no pausal forms. No rule applies at a sentence end or at a comma beyond the ordinary word-final rules, which hold in every position: final unstressed vowels are always [ɪ ʊ ɐ] (leite [ˈlejtɪ], menino [mɛˈninʊ]). Word-final /S/ before a pause is [ʃ] (mais [ˈmajʃ]). A final non-verbal -r before a pause is [h] (mar [ˈmah]). Case endings and nunation (tanwīn) do not apply to Portuguese.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2-3 (vowel reduction); Callou & Leite 2001 (final consonants).

## Connected speech

Only consonantal sandhi at word edges is written, and each word keeps its own slot. (1) Word-final /S/ before a vowel-initial word is [z]: os amigos [ʊz aˈmiɡʊʃ]. Before a voiced consonant it is [ʒ]: as mesas [ɐʒ ˈmezɐʃ]. Before a voiceless consonant or a pause it is [ʃ]: os pés [ʊʃ ˈpɛʃ]. (2) A word-final non-verbal -r before a vowel-initial word is the tap [ɾ]: mar azul [ˈmaɾ aˈzuw]. Otherwise it is [h]. (3) Word-final -l stays [w] in all positions: mal estar [ˈmaw iʃˈtah]. Vowel sandhi is not written, and each word keeps its full vowels. This covers elision (de outro), degemination (casa amarela) and diphthongisation across the boundary: de outro [dɪ ˈowtɾʊ], casa amarela [ˈkazɐ amaˈɾɛlɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 7 (sandhi and resyllabification of /S/ and /r/); Silva 2002 (external sandhi).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. Oral diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], leite [ˈlejtɪ], mau [ˈmaw], sol [ˈsɔw]. Nasal diphthongs use j̃ and w̃: mão [ˈmɐ̃w̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃], põe [ˈpõj̃]. A stressed high vowel followed by a vowel is a hiatus: tia [ˈtiɐ], rua [ˈhuɐ]. A posttonic unstressed i or u before a vowel is a glide: história [iʃˈtɔɾjɐ], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. The glide in qu and gu before a or o is [w]: quando [ˈkwɐ̃dʊ]. No epenthetic yod is written before coda /S/: mas [maʃ], três [ˈtɾeʃ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 (glides and diphthongs); Silva 2002 (diphthongs, yod epenthesis treated as optional).

## Other choices

### /t d/ before [i ɪ]

/t d/ before [i ɪ] are never palatalised; write [t d]: tia [ˈtiɐ], dia [ˈdiɐ], noite [ˈnojtɪ], de [dɪ].

Source: Cardoso et al. 2014 (ALiB, palatalisation isogloss, Recife about 10%); spec note.

### pretonic mid vowels

Oral pretonic e and o are open [ɛ ɔ]: menino [mɛˈninʊ], boneca [bɔˈnɛkɐ], morar [mɔˈɾa]. Before a nasal coda they are [ẽ õ]: sentado [sẽˈtadʊ]. Raising to [i u] is never written.

Source: Cardoso et al. 2014 (ALiB pretonic-vowel isogloss); Callou & Leite 2001.

### rhotics

Word-initial r, rr, and r after l, n or s are [h]: rato [ˈhatʊ], carro [ˈkahʊ]. Single r between vowels and in clusters is [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾʊ], prato [ˈpɾatʊ]. Coda r inside a word is [h]: porta [ˈpɔhtɐ]. Final r in a noun or adjective is [h]: mar [ˈmah]. Final r in verb forms (infinitive, future subjunctive) is deleted: falar [faˈla], comer [kɔˈme], quando for [ˈkwɐ̃dʊ ˈfɔ]. Here we follow the sociolinguistic descriptions of near-categorical infinitival -r deletion rather than the spec note's blanket aspiration.

Source: Callou & Leite 2001 (rhotics); Silva 2002.

### coda /S/

Coda /S/ is palatal everywhere, inside and at the end of words: [ʃ] before a voiceless consonant or a pause, [ʒ] before a voiced consonant. Examples: festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ], mesmo [ˈmeʒmʊ], estar [iʃˈtah]. This follows the spec note. Some studies report alveolar [s] at a pause in Recife, and we do not follow them.

Source: Callou & Leite 2001 (coda /S/ regional variants); spec note.

### final unstressed vowels

Final unstressed -e(s), -o(s) and -a(s) are [ɪ(ʃ) ʊ(ʃ) ɐ(ʃ)]: noites [ˈnojtɪʃ], livros [ˈlivɾʊʃ]. Word-initial unstressed es- is [iʃ]: escola [iʃˈkɔlɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2; Silva 2002.

### nasal vowels

A vowel before a coda nasal is a nasal vowel, and no nasal consonant is written: campo [ˈkɐ̃pʊ], tempo [ˈtẽpʊ], fim [ˈfĩ]. A stressed vowel before an onset m, n or nh is nasalised, and a is raised to [ɐ̃]: cama [ˈkɐ̃mɐ], banho [ˈbɐ̃ɲʊ]. Nh is [ɲ] and lh is [ʎ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 8 (nasality); Silva 2002.

### coda /l/

Coda /l/ is always [w]: sal [ˈsaw], alto [ˈawtʊ].

Source: Silva 2002; Callou & Leite 2001.

### stressed mid vowels

Stressed e and o take their lexical quality, [e o] or [ɛ ɔ], as in standard Brazilian usage: mesa [ˈmezɐ], pé [ˈpɛ], novo [ˈnovʊ], nova [ˈnɔvɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2.

### epenthesis in consonant clusters

Write an epenthetic [ɪ] inside word-internal clusters whose first member is a stop or f: advogado [adɪvɔˈɡadʊ], ritmo [ˈhitɪmʊ], pneu [pɪˈnew]. Because /t d/ do not palatalise, the epenthetic vowel is written after plain [t d].

Source: Silva 2002 (vowel epenthesis in consonant sequences).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
