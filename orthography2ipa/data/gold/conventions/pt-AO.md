# Transcription conventions: Angolan Portuguese (pt-AO)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: casa [ˈkaza], pé [ˈpɛ], falar [faˈlaɾ]. Words in the unstressed list under function_words get no mark. Placement follows Portuguese lexical stress as the spelling shows it. (1) A written accent (á é í ó ú â ê ô ã õ) marks the stressed syllable, except that ã or õ followed by a vowel letter (-ão, -ãe, -õe) still counts as final stress: médico [ˈmɛdiku], você [voˈse], irmã [iɾˈmã], coração [koɾaˈsãw̃]. (2) With no accent, words ending in -a, -e, -o, -as, -es, -os, -am, -em or -ens stress the penultimate syllable: mesa [ˈmeza], falam [ˈfalãw̃], homem [ˈɔmẽj̃]. (3) With no accent, other words stress the final syllable. These are words ending in -i, -u, -is, -us, -l, -r, -z, -x, -im, -um, -ns other than -ens, or an oral diphthong: aqui [aˈki], papel [paˈpɛɫ], falei [faˈlej]. Secondary stress is not marked. The lengthening of pre- and post-tonic syllables that Chavagne reports is not written. Long vowels do not apply: Portuguese has no phonemic vowel length, so ː is never written. Geminates do not apply: ss and rr spell single consonants, as in passo [ˈpasu] and carro [ˈkaru]. Case endings do not apply: Portuguese nouns have no case.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (word stress); Cunha & Cintra 1984, chapter on accentuation; stress-mark style as in Undolo 2014:183-184 ([ˈkaza], [moˈrar]).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Write a closed list of words unstressed, with no ˈ. Definite articles: o a os as [o a os as]. Simple prepositions: a de em por para com sem [a de ẽj̃ poɾ paɾa kõ sẽj̃]. Preposition plus article contractions: ao à aos às do da dos das no na nos nas pelo pela pelos pelas [aw a aws as do da dos das no na nos nas pelo pela pelos pelas]. Conjunctions: e nem ou mas que se porque [e nẽj̃ o mas ke se poɾke]. Relative que. Atonic object and reflexive pronouns, wherever they stand, including after a hyphen: me te se lhe lhes nos vos o a os as lo la los las no na mo ta [me te se ʎe ʎes nos vos o a os as lo la los las no na mo ta]. Vowels in unstressed words are always written full: e as [e], o as [o], a as [a]. They are never [ɨ], [u] or [ɐ], whatever their position: de casa [de ˈkaza], o carro [o ˈkaru], disse-me [ˈdisɨ me], vê-lo [ˈve lo]. Everything else carries stress and gets ˈ. This covers indefinite articles (um uma [ˈũ ˈuma]), subject and prepositional pronouns (eu ele mim você [ˈew ˈelɨ ˈmĩ voˈse]), possessives, demonstratives, não [ˈnãw̃], and all verbs including auxiliaries and copulas (é foi tem está vai há [ˈɛ ˈfoj ˈtẽj̃ esˈta ˈvaj ˈa]).

Source: Cunha & Cintra 1984, section on stressed and unstressed monosyllables; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (clitics). Full vowels in atonic words follow Undolo 2014:184 summary (d), no raising or centralisation in atonic position, and Chavagne 2005:64, atonic vowels keep the timbre of tonic ones.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so every word is written the same at a comma, at the sentence end and inside the phrase. At a pause, final /s/ is [s] and final /r/ is [ɾ], as in falar. [faˈlaɾ]. No vowel is added or dropped.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (no pausal morphology in Portuguese); coda /s/ as alveolar [s] per the specification note (4).

## Connected speech

Only one cross-word process is written: voicing of a word-final sibilant. A word-final /s/ (spelled s, z or x) is written [z] when the next word in the same phrase begins with a vowel or a voiced consonant. It stays [s] before a voiceless consonant, at a comma and at the sentence end. The change is written in the left-hand word only: os amigos [oz aˈmiɡus], três dias [ˈtɾez ˈdias], as casas [as ˈkazas], feliz. [feˈlis]. The same rule applies inside words: mesmo [ˈmezmu]. The sibilant is always alveolar, never [ʃ] or [ʒ]. Nothing else is written across word boundaries. Vowels are not elided and vowels in contact are not merged into a diphthong: de alguém [de aɫˈɡẽj̃], que eu [ke ˈew]. Resyllabification and linking are not shown, and no ‿ is used. Nasals do not assimilate across words. Each word keeps its own segments.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 and 7 (sibilant voicing and resyllabification across words), with the alveolar coda sibilant of the specification note (4). Avoiding vowel sandhi follows Chavagne 2005:64, who reports weak tonic/atonic contrast and full atonic syllables.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vowels as j and w, never i̯ or u̯. In nasal diphthongs, write j̃ and w̃ after the nasal vowel. Falling diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], rei [ˈrej], primeiro [priˈmejɾu], céu [ˈsɛw], foi [ˈfoj]. Nasal diphthongs: pão [ˈpãw̃], mãe [ˈmãj̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], muito [ˈmũj̃tu], falam [ˈfalãw̃]. Final stressed or unstressed -em/-ém is [ẽj̃], not EP [ɐ̃j̃]: bem [ˈbẽj̃], também [tãˈbẽj̃]. The spelling -ei is [ej], not EP [ɐj]. The spelling ou is the monophthong [o]: outro [ˈotɾu], falou [faˈlo]. Rising glides are written only in two cases. The first is gu or qu before a pronounced vowel: água [ˈaɡwa], quando [ˈkwãdu]. The second is unstressed i or u plus a vowel after the stress: história [isˈtɔɾja], série [ˈsɛɾjɨ]. Elsewhere, vowel sequences are hiatus with full vowels: dia [ˈdia], piano [piˈano], saúde [saˈudɨ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 (glides and diphthongs). The [ẽj̃] and [ej] values follow Undolo 2014:184, who reports loss of PE [ɐ] in PA.

## Other choices

### unstressed oral vowels

Pretonic vowels and non-final post-tonic vowels are written as spelled: a [a], e [e], o [o], i [i], u [u]. They are never raised or centralised, never [ɐ], [ɨ] or [u] for o, and never opened to [ɛ] or [ɔ]: atropelar [atropeˈlaɾ], morar [moˈraɾ], estar [esˈtaɾ], número [ˈnumeɾu]. Word-final unstressed vowels follow fixed rules. Final -a/-as is [a]/[as]: casa [ˈkaza]. Final -e/-es is [ɨ]/[ɨs]: disse [ˈdisɨ], presidente [preziˈdẽtɨ]. Final -o/-os is [u]/[us]: carro [ˈkaru]. The rule for final -o is this file's choice. It is shared with the EP and BP norms, which raise final o to [u]. Undolo states that no raising or centralisation occurs in atonic position, so his wording could be read as keeping [o] here.

Source: Undolo 2014:183-184 for [a], [e], [o] and for final-only [ɨ]; Chavagne 2005:64; final -o > [u] after Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2.

### stressed mid vowels

Write é and ó as [ɛ] and [ɔ], and ê and ô as [e] and [o]. Unaccented stressed e and o take their lexical standard quality: café [kaˈfɛ], avô [aˈvo], porta [ˈpɔɾta], mesa [ˈmeza]. Where the lexical quality is uncertain or the norms disagree, write the open vowel, following the PA tendency to open stressed mid vowels. Before a nasal consonant, stressed a and e stay oral and unreduced: cama [ˈkama], tenho [ˈteɲu].

Source: Undolo 2014:183-184, on opening of stressed mid vowels and on PE [ɐ] not occurring in PA; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2.

### nasal vowels

A vowel followed by m or n in the coda is written as a nasal vowel, with no nasal consonant after it: ã ẽ ĩ õ ũ. Examples: campo [ˈkãpu], dente [ˈdẽtɨ], fim [ˈfĩ], um [ˈũ]. The low nasal vowel is [ã], not EP [ɐ̃]: irmã [iɾˈmã], Angola [ãˈɡɔla].

Source: Undolo 2014:184 ([ã] in PA vs [ɐ̃] in PE).

### rhotics

Write [r] for word-initial r, for rr, and for r after n, l or s: rio [ˈriu], carro [ˈkaru], honra [ˈõra]. Write [ɾ] for single intervocalic r, for r in clusters, and for r in the coda, word-final included: caro [ˈkaɾu], três [ˈtɾes], porta [ˈpɔɾta], falar [faˈlaɾ]. Finer variation in the realisation of r is not transcribed.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 1-3 (tap/trill contrast); the specification note leaves allophonic rhotic detail to downstream engines.

### coda l and other consonants

Write coda l as velarised [ɫ], never as the BP vocalised [w]: Portugal [poɾtuˈɡaɫ], alguém [aɫˈɡẽj̃]. Onset l is [l]. The consonants t and d are never affricated before i or final [ɨ]: dia [ˈdia], noite [ˈnojtɨ]. Other values: ch [ʃ], j and ge/gi [ʒ], lh [ʎ], nh [ɲ].

Source: Undolo 2014:183 ([atropeˈɫar]); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 1.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
