# Transcription conventions: Terceirense Portuguese (pt-PT-x-terceira)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical. Mark primary stress with ˈ before the first segment of the stressed syllable, including its onset consonant and any on-glide: bicho [ˈbwiʃu], porco [ˈpwoɾku], cidade [siˈdadɨ]. This disagrees with the spec note and Mikołajczak, who write [bwˈiʃu]; we use the IPA syllable-initial position. Secondary stress is not marked. Every lexical word gets exactly one mark, monosyllables included: pé [ˈpɛ], é [ˈɛ], sol [ˈsɔɫ]. Unstressed function words get none (see function_words). Placement: (1) an acute or circumflex accent, or a tilde on a final ã/ão/õe, marks the stressed syllable: café [kɐˈfɛ], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃pɐdɐ], irmão [iɾˈmɐ̃w̃]. (2) Otherwise, words ending in -a, -e, -o (+s), -am, -em, -ens take penultimate stress: casa [ˈkazɐ], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. (3) Words ending in other letters (-r, -l, -z, -i, -u, -im, -um, -om, consonant clusters) take final stress: falar [fɐˈlaɾ], papel [pɐˈpɛɫ], aqui [ɐˈki]. A hyphenated enclitic is a separate unstressed word: dá-me [ˈda] [mɨ]. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so nothing is lengthened or doubled; spelled rr and ss are single consonants: carro [ˈkaru], passo [ˈpasu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. on stress (EP word stress and its predictability from the word ending); stress-mark placement follows IPA practice rather than Mikołajczak 2014: 421-422.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed, carry no ˈ, and take reduced vowels. Articles: o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ]; um [ũ] and uma [ˈumɐ] are an exception, written stressed. Monosyllabic and disyllabic prepositions: de [dɨ], em [ɐ̃j̃], a [ɐ], por [puɾ], para [pɐɾɐ], com [kõ], sem [sɐ̃j̃]. Contractions: do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], ao [aw], à [a], pelo [pɨlu], num [nũ]. Conjunctions and complementisers: e [i], ou [o], mas [mɐʃ], que [kɨ], se [sɨ], nem [nɐ̃j̃]. Clitic pronouns, whether free-standing or hyphenated: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [sɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], o [u], a [ɐ], nos [nuʃ], vos [vuʃ], lhes [ʎɨʃ]. Relative que [kɨ]. These are stressed: strong pronouns (eu [ˈew], tu [ˈtu], ele [ˈelɨ], nós [ˈnɔʃ], mim [ˈmĩ], você [voˈse]), demonstratives, possessives, numerals, interrogatives (quê [ˈke], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du]), adverbs (não [ˈnɐ̃w̃]), and all verbs, auxiliaries and copulas included: é [ˈɛ], está [ɨʃˈta], foi [ˈfoj], tem [ˈtɐ̃j̃], vai [ˈvaj]. The Terceira stressed-syllable rules (a-metaphony, crescent [w]) never apply to unstressed words.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (clitics and unstressed-vowel reduction in EP); Mikołajczak 2014: 421-423 restricts the Terceira on-glide and metaphony to stressed vowels.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable as a separate pausal grammar: Portuguese has no case endings, no nunation and no pausal vowel loss. Every word is written in the same form at a sentence end, at a comma and sentence-medially, except that word-final /s/ before a pause is [ʃ] (see connected_speech). Final unstressed -e is always written [ɨ] and is never deleted: noite [ˈnojtɨ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (EP final unstressed vowels and final sibilant); no pausal phenomena are described for Terceira in Mikołajczak 2014.

## Connected speech

Only one cross-word process is written: voicing of word-final /s/ (spelled s, z, x), which changes only the first word. Before a vowel-initial word it becomes [z]: os amigos [uz ɐˈmiɡuʃ], dez anos [ˈdɛz ˈɐnuʃ]. Before a voiced-consonant-initial word it becomes [ʒ]: os dias [uʒ ˈdiɐʃ], mas não [mɐʒ ˈnɐ̃w̃]. Before a voiceless consonant or a pause it stays [ʃ]: os pés [uʃ ˈpɛʃ]. The prevocalic value is [z], not the [ʒ] described for São Miguel. The following are not written: vowel elision, coalescence or crasis across words (de um stays [dɨ] [ũ]; casa amarela stays [ˈkazɐ] [ɐmɐˈɾɛlɐ]); resyllabification; nasal or place assimilation; flapping changes. Word-final r is always [ɾ] and word-final l is always [ɫ], even before a vowel: mar azul [ˈmaɾ ɐˈzuɫ]. Each word keeps its own IPA word, separated by single spaces.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (EP final-sibilant voicing sandhi); spec note (c) after Mikołajczak 2014, keeping [z] for the northern-influenced central group.

## Glides and diphthongs

Non-syllabic vowels are written [j] and [w], never i̯ or u̯, both as off-glides and as on-glides. Off-glides: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], leite [ˈlejtɨ], foi [ˈfoj]. Nasal diphthongs carry a tilde on both elements: pão [ˈpɐ̃w̃], mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], bem [ˈbɐ̃j̃], muito [ˈmũj̃tu]. Word-internal on-glides: unstressed i/u directly before another vowel is [j]/[w]: história [iʃˈtɔɾjɐ], água [ˈaɡwɐ], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du]. Spelled stressed í/ú stay syllabic: país [pɐˈiʃ], saúde [sɐˈudɨ]. Terceira crescent on-glide: insert [w] between a single labial onset (p, b, m, f, v) and a stressed oral monophthong [i e ɛ o ɔ] of a lexical word: bicho [ˈbwiʃu], porco [ˈpwoɾku], mesa [ˈmwezɐ], pedra stays [ˈpɛdɾɐ] (cluster onset). It does not apply before [u] or [a] (muda [ˈmudɐ], pato [ˈpɔtu]), nor to an [ɔ] produced by a-metaphony. It does not apply after a consonant cluster (prato [ˈpɾatu]), before nasal vowels or diphthongs (vinho [ˈviɲu] is inside scope: [ˈbwiɲu]-type only when the onset is labial, so vinho → [ˈvwiɲu]; but bem [ˈbɐ̃j̃], vai [ˈvaj]), or in unstressed syllables. The unmodelled harmonic glides are not written: ceifar [sejˈfaɾ], fumar [fuˈmaɾ], morrer [muˈreɾ]. Note: the spec note writes porco [ˈpwɔɾku]; we write [ˈpwoɾku], with the closed singular vowel of standard EP that Mikołajczak also gives ([pwˈorku]).

Source: Mikołajczak 2014: 421-422 (crescent/anti-hiatic diphthongisation, labial-conditioned subset as in the spec); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 for EP glide and diphthong inventory.

## Other choices

### Stressed /u/

Write back [u] in stressed position, never [y] or [ʉ]: muda [ˈmudɐ], tudo [ˈtudu], uva [ˈuvɐ].

Source: Mikołajczak 2014: 417-418, 424 (the labialisation/fronting of [u] does not apply in Terceira).

### A-metaphony

Stressed oral /a/ becomes [ɔ] when the word ends in unstressed -o or -os [u, uʃ] and the /a/ is in the penultimate syllable: pato [ˈpɔtu], gato [ˈɡɔtu], gatos [ˈɡɔtuʃ], prato [ˈpɾɔtu]. It does not apply to nasalised or pre-nasal [ɐ] (ano [ˈɐnu], banco [ˈbɐ̃ku]), to proparoxytones, or to words with other final vowels (gata [ˈɡatɐ]).

Source: Mikołajczak 2014: 423, after Segura & Saramago 2001: 230 (applied regularly, as in the spec).

### Unstressed vowel reduction

Use the standard EP reduction. Unstressed a → [ɐ]; unstressed e → [ɨ], or [i] word-initially before s and in hiatus (estar [ɨʃˈtaɾ]); unstressed o → [u]. Examples: pequeno [pɨˈkenu], morar [muˈɾaɾ], cidade [siˈdadɨ]. Lexical exceptions with unreduced pretonic vowels follow EP norm (padeiro [pɐˈdejɾu], but ação [aˈsɐ̃w̃]).

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 (EP vowel reduction).

### Diphthong ei

Write ei as [ej], not the Lisbon centralised [ɐj]: leite [ˈlejtɨ], primeiro [pɾiˈmejɾu], ceifar [sejˈfaɾ].

Source: Mikołajczak 2014: 421-422 (ceifar transcribed with [ej]); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 describe [ɐj] as a Lisbon feature.

### Strong r

Write word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s as the alveolar trill [r], not uvular [ʁ]: rato [ˈrɔtu], carro [ˈkaru], honra [ˈõrɐ]. Single intervocalic and coda r is [ɾ].

Source: Mikołajczak 2014: 422 (morrer transcribed with [r]); differs from Lisbon [ʁ] in Mateus & d'Andrade 2000.

### Coda consonants

Coda /l/ is [ɫ] and onset /l/ is [l]: sal [ˈsaɫ], alto [ˈaɫtu]. Coda sibilants are [ʃ] before a voiceless consonant or a pause and [ʒ] before a voiced consonant: festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ], mesmo [ˈmeʒmu]. Write b d g as [b d ɡ] in all positions, without spirant allophones.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (EP coda /l/ and sibilants); inherited per spec note.

### Mid-vowel raising

Do not apply the northern-type raising of stressed mid vowels; use standard EP qualities: flor [ˈfloɾ], soa [ˈsoɐ], vergonha [vɨɾˈɡoɲɐ].

Source: Vasconcelos 1892: 293 describes the raising as lexical and variable; it is left unmodelled per the spec.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
