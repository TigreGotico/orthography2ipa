# Transcription conventions: Equatoguinean Spanish (es-GQ)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress follows the ordinary lexical stress of Spanish. Written accents mark it explicitly. Unaccented words ending in a vowel, n or s are stressed on the penult; other unaccented words are stressed on the final syllable. Put ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable. A prenuclear glide counts as part of the onset. Examples: casa ˈkasa, ciudad sjuˈdad, árbol ˈaɾbol, nuevo ˈnwebo, país paˈis. Mark every stressed word, monosyllables included: sol ˈsol, sí ˈsi, voy ˈboi̯. Leave unstressed function words unmarked (see function_words). Do not mark secondary stress. Adverbs in -mente are the one exception and take a single mark on -mente: rápidamente rapidaˈmente. The substrate-influenced tonal patterns and occasional stress shifts reported for the lect are not written; the lexical stress position is kept. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels or geminates, and no case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply. Orthographic double consonants are written as pronounced: innato inˈnato, acción akˈsjon.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on word stress; Quilis 1993, chapter on accent. Tonal and stress-shift reports for Equatorial Guinea are from Lipski 1985 (The Spanish of Equatorial Guinea) and Quilis & Casado-Fresnillo 1995 (La lengua española en Guinea Ecuatorial); these are not represented.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following words are unstressed and carry no ˈ: definite articles el la los las lo (el ˈniɲo → el ˈniɲo); unstressed possessives mi tu su mis tus sus nuestro-type forms only when prenominal mi/tu/su (mi ˈkasa); object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos os los las les, whether written separately or hyphen-split (me ˈdixo, ˈdame → ˈdame as one word when written joined); all simple prepositions except según (a, ante, bajo, con, contra, de, desde, en, entre, hacia, hasta, para, por, sin, sobre, tras: de ˈmadɾid, para ˈti); conjunctions y e o u ni que pero sino si (conditional) aunque como (conjunctive) porque (y → i, o → o); and non-interrogative relatives que quien donde cuando cual (el ˈke ˈbjene is NOT this case; la ˈkasa donde ˈbibo → la ˈkasa donde ˈbibo); also tan and the titles don doña before a name. The following words are stressed and marked: indefinite articles un una unos unas (ˈun ˈlibɾo); demonstratives (ˈeste, ˈesa); tonic and prepositional pronouns yo tú él ella usted nosotros mí ti sí (ˈjo is NOT used; see glides: yo → ˈʝo); interrogatives and exclamatives qué quién cómo dónde cuándo cuánto (ˈke, ˈkomo); según (seˈɡun); numerals; postnominal possessives mío tuyo suyo; all verb forms, including auxiliaries haber ser estar (ˈa ˈbenido, ˈesta ˈablando); and all adverbs (ˈno, ˈja is NOT used: ya → ˈʝa, ˈmui̯).

Source: Quilis 1993, chapter on accented and unaccented words; Hualde 2005, chapter on stress (stressed vs unstressed words).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Words are written the same at a comma, at sentence end and sentence-internally. Final /s/ and final vowels are kept in full everywhere, in line with the conservative coda of the lect: ¿Vienes? ˈbjenes.

Source: Lipski 1985 and Quilis & Casado-Fresnillo 1995 on retention of final /s/; Hualde 2005.

## Connected speech

Write every word in its citation form. Do not transcribe processes across word boundaries. Resyllabification (los amigos [lo.sa.ˈmi.ɡos]) is not shown; there are no syllable dots and no linking marks: los aˈmiɡos. Vowels in contact across words are not merged, deleted or glided (synalepha is not written): la ˈotɾa, ˈkasi ˈemos. Nasal assimilation is not written across a word boundary, because word-final /n/ is described as alveolar [n] in this lect: un ˈbaso, en ˈkasa. Inside a word, write the nasal homorganic before a stop: m before p b (including orthographic n: imˈbjeɾno, ˈkampo), ŋ before k ɡ x (ˈteŋɡo, ˈbaŋko, ˈaŋxel). Elsewhere write n. /s/ voicing before voiced consonants is not written, in words or across them: ˈmismo, las ˈmanos.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on syllabification, nasal assimilation and synalepha; final alveolar /n/ and lack of velarisation in Lipski 1985 and Quilis & Casado-Fresnillo 1995.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write prenuclear glides as j and w: bien ˈbjen, piano ˈpjano, cuando ˈkwando, huevo ˈwebo, pingüino piŋˈɡwino. Write postnuclear glides as i̯ and u̯: hay ˈai̯, rey ˈrei̯, hoy ˈoi̯, muy ˈmui̯, auto ˈau̯to, deuda ˈdeu̯da. Triphthongs combine the two: buey ˈbwei̯. Orthographic y or ll before a vowel is the consonant ʝ (yeísmo), not a glide: yo ˈʝo, calle ˈkaʎe is NOT used, calle → ˈkaʝe, ya ˈʝa, mayo ˈmaʝo. The conjunction y is i. A written accent on i or u marks hiatus; write both full vowels: día ˈdia, río ˈrio, reúne reˈune, país paˈis. Unaccented i/u next to another vowel is a glide even where careful speech may give hiatus: piano ˈpjano, cruel ˈkɾwel.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on glides, diphthongs and hiatus (j w / i̯ u̯ notation); yeísmo for the lect per Lipski 1985 and Quilis & Casado-Fresnillo 1995.

## Other choices

### seseo

c before e/i and z are always s; θ is never written: cielo ˈsjelo, zapato saˈpato, hacer aˈseɾ.

Source: Lipski 1985; Quilis & Casado-Fresnillo 1995; the specification notes (seseo).

### voiced stops b d g

Always write b d ɡ, never β ð ɣ. Equatoguinean Spanish is described as often keeping occlusive realisations intervocalically, and broad transcription does not write spirant allophones in any case: lado ˈlado, haba ˈaba, lago ˈlaɡo, todo ˈtodo. Write v as b: vino ˈbino. Word-final d is written: Madrid maˈdɾid, verdad beɾˈdad.

Source: Lipski 1985 on occlusive /b d g/ in Equatorial Guinea; Hualde 2005 on the spirantisation that is not followed here.

### rhotics

Keep the phonemic tap/trill contrast as spelled, following the normative description, even though Lipski reports frequent neutralisation in the lect. Write r for rr, for word-initial r and for r after n, l or s: perro ˈpero, rosa ˈrosa, honra ˈonra, Israel israˈel. Write ɾ elsewhere: pero ˈpeɾo, tres ˈtɾes, comer koˈmeɾ.

Source: Quilis 1993 and Hualde 2005 for the contrast; neutralisation is reported by Lipski 1985 and Quilis & Casado-Fresnillo 1995 but is not written.

### coda /s/

Coda and final /s/ is always s, never h or zero: estos ˈestos, más ˈmas, mismo ˈmismo.

Source: Lipski 1985; Quilis & Casado-Fresnillo 1995 (retention of /s/, conservative feature).

### velar fricative

Write j and g before e/i as x: ojo ˈoxo, gente ˈxente, México ˈmexiko.

Source: Quilis & Casado-Fresnillo 1995; Hualde 2005.

### prenasalisation

Not written. Substrate prenasalisation (e.g. [ᵐb], [ⁿd]) is sporadic and not part of the standard: bola ˈbola, dedo ˈdedo.

Source: Lipski 1985 (sporadic, speaker-dependent); specification note ('possible').

### other spellings

h is silent: hombre ˈombɾe. ch → tʃ: chico ˈtʃiko. ñ → ɲ: año ˈaɲo. qu and gu before e/i → k and ɡ: queso ˈkeso, guerra ˈɡera. x → ks between vowels and before a consonant: examen ekˈsamen, texto ˈteksto. Initial x → s: xilófono siˈlofono. Write vowels as a e i o u with no laxing: ˈpeɾo, ˈoxo.

Source: Hualde 2005; Quilis 1993.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
