# Transcription conventions: Omani Arabic (ar-OM)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables. Leave monosyllables unmarked, e.g. [beːt], [kaːn]. Polysyllabic unstressed function words are also unmarked (see function_words). Count syllables on the transcribed form, after case endings are dropped and the article is adjusted. A syllable is heavy if it is CVV or CVC; a geminate closes the first syllable, so CVC.C counts as heavy. Apply these rules in order: (1) A final superheavy syllable (CVVC or CVCC) takes stress: [kiˈtaːb] 'book', [sikˈkiːn] 'knife', [ʃaːˈfuːh] 'they saw him'. (2) Otherwise a heavy penult takes stress: [muˈʕallim] 'teacher', [sajˈjaːra] 'car'. (3) Otherwise stress the antepenult, or the first syllable of a disyllable: [ˈmadrasa] 'school' (Gulf/Peninsular type, not Cairene madˈrasa), [ˈkatab] 'he wrote'. Word-final long vowels are realised short and do not attract stress: [ˈʃaːfu] 'they saw'. A long vowel created before a suffix counts as heavy, so stress moves: [ˈʃaːfu] but [ʃaːˈfuːh]. Classical case endings play no part because they are not pronounced (see pausal_forms). Only lexicalised adverbial -an is kept, e.g. [ˈʃukran], [ˈʔabadan]. Pronominal suffixes count as part of the word.

Source: Peninsular/Gulf stress rule as in Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic (Routledge Descriptive Grammars), phonology chapter, word stress; Watson 2002, The Phonology and Morphology of Arabic, ch. 5 (stress); Omani data consistent with Glover 1988 (Muscat dialect) as cited in Al-Balushi 2016 pp. 85-90.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following proclitics and particles are unstressed and get no ˈ mark, even when they have two syllables. Article: il/l, e.g. [ilˈbeːt]; stress falls on the noun. Prepositions: b(i), l(i), f(i), min, ʕan, maʕ, ʕala, e.g. [ʕala], [min]. Conjunctions: w/u 'and', ʔaw 'or', bas 'but', laːkin, e.g. [laːkin] unmarked. Relative: illi, e.g. [illi]. Negators: maː, muː/mub. Future/intentional prefix: ba-/bi-, which sits inside the verb word, so the verb takes the stress. Pronominal suffixes are part of their host and can attract stress by making a final superheavy syllable: [ˈbeːtah] 'his house', [ʕaˈleːh] 'on him'. The following carry normal lexical stress: independent pronouns [ˈʔana], [ˈhuwwa], [ˈhijja]; demonstratives [ˈhaːða], [ˈhaːði]; interrogatives [ˈwiːn], [ˈmeːtaː] or similar as written; and the auxiliary/copular kaːn and its forms [ˈkaːnat], [ˈkaːnu]. Monosyllabic forms such as kaːn are unmarked by the general rule.

Source: Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic, sections on the article, prepositions and particles; Holes 2010, Colloquial Arabic of the Gulf (Routledge), introduction on pronunciation; applied to Omani forms as in Al-Balushi 2016 pp. 85-90.

## Pausal forms

The lect has no case inflection, so the pausal (caseless) form applies everywhere: at sentence end, at commas, and sentence-internally alike. Drop all classical case vowels (-u, -a, -i) and all nunation (-un, -an, -in) even if the tashkeel writes them: كِتَابٌ → [kiˈtaːb]. Lexicalised adverbs in -an keep it: [ˈʃukran], [ˈʔabadan], [ˈtaqriːban]. Tāʾ marbūṭa is [a] in the free state, [sajˈjaːra], and [at] in the construct state before a following possessor noun: [sajˈjaːrat] in 'Ahmad's car'. It is also [at] before suffixes: [sajjaːˈrati] or [sajˈjaːrti] as the tashkeel gives it. Do not add pausal [h] after final -a. Final short vowels that belong to the dialect word itself, such as verb suffixes like [ˈʃaːfu] and [ˈkatabu], are kept in all positions.

Source: Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic, morphology of nouns (tāʾ marbūṭa, construct state); Watson 2002 ch. 1 on caseless dialect nouns; diverges from Classical pausal rules, which this lect does not have.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form. The only exception is the article inside a word, which behaves as follows. (1) Before coronal 'sun' consonants (t d s z ʃ sˤ tˤ ðˤ θ ð n r l, and dʒ is not included), the article assimilates as total gemination: [iʃˈʃams], [inˈnaːs]. Before other consonants it is [il]: [ilˈqamar], [ilˈbeːt]. (2) Utterance-initially or after a consonant-final word, write the article with its vowel: [il]/[iC]. After a vowel-final word, drop the article vowel inside the noun's own word: [fi lbeːt], [ʕala ʃʃams]. Never write [ʔ] before the article. Do not write any other cross-word process: no assimilation of place or voicing, no elision of other vowels or of hamza, no linking or epenthetic vowels between words, no resyllabification marks. Written word-initial hamza (hamzat al-qaṭʿ) is always [ʔ]: [ˈʔana].

Source: Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic, phonology (article assimilation and elision of the article vowel after vowels); Watson 2002 ch. 10 (assimilation); restricted to the article by convention for consistency.

## Glides and diphthongs

Glides are always consonant symbols: [j] and [w], geminated [jj] and [ww]. Never use i̯ or u̯. Old diphthongs written ـَيْ and ـَوْ are transcribed as the long monophthongs [eː] and [oː], as in the sedentary coastal (Muscat) norm: بَيْت → [beːt], يَوْم → [joːm], عَلَيْه → [ʕaˈleːh]. Do not write [aj] or [aw] for them; interior/Bedouin diphthong retention is not transcribed. Where a glide is a full consonant or a geminate, write it as a consonant: [sajˈjaːra], [ˈhuwwa], [ˈwalad], [jiˈruːħ].

Source: Holes 1989, 'Towards a dialect geography of Oman' (BSOAS 52); Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic, phonology (monophthongisation); Glover 1988 (Muscat) as cited in Al-Balushi 2016 p. 89.

## Other choices

### qāf

ق is [q] (sedentary Muscat/Ṣalālah default): [ilˈqamar], [ħaqˈquːti]. Do not use Bedouin [ɡ], mountain [k] or Sur/Ṣoħār [dʒ].

Source: Al-Balushi 2016 p. 88; Holes 2014; matches the spec note listing [q] first.

### jīm

ج is [dʒ] in every word: [ˈdʒabal], [raˈdʒaːl]. Do not write the [ɡ] free variant.

Source: Al-Balushi 2016 p. 89 citing Glover 1988:38 on /dʒ/~[ɡ] variation; [dʒ] chosen as the single consistent value.

### kāf affrication

ك is always [k], including next to front vowels and in the 2fem suffix: [ˈkalb], [ˈbeːtik]. OM_K_AFFRICATION ([tʃ]) is a Bedouin/interior feature and is not applied to the sedentary default.

Source: Al-Balushi 2016 p. 89 (Holes 2013); Alshammari 2026 on lexicalised Peninsular affrication; deviates from applying OM_K_AFFRICATION.

### interdentals and emphatics

ث ذ are [θ ð]. ض and ظ both are [ðˤ] (merged). ص ط are [sˤ tˤ]. ح ع are [ħ ʕ], خ غ are [x ɣ]. Examples: [ˈθalaːθa], [ˈðˤuhur], [ˈmarað] for مرض is written [ˈmaraðˤ].

Source: Al-Balushi 2016 p. 87 (retention of interdentals and emphatics); Holes 1990 on the ḍ/ẓ merger in Peninsular Arabic.

### emphatic backing

Broad transcription: write /a/ as [a] next to emphatics too; do not write [ɑ]. Example: [ˈmaraðˤ], not [mɑrɑðˤ].

Source: Al-Balushi 2016 p. 90 describes the [ɑ] allophone; it is omitted here as allophonic.

### vowels and length

Short vowels are [a i u]. Long vowels are [aː iː uː eː oː]. Follow the tashkeel for presence or absence of short vowels: sukūn means no vowel, and initial clusters such as [ktaːb] are written if the text gives them. Geminates are written as doubled letters, never with ː: [muˈʕallim]. Word-final long vowels of the spelling are written short: [ˈʃaːfu].

Source: Holes 1990, Gulf Arabic, phonology (vowel inventory, final vowel shortening); Glover 1988 as cited in Al-Balushi 2016 pp. 85-90.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
