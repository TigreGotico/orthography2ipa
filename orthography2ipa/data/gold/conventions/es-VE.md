# Transcription conventions: Venezuelan Spanish (es-VE)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is lexical and follows the standard spelling. A written accent marks the stressed vowel. Without one, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the penult, and any other word on the final syllable. The orthographic -n/-s decides stress even though they surface as [ŋ]/[h]. Write ˈ before the stressed syllable of every word of two or more syllables. Never mark a monosyllable, whether it is a content or a function word: sol [sol], mar [maɾ], ya [ʝa]. Syllabify within the word to place the mark. Onsets are maximal (pl, pɾ, bl, tɾ, etc.) and an on-glide belongs to the onset, so the mark goes before the whole cluster: plato [ˈplato], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [sjuˈða], tiempo [ˈtjempo]. Coda [h] from /s/ stays with the preceding syllable: este [ˈehte], desastre [deˈsahtɾe]. Adverbs in -mente get ˌ on the adjective's stressed syllable and ˈ on -men-: rápidamente [ˌrapiðaˈmente]. Enclitics written solid with the verb do not move the mark: dámelo [ˈdamelo], diciéndole [diˈsjendole]. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Spanish has none. Never write ː, and write double consonants inside a word as one, e.g. innato is [iˈnato].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on word stress (stress rules, -mente double stress, clitic placement); Real Academia accentuation rules as summarized there.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Because monosyllables are never marked, this choice only decides whether a polysyllabic function word gets ˈ. Unstressed words get no mark. These are the definite articles; all prepositions except según (para [paɾa], desde [dehde], hacia [asja], sobre [soβɾe], entre [entɾe], hasta [ahta], contra [kontɾa]); and the conjunctions pero [peɾo], porque [poɾke], aunque [awŋke], como [komo] and mientras [mjentɾah]. Relative donde, cuando and como (without accent) are also unstressed, as are prenominal possessives (nuestro [nwehtɾo], nuestra casa [nwehtɾa ˈkasa]). Object clitics are unstressed, as is tan. Stressed words get ˈ. These are según [seˈɣuŋ]; indefinite articles (una [ˈuna], unos [ˈunoh]); numerals; tonic pronouns (ella [ˈeʝa], nosotros [noˈsotɾoh], usted [uhˈte]); and interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent (cómo [ˈkomo], dónde [ˈdonde], cuándo [ˈkwando]). Demonstratives (esta [ˈehta]), postnominal possessives (nuestro used predicatively, mío [ˈmio]) and adverbs are also stressed. All verb forms are stressed, including auxiliary haber and ser/estar (hemos [ˈemoh], había [aˈβia], estaba [ehˈtaβa]).

Source: Hualde 2005, section on stressed and unstressed words (the tonic/atonic word classes, following Navarro Tomás and Quilis).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Word-final segments are realized the same way before a pause, a comma or another word. These realizations are final /s/ as [h], final /n/ as [ŋ] and final /d/ as zero; see connected_speech and other. The only effect of a pause is on the stop/approximant choice for the next word's initial /b d ɡ/.

Source: Hualde 2005 (absence of pausal morphology); Fontanella de Weinberg 1992, Caribbean/Venezuelan chapter, for invariant final realizations.

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its own place. Across a boundary, only the realization of the edge segments changes, and the words are never joined. (1) Synalepha and vowel merger are not written. Each word keeps all its vowels and no glide is formed across the boundary: la amiga [la aˈmiɣa], me ha dicho [me a ˈditʃo], su hijo [su ˈiho]. (2) Resyllabification is not written, and final /s/ stays [h] before a vowel: los amigos [loh aˈmiɣoh]. (3) Spirantization applies across boundaries. Word-initial /b d ɡ/ is a stop [b d ɡ] in the first word of the sentence, after a comma or other pause punctuation, and after a nasal; /d/ is also a stop after [l]. Elsewhere, after a vowel, [h], [ɾ], [r] or [l] (except /d/ after [l]), it is an approximant [β ð ɣ]: el dedo [el ˈdeðo], un gato [uŋ ˈɡato], la boca [la ˈβoka], los dedos [loh ˈðeðoh], por donde [poɾ ðonde]. Word-internally the same rule holds: hombre [ˈombɾe], caldo [ˈkaldo], algo [ˈalɣo], verde [ˈbeɾðe]. (4) Word-final /n/ is [ŋ] in every context (before a vowel, a consonant or a pause) and does not assimilate across the boundary: un beso [uŋ ˈbeso], en agua [eŋ ˈaɣwa], son [soŋ]. A word-internal coda nasal takes the place of the following consonant: [m] before p b m, [ŋ] before k ɡ and [h] from /x/, [n] elsewhere (campo [ˈkampo], cinco [ˈsiŋko], ángel [ˈaŋhel], enfermo [enˈfeɾmo]). (5) Word-initial /r/ is the trill [r] everywhere, including after a vowel-final word: la rosa [la ˈrosa]. Final /ɾ/ before a vowel stays a tap: por eso [poɾ ˈeso]. (6) No voicing of [h] is written.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on spirantization of /b d ɡ/, nasal assimilation and velarization, and syllabification across word boundaries; Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 on velar -n and aspiration in Caribbean Spanish.

## Glides and diphthongs

Unstressed i/u (spelled i, u, y) next to another vowel in the same word is non-syllabic. Write a prenuclear glide as [j], [w] and a postnuclear glide as [i̯], [u̯]: tiene [ˈtjene], cuatro [ˈkwatɾo], Venezuela [beneˈswela], aire [ˈai̯ɾe], hoy [oi̯], muy [mui̯], causa [ˈkau̯sa], aunque [au̯ŋke], ciudad [sjuˈða]. (In function_words examples, aunque follows this rule: [au̯ŋke].) Triphthongs combine the two: buey [bwei̯]. Spelling hie-/ye- and ll give the consonant [ʝ], not [j]: hielo [ˈʝelo], yo [ʝo]. Spelling hue- gives [w] (huevo [ˈweβo]). Güe/güi gives [ɡw]/[ɣw] by the spirantization rule (agua [ˈaɣwa]). Hiatus is kept where the spelling has an accented í/ú (país [paˈih], día [ˈdia], baúl [baˈul]). It is also kept for two non-high vowels (poeta [poˈeta], teatro [teˈatɾo]): no colloquial glide formation is written. The conjunction y is [i] in every context.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on vowel sequences, diphthongs and hiatus (notation [j w] prenuclear, [i̯ u̯] postnuclear).

## Other choices

### coda /s/ (aspiration)

This follows the Caracas/coastal Caribbean norm, not the conservative Andean highlands. Every syllable-final /s/ (spelled s, z, or x before a consonant) is written [h], word-internally and word-finally, before consonants, vowels and pauses. It is never deleted and never written [ɦ]: mismo [ˈmihmo], los niños [loh ˈniɲoh], más [mah], luz [luh], gracias [ˈɡɾasjah]. Variable s-deletion, though frequent in casual speech, is not written.

Source: Fontanella de Weinberg 1992, Caribbean area / Venezuela; Hualde 2005, section on aspiration of /s/.

### /x/

Spelled j and ge/gi (and x in México and similar words) are written [h]: gente [ˈhente], jamás [haˈmah], México [ˈmehiko]. Spelled h is silent.

Source: Fontanella de Weinberg 1992; Hualde 2005, section on dialectal realizations of /x/.

### seseo and yeísmo

Spelled c(e,i), z and s are all /s/: cinco [ˈsiŋko], caza = casa [ˈkasa]. Spelled ll and y (consonantal) are both [ʝ] in every position: calle [ˈkaʝe], llamar [ʝaˈmaɾ], cónyuge [ˈkoɲʝuhe]. No [ɟʝ] or [ʎ] is written.

Source: Penny 2002, chapters on seseo and yeísmo in American Spanish; Hualde 2005, section on /ʝ/.

### final /d/ and intervocalic -d-

Word-final /d/ is deleted and the stress mark stays: verdad [beɾˈða], usted [uhˈte], Madrid [maˈðɾi]. Intervocalic /d/, including participial -ado, is kept as [ð]: cansado [kanˈsaðo], dedo [ˈdeðo]. Colloquial [ˈao] is not written.

Source: Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 (Caribbean -d loss); Hualde 2005 for [ð] retention in the standard.

### rhotics and laterals

Use the trill [r] word-initially, after n, l and s, and for spelled rr: perro [ˈpero], honra [ˈonra], Israel [ihraˈel]. Use the tap [ɾ] elsewhere, including in codas and word-finally: pero [ˈpeɾo], comer [koˈmeɾ]. Coda /ɾ/ and /l/ are kept distinct: no lateralization or rhotacism is written (Venezuelan norm, unlike Puerto Rican).

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on rhotics; Fontanella de Weinberg 1992 on Venezuelan maintenance of /ɾ/ vs /l/.

### segment symbols

Use the five vowels [a e i o u] with no lax or nasal allophones. Write the affricate ch as [tʃ] (mucho [ˈmutʃo]) and ñ as [ɲ]. Write x between vowels as [ks] (examen [ekˈsameŋ]) and word-initial x- as [s]. Write f as [f].

Source: Hualde 2005, inventory chapters.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
