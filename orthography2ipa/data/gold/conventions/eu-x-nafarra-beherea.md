# Transcription conventions: Lower Navarrese Basque (eu-x-nafarra-beherea)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Post-initial stress. Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable on every stressed word of two or more syllables. Leave monosyllables unmarked. Count syllables on the whole inflected word, including the suffixed article, plural, case and postpositional endings. A falling diphthong (ai, ei, oi, au, eu, ui) is one syllable; any other vowel sequence is two. (1) Words of three or more syllables take stress on the second syllable: etxea [eˈtʃea], gizona [ɡiˈs̻ona], mendia [menˈdia], liburua [liˈbuɾua], euskara [eu̯s̺ˈkaɾa]. (2) Disyllables take stress on the first syllable: gizon [ˈɡis̻on], etxe [ˈetʃe], mendi [ˈmendi]. Adding case endings never moves the stress further right: liburuarekin [liˈbuɾuaɾekin], etxeetan [eˈtʃeetan]. There are no phonemic long vowels: write a sequence of identical vowels across a morpheme boundary as two vowels, never with ː (etxeetan [eˈtʃeetan]). There are no geminates: rr is the trill [r], ll is [ʎ], tt is [c] and dd is [ɟ], all single segments. Stress is phonetically weak in this dialect, but it is still marked, for consistency. Do not use the penultimate pattern of eastern (Amikuze) Low Navarrese and Souletin.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Accentuation', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), A Grammar of Basque, ch. 1 (accent section following the segmental phonology, pp. 65ff), on the post-initial system of the non-Souletin varieties. Hualde (1999), 'Basque accentuation', in van der Hulst (ed.), Word Prosodic Systems in the Languages of Europe, on penultimate stress in Soule and the bordering Low Navarrese, which is not followed here. The post-initial choice agrees with the old stress window of northern aspiration (Michelena FHV, reported in Hualde 2018: 5-6).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Basque has no free articles or prepositions. Articles, case endings and most postpositions are suffixes inside the word, so they share the host's stress (etxean [eˈtʃean]). (1) Unstressed (no ˈ) are all finite forms of izan, *edun/ukan, *edin and *ezan, whether auxiliary or copula: dira [diɾa], ditut [ditut], zuen [s̻uen], gara [ɡaɾa]. Also unstressed are the conjunctions and particles eta [eta], edo [edo], baina [bai̯na], ere [eɾe] and ba [ba]. (2) Stressed are synthetic main verbs (dakit [ˈdakit], nago [ˈnaɡo]); personal pronouns (zuek [ˈs̻uek], haiek [ˈhai̯ek]); demonstratives (hori [ˈhoɾi], hauek [ˈhau̯ek]); free postpositions (gabe [ˈɡabe], ondoan [onˈdoan]); and nonfinite verbs (etorri [eˈtori]). The negator ez and other monosyllables are prosodically stressed but carry no mark, because monosyllables are never marked.

Source: Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (2003), A Grammar of Basque: Hualde's accent section (ch. 1), on the auxiliary forming an unstressed group with the preceding stressed word; Saltarelli (1988), Basque, on the morphology of suffixed articles and case.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Basque has no pausal forms and no nunation. Every word is written identically at sentence end, at a comma and sentence-internally, and final vowels and case endings are never dropped or altered: etxean [eˈtʃean] in every position.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.): no pausal alternations are described.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form, except for one sandhi process. After the negator ez, a following word-initial b d g is written devoiced as [p t k], while ez itself stays [es̻]: ez dut [es̻ tut], ez da [es̻ ta], ez gara [es̻ kaɾa], ez baita [es̻ pai̯ta]. Do not write anything else across word boundaries: no vowel elision, no liaison, no resyllabification marking, and no cross-word nasal place assimilation (zen bat [s̻en bat]). Inside a word, write the place assimilation of n before a labial as [m], as in zenbat [ˈs̻embat], and leave n unassimilated elsewhere.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), pp. 15-65, sections on obstruent devoicing after sibilants and on nasal assimilation (the ez + stop sandhi, cf. northern spellings like eztut).

## Glides and diphthongs

(1) Write falling diphthongs with the non-syllabic diacritic, one syllable each: ai [ai̯], ei [ei̯], oi [oi̯], ui [ui̯], au [au̯], eu [eu̯]. Examples: baina [bai̯na], gauza [ˈɡau̯s̻a], euskara [eu̯s̺ˈkaɾa]. (2) Write orthographic j and y as the consonant [j], following northern usage rather than Gipuzkoan [x]: jan [jan], jaun [jau̯n]. (3) Any other i or u next to a vowel is a full vowel in hiatus, counted as its own syllable: egia [eˈɡia], suak [ˈs̺uak], ondoan [onˈdoan]. (4) Do not write automatic palatalization after i unless the spelling shows it (ñ [ɲ], ll [ʎ], tt [c], dd [ɟ]).

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), sections on diphthongs and glides and on the dialectal realization of j (northern [j]); Saltarelli (1988).

## Other choices

### aspiration: /h/

Write [h] wherever the spelling has h, including after a consonant in traditional northern spellings: hura [ˈhuɾa], ehun [ˈehun], mahai [ˈmahai̯], elhe [ˈelhe]. Never drop it. Do not add an h that the spelling lacks.

Source: Hualde (2018), 'Aspiration in Basque', Papers in Historical Phonology 3, pp. 1-3.

### aspirated stops

Write [pʰ tʰ kʰ] only where the spelling shows ph, th or kh (traditional Navarro-Lapurdian spelling): aphez [ˈapʰes̻], athe [ˈatʰe], ekharri [eˈkʰari]. Where the spelling has plain p t k, as in Batua spelling, write plain [p t k]. This departs from the specification note, which lists [pʰ tʰ kʰ] as conditioned variants. Hualde treats Low Navarrese aspirates as lexically and historically restricted (to the stressed onset within the first two syllables) and not systematically present, so they are not inserted by rule.

Source: Hualde (2018), pp. 2 and 5-6 (after Michelena FHV).

### sibilants and affricates

Keep the three-way contrast: z [s̻], s [s̺], x [ʃ], tz [ts̻], ts [ts̺], tx [tʃ]. Examples: zu [s̻u], su [s̺u], xori [ˈʃoɾi], hitz [hits̻], hots [hots̺], etxe [ˈetʃe]. Do not merge laminal and apical sibilants.

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.); Ladefoged & Maddieson (1996), on Basque laminal and apical sibilants.

### rhotics

Use the conservative alveolar system, not the uvular [ʁ] that is spreading among younger northern speakers under French influence. Write single intervocalic r as the tap [ɾ] (liburua [liˈbuɾua]). Write rr, and r before a consonant or word-finally, as the trill [r]: etorri [eˈtori], ur [ur], ardo [ˈardo].

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), rhotic section, including the note on northern uvular r.

### voiced stops

Write /b d ɡ/ as [b d ɡ] in all positions. Do not write the spirantized allophones [β ð ɣ] in this broad transcription: ogia [oˈɡia], ados [ˈados̺].

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), on spirantization as allophonic.

### front rounded vowel

Low Navarrese has no /y/ (that vowel is Souletin). Write u as [u] everywhere: su [s̺u], hura [ˈhuɾa].

Source: Hualde (2003), 'Segmental phonology', in Hualde & Ortiz de Urbina (eds.), on vowels: /y/ is restricted to Souletin and its immediate border area.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
