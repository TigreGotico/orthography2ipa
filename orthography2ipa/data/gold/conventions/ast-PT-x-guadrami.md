# Transcription conventions: Guadramilese (ast-PT-x-guadrami)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is lexical and falls on one of the last three syllables, as in the rest of Astur-Leonese. Mark it with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable. Mark no other syllable boundaries and no secondary stress. Default rule when the spelling gives no accent: (a) words ending in a vowel, -s or -n are stressed on the penult: cuorpo [ˈkwɔɾpo], muarto [ˈmwɐɾto], noibo [ˈnojbo], diban [ˈdiban]; (b) words ending in any other consonant are stressed on the last syllable: cumbersar [kumbeɾˈsaɾ], almuorço [alˈmwɔɾso] follows (a). A written acute or circumflex overrides the default: salíen [saˈli.en] is written [saˈlien]. Imperfects in -aba/-ában and -ie/-ien keep the stem vowel stressed: cumbidaba [kumbiˈdaba], dezien [deˈzien] → see 'other' for the sibilant, written [deˈsien]. Content monosyllables also get ˈ: you [ˈjow]. A diphthong counts as one syllable. The stress mark goes before the glide of a rising diphthong: [ˈkwɔɾpo], not [kˈwɔɾpo]. The lect has no long vowels, no geminates and no case endings. Orthographic ⟨rr⟩ is a single trill [r] and is not lengthened.

Source: General Romance/Astur-Leonese stress placement as in Menéndez Pidal (1906) El dialecto leonés, and the Portuguese-side border descriptions of Santos (1967) 'Os falares fronteiriços de Trás-os-Montes'. Mark placement follows the inherited ast-PT-x-rionor grapheme specification.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed proclitics and carry no ˈ: the definite articles (masculine singular is Portuguese o, the rest Leonese la, los, las), prepositions, the conjunctions and complementizers i/e, que, se, ni, mas, and the clitic object and reflexive pronouns me, te, se, le, lo, la, mos, bos, les/lhes. Examples: o noibo [o ˈnojbo], la boda [la ˈboda], de la tierra [de la ˈtjera], que se casában [ke se kaˈsaban]. Write the article o as [o], following the grapheme rule. The Portuguese [u] realization is plausible but not written, for consistency. The following carry ˈ even when monosyllabic: subject and prepositional pronouns (you [ˈjow], tu [ˈtu], el [ˈel], mi [ˈmi]), demonstratives, possessives used as pronouns, adverbs, numerals, and every finite verb form, including auxiliaries and copulas. Examples: ye [ˈje], ban [ˈban], stában [sˈtaban]. Hyphen-joined enclitics are separate words and stay unstressed: dixo-le [ˈdiʃo le].

Source: Clitic/stress-word distinction as standard for Astur-Leonese in Menéndez Pidal (1906). The article paradigm (o/la/los/las) follows the lect description (Miro 2026, Paper III §5.1, as cited in the specification).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Guadramilese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Words are written identically at a comma, at sentence end and sentence-internally, and final vowels are never dropped or changed before pause: [ˈmwɐɾto] everywhere.

Source: No published description of Guadramilese or Rionorese (Vasconcelos 1900; Santos 1967) reports pausal alternations.

## Connected speech

Write every word in its citation (isolation) form. Apply no cross-boundary process. Specifically: do not elide or merge vowels across words (no sinalefa), so la amiga is [la aˈmiga]. Do not resyllabify final consonants onto a following vowel, and add no link marks. Do not voice or palatalize a word-final /s/ before a voiced sound or a vowel: los noibos [los ˈnojbos], not [loʒ]/[loz]. Do not assimilate a word-final nasal to the next consonant: en Guadramil [en gwadɾaˈmil]. Rhotics are fixed by their position inside the word: a word-initial ⟨r⟩ stays trill [r] even after a vowel-final word (la ropa [la ˈropa]), and a word-final ⟨r⟩ stays flap [ɾ] even before a vowel (dar a [ˈdaɾ a]). Word-internal processes given by the grapheme rules are written: nasal place before a stop inside a word is written as the homorganic nasal, cumbidar [kumbiˈdaɾ].

Source: Citation-form convention of the inherited ast-PT-x-rionor grapheme specification. No published description of the lect (Vasconcelos 1900; Santos 1967; Frías-Conde 2005) documents cross-word sandhi in enough detail to transcribe it consistently.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vocoids with the consonant symbols j and w, never i̯ u̯, in both rising and falling diphthongs.
- Rising diphthongs from diphthongization: ie [je] as in tierra [ˈtjera]; uo [wo]; uô [wɔ] as in cuorpo [ˈkwɔɾpo]; uâ [wɐ] as in cuarda [ˈkwɐɾda], muarto [ˈmwɐɾto]. The extreme Guadramil variants with [wɐ] are written as spelled.
- After g/q: [gw], [kw], as in Guadramil [gwadɾaˈmil].
- Falling diphthongs: ai [aj], ei [ej], oi [oj] as in noibo [ˈnojbo], ou [ow] as in you [ˈjow].
- Word-initial ⟨y⟩ or ⟨i⟩ before a vowel is [j].
- Vowels in hiatus, usually marked by an accent, are two syllables written as two full vowels with no glide: salíen [saˈlien].

Source: Diphthong inventory from Frías-Conde (2005) El Habla de Sanabria, p. 3 comparative table, and the ast-PT-x-rionor graphemes (⟨uô⟩=/wɔ/, ⟨uâ⟩=/wɐ/). Symbol choice j/w follows standard broad-IPA practice for Ibero-Romance.

## Other choices

### Sibilants (seseo)

⟨ç⟩, ⟨z⟩ and ⟨c⟩+e/i are all [s], never [θ]: almuorço [alˈmwɔɾso], fazien [faˈsien]. ⟨s⟩ is [s] in all positions, with no voicing contrast written. ⟨ch⟩ and ⟨x⟩ are [ʃ], ⟨tch⟩ is [tʃ], and ⟨g⟩+e/i is [x]. In the 'dizer' paradigm, follow the spelling through these rules. Do not write [θ] for a spelling like diç, which is written [ˈdis]; the possible dental reflex is unconfirmed.

Source: Vasconcelos (1900) Estudos de Philologia Mirandesa II, p. 54 (guadramilês merges with Galician [s]); ast-PT-x-rionor grapheme specification.

### Labials and voiced stops

Full betacism: ⟨v⟩ and ⟨b⟩ are both written [b]. Write /b d g/ as stops in all positions; the spirant allophones [β ð ɣ] are not written in this broad transcription. Examples: noibo [ˈnojbo], boda [ˈboda].

Source: Betacism as described for Leonese by Menéndez Pidal (1906) and for the Trás-os-Montes border by Santos (1967). Broad-stop convention from the specification notes.

### Rhotics

Write trill [r] word-initially, after a consonant (n, l, s) and for ⟨rr⟩. Write flap [ɾ] elsewhere, including syllable-finally and word-finally: rio [ˈrio], honra [ˈonra], cuarda [ˈkwɐɾda].

Source: Positional rhotic rule of the ast-PT-x-rionor specification, consistent with Ibero-Romance descriptions (Penny 2002).

### Unstressed vowels and nasality

Write unstressed vowels with their full values as the grapheme rules give them: final -o is [o], final -e is [e], -a is [a]. Portuguese-type raising and reduction ([u], [ɨ], [ɐ]) is reported for Portuguese-side speech but is not written, except where the spelling ⟨â⟩ gives [ɐ]. Write a vowel before a nasal as an oral vowel plus a nasal consonant, with no tilde: diban [ˈdiban], not [ˈdibɐ̃w̃]. Write unstressed con- raised to [u] only where the spelling has cum-: cumbidaba [kumbiˈdaba].

Source: ast-PT-x-rionor grapheme specification. The raising is documented in the lect description (Miro 2026 as cited). Mateus & d'Andrade (2000) describe the Portuguese reduction that is deliberately not followed here.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
