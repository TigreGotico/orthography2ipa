# Transcription conventions: Micaelense Portuguese (pt-PT-x-sao-miguel)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Lexical stress follows standard European Portuguese placement, recoverable from spelling. (1) A written acute or circumflex accent marks the stressed syllable: café [kɐˈfɛ], avô [ɐˈvo], público [ˈpybliku]. A tilde marks it unless another accent is present: irmã [iɾˈmɐ̃], órgão [ˈɔɾɡɐ̃w̃]. (2) Unaccented words ending in -a, -e or -o (plus optional -s), or in -am, -em or -ens, take penultimate stress: fruta [ˈfɾytɐ], noite [ˈnøtɨ]. (3) Other unaccented words take final stress: azul [ɐˈzyɫ], comer [kuˈmeɾ], aqui [ɐˈki]. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable, with maximal legal onsets: [ˈfɾytɐ], not [ˈf.ɾytɐ]. Mark stress on every word of two or more syllables, including function words. Leave monosyllables unmarked: cruz [kɾyʃ], tu [ty]. Write no secondary stress. Stressed-syllable segmental changes apply only in the syllable marked ˈ, or in a stressed monosyllable: oral stressed /u/ becomes [y]. Portuguese has no phonemic vowel length, so write no ː, even though Silva 2008 writes leite [leːt]. It has no geminates, so double ⟨ss⟩ and ⟨rr⟩ are single segments [s] and [ʁ]. It has no case endings, so that part does not apply.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, chapter on word stress (EP stress placement). The fronting gated by stress follows Silva 2008: 4, list (1), and Rogers 1948.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed in connected speech, written with reduced vowels and no stress mark:
- definite articles: o/os [u]/[uʃ], a/as [ɐ]/[ɐʃ];
- prepositions and contractions: de [dɨ], em [ɐ̃j̃], a/à [ɐ]/[a], por [puɾ], com [kõ], do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], ao [aw];
- conjunctions: e [i], ou [ø] (by the ⟨ou⟩ rule), que [kɨ], se [sɨ], mas [mɐʃ], nem [nɐ̃j̃];
- object and reflexive clitics: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [sɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], o/a [u]/[ɐ], nos [nuʃ], vos [vuʃ], lhes [ʎɨʃ].
Unstressed ⟨u⟩ in these words is never fronted: o [u], not [y]. Disyllabic function words keep their lexical stress mark and full stressed vowel: para [ˈpɐɾɐ], pelo [ˈpelu], uma [ˈymɐ], sobre [ˈsobɾɨ]. Do not write the colloquial contraction [pɾɐ]. Stressed and treated as content words: subject and strong pronouns, tu [ty], eu [ew], ele [ˈelɨ], nós [nɔʃ], mim [mĩ]; possessives; demonstratives; quantifiers; numerals, including um [ũ]; negation não [nɐ̃w̃]; all auxiliary and copula forms, é [ɛ], está [ɨʃˈta], sou [sø], foi [fø], tem [tɐ̃j̃]. Since monosyllables carry no ˈ, stress on a stressed monosyllable shows only in its unreduced vowel and in fronting: tu [ty], sul [syɫ]. Write não as [nɐ̃w̃], not the lexical 'non' variant.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, on clitics and unstressed vowel reduction. Micaelense tu [ty] is from Portuguese With Leo 2021 (São Miguel) and Silva 2008: 4.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word before a comma or at the end of the sentence is written exactly as in isolation. Final /s/ there is [ʃ]: casas [ˈkazɐʃ]. Final unstressed vowels are kept in full: oito [ˈøtu], noite [ˈnøtɨ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, on EP word-final segments. Final-vowel deletion per Silva 1998 and Silva 2008: 4 is not written; see 'other'.

## Connected speech

Write only one cross-word process: the voicing of word-final /s/ (spelled -s, -z, or -x where it is [ʃ]), applied to the final segment of the first word.
- Before a pause or a voiceless consonant: [ʃ], as in as casas [ɐʃ ˈkazɐʃ], dez pés [dɛʃ pɛʃ].
- Before a voiced consonant: [ʒ], as in os dias [uʒ ˈdiɐʃ], mais nada [majʒ ˈnadɐ].
- Before a vowel-initial word: [ʒ] in this lect, not the standard [z]. Examples: os amigos [uʒ ɐˈmiɡuʃ], todos os [ˈtoduʒ uʃ], és um [ɛʒ ũ].
The vowel-initial rule also applies across h- (os homens [uʒ ˈɔmɐ̃j̃ʃ]) and when the next word starts with a glide (os ouvidos [uʒ øˈviduʃ]).
Write no vowel elision, crasis or diphthongisation across words: de amigo [dɨ ɐˈmiɡu], que é [kɨ ɛ], not [dɐˈmiɡu]. Write no liaison or resyllabification. Final /ɾ/ stays [ɾ] before a vowel: comer a [kuˈmeɾ ɐ]. Final /l/ stays [ɫ]: azul escuro [ɐˈzyɫ ɨʃˈkuɾu]. Each word keeps its own segments in its own place.

Source: Portuguese With Leo 2021, 'The 8 Portuguese accents of Portugal': Azorean–Algarvean prevocalic [ʒ] ('quijentrar', 'Todojos', 'Éjunúmerúm'). The preconsonantal [ʃ]/[ʒ] alternation is from Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, on sibilant sandhi. This departs from standard EP prevocalic [z].

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides with the consonant letters j and w, never i̯ or u̯.
- Falling oral diphthongs: pai [paj], mau [maw], eu [ew], céu [sɛw], herói [iˈɾɔj], fui [fyj].
- Nasal diphthongs: mãe [mɐ̃j̃], põe [põj̃], muito [ˈmũj̃tu], cão [kɐ̃w̃], são [sɐ̃w̃], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃].
- Final -em/-ens: [ɐ̃j̃]/[ɐ̃j̃ʃ], as in tem [tɐ̃j̃].
- Rising sequences: unstressed ⟨i⟩/⟨u⟩ after a consonant and before a vowel are [j]/[w]. Examples: história [iʃˈtɔɾjɐ], água [ˈaɡwɐ], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du].
Micaelense monophthongs:
(a) Tautosyllabic ⟨oi⟩ and ⟨ou⟩, stressed or not, are the single vowel [ø]: oito [ˈøtu], noite [ˈnøtɨ], dois [døʃ], pouco [ˈpøku], outubro [øˈtybɾu]. Accented ⟨ói⟩ keeps [ɔj] (dói [dɔj]). Hiatus ⟨o-i⟩ keeps two vowels (moinho [muˈiɲu]).
(b) ⟨ei⟩ before a consonant is the monophthong [e]: leite [ˈletɨ], primeiro [pɾiˈmeɾu], feito [ˈfetu]. Word-finally and before a vowel it stays [ej]: sei [sej], ideia [iˈdejɐ]. This departs from the inherited Lisbon-type [ɐj] of the pt-PT base.
The singular ⟨ão⟩ stays [ɐ̃w̃], not the variable 'cã' monophthong, as the specification notes say.

Source: Silva 2008: 4, list (1) for ⟨oi⟩/⟨ou⟩ → [ø], and list (2) for ⟨ei⟩ monophthongisation. Glide notation follows Mateus & d'Andrade 2000. The variable ⟨ão⟩ is from Portuguese With Leo 2021 (São Miguel).

## Other choices

### Stressed /u/ fronting

Every oral stressed /u/ is [y], in open and closed syllables alike: uva [ˈyvɐ], fruta [ˈfɾytɐ], cruz [kɾyʃ], azul [ɐˈzyɫ], tu [ty], uma [ˈymɐ], público [ˈpybliku]. Unstressed /u/ stays [u]: fumar [fuˈmaɾ], oito [ˈøtu]. Nasal /ũ/ stays [ũ]: um [ũ], mundo [ˈmũdu]. ⟨u⟩ as a glide is [w]: água [ˈaɡwɐ].

Source: Silva 2008: 4, list (1), where fronting applies before a coda too. This supersedes the open-syllable restriction in Rogers 1948.

### ⟨o⟩ before [ʒ]

Stressed ⟨o⟩ before a [ʒ] spelled ⟨j⟩ or ⟨g⟩ is [ø]: hoje [ˈøʒɨ], loja [ˈløʒɐ]. This follows Silva, although the specification notes say this case is not covered by its rule.

Source: Silva 2008: 4, list (1) (hoje [øʒ]).

### /l/ after /i/

Word-internal intervocalic /l/ immediately after /i/, stressed or not, is [ʎ]: quilo [ˈkiʎu], Filipa [fiˈʎipɐ], vila [ˈviʎɐ]. It does not apply across word boundaries or after other vowels (bola [ˈbɔlɐ]). Coda /l/ is [ɫ] (mil [miɫ]). Silva writes [ɐˈzyl], but this transcription writes [ɐˈzyɫ] for the same phoneme.

Source: Portuguese With Leo 2021, 'The 8 Portuguese accents' (Filipa → 'Flhipa'); Segura 2013, pp. 85-142. Coda [ɫ] is from Mateus & d'Andrade 2000.

### Mid vowel before palatals

Stressed /e/ before a palatal stays [e], with no Lisbon centralisation to [ɐ]: coelho [kuˈeʎu], espelho [ɨʃˈpeʎu], venho [ˈveɲu], vejo [ˈveʒu]. The notes' rendering [ˈkɔɛʎu] is not followed; this transcription writes [kuˈeʎu].

Source: Portuguese With Leo 2021, 'The 8 Portuguese accents' (São Miguel 'coêlho' vs Madeira 'coâlho').

### Tonic chain shift and final-vowel deletion

Neither is written; both are treated as variable stereotypes. Stressed vowels other than /u/ keep their standard EP values: sete [ˈsɛtɨ], avó [ɐˈvɔ], avô [ɐˈvo], not [sæt], [ɐˈvo], [ɐˈvu]. Final unstressed [ɐ ɨ u] are always written: oito [ˈøtu], noite [ˈnøtɨ]. This follows the specification notes rather than Silva's transcriptions.

Source: Silva 2008: 3-4, lists (1)-(2) and abstract; Silva 1998 on variable final-vowel elision.

### Unstressed vowels and rhotics

Use standard EP reduction: unstressed ⟨a⟩ → [ɐ], ⟨e⟩ → [ɨ] (word-initial e- → [i], as in estar [ɨʃˈtaɾ]; conjunction e → [i]), and ⟨o⟩ → [u]. Write the tap as [ɾ] (fruta [ˈfɾytɐ], comer [kuˈmeɾ]), with final -r kept, not deleted as in 'comê'. Write the strong r (initial r-, rr, or r after n, l, s) as [ʁ]: rua [ˈʁyɐ], carro [ˈkaʁu]. The notes' [r] in fruta/cruz is the tap [ɾ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, on vowel reduction and rhotics. Final-R deletion is from Portuguese With Leo 2021 (São Miguel), not written.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
