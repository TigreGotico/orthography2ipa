# Transcription conventions: Valluno (Valle del Cauca) Spanish (es-CO-x-valluno)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Spanish stress is lexical and is read from the spelling. A written accent marks the stressed vowel (está esˈta, árbol ˈaɾbol, teléfono teˈlefono). With no accent mark, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s takes penultimate stress (casa ˈkasa, hablan ˈablan, lunes ˈlunes). Any other word takes final stress (ciudad sjuˈdad, hablar aˈblaɾ, reloj reˈloh). Write ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included (sol ˈsol, mar ˈmaɾ, pan ˈpan). Words in -mente keep the stress of the adjective only (rápidamente ˈrapidamente). Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Enclitics written solid with the verb keep the verb's stress (dámelo ˈdamelo, diciéndole diˈsjendole). Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so those parts of this topic do not apply. Double vowels are two syllables (leer leˈeɾ, creemos kɾeˈemos).

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, CUP, ch. 11 (stress); Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, Gredos, chapter on the accent; RAE/ASALE (2010), Ortografía de la lengua española, ch. 2 (accentuation rules).

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words are unstressed and written with no ˈ. Definite articles: el la los las lo (el ˈniɲo → el). All prepositions except según, including the disyllables para, desde, hacia, entre, sobre (para paɾa, desde desde). Conjunctions: y e o u ni que pero sino si (conditional) como (conjunction) aunque pues mas (y i, pero peɾo, si si). Atonic object and reflexive clitics: me te se lo la le nos les los las (se lo ˈdio → se lo ˈdio). Prenominal possessives: mi tu su mis tus sus (mi ˈkasa → mi). Relatives without an accent mark: que quien donde cuando cual como (la ˈkasa donde ˈviβo → donde). Also tan before an adjective. These words are stressed and take ˈ: indefinite articles un una unos unas (un ˈun, una ˈuna). Demonstratives (este ˈeste, eso ˈeso). Subject pronouns and prepositional pronouns (yo ˈʝo, él ˈel, mí ˈmi, ti ˈti, usted usˈted). Postnominal and pronominal possessives (mío ˈmio, nuestro ˈnwestɾo). Interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent mark (qué ˈke, dónde ˈdonde). Según seˈɣun. Negation no ˈno. All verb forms, auxiliaries included (ha ˈa, he ˈe, hemos ˈemos, está esˈta, va ˈba). All numerals, adverbs, nouns, adjectives and main verbs are also stressed.

Source: Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, chapter on the accent (inventory of tonic and atonic words); Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1977), Manual de pronunciación española, the sections on palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas; Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 11.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, no case endings and no nunation: every word is written the same at a comma, at sentence end and inside a phrase (casas ˈkasas everywhere).

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish (no pausal morphology is described for Spanish); Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Colombia (Cali keeps final /s/ in standard speech, so no phrase-final change is written).

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its citation form, in its own slot. Resyllabification across word boundaries is not written, and no syllable dots are used (los otros los ˈotɾos, not lo.ˈso.tɾos). Synalepha and vowel fusion between words are not written, so both vowels are kept in full (la escuela la esˈkwela, de ella de ˈeʝa, mi hijo mi ˈiho). Nasal place assimilation across a boundary is not written (un poco ˈun ˈpoko, not ˈum). Voicing of /s/ before a voiced consonant is not written (las manos las ˈmanos). Within words, /n/ is also written n before any consonant (tango ˈtanɡo, enfermo enˈfeɾmo). Word-final /n/ is n; its velarized realization [ŋ] in Cali speech is not written. The conjunction y between vowels stays i (Juan y Ana ˈhwan i ˈana).

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chs. 6–7 and 10 (resyllabification, nasal assimilation, synalepha), treated as allophonic and so left out of a broad transcription. Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Colombia, for velar /n/ in the Pacific/Valle region, which is likewise not written in this broad transcription.

## Glides and diphthongs

Follow Hualde: unstressed i/u before the nucleus are the glides j w (tierra ˈtjera, bueno ˈbweno, ciudad sjuˈdad, cuatro ˈkwatɾo). Unstressed i/u after the nucleus are i̯ u̯ (aire ˈai̯ɾe, seis ˈsei̯s, causa ˈkau̯sa, hoy ˈoi̯, muy ˈmui̯). In ui/iu the second vowel is the nucleus (muy ˈmui̯ is the fixed exception; ruido ˈrwido, viuda ˈbjuda). An accented í/ú forms a hiatus and is a full vowel (tía ˈtia, país paˈis, baúl baˈul). Consonantal y/ll is the yeísta fricative ʝ, never j: calle ˈkaʝe, yo ˈʝo, mayo ˈmaʝo, and initial hie- as in hielo ˈʝelo. Word-final y is i̯ (rey ˈrei̯, hay ˈai̯). Initial hu+vowel is w with no inserted consonant (hueso ˈweso, huevo ˈwebo).

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 10 (glides and diphthongs: j w vs i̯ u̯) and ch. 9 on the palatal /ʝ/; yeísmo per the specification notes, after Lipski (1994).

## Other choices

### voiced stops /b d ɡ/

Write the phonemes b d ɡ in every position: v and b → b, d → d, g before a o u and gu before e i → ɡ. Spirant allophones [β ð ɣ] are not written (lobo ˈlobo, cada ˈkada, lago ˈlaɡo, abogado aboˈɡado). Intervocalic -d- is kept even where casual speech drops it (cansado kanˈsado). Interior Colombian speech keeps stops after consonants (algo, desde), and this does not change the broad form.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 8 (spirantization treated as allophonic); Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Colombia (occlusive /b d ɡ/ after non-nasal consonants in the interior).

### velar/glottal fricative (j, g before e/i, x in México)

Write h, following the Colombian realization as a weak glottal aspiration (joven ˈhoben, gente ˈhente, reloj reˈloh, México ˈmehiko). This departs from the x of the es-ES/es-419 ancestry; the notes are silent on it.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Colombia (/x/ realized as weak [h] throughout Colombia).

### implosive /s/

Keep syllable-final and word-final /s/ as s, as in Cali standard speech (mismo ˈmismo, las casas las ˈkasas, estamos esˈtamos). The aspiration and deletion of the nearby Pacific-coast zone and of popular speech are not written. Seseo applies: c before e/i and z → s (cielo ˈsjelo, zapato saˈpato).

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Colombia (Cali/Valle keeps /s/ more than the Pacific littoral); seseo per the specification notes.

### rhotics

Write the trill r for rr, for word-initial r and for r after n, l, s (perro ˈpero, rosa ˈrosa, honra ˈonra, alrededor alɾedeˈdoɾ is an exception only where l and r belong to different morphemes; default after l is r: alrededor alreðe- is NOT written, use alredeˈdoɾ). Write the tap ɾ elsewhere, including at the end of a syllable or word (pero ˈpeɾo, tres ˈtɾes, hablar aˈblaɾ). Cali has no assibilated rhotics.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 9 (rhotics); Lipski (1994), chapter on Colombia (assibilation is a highland feature, not Valluno).

### other consonant spellings

ch → tʃ (mucho ˈmutʃo). ñ → ɲ (año ˈaɲo). h is silent (hora ˈoɾa). qu and c before a/o/u → k (queso ˈkeso). gü → ɡw (pingüino pinˈɡwino). x between vowels → ks (examen ekˈsamen). Use the IPA ɡ, not g.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chs. 3–4 (orthography–phoneme correspondences).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
