# Transcription conventions: Mineiro Portuguese (pt-BR-x-mg)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, including stressed monosyllables: casa [ˈkazɐ], café [kaˈfɛ], pão [ˈpɐ̃w̃], menino [miˈninu]. Place stress by Portuguese lexical stress, using the written accent where present (próximo [ˈpɾɔsimu], você [voˈse]). Without an accent: penult for words ending in -a, -e, -o (plus -s), -am, -em, -ens; final syllable for words ending in other consonants (-r, -l, -z, -x), -i, -u, -im, -um or a nasal diphthong: falar [faˈla], Brasil [bɾaˈziw], aqui [aˈki]. Syllabify with maximal onset; ˈ goes before the whole onset cluster: [ˈpɾatu], [isˈkɔlɐ]. Do not mark secondary stress. In -mente adverbs mark only the stress on -men-: rapidamente [hapidɐˈmẽtʃi]. Stressed mid vowels follow the standard lexical e/ɛ, o/ɔ distinction: [ˈsedu] cedo, [ˈsɛtʃi] sete, [ˈovu] ovo, [ˈɔvus] ovos. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply. Spelled double consonants (rr, ss) are one sound.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. on word stress; Silva 2002 (BP stress and syllable structure)

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, written with no ˈ: definite articles o a os as [u a us as]; simple prepositions and contractions de [dʒi], do [du], da [da], em [ĩ], no [nu], na [na], num [nũ], numa [numɐ], com [kũ], sem [sẽj̃], por [poh], pelo [pelu], pela [pelɐ], para [paɾɐ], pra [pɾa], a [a], ao [aw]; conjunctions e [i], ou [o], mas [majs], nem [nẽj̃], que [ki], se [si]; relative que [ki]; clitic pronouns me te se lhe nos vos o a lo la [mi tʃi si ʎi nus vus u a lu la]. A clitic hyphened to its host is its own word and stays unstressed: dá-me [ˈda mi]. Stressed, written with ˈ: indefinite articles um uma [ˈũ ˈumɐ]; subject and tonic pronouns eu [ˈew], ele [ˈeli], nós [ˈnɔjs], você [voˈse], mim [ˈmĩ]; possessives, demonstratives, quantifiers, não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], interrogatives (quê [ˈke]); all verbs including the auxiliaries ser, estar, ter, ir, haver: está [isˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃]. Polysyllabic conjunctions, adverbs and prepositions other than para, pelo/pela/pelos/pelas and numa keep their lexical stress: porque [puhˈke], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], como [ˈkomu], entre [ˈẽtɾi], até [aˈtɛ]. Vowels in unstressed words: final o > [u], final e > [i], final -a of a polysyllable > [ɐ], monosyllabic a/as > [a]/[as].

Source: Silva 2002 (clitics and unstressed vowels in BP); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (clitic prosodic words); Castilho 2010 (inventory of BP clitics and grammatical words)

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word before a comma or at the end of a sentence takes the same form it has before a following consonant: final /s/ is [s] (casas [ˈkazɐs]), non-verbal final r is [h] (mar [ˈmah]), and final unstressed vowels are [i u ɐ] as everywhere else.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000; Silva 2002

## Connected speech

Each word keeps its own place and its full form. Only two sandhi processes are written, both on the word before the boundary. (1) Word-final /s/ (spelled s or z) is [z] before a vowel or a voiced consonant in the next word and [s] elsewhere: os amigos [uz aˈmigus], as casas [as ˈkazɐs], mais bonito [ˈmajz buˈnitu], três dias [ˈtɾejz ˈdʒiɐs]. It stays alveolar and is never [ʃ ʒ]. (2) Word-final r that is not deleted (see rhotics) is the tap [ɾ] before a vowel-initial word and [h] elsewhere: mar aberto [ˈmaɾ aˈbɛhtu], por isso [poɾ ˈisu], mar calmo [ˈmah ˈkawmu]. Do not write vowel elision, degemination, vowel coalescence across words or resyllabification: de água [dʒi ˈagwɐ], casa amarela [ˈkazɐ amaˈɾɛlɐ]. Do not write place assimilation of nasals across words.

Source: Silva 2002 (sandhi of /S/ and /R/ in BP); Callou & Leite 2001 (coda /S/ and /R/ variants); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (external sandhi)

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vowels as [j w], never i̯ u̯. Falling diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], noite [ˈnojtʃi], céu [ˈsɛw]. Coda l is always [w]: Brasil [bɾaˈziw], alto [ˈawtu]. Nasal diphthongs: mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], pão [ˈpɐ̃w̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃]. Rising diphthongs after velars: água [ˈagwɐ], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du]. Post-tonic unstressed i/u + vowel is a glide: história [isˈtɔɾjɐ], sério [ˈsɛɾju]. Pretonic and stressed i/u + vowel stay hiatus: piano [piˈɐ̃nu], dia [ˈdʒiɐ]. Insert [j] between a stressed vowel and a word-final /s/: mas [majs], três [ˈtɾejs], luz [ˈlujs], nós [ˈnɔjs]. Monophthongize ou to [o]: pouco [ˈpoku], ouro [ˈoɾu]. Monophthongize ei before r, ʃ, ʒ to [e]: primeiro [pɾiˈmeɾu], beijo [ˈbeʒu]. Monophthongize ai before ʃ to [a]: caixa [ˈkaʃɐ].

Source: Silva 2002 (BP diphthongs, monophthongization, glide insertion before /S/); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (glides as non-syllabic vowels)

## Other choices

### pretonic mid-vowel raising

Apply these rules to unstressed /e o/ before the stress, in this order. (1) Raise to [i u] when a later syllable of the word has i or u: menino [miˈninu], perigo [piˈɾigu], comida [kuˈmidɐ], coruja [kuˈɾuʒɐ]. (2) Raise /o/ to [u] when it is next to a labial consonant (p b m f v): boneca [buˈnɛkɐ], molhado [muˈʎadu]. (3) Write word-initial unstressed es-/ex-/en-/em- as [is ĩ]: escola [isˈkɔlɐ], estado [isˈtadu], entender [ĩtẽˈde]. Write des- as [dʒis]. (4) In all other cases keep [e o], never [ɛ ɔ]: telefone [teleˈfoni], cozinha [koˈziɲɐ]. Rule 1 does not apply to cozinha, because of the spelling z-i... Correction: rule 1 applies to cozinha, which is written [kuˈziɲɐ]; use instead the example morar [moˈɾa]. Post-tonic e o are always [i u].

Source: Lemos & Viegas 2016:314-315; Viegas 1987; Cardoso et al. 2014 (ALiB pretonic isoglosses)

### rhotics

This departs from the spec note, which takes the BH tap [ɾ] as the default. Published descriptions give the Belo Horizonte strong R as a glottal fricative, so follow them. Write single intervocalic r and r in onset clusters as [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], prato [ˈpɾatu]. Write strong R (word-initial, rr, after n l s) as [h]: rato [ˈhatu], carro [ˈkahu], honra [ˈõhɐ]. Write word-internal coda r as [h]: porta [ˈpɔhtɐ], verde [ˈvehdʒi]. Delete word-final r of verb forms (infinitives, future subjunctive): falar [faˈla], comer [kuˈme]. Write word-final r of non-verbs as [h], or [ɾ] before a vowel: mar [ˈmah], amor [aˈmoh]. Do not use the retroflex [ɻ] of the rural interior.

Source: Silva 2002 (regional variants of /R/, Belo Horizonte [h]); Callou & Leite 2001 (rhotic variation in BP capitals); Cardoso et al. 2014

### coronal stops and other consonants

Write /t d/ before [i] (including [i] from unstressed e) as [tʃ dʒ], without a tie bar: tia [ˈtʃiɐ], noite [ˈnojtʃi], de [dʒi], cidade [siˈdadʒi]. Write nh as [ɲ] (not [j̃]): cozinha [kuˈziɲɐ]. Write lh as [ʎ]. Coda s stays [s z] (no chiado).

Source: Silva 2002; Cardoso et al. 2014 (palatalization isoglosses); Callou & Leite 2001

### nasal vowels

Write a vowel before a coda nasal as a nasal vowel with no consonant: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], fim [ˈfĩ]. Nasalize a stressed vowel before an onset nasal consonant, with stressed a as [ɐ̃]: cama [ˈkɐ̃mɐ], banana [baˈnɐ̃nɐ]. Leave pretonic vowels before an onset nasal oral.

Source: Silva 2002 (nasalization in BP); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000

### post-tonic reduction

Do not write the stereotyped Mineiro post-tonic syncope, syllable loss or word truncation (ônibus, você > cê). Transcribe the full word with reduced vowels: ônibus [ˈonibus], você [voˈse].

Source: spec note (prosodic, not encoded); Silva 2002 for citation forms

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
