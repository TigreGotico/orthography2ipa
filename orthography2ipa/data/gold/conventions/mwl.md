# Transcription conventions: Mirandese (mwl)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every content word, including monosyllables (pan [ˈpɐ̃], yê [ˈʝe]). Stress falls on the syllable that the Convenção spelling rules imply. (1) A written acute or circumflex accent fixes the stress: bóbida [ˈboβiðɐ], abó [ɐˈβo], mirandés [miɾɐ̃nˈdes̺], série [ˈs̺eɾɪə]. (2) With no accent, the last syllable is stressed in words ending in -r, -l, -z, -ç, -i, -u, -is, -us, -in, -un, -on, -ns, a falling diphthong (-ei, -ou, -éu, -y), or -ie: falar [fɐˈlaɾ], rapaç [rɐˈpas], coraçon [kuɾɐˈsɵ̃w̃], chapéu [tʃɐˈpew], fazie [fɐˈzɪə]. (3) Otherwise the penultimate syllable is stressed. This covers words in -a, -e, -o, -as, -es, -os and -en: gata [ˈɡatɐ], lhobo [ˈʎoβu], fugien [ˈfuʒjɪ̃]. In hiatus final -ie and -ue the stress is on the i or u (rue [ˈrʊə]). Unstressed vowels reduce: a [ɐ], e [ɨ], o and u [u]. Secondary stress is not marked. Mirandese has no phonemic vowel length, no geminates (ss and rr are single consonants, [s̺] and [r]) and no case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply.

Source: Convenção Ortográfica da Língua Mirandesa (Ferreira & Raposo 1999), accentuation rules §2.1.2 (PDF p. 20); reduction values from Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §24, p. 200.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed: they get no ˈ and keep reduced vowels. Articles: l, la, ls, las, un, ua. Prepositions and their contractions: de, a, an, por/pur, para/pa, cun, del, al, ne l. Conjunctions: i, que, mas, se, ou. Clitic object and reflexive pronouns: me, te, se, le/lhe, lo, la, nos, bos. The relative que. Examples: la gata [lɐ ˈɡatɐ], de [dɨ], que [kɨ], me [mɨ], ls dedos [ɫz̺ ˈðeðus̺], del cielo [ðeɫ ˈsjɪlu]. The following are stressed: nouns, adjectives, adverbs, numerals other than un/ua, demonstratives, stressed possessives (mie, tue), subject and disjunctive pronouns (you, tu, el, mi) and every finite verb, including auxiliaries and the copula: yê [ˈʝe], hai, ten [ˈtẽ]. The negator non is the word exception [ˈno] and is written stressed.

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §13 (article l as a syllabic coda lateral, p. 188) and §110a (non, p. 257); unstressed vowel values §24, p. 200.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Mirandese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, and a word has the same segments at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside a phrase. The only effect of a pause is on the edge segments. A word-final sibilant before a pause stays voiceless (las casas [lɐs̺ ˈkaz̺ɐs̺]). A word-initial b, v, d, g after a pause is a stop (bida at the start of a sentence: [ˈbiðɐ]). The full diphthong [wo] that LdV allows in emphatic or slow speech is not used; ⟨uo⟩ stays [ʊ] everywhere.

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §13, pp. 187-190 (stop after pause; final sibilant voiced only before a voiced consonant) and p. 182, Obs. 2.

## Connected speech

Each word stays whole and in its own place. Sandhi changes only the edge segment of the affected word, and no ties or joining are written. Five processes apply. (1) Sibilant voicing: word-final ⟨s⟩ [s̺] becomes [z̺] and final ⟨ç, z⟩ [s] becomes [z] before a word starting with a voiced consonant (nasals, liquids, b d g v included). Before a vowel, a voiceless consonant or a pause it stays voiceless: ls nius [ɫz̺ ˈnius̺], lhuç del cielo [ˈʎuz ðeɫ ˈsjɪlu], ls amigos [ɫs̺ ɐˈmiɣus̺]. (2) Spirantisation of the next word's initial consonant. Initial b/v is [β] after a vowel or a voiced consonant (la bida [lɐ ˈβiðɐ]) and [b] after a pause or nasal (un burro [ũ ˈburu]). Initial d is [ð] after a vowel, s, ç, r (ls dedos [ɫz̺ ˈðeðus̺]) and [d] after a pause, nasal or l (al dedo [aɫ ˈdeðu]). Initial g is [ɣ] after s, l, r (ls gatos [ɫz̺ ˈɣatus̺]) and stays [ɡ] after a vowel (la gata [lɐ ˈɡatɐ]). (3) The article l is [ɫ] before a consonant and [l] before a vowel (l home [l ˈomɨ]); other word-final l is always [ɫ]. (4) A word-final nasal is a nasal vowel with no consonant (cun [kũ]). After word-final [ũ] (un, cun) before a vowel-initial word, the next word starts with [ŋ], as in LdV's ũŋa (un amigo [ũ ŋɐˈmiɣu]). (5) No vowel elision, liaison or resyllabification is written; orthographic elisions with an apostrophe are already separate words.

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §13, pp. 187-192 (contexts of b, d, g; s/ç voicing p. 190; coda ł p. 188; ũŋa p. 192). Where LdV is silent (g after a vowel) the spec's stop is kept.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vocoids as [j w] (nasal [j̃ w̃]), never i̯ u̯. Falling diphthongs: ei [ej], ai [aj], ou [ow], au [aw], ui [uj], éu [ew], word-final y [j]: purmeiro [puɾˈmejɾu], rey [ˈrej], chapéu [tʃɐˈpew]. Rising diphthongs follow LdV, not the Convenção's [je wo]: ie/iê is [jɪ] (cielo [ˈsjɪlu]) and uo/uô is the monophthong [ʊ] (puorta [ˈpʊɾtɐ]). Final -on is [ɵ̃w̃] and -ion is [jɵ̃w̃] (coraçon [kuɾɐˈsɵ̃w̃]). Glide before a vowel: u in gu/qu is [w] (lhéngua [ˈʎẽŋɡwɐ]). Initial y is the fricative [ʝ] (yê [ˈʝe]). Final unstressed ie/ue after a stressed vowel is the hiatus [ɪə]/[ʊə], two syllables with no glide mark (fazie [fɐˈzɪə], rue [ˈrʊə]). Exception: pie [ˈpjɪ]. Final unstressed -uo after an accent is [uu] (mútuo [ˈmutuu]).

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §2 p. 173, p. 177, p. 182 Obs. 1-2; §11 pp. 184-185 Obs. 4; §13 p. 191 (y); §110 p. 257 (-on). The Convenção 1999 (fn. 13, [je wo]) is the departed-from tradition.

## Other choices

### Sibilants

Keep the four-way contrast. Apical ⟨s, ss⟩ is [s̺], with intervocalic ⟨-s-⟩ and ⟨s⟩ before a voiced consonant as [z̺]. Dorsal ⟨c(e,i), ç⟩ is plain [s] and ⟨z⟩ is plain [z]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ], ⟨j, g(e,i)⟩ is [ʒ], and ⟨ch⟩ is the affricate [tʃ]. Examples: casa [ˈkaz̺ɐ], caça [ˈkasɐ], mesmo [ˈmez̺mu], chube [ˈtʃuβɨ].

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §12-13, pp. 189-191; the spec's notation marks only the apical series.

### Open mid vowels

Stressed e, o, é, ó are the Spanish-like mid [e o], never [ɛ ɔ]: pé [ˈpe], abó [ɐˈβo], nó [ˈno]. This follows LdV over the Convenção's open [ɛ ɔ].

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §22, p. 198.

### Unstressed vowels

Unstressed a is [ɐ], unstressed e is [ɨ] (including word-final -e), and unstressed o and u are [u]. The narrower [ʉ] and [e̞] are not used. Examples: home [ˈomɨ], fazer [fɐˈzeɾ], lhobo [ˈʎoβu].

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §2 and §24, p. 200.

### Nasal vowel before a stop

Inside a word, keep a homorganic nasal consonant after the nasal vowel before a stop: santo [ˈs̺ɐ̃ntu], ambos [ˈɐ̃mbus̺], lindo [ˈlĩndu], pongo [ˈpõŋɡu], brando [ˈbɾɐ̃ndu]. This follows LdV over the gold's absorbed nasal, and over the single note example [ˈbɾɐ̃du]. Before a fricative or at the end of a word, write only the nasal vowel. Nasal e and o are [ẽ õ].

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §13, pp. 187-188 (ã-mbos, sã-nto, lĩ-ndo, põ-ŋgo).

### Laterals and rhotics

⟨lh⟩ is [ʎ] and ⟨nh⟩ is [ɲ]. Initial ⟨l⟩ is [l], coda l is [ɫ] (sol [ˈs̺oɫ]). Initial r and rr are the trill [r]; other r is the tap [ɾ] (rapaç [rɐˈpas], falar [fɐˈlaɾ]).

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §13, p. 188; §111, p. 259; §113, p. 265.

### ei diphthong

Always write ⟨ei⟩ as the normative [ej], not the attested variant [ɐj]/[aj]: purmeiro [puɾˈmejɾu].

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §11, p. 184 (ei~ai oscillation noted; central norm kept).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
