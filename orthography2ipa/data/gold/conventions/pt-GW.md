# Transcription conventions: Guinea-Bissau Portuguese (pt-GW)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical, as in standard Portuguese. Write ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: casa [ˈkaza], mar [ˈmaɾ], já [ˈʒa]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. The default rule has three parts. Words ending in a vowel letter (a, e, o, optionally followed by s or m/ns) take penultimate stress: livro [ˈlivɾu], falam [ˈfalãw̃]. Words ending in a consonant (r, l, z, x), in i/u (±s), or in a nasal vowel or diphthong take final stress: falar [faˈlaɾ], papel [paˈpɛl], aqui [aˈki], irmão [iɾˈmãw̃]. A written accent overrides both: médico [ˈmɛdiku], café [kaˈfɛ], açúcar [aˈsukaɾ]. Verb forms keep their standard stress: falávamos [faˈlavamus]. Do not mark secondary stress. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings. Write double letters as single sounds: ss [s], rr [r] (passo [ˈpasu], carro [ˈkaru]). Never mark length.

Source: Standard Portuguese word-stress rule as in Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), The Phonology of Portuguese, chapter on word stress. The stress of pt-GW does not differ from it lexically: Couto (1994) and Kihm (1994) describe Kriol stress as inherited from Portuguese etyma. No pt-GW-specific source gives a different rule.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, with no ˈ, and written with their reduced vowels: (1) definite articles o os a as [u us a as]; (2) simple prepositions, including disyllabic ones: de [di], em [ẽj̃], a [a], por [puɾ], para [paɾa], com [kõ], sem [sẽj̃], sobre [sobɾi], entre [ẽtɾi]; (3) preposition+article contractions: do [du], da [da], no [nu], na [na], ao [aw], pelo [pelu]; (4) conjunctions and complementisers: e [i], ou [ow], que [ki], se [si], mas [mas], nem [nẽj̃], porque [puɾki]; (5) relative que [ki]; (6) object, reflexive and indirect clitics: me te se lhe nos vos lhes o a os as [mi ti si ʎi nus vus ʎis u a us as]. Stressed and marked ˈ: indefinite um uma [ˈũ ˈuma]; subject and tonic pronouns eu tu ele ela nós você mim ti si [ˈew ˈtu ˈeli ˈɛla ˈnɔs voˈse ˈmĩ ˈti ˈsi]; possessives, demonstratives and quantifiers; the negator não [ˈnãw̃]; adverbs; every verb form, auxiliaries included (é [ˈɛ], está [isˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], foi [ˈfoj], vai [ˈvaj]).

Source: Classification of atonic words (clitic articles, prepositions, conjunctions and pronouns) in Cunha & Cintra (1984), Nova Gramática do Português Contemporâneo, section on atonic words, and in Mateus & d'Andrade (2000) on clitics. Full unreduced vowel quality follows the lect note. The high final vowels in clitics follow the Kriol-substrate pattern of final i/u described in Kihm (1994, phonology overview) and Couto (1994).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word has the same form at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside a phrase. Do not devoice, delete or lengthen final vowels before a pause: ...em casa. [ẽj̃ ˈkaza].

Source: Not applicable: no description of Portuguese (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000) or of the Guinean lect (Couto 1994; Kihm 1994) posits pausal forms.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its own citation form. The only exception is the unstressed function-word form given under function_words. No cross-word process is transcribed. Do not voice word-final s before a vowel or a voiced consonant: os amigos [us aˈmigus], mais grande [ˈmajs ˈɡɾãdi]. Do not elide or merge vowels at boundaries: de água [di ˈaɡwa], que ele [ki ˈeli]. Do not write nasal place assimilation across words. Spelled contractions (do, na, dele, nesta) are single words and are written as such: dele [ˈdeli]. This choice deliberately departs from the European/Brazilian sandhi of Mateus & d'Andrade (2000): [z] liaison and vowel sandhi are not written, because pt-GW speech is largely syllable-timed and L2-based, and Kriol has no sibilant liaison. The departure keeps every row consistent.

Source: Departure from the sandhi rules of Mateus & d'Andrade (2000). It is justified by the lect note (coda [s], full vowels) and by the Kriol substrate as described in Kihm (1994) and Couto (1994). No published pt-GW study documents systematic liaison.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic glides as j and w, never as i̯ u̯. Oral diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], meu [ˈmew], noite [ˈnojti], ouro [ˈowɾu] (spelled ou is written [ow], not monophthongised), água [ˈaɡwa], quando [ˈkwãdu]. Nasal diphthongs take nasalised vowel plus nasalised glide: mão [ˈmãw̃], mãe [ˈmãj̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃], falam [ˈfalãw̃]. Since the lect has no [ɐ], write ão as [ãw̃], not [ɐ̃w̃]. Vowel sequences across a syllable boundary stay syllabic: saúde [saˈudi], rio [ˈriu].

Source: Glide analysis of Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), chapter on syllable structure and diphthongs, with [a] for [ɐ] per the five-vowel unstressed system in the lect note. The nasal diphthong qualities follow the same source.

## Other choices

### Vowel qualities

Stressed vowels keep the seven Portuguese qualities [i e ɛ a ɔ o u] by lexicon: pé [ˈpɛ], medo [ˈmedu], avó [aˈvɔ], avô [aˈvo]. Unstressed vowels never reduce to [ɨ] or [ɐ]. Word-final unstressed -e(s) becomes [i(s)] and -o(s) becomes [u(s)], while -a(s) stays [a(s)]: noite [ˈnojti], livros [ˈlivɾus], casa [ˈkaza]. Other unstressed vowels take the quality the spelling suggests, with mid vowels close: pequeno [peˈkenu], morar [moˈɾaɾ], senhora [seˈɲoɾa]. Nasal vowels are [ã ẽ ĩ õ ũ], and a following nasal consonant letter is not written: campo [ˈkãpu], ponte [ˈpõti].

Source: The note's five-vowel unstressed system (no /ɨ/). The seven stressed qualities follow the Portuguese lexicon (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000) and match the seven-vowel Kriol system in Kihm (1994) and Couto (1994). Final [i u] follows the Kriol pattern (e.g. Kriol noti, obu) in the same sources. The note's '(1) five vowels' is read as applying to unstressed position.

### Rhotics

Strong r is the trill [r]: word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s (rua [ˈrua], carro [ˈkaru], honra [ˈõra]). Single intervocalic r, r in onset clusters and r in coda are the tap [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], prato [ˈpɾatu], porta [ˈpɔɾta], falar [faˈlaɾ]. Keep word-final r; do not drop it.

Source: Tap/trill distribution of Mateus & d'Andrade (2000) with an alveolar trill as the strong rhotic. The note's 'alveolar trill [r]' is taken to describe the strong rhotic, not every r.

### Coda sibilants and laterals

Write coda s, z and x as [s] everywhere, with no palatalisation and no voicing: escola [isˈkɔla], mesmo [ˈmesmu], paz [ˈpas]. Word-initial unstressed es- is [is]. Coda l is plain [l], neither velarised nor vocalised: Brasil [bɾaˈzil], alto [ˈaltu]. Intervocalic s is [z], as in standard spelling-pronunciation: casa [ˈkaza].

Source: Lect note (2), which departs from European [ʃ] and Brazilian Rio [ʃ]/[w]. The coda sibilant follows Kriol phonotactics as in Couto (1994).

### Palatals and t/d before i

t and d stay [t d] before [i]: tia [ˈtia], dia [ˈdia], noite [ˈnojti], with no Brazilian [tʃ dʒ]. Write lh [ʎ], nh [ɲ], ch [ʃ], j/ge/gi [ʒ]: filho [ˈfiʎu], banho [ˈbaɲu], chave [ˈʃavi], gente [ˈʒẽti]. Kriol-influenced [j] for lh is not written.

Source: Standard European-norm consonant inventory (Mateus & d'Andrade 2000), the norm taught in Guinea-Bissau. Kriol lh>j variation (Kihm 1994) is treated as non-standard.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
