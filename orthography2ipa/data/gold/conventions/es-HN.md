# Transcription conventions: Honduran Spanish (es-HN)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ at the start of the stressed syllable, before its onset consonant(s): padre [ˈpadɾe], hablar [aˈblaɾ], ciudad [sjuˈdad]. Placement follows the spelling. A written accent marks the stressed vowel: café [kaˈfe], árbol [ˈaɾbol], día [ˈdia]. With no accent, words ending in a vowel, -n or -s are stressed on the penultimate syllable: casa [ˈkasa], tienen [ˈtjeneŋ], mesas [ˈmesah]. All other unaccented words are stressed on the final syllable: comer [koˈmeɾ], reloj [reˈloh]. The rule is applied to the spelling before /s/-aspiration, so aspiration never shifts stress. Voseo verb forms carry their written final accent: tenés [teˈneh], vení [beˈni]. Every stressed word gets ˈ, including stressed monosyllables: sol [ˈsol], vos [ˈboh], yo [ˈʝo]. Adverbs in -mente get two marks: rápidamente [ˈrapidaˈmente]. No secondary stress is written. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply to Spanish: there is no vowel length, no geminates and no case morphology.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 14 (stress); Real Academia Española & ASALE (2011), Nueva gramática: Fonética y fonología, ch. 9 (stress rules and accentual classes).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed words get no ˈ. These are the definite articles el la lo los las [el la lo loh lah]; prenominal possessives mi tu su mis tus sus [mi tu su mih tuh suh]; object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos les [me te se lo la le noh leh], whether free or hyphen-attached; all simple prepositions except según (a de en con por para sin sobre hasta desde: [a de eŋ koŋ poɾ ˈpaɾa]); conjunctions y e o u ni pero que si como, and porque; and relative que, donde, cuando, como, quien. Stressed words get ˈ. These are indefinites un una unos unas [ˈuŋ ˈuna ˈunoh ˈunah]; demonstratives (este [ˈehte], ese [ˈese]); subject and prepositional pronouns (yo [ˈʝo], vos [ˈboh], usted [uhˈted], él [ˈel], nosotros [noˈsotɾoh], mí [ˈmi]); interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent (qué [ˈke], cómo [ˈkomo], dónde [ˈdonde]); numerals; según [seˈɡuŋ]; and all verb forms, auxiliaries included: ha venido [ˈa beˈnido], está comiendo [ehˈta koˈmjendo]. Polysyllabic unstressed words (para, pero, desde, donde, porque) also get no ˈ, and their vowels are not reduced.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, §14.2 (stressed and unstressed words); Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, ch. on unstressed words.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply in the Arabic sense: Spanish has no pausal forms, no case endings and no nunation. Every word is written the same way at the end of a sentence, at a comma and medially. Final vowels are kept: casa [ˈkasa]. Word-final /s/ is [h] before pause as everywhere else: vamos. [ˈbamoh]. It is not deleted, even though utterance-final loss is frequent in casual speech. Word-final /n/ is [ŋ]: pan. [ˈpaŋ].

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Honduras (word-final /s/ and /n/); Lipski, J.M. (1983), 'Reducción de /s/ en el español de Honduras', Nueva Revista de Filología Hispánica 32.

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its citation form for this lect, with no cross-word process except the Honduran word-final rules, which hold in every context. (1) Word-final /s/ is [h] before a consonant, a vowel or a pause. Honduras aspirates even prevocalic word-final /s/: los otros [loh ˈotɾoh], es así [ˈeh aˈsi], más tarde [ˈmah ˈtaɾde]. (2) Word-final /n/ is [ŋ] in all contexts, with no place assimilation to the next word: un beso [ˈuŋ ˈbeso], en casa [eŋ ˈkasa], con ella [koŋ ˈeʝa]. (3) Resyllabification across words is not shown, because there are no syllable marks other than ˈ and the consonant stays in its own word: el agua [el ˈaɡwa]. (4) No synalepha or vowel elision is written: both vowels stay, as in de ella [de ˈeʝa] and la otra [la ˈotɾa]. A vowel is never deleted or turned into a glide across a boundary. (5) No cross-word voicing, degemination or lenition is written: los dos [loh ˈdoh], el lado [el ˈlado]. Word-internal nasal place assimilation is written, because it is phonemically neutralised: campo [ˈkampo], tengo [ˈteŋɡo], conmigo [koŋˈmiɡo].

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Honduras (prevocalic aspiration of word-final /s/, velarisation of final /n/); Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 10 (nasal assimilation) and ch. 15 (synalepha, not transcribed here).

## Glides and diphthongs

Non-syllabic high vowels in diphthongs and triphthongs are written [j] and [w], never i̯ u̯. Rising diphthongs: tiene [ˈtjene], bueno [ˈbweno], cuatro [ˈkwatɾo], ciudad [sjuˈdad]. Falling diphthongs: aire [ˈajɾe], hay [ˈaj], muy [ˈmuj], rey [ˈrej], causa [ˈkawsa], deuda [ˈdewda]. Triphthong: buey [ˈbwej]. An accented i or u forms a hiatus and stays a full vowel: día [ˈdia], país [paˈih], baúl [baˈul]. Unaccented ui and iu are written with the first element as the glide: ruido [ˈrwido], viuda [ˈbjuda]. Word-initial hu+vowel is [w]: huevo [ˈwebo]. hi+vowel and y/ll as onsets are the consonant [ʝ], not a glide: hielo [ˈʝelo], yo [ˈʝo], calle [ˈkaʝe]. The conjunction y is [i] as a word of its own; it is not turned into a glide before or after a vowel.

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 6 (glides and diphthongs); RAE & ASALE (2011), Fonética y fonología, ch. 7 (diphthongs, hiatus).

## Other choices

### /s/-aspiration (disagrees with the specification note)

The note models only the pan-Latin-American baseline. Following the standard Honduran pronunciation, coda /s/ (spelled s, z, or syllable-final x) is written [h] word-internally before a consonant and word-finally in every context: este [ˈehte], mismo [ˈmihmo], luz [ˈluh], extraño [ehˈtɾaɲo]. Onset /s/ stays [s]: casa [ˈkasa], semana [seˈmana]. Rural aspiration of initial and intervocalic /s/ is not written.

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Honduras; Lipski (1983), NRFH 32.

### Velar fricative /x/ (disagrees with the specification note)

/x/ (spelled j, or g before e/i) is the weak glottal [h] throughout: joven [ˈhobeŋ], gente [ˈhente], México [ˈmehiko]. Silent h is not written: hora [ˈoɾa].

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Central American chapters (weak aspirated /x/).

### Word-final /n/ velarisation (disagrees with the specification note)

Word-final /n/ is [ŋ] everywhere: pan [ˈpaŋ], también [tamˈbjeŋ], hablan [ˈablaŋ]. Word-internal coda /n/ assimilates in place: tanto [ˈtanto], cambio [ˈkambjo].

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, chapter on Honduras.

### /b d g/ allophony

Broad transcription writes /b d ɡ/ as [b d ɡ] in every position. The approximants [β ð ɣ], and the Central American occlusive realisation after non-nasal consonants, are not written: lado [ˈlado], algo [ˈalɡo], desde [ˈdehde], abogado [aboˈɡado]. v and b are both [b]: vida [ˈbida]. Use IPA ɡ, not g.

Source: Specification note (occlusive /b d g/) and Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Central American chapters; broad-transcription practice in Hualde (2005), ch. 8.

### Seseo and yeísmo

c before e/i and z are [s] (in coda, [h] by the aspiration rule): cielo [ˈsjelo], hacer [aˈseɾ], vez [ˈbeh]. Both y and ll are [ʝ] in every position. Weakening or loss next to front vowels is not written: gallina [ɡaˈʝina], ella [ˈeʝa], ya [ˈʝa].

Source: Specification note (seseo, yeísmo; Lipski 1994); Lipski, J.M. (1994), chapter on Honduras (weak /ʝ/, not transcribed).

### Rhotics

The trill [r] is written for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l or s: perro [ˈpero], rojo [ˈroho], honra [ˈonra], Israel [ihraˈel]. The tap [ɾ] is written elsewhere, including in coda and word-finally before a vowel: pero [ˈpeɾo], tarde [ˈtaɾde], ser amigos [ˈseɾ aˈmiɡoh].

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 11; Lipski (1994), chapter on Honduras (no assibilation).

### Other consonant spellings

ch is [tʃ]: noche [ˈnotʃe]. ñ is [ɲ]: año [ˈaɲo]. Intervocalic x is [ks]: taxi [ˈtaksi]; in México and its derivatives x is [h]. qu and gu before e/i are [k] and [ɡ]: queso [ˈkeso], guerra [ˈɡera]. gü is [ɡw]: pingüino [piŋˈɡwino]. Word-final d is kept as [d], not deleted: usted [uhˈted], verdad [beɾˈdad].

Source: RAE & ASALE (2011), Fonética y fonología; Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
