# Transcription conventions: Andean Ecuadorian Spanish (es-EC-x-andino)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is lexical and follows standard Spanish placement. A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel. Without one, words ending in a vowel, -n or -s are stressed on the penultimate syllable, and all other words on the final syllable. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable, keeping the onset maximal: casa [ˈkasa], ciudad [sjuˈðað], árbol [ˈaɾβol], canción [kanˈsjon]. Mark every stressed word, monosyllables included: sol [ˈsol], ya [ˈʝa]. Write no secondary stress. Exception: adverbs in -mente keep the stress of their adjective base and also get ˈ on -men-: rápidamente [ˈrapiðaˈmente], but with the trill written [ʐ] as under 'other', so [ˈʐapiðaˈmente]. There is no phonemic vowel length, gemination or case ending in Spanish, so these do not apply. A double letter is one segment except cc [ks] and nn [nn]: acción [akˈsjon], innato [inˈnato].

Source: Hualde, J.I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 13 (stress); RAE/ASALE (2010), Ortografía de la lengua española, §3.4 (accentuation rules).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed function words get no ˈ. These are: definite articles el, la, los, las, lo; prenominal possessives mi, tu, su, nuestro (plural forms too); object and reflexive clitics me, te, se, nos, lo, la, le, los, las, les; all simple prepositions except según; the conjunctions y, e, o, u, ni, pero, que, si, porque, aunque; relatives que, quien, donde, cuando, como; and tan, cuan. Examples: la casa [la ˈkasa], de mi tío [de mi ˈtio], se lo dio [se lo ˈðjo]. Stressed words get ˈ. These are: subject and prepositional pronouns yo, tú, él, ella, usted, nosotros, mí, ti; interrogatives and exclamatives qué, quién, cómo, dónde, cuándo, cuánto; indefinite articles un, una; numerals; demonstratives; postnominal possessives mío, tuyo; según; and every verb form, auxiliaries included. Examples: un perro [ˈun ˈpero], but with the trill written [ʐ] as under 'other', so [ˈun ˈpeʐo]; ha venido [ˈa βeˈniðo]; está cansado [esˈta kanˈsaðo]; qué quieres [ˈke ˈkjeɾes].

Source: Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1977), Manual de pronunciación española, §§ on palabras inacentuadas; Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, §13.2. On un/una and auxiliaries as stressed, this follows Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, ch. on accent.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Words before a comma or at sentence end are written as elsewhere, with final vowels and /s/ kept in full: vamos. [ˈbamos]. Andean Ecuadorian reduces and devoices unstressed vowels next to /s/ (Lipski 1994), but this transcription does not write it.

Source: Not applicable. On the unwritten vowel reduction: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Ecuador chapter (highland vowel reduction).

## Connected speech

Each word stays in its own slot, and its edge segments are adjusted to the neighbouring word. (1) Andean prevocalic /s/ voicing: word-final /s/ before a word beginning with a vowel is written [z]: los amigos [loz aˈmiɣos], más o menos [ˈmaz o ˈmenos]. Final /s/ before a voiced consonant is also [z]: las manos [laz ˈmanos]; before a pause or a voiceless consonant it is [s]. (2) Nasal place: word-final /n/ takes the place of a following consonant across the boundary: un beso [ˈum ˈbeso], con queso [koŋ ˈkeso], en Quito [eŋ ˈkito]. Before a vowel or pause it stays alveolar [n], with no velar [ŋ]: pan [ˈpan], en esto [en ˈesto]. (3) Voiced obstruents b, v, d, g are written [b d ɡ] after a pause, after a nasal, and for /d/ also after /l/. They are written [β ð ɣ] elsewhere, judging by the last segment of the preceding word: la boca [la ˈβoka], un dedo [ˈun ˈdeðo], el dedo [el ˈdeðo], las gatas [laz ˈɣatas]. (4) No elision or vowel merger: vowels in contact across words are all written, with no syllable marks. Examples: lo hizo [lo ˈiso], de agua [de ˈaɣwa]. (5) Resyllabification of a final consonant onto the next word is not marked.

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Ecuador chapter (prevocalic voicing of /s/ and alveolar final /n/ in the highlands); Toscano Mateus, H. (1953), El español en el Ecuador; Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chs. 8 and 10 (spirantization, nasal assimilation).

## Glides and diphthongs

Unstressed i, u next to another vowel in the same word are written as the non-syllabic glides [j w], never [i̯ u̯]: tiene [ˈtjene], bueno [ˈbweno], aire [ˈajɾe], auto [ˈawto], ley [ˈlej], hoy [ˈoj], muy [ˈmuj]. A written accent on i or u marks a full vowel in hiatus: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis], baúl [baˈul]. Within a word, iu and ui are written as glide plus vowel, with the second element stressed: ciudad [sjuˈðað], cuidado [kwiˈðaðo]. Across word boundaries, vowels stay syllabic. The exception is the conjunction y, written [i] between consonants and [j] next to a vowel: y ella [j ˈeʒa], pan y vino [ˈpan i ˈβino]. The palatal consonant spelled y in an onset is [ʝ], not the glide: mayo [ˈmaʝo], yo [ˈʝo]. Per the spec note, word-initial ⟨hie-, hia-, hio-⟩ is the glide [j]: hielo [ˈjelo], hierba [ˈjeɾβa].

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 9 (glides and diphthongs). The hie- rule follows the spec note (Colomina 2018 p.244 as cited by es-AR), extended by analogy and not attested for Ecuador.

## Other choices

### ll versus y (departs from the spec note)

The stub models yeísmo with ll → /ʝ/. This transcription instead follows the published standard for the Quito highlands, which keeps ll distinct as rehilado [ʒ] and y as [ʝ]: calle [ˈkaʒe], llama [ˈʒama], ella [ˈeʒa], but mayo [ˈmaʝo], yeso [ˈʝeso]. After n or l, y is [ɟʝ]: cónyuge [ˈkoɲɟʝuxe], el yeso [el ˈɟʝeso].

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Ecuador chapter (retention of /ʎ/ as [ʒ] in the highlands); Toscano Mateus (1953), El español en el Ecuador.

### Trill (rr, word-initial r, r after n, l, s)

The trill /r/ is written as the assibilated fricative [ʐ]: perro [ˈpeʐo], rosa [ˈʐosa], honra [ˈonʐa], Israel [izʐaˈel]. The tap /ɾ/ stays [ɾ] in all other positions, including codas and the tr cluster: pero [ˈpeɾo], mar [ˈmaɾ], tres [ˈtɾes].

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Ecuador chapter (assibilated /r/ in the Andean zone); Toscano Mateus (1953).

### Seseo, coda /s/ and /x/

Seseo applies: c before e, i and z are [s]: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato]. Coda /s/ is never aspirated or deleted; its voicing follows the connected_speech rule: este [ˈeste]. The letters j and g before e, i are the velar [x], not [h]: jefe [ˈxefe], gente [ˈxente]. The letter x between vowels is [ks]: examen [ekˈsamen]; in México and Quixote it is [x].

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Ecuador chapter (consonantal Andean zone: /s/ retained, velar /x/), and the spec note on seseo.

### Intervocalic /d/

Intervocalic /d/ is kept as [ð], including in -ado and -ido: cansado [kanˈsaðo], comido [koˈmiðo]. Word-final d is [ð]: verdad [beɾˈðað].

Source: Lipski, J.M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, Ecuador chapter (consonant retention in the highlands); Hualde (2005), ch. 8.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
