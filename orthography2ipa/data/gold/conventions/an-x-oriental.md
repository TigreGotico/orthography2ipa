# Transcription conventions: Eastern Aragonese (Ribagorçan) (an-x-oriental)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every content word, monosyllables included: [ˈkasa], [ˈpan], [ˈdʒen]. Default placement: a word ending in a vowel, -s or -n is stressed on the penult ([ˈkasa], [ˈkasas]). A word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the last syllable ([paˈret], [kanˈtau]). A written accent (á é í ó ú à è ò) always overrides the default and marks the stressed vowel ([kanˈta], [ˈarbol]). Infinitives whose final -r is silent keep final stress ([kanˈta]). Stress falls in the same place whatever the sentence position. Mark no secondary stress. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not occur in this lect, so this convention has no rule for them.

Source: Default Ibero-Romance stress rule as in Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on stress; Aragonese paroxytone preference and accent-based orthography as in Nagore Laín (2001), Gramática de la Lengua Aragonesa, phonology section; Benasquese oxytone infinitives as in Ballarín Cornel (1976), Elementos de gramática benasquesa.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are written without ˈ (unstressed). Definite articles: lo, la, los, les, el, es → [lo la los les el es]. Prepositions: a, de, en, per, pa, con, sin, ta → [a de en per pa kon sin ta]. Conjunctions: i/y, e, o, que, si, pero, ni → [i e o ke si pero ni]. Unstressed object and reflexive clitics, including hyphen-joined ones: me, te, se, lo, la, los, les, mos, tos, ne/en, hi/bi → [me te se lo la los les mos tos ne bi]. The relative que. Auxiliary forms of haber/habé in compound tenses: he, has, ha, hem, hez, han → [e as a em es an]. These are written with ˈ (stressed). Subject and prepositional pronouns: yo/jo, tu, ell, ella, nusatros, vusatros → [ˈjo ˈtu ˈeʎ ˈeʎa]. Demonstratives, numerals and strong possessives. Negative no → [ˈno]. Interrogatives and exclamatives: qué, quí, án, cuán → [ˈke ˈki]. All forms of ser/está and of main-verb haber. Adverbs.

Source: Unstressed/stressed word classes as listed for Spanish in Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on stress and the prosodic word; Aragonese clitic and article inventory as in Nagore Laín (2001), morphology chapters; Benasquese forms (les, bi, mos, tos) as in Ballarín Cornel (1976).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply to this lect: words are written the same before a pause, at a comma and sentence-finally, and there are no case endings or nunation. Some word-final features hold in every position and are not pausal. Infinitive -r is silent ([kanˈta]). Final -d/-t is written [t] ([paˈret]).

Source: Ballarín Cornel (1976), Elementos de gramática benasquesa (silent infinitive -r, final devoicing); Haensch (1960), Las hablas de la Alta Ribagorza.

## Connected speech

Each word is transcribed in its own slot. There are no linking marks (‿), no deleted vowels and no moved consonants. Only two processes are written, and both change only the final segment of the first word. (1) Nasal place assimilation: word-final n becomes [m] before p, b, m (un baso → [un] → [um ˈbaso]) and [ŋ] before k, g (en casa → [eŋ ˈkasa]). Otherwise it stays [n]. (2) Sibilant voicing: word-final s becomes [z] before a vowel or a voiced consonant (les ames → [lez ˈames]; dos días → [ˈdoz ˈdias]). Before a voiceless consonant or a pause it stays [s]. Not written: vowel contact across words (synalepha or elision; la aigua → [la ˈajgwa], both vowels kept), resyllabification, and spirantization of b d g.

Source: Nasal assimilation as in Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chs. on nasals and on connected speech; voicing of final -s before voiced segments in the Catalan-transition Ribagorçan varieties as in Haensch (1960), Las hablas de la Alta Ribagorza, and Ballarín Cornel (1976).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vowels as consonantal [j] and [w], in rising and falling diphthongs and in triphthongs alike. Never use i̯ or u̯. Examples: tierra [ˈtjera], cuatro [ˈkwatro], aire [ˈajre], aigua [ˈajgwa], nuei [ˈnwej], cantau [kanˈtau] → [kanˈtaw]. Unstressed i/u next to another vowel is a glide. A written accent on i or u (día, continúa) marks hiatus, so the vowel stays syllabic: [ˈdia]. Orthographic y between vowels or word-initially before a vowel is [j]: yo [ˈjo].

Source: Glide-as-consonant broad notation of Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on diphthongs and glides; Aragonese diphthongization of Ĕ/Ŏ (ie, ue) as in Nagore Laín (2001).

## Other choices

### Palatal lateral vs. yeísmo

Keep /ʎ/ distinct from /j/ everywhere. The specification note allows possible partial yeísmo; this convention does not follow it and writes no yeísmo. ll → [ʎ]: ell [ˈeʎ], cllau [ˈkʎaw], pllorá [pʎoˈra], fllo [ˈfʎo]. The Benasquese clusters pl-, cl-, fl- + palatal lateral are written [pʎ kʎ fʎ]. Only y/i is [j].

Source: Ballarín Cornel (1976, 1978 Vocabulario del benasqués); Haensch (1960) — both describe preserved /ʎ/ and the pʎ/kʎ/fʎ clusters.

### Prepalatal affricates and fricative

Following the specification note: j, and g before e/i, are [dʒ] (chen/gen → [ˈdʒen]). ch/tx are [tʃ] (chiquet → [tʃiˈket]). x is [ʃ] (caixa → [ˈkajʃa]). Do not write [x]: there is no velar fricative.

Source: Specification note; voiced prepalatal series in Benasquese as in Haensch (1960) and Ballarín Cornel (1976).

### Seseo

Following the specification note: write z, ç, and c before e/i as [s] (sinc/cinc → [ˈsiŋk]; plaza → [ˈplasa]). Do not write [θ]. Descriptions of neighbouring Aragonese localities report [θ]; this convention does not use it.

Source: Specification note; Ribagorçan seseo as in Haensch (1960).

### Intervocalic s

A single s between vowels inside a word is [z] (casa → [ˈkaza], rosa → [ˈroza]). Written ss is [s].

Source: Haensch (1960), Las hablas de la Alta Ribagorza; Ballarín Cornel (1976) on the Benasquese voiced sibilant.

### Mid vowels

The specification lists seven vowels. Published Benasquese descriptions do not give a predictable rule for open vs. close mid vowels, so the transcription keys them to spelling. Write [ɛ] and [ɔ] only for a stressed vowel written è or ò (pèl → [ˈpɛl], còs → [ˈkɔs]). All other e and o, stressed or not, are [e] and [o].

Source: Specification note (seven-vowel inventory); Ballarín Cornel (1976) and Haensch (1960) for the unstable open/close contrast in the transition zone.

### Rhotics and other consonants

rr and word-initial r are [r] (tierra [ˈtjera], ríu [ˈriw]). Single intervocalic and coda r is [ɾ] (pero [ˈpeɾo], per [peɾ]). Infinitive-final -r is not written (cantar → [kanˈta]). ny/ñ is [ɲ]. b/v are both [b]. h is silent. b, d, g are always written as stops.

Source: Hualde (2005) for the tap/trill contrast and broad stop notation; Ballarín Cornel (1976) for the silent infinitive -r.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
