# Transcription conventions: Curitibano/Paranaense Portuguese (pt-BR-x-pr)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, including stressed monosyllables: pé [ˈpɛ], mar [ˈmaɾ], casa [ˈkazɐ], café [kaˈfɛ], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃padɐ]. Unstressed function words get no mark (see function_words). Do not mark secondary stress. Place the mark at the start of the syllable, with the onset taken as large as Portuguese allows: festa [ˈfɛstɐ], outro [ˈotɾu]. Stress is lexical and follows the orthography. A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed syllable. Without one, words ending in a, e, o (with or without s), -em or -am stress the penult: mesa [ˈmezɐ], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. Words ending in other consonants, in i or u, or in a nasal vowel or diphthong (ã, ão, õe, im, um) stress the final syllable: Brasil [bɾaˈziw], falar [faˈlaɾ], aqui [aˈki], irmã [iɾˈmɐ̃], cantarão [kɐ̃taˈɾɐ̃w̃]. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Portuguese has no phonemic length, no geminates and no case morphology. Double letters (rr, ss) stand for single consonants.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 (stress); Silva (2002), accentuation section; orthographic stress rules of the standard written norm.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, so no ˈ, and written with their reduced vowels. Definite articles: o [u], a [a], os [us], as [as]. Monosyllabic prepositions: de [de], em [ẽj̃], com [kõ], por [poɾ], sem [sẽj̃], a [a]. Contractions: do [du], da [da], no [nu], na [na], ao [aw], pelo [pelu], pela [pelɐ], num [nũ], dum [dũ]. The preposition para [paɾɐ] and pra [pɾa]. Conjunctions: e [i], mas [mas], que [ke], se [se], nem [nẽj̃], ou [o], porque [poɾke]. Relative que [ke]. Atonic clitic pronouns: me [me], te [te], se [se], lhe [ʎe], nos [nus], o [u], a [a], lo [lu], la [la]. Stressed, so they take ˈ: indefinite articles um [ˈũ] and uma [ˈumɐ]; tonic and subject pronouns eu [ˈew], você [voˈse], ele [ˈele], nós [ˈnɔs], mim [ˈmĩ]; demonstratives and possessives (esse [ˈese], meu [ˈmew]); polysyllabic prepositions and adverbs (sobre [ˈsobɾe], entre [ˈẽtɾe], até [aˈtɛ], desde [ˈdezde]); all verb forms, including auxiliaries and copulas (é [ˈɛ], está [esˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], vai [ˈvaj], foi [ˈfoj]); interrogative quê [ˈke] and por quê [poɾ ˈke]; não [ˈnɐ̃w̃].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 on clitics and the prosodic word; Silva (2002) on unstressed monosyllables. The Curitiba [e] in de, que, se and me follows the spec note on retained final /e/.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word before a comma or at the end of a sentence is written exactly as elsewhere, with final vowels kept: leite [ˈlejte], tudo [ˈtudu]. Final /s/ before a pause is [s].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000) on Portuguese morphophonology; no published description of the lect posits pausal alternations.

## Connected speech

Each word keeps its own citation form, with one exception: word-final /s/ takes its sandhi value from what follows. Before a vowel-initial word or a voiced consonant it is written [z]: os amigos [uz aˈmiɡus], as mesas [az ˈmezɐs], mais nada [ˈmajz ˈnadɐ]. Before a voiceless consonant or a pause it is [s]: os pés [us ˈpɛs]. Nothing else across word boundaries is written: no vowel elision or degemination, no glide formation between words, no assimilation of nasals. So casa amarela is [ˈkazɐ amaˈɾɛlɐ], not *[ˈkaz amaˈɾɛlɐ], and que eu is [ke ˈew]. Final /ɾ/ stays [ɾ] before a vowel: mar azul [ˈmaɾ aˈzuw]. Final /l/ stays [w] before a vowel: mal amado [ˈmaw aˈmadu]. Resyllabification is not shown, because the transcription has no syllable dots.

Source: Silva (2002) and Callou & Leite (2001) on coda /S/ voicing in sandhi; Mateus & d'Andrade (2000) on vowel sandhi, which is optional and therefore not written in this broad transcription.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vocoids as the consonant symbols [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. Oral diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], lei [ˈlej], mau [ˈmaw], céu [ˈsɛw], boi [ˈboj], água [ˈaɡwɐ], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], história [isˈtɔɾjɐ]. In nasal diphthongs both elements take the tilde: mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], tem [ˈtẽj̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], muito [ˈmũj̃tu]. A written accent marks hiatus, so no glide is written: saúde [saˈude], país [paˈis]. Monophthongization is written as follows. Orthographic ou is always [o]: outro [ˈotɾu], ou [o]. Orthographic ei before [ɾ ʃ ʒ] is [e]: primeiro [pɾiˈmeɾu], beijo [ˈbeʒu]. Orthographic ai before [ʃ] is [a]: caixa [ˈkaʃɐ]. No epenthetic [j] is written before coda /s/, so mas [mas] and três [ˈtɾes] stay distinct from mais [ˈmajs].

Source: Silva (2002), sections on diphthongs and monophthongization; Callou & Leite (2001) on BP diphthong reduction; Bisol's analysis of BP glides as underlying vowels, as summarized in Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 3.

## Other choices

### Final unstressed vowels and /t d/ palatalization

Follow the spec note's conservative Curitiba default. Final unstressed /e/ is written [e] and /t d/ stay [t d]: leite quente [ˈlejte ˈkẽte], cidade [siˈdade], noite [ˈnojte]. /t d/ also stay unpalatalized before a lexical /i/: tia [ˈtiɐ], dia [ˈdiɐ]. Never write [tʃ dʒ], although younger and Paulista-influenced Curitiba speech palatalizes. Final unstressed /o/ is written [u], since the published variationist work finds /o/ raising much more frequent than /e/ raising in Curitiba: tudo [ˈtudu], carro [ˈkaxu]. Here the description departs from the note's 'e/o fechados' wording. Final unstressed /a/ is [ɐ]: casa [ˈkazɐ].

Source: Spec note; Cardoso et al. (2014, ALiB), palatalization isoglosses; Vieira (2002), 'As vogais médias postônicas', in Bisol & Brescancini (eds.), Fonologia e variação, on lower raising of /e/ than /o/ in Curitiba.

### Pretonic mid vowels

Write orthographic pretonic e and o as close [e] and [o], following the Southern pattern. Apply no vowel harmony or raising: menino [meˈninu], costume [kosˈtume], pedido [peˈdidu]. Never use the Northeastern open pretonic [ɛ ɔ]. Stressed mid vowels follow the lexicon: festa [ˈfɛstɐ], mesa [ˈmezɐ], porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ], bolo [ˈbolu].

Source: Cardoso et al. (2014, ALiB), pretonic-vowel isoglosses; Callou & Leite (2001).

### Rhotics

Coda /R/ is an alveolar tap [ɾ] word-internally and word-finally, and it is kept in infinitives: porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ], falar [faˈlaɾ]. Never use the retroflex caipira [ɻ]. The intervocalic single r is [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu]. The strong R is written [x]. This covers word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s: rato [ˈxatu], carro [ˈkaxu], honra [ˈõxɐ]. The alveolar trill of older or Slavic- and Italian-descended speakers is not written.

Source: Spec note; Amaral (1920) §6b, cited for contrast only; Silva (2002) and Callou & Leite (2001) on BP rhotic allophones.

### Coda /S/ and /l/

Coda /S/ is alveolar, never palatal [ʃ ʒ]. It is [s] before voiceless consonants and pause, and [z] before voiced consonants: festa [ˈfɛstɐ], mesmo [ˈmezmu]. Coda /l/ is vocalized to [w]: Brasil [bɾaˈziw], alto [ˈawtu], sol [ˈsɔw].

Source: Spec note; Callou & Leite (2001); Silva (2002).

### Nasality

Write vowels before a tautosyllabic nasal, or with a tilde, as nasal vowels with no following nasal consonant: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], tempo [ˈtẽpu], fim [ˈfĩ], um [ˈũ]. Nasal /a/ is [ɐ̃]. A stressed a before a heterosyllabic m, n or nh is written [ɐ̃]: cama [ˈkɐ̃mɐ], banho [ˈbɐ̃ɲu]. Other vowels before a heterosyllabic nasal are written oral: menino [meˈninu]. Orthographic nh is [ɲ] and lh is [ʎ]: filho [ˈfiʎu].

Source: Silva (2002), section on nasal vowels; Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2.

### Epenthetic vowels in consonant clusters

Write the epenthetic vowel as [i], with no palatalization of a preceding /t d/: advogado [adivoˈɡadu], ritmo [ˈxitimu], pneu [piˈnew].

Source: Silva (2002) on epenthesis in BP; non-palatalization follows the spec note.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
