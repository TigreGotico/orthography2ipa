# Transcription conventions: Eastern Galician (gl-x-oriental)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every content word of two or more syllables. Monosyllables get no mark, whether stressed or not: can /kaŋ/, sol /sɔl/, é /ɛ/. Stress is lexical and follows the standard spelling rules. An unaccented word ending in a vowel, -n or -s (also -ns) is stressed on the penult: casa /ˈkasa/, home /ˈɔme/, xoven /ˈʃɔβen/ → write /ˈʃoben/ per the rules below, cantan /ˈkantaŋ/. An unaccented word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the final syllable: cantar /kanˈtaɾ/, papel /paˈpɛl/, feliz /feˈliθ/. A written acute or grave accent marks the stressed vowel: irmán /iɾˈmaŋ/, árbore /ˈaɾboɾe/, mércores /ˈmɛɾkoɾes/. The stressed vowel carries the seven-way contrast /i e ɛ a ɔ o u/, chosen from the standard lexicon: pé /pɛ/, medo /ˈmedo/, porta /ˈpɔɾta/, boca /ˈboka/. Unstressed syllables have only /i e a o u/, never /ɛ ɔ/: portiño /poɾˈtiɲo/. Final unstressed vowels are written broad /e a o/, not [ɪ ɐ ʊ]: leite /ˈlejte/. Galician has no phonemic vowel length, no geminates and no case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply.

Source: Freixeiro Mato 1998 (Gramática da Lingua Galega I, Fonética e fonoloxía: vogais and acento); Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2) 'Galician' for the seven-vowel stressed and five-vowel unstressed system; RAG/ILG Normas ortográficas e morfolóxicas for accent rules.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words are unstressed and never get ˈ, even when they have two syllables. Their mid vowels are always /e o/. (1) Definite articles o a os as and the allomorphs lo la los las: /o a os as lo la/. (2) Simple prepositions a, ante, ata, cara, con, contra, de, desde, en, entre, para, por, sen, sobre, tras: para /paɾa/, sobre /sobɾe/. Also their contractions with articles: do /do/, na /na/, polo /polo/, coa /koa/, dun /duŋ/, nunha /nuŋa/, ao /aw/. Write ó as /ɔ/. (3) Coordinating and subordinating conjunctions e, ou, nin, mais, pero, que, se, porque, cando, como/coma: porque /poɾke/, cando /kando/. (4) Relative que, quen, onde: write relative onde as /onde/. (5) Atonic object and reflexive clitics me, te, che, lle, lles, o, a, os, as, lo, la, no, na, nos, vos, se, including clitics after a hyphen: me /me/, lle /ʎe/, che /tʃe/, nolo /nolo/. These words keep stress and are marked when they have two or more syllables: indefinite un /uŋ/, unha /ˈuŋa/, unhas /ˈuŋas/; subject and prepositional pronouns eu, ti, el /ɛl/, ela /ˈɛla/, nós /nɔs/, vós; possessives meu /mew/, nosa /ˈnɔsa/; demonstratives este /ˈeste/, aquel /aˈkɛl/; interrogatives que, quen, onde /ˈonde/, cando /ˈkando/ when interrogative; negation non /noŋ/; all verb forms including auxiliaries haber, ter, ser, estar: había /aˈβia/ → write /aˈbia/, ten /tɛŋ/, foi /foj/, é /ɛ/.

Source: Freixeiro Mato 1998 (vol. I, acento: palabras átonas; vol. II, pronomes persoais átonos and artigo); Álvarez & Xove 2002 Gramática da lingua galega for the list of atonic words.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Galician has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word is transcribed the same at a comma, at the end of a sentence and inside a phrase: casa /ˈkasa/ everywhere, and final -n is /ŋ/ everywhere, as in can /kaŋ/.

Source: Freixeiro Mato 1998 (Fonética e fonoloxía); Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its own place in its citation form. Do not mark cross-word assimilation, vowel elision, synaloepha or resyllabification. Only the spelling records sandhi: contractions such as do, coa, nunha, and the second article form after -r/-s, as in comelo or come-lo, where the verb is written without its final consonant and the clitic as /lo/. Transcribe these exactly as spelled: come-lo → /ˈkome/ /lo/. Word-final n stays velar /ŋ/ before a vowel and is not linked to it: un home → /uŋ/ /ˈɔme/, non é → /noŋ/ /ɛ/. Final -s is written /s/ before voiced sounds too: os dous → /os/ /dows/. Identical vowels across a boundary are both kept: de exemplo → /de/ /eˈsemplo/.

Source: Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2) for the word-final velar nasal before vowels; Freixeiro Mato 1998 (contraccións and second article form). Choosing not to write optional synaloepha follows the broad practice of Regueira 1996.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic i and u as the consonant symbols j and w, never i̯ u̯, in both rising and falling diphthongs. Falling diphthongs: pai /paj/, lei /lej/, boi /boj/, ouro /ˈowɾo/, auga /ˈawɡa/, meu /mew/, cuidar /kujˈdaɾ/. Rising diphthongs: cuarto /ˈkwaɾto/, auga, igual /iˈɡwal/, bacía and other words with written accents are hiatus, so no glide: río /ˈrio/, día /ˈdia/, rúa /ˈrua/, ceo /ˈθɛo/. Unaccented i/u next to a vowel is a glide unless the standard lexicon gives a hiatus. Eastern Ĕ/Ŏ diphthongs are written /je we/ only when the text spells them, as in local ie/ue forms of the Ancares and Eo-Navia border. Words spelled with e/o get the standard monophthong: pedra /ˈpɛdɾa/, porta /ˈpɔɾta/. This differs from the specification note: Fernández Rei limits such diphthongs to the Asturleonese transition, and standard Eastern Galician has monophthongs.

Source: Freixeiro Mato 1998 (ditongos e hiatos); Fernández Rei 1990 (bloque oriental and área de transición asturiana, for the limits of diphthongisation).

## Other choices

### palatal affricate ⟨ch⟩

Always write /tʃ/, never /ʃ/: chuvia /ˈtʃubja/, noite → n/a, che /tʃe/, coche /ˈkotʃe/.

Source: Fernández Rei 1990 (bloque oriental); Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2).

### lateral palatal ⟨ll⟩

Always write /ʎ/, never /j/ or /ʝ/. Yeísmo is not written, which follows the standard pronunciation and the specification note: lle /ʎe/, cabalo → n/a, folla /ˈfoʎa/, ollo /ˈoʎo/.

Source: Fernández Rei 1990; Freixeiro Mato 1998 (consoantes palatais).

### ⟨g, gu⟩ and gheada

No gheada. ⟨g⟩ before a/o/u and ⟨gu⟩ before e/i are always /ɡ/: gato /ˈɡato/, guerra /ˈɡera/, auga /ˈawɡa/.

Source: Fernández Rei 1990 (gheada maps: absent in the eastern block).

### ⟨z, ce, ci⟩ and seseo

No seseo. ⟨z⟩, ⟨ce⟩ and ⟨ci⟩ are always /θ/ and ⟨s⟩ is /s/: cinco /ˈθiŋko/, facer /faˈθeɾ/, paz /paθ/, casa /ˈkasa/.

Source: Fernández Rei 1990 (seseo is western and coastal only).

### voiced stops (no spirant allophones)

Write ⟨b v⟩ as /b/ and ⟨d⟩ ⟨g⟩ as /d ɡ/ in every position. Do not write [β ð ɣ]: vida /ˈbida/, cabalo /kaˈbalo/, auga /ˈawɡa/. Any β or ð slips elsewhere in these conventions are to be read as b and d.

Source: Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2), which treats [β ð ɣ] as allophones of /b d ɡ/; broad practice.

### nasals

Word-final ⟨n⟩ and ⟨nh⟩ are /ŋ/: can /kaŋ/, irmán /iɾˈmaŋ/, unha /ˈuŋa/, ningunha /niŋˈɡuŋa/. ⟨ñ⟩ is /ɲ/: ano /ˈano/, ano vs año → n/a, viño /ˈbiɲo/. ⟨n⟩ before a velar inside a word is /ŋ/: branco /ˈbɾaŋko/. Otherwise write ⟨n m⟩ as /n m/.

Source: Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2); Freixeiro Mato 1998 (consoantes nasais).

### rhotics, sibilants, ⟨x⟩, ⟨h⟩

Single intervocalic and coda ⟨r⟩ is /ɾ/. ⟨rr⟩, word-initial ⟨r⟩, and ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s are /r/: pero /ˈpeɾo/, terra /ˈtɛra/, rato /ˈrato/, honra /ˈonra/. ⟨x⟩ is /ʃ/: xente /ˈʃente/, except in learned words, where it is /ks/: taxi /ˈtaksi/. ⟨h⟩ is silent: home /ˈɔme/.

Source: Freixeiro Mato 1998; RAG/ILG Normas ortográficas e morfolóxicas.

### eastern morphological variants

Transcribe dialectal forms exactly as spelled in the text, such as plurals in -ns, cans /kaŋs/, -án/-á endings, or ye for é. Do not change a standard spelling into a local variant, and do not change a local spelling into the standard form.

Source: Fernández Rei 1990 (bloque oriental: plurais and -ANU/-ANA outcomes).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
