# Transcription conventions: Aranese (Gascon Occitan) (oc-x-aranes)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every content word, including monosyllables (can [ˈkan]). Do not use secondary stress marks. A written accent (à è é í ò ó ú) marks the stressed vowel and overrides the default rule: istòria [isˈtɔɾia], aué [aˈwe]. Without an accent, a word is paroxytone (stress on the penult) if it ends in a, e, o, or in a, e, o followed by the inflectional endings -s or -n: casa [ˈkaza], cases [ˈkazes], parlen [ˈpaɾlen]. Otherwise it is oxytone (stress on the final syllable), which covers words ending in -i, -u, a falling diphthong, or any other consonant: Aran [aˈɾan], vedèth [beˈdɛt], cantar [kanˈta]. Hiatus ⟨ua⟩ after a consonant counts as two syllables: lua [ˈlya]. Aranese has no phonemic vowel length, no geminates and no case endings, so these do not affect stress. ⟨ll⟩ is a single [l], never a geminate.

Source: Alibert 1976 (accentuation chapter); Bec 1973 (Gascon section); Coromines 1990, El parlar de la Vall d'Aran; Conselh Generau d'Aran orthographic norms

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following get no stress mark: the definite articles eth [et], era [eɾa], es [es], eres [eɾes]; the indefinite articles un [yn], ua [ya]; the prepositions de [de], a [a], en [en], per [peɾ], damb [dam], entà [enta]; the conjunctions e [e], o [u], que [ke], mès [mɛs]; the clitic pronouns me, te, se, lo, la, li, les, mos, vos, ac, i, ne/en; the possessive determiners mon, ma, sa; and monosyllabic finite forms of the auxiliaries auer and èster when they are used as auxiliaries (a [a], è [ɛ], son [sun]). Write the lexical stress of entà only when it stands at the end of a phrase. The following do carry stress: strong and subject pronouns (jo [ˈʒu], tu [ˈty], nosati [nuˈsati]); eth and era when they are pronouns rather than articles ([ˈet], [ˈeɾa]); demonstratives; polysyllabic auxiliary forms (auem [awˈem]); and all lexical verbs.

Source: Bec 1973 (articles, pronouns and clitics); Alibert 1976 (morphology of determiners and pronouns); Coromines 1990 for the Aranese article forms eth/era/es/eres

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Aranese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word at the end of a sentence or before a comma keeps exactly its non-final form, and final unstressed vowels are never dropped or reduced in the transcription: era casa [eɾa ˈkaza].

Source: Bec 1973; Coromines 1990 (no pausal alternation described)

## Connected speech

Transcribe each word in its citation form, in its own slot. The only cross-word process written is the elision that the spelling already shows with an apostrophe. The elided clitic is a separate word written as its remaining consonant: d' [d], l' [l], qu' [k], s' [s], m' [m], n' [n], er' [eɾ]. For example, d'Aran is written [d aˈɾan]. Do not write any other cross-word process: no unwritten vowel elision in hiatus (era escòla [eɾa esˈkɔla]), no voicing of final -s before a vowel or voiced consonant (es amics [es aˈmiks], not [ez]), no nasal place assimilation, no resyllabification and no linking marks.

Source: Conselh Generau d'Aran orthographic norms (apostrophe use); Bec 1973 (sandhi treated as non-distinctive); broad-transcription practice of the specification

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic i and u as [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. This applies to falling diphthongs (aiga [ˈajga], nau [ˈnaw], lèit [ˈlɛjt]), to rising diphthongs (uèlh [ˈwɛʎ]), and when the glide begins a word after a silent ⟨h⟩ (huec [ˈwɛk]). ⟨i⟩ and ⟨u⟩ stay vocalic [i] and [y] in hiatus and when they carry stress or a written accent: lua [ˈlya], país [paˈis]. The ⟨u⟩ of ⟨qu⟩ and ⟨gu⟩ before e or i is silent.

Source: Bec 1973 (diphthongs); Alibert 1976 (diphthongs and triphthongs); IPA usage per Ladefoged & Maddieson 1996

## Other choices

### letter h

⟨h⟩ is silent everywhere in standard Aranese: hèr [ˈɛɾ], haria [aˈɾia], huec [ˈwɛk]. ⟨n·h⟩ and ⟨s·h⟩ are written [n] and [s] respectively. The [h] of Bausen and Canejan is not written. This departs from the note, whose gold gives [h] as the first reading.

Source: Coromines 1990 (deaspiration in Aranese); Bec 1973 (Gascon /h/ and its loss in Aran)

### intervocalic s

Single ⟨s⟩ between vowels is [z], and ⟨ss⟩ is [s]: casa [ˈkaza], passar [paˈsa]. The orthography's ⟨s⟩/⟨ss⟩ contrast encodes exactly this distinction. This departs from the note's gold, which keeps [s].

Source: Alibert 1976; Bec 1973 (sibilants); Coromines 1990

### unstressed and final a

Write [a] in all positions: casa [ˈkaza], parlava [paɾˈlaba]. The slightly backed [ɑ] reported for Aranese is treated as sub-phonemic and is not written in a broad transcription. Do not use [ɔ] or [ə].

Source: Specification note (following Alibert 1976 and Bec 1973); Coromines 1990 for the Aranese realization

### vowel letters

Use these values: ⟨o⟩ and ⟨ó⟩ [u]; ⟨ò⟩ [ɔ]; ⟨u⟩ [y]; ⟨é⟩ and unaccented ⟨e⟩ [e]; ⟨è⟩ [ɛ]. Do not nasalize vowels, and pronounce final -n as [n]: Aran [aˈɾan]. Examples: hòc [ˈɔk], bon [ˈbun], lua [ˈlya].

Source: Alibert 1976; Bec 1973

### consonant letters

Use these values: ⟨v⟩ [b], with betacism (vedèth [beˈdɛt]); ⟨th⟩ [t]; ⟨nh⟩ [ɲ]; ⟨lh⟩ [ʎ]; ⟨sh⟩ [ʃ]; ⟨ch⟩ [tʃ]; ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩ before e or i [ʒ]; single intervocalic ⟨r⟩ [ɾ]; word-initial ⟨r⟩ and ⟨rr⟩ [r]. Do not write spirant allophones [β ð ɣ]. Final -r of the infinitive endings -ar, -er, -ir is silent (cantar [kanˈta]); other written consonants are pronounced.

Source: Bec 1973 (Gascon consonantism); Coromines 1990 (Aranese th, infinitive -r); specification notes

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
