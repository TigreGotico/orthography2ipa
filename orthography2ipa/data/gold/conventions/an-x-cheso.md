# Transcription conventions: Cheso Aragonese (Hecho/Echo valley) (an-x-cheso)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark one primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: [ˈso], [ˈfiʎo]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Do not mark secondary stress. Place the stress by the orthographic rules. (1) A written acute accent fixes the stressed vowel: público [ˈpubliko], Chaca? no; está [esˈta]. (2) Unmarked words ending in a vowel, -n or -s are stressed on the penult: fillo [ˈfiʎo], piedra [ˈpjedɾa], cantaban [kanˈtaban], casas [ˈkasas]. (3) Unmarked words ending in any other consonant are stressed on the final syllable: cantar [kanˈtaɾ], chent [ˈtʃent], cantaz [kanˈtaθ]. (4) A final falling diphthong (-au, -eu, -iu, -ai, -ei, -oi, -ui) counts as consonant-final, so the word is oxytone: cantau [kanˈtaw], partiu [paɾˈtiw]. (5) The plural -s never moves the stress; the plural keeps the stress of the singular: cantaus [kanˈtaws]. A Spanish-looking proparoxytone written without an accent follows rule 2, in line with the Aragonese drift toward paroxytones: paxaro [paˈʃaɾo]. Adverbs in -ment have one stress on -ment: solament [solaˈment]. Vowels are never long, there are no phonemic geminates, and there are no case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply.

Source: Nagore Laín 2001, Gramática de la Lengua Aragonesa (accentuation and orthography chapter, following the 1987 Uesca orthographic norms); Kuhn 1935, cited via the es.wikipedia article 'Aragonés cheso', for the Western High-Aragonese tendency toward paroxytones.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and written without ˈ. Definite articles: o, a, os, as, and neuter lo. Elided or contracted pieces written with an apostrophe: d', l', t', 'l, 'n. Prepositions: a, de, en, con, per, por, ta, enta, dica, sin, sobre, entre, dende. Conjunctions: y/e, u/o, ni, que, pero, si, and cuan or como used as conjunctions. Relatives: que, qui, do. Clitic object pronouns, whether proclitic or hyphenated enclitic: me, te, se, lo, la, li, nos, vos, los, las, les, ne/en, bi/i/hi. Short prenominal possessives: mi, tu, su. Titles before a name: don, dona. Examples: o fillo [o ˈfiʎo]; ta casa [ta ˈkasa]; me'n voi becomes [me] [n] [ˈboj]; fer-lo [ˈfeɾ] [lo]. The following are stressed. Subject and tonic pronouns: yo [ˈʝo], tu as subject [ˈtu], el [ˈel], ella [ˈeʎa], nusatros [nuˈsatɾos]. Possessives after the article: o mío [o ˈmio]. Demonstratives, numerals, negative no [ˈno], interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent: qué [ˈke], cómo [ˈkomo]. All nouns, adjectives and adverbs. All verb forms, including haber and ser as auxiliaries and as copula: ha feito [ˈa ˈfejto], ye [ˈje]. Tell homographs apart by function: tu as subject is [ˈtu], tu as possessive is [tu].

Source: Navarro Tomás 1918/1932, Manual de pronunciación española, chapter on palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas. Its Spanish list is applied to the corresponding Aragonese forms; the Aragonese pronoun and article inventory is taken from Nagore Laín 2001, chapters on the article and personal pronouns.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Cheso has no pausal forms, no case endings and no nunation. Every word is written the same at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside a phrase. Final vowels are never dropped or reduced. The apocope of final -e/-o in Aragonese is lexical and already spelled, so write what is spelled: chent [ˈtʃent], fillo [ˈfiʎo]. A final infinitive -r is kept as [ɾ] before a pause too: cantar [kanˈtaɾ].

Source: Nagore Laín 2001 (phonology chapter); the retention of infinitival -r in Hecho follows Kuhn 1935 via es.wikipedia 'Aragonés cheso'.

## Connected speech

Write every word in its citation form. Do not write any process across a word boundary: no synalepha or vowel elision, no linking marks (‿), no resyllabification, no nasal or voicing assimilation, and no change of /s/ before voiced consonants. So con bino is [kon ˈbino], not [kom ˈbino], and de a is [de a]. Elision that the orthography marks with an apostrophe stays in its own word as a bare consonant: d'a casa [d] [a] [ˈkasa]; l'aire [l] [ˈajɾe]; m'en [m] [en]; me'n [me] [n]. Hyphenated enclitics are separate unstressed words. The host keeps its own stress and its final consonant: fer-lo [ˈfeɾ] [lo]; minchar-ne [minˈtʃaɾ] [ne]; da-me-lo [ˈda] [me] [lo]. In Cheso the infinitive /ɾ/ is kept before enclitics, unlike Ansotano, which deletes it. Never write fe-lo.

Source: Note on Cheso r-retention before enclitics after Kuhn 1935 (via es.wikipedia 'Aragonés cheso'); enclitic and elided pronoun forms per Nagore Laín 2001 (personal pronouns, combinations of clitics).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write every non-syllabic high vocoid as [j] or [w], never as i̯ or u̯, in both rising and falling diphthongs. Rising diphthongs: piedra [ˈpjedɾa], fuego [ˈfweɡo], cielo [ˈθjelo], cuan [ˈkwan]. Falling diphthongs: feito [ˈfejto], muito [ˈmujto], cantau [kanˈtaw], boi [ˈboj]. An accented í or ú marks hiatus, written as two syllabic vowels: mía [ˈmia], continúa [kontiˈnua]. Consonantal ⟨y⟩ in syllable onset is [ʝ], so it stays distinct from the glide: yo [ˈʝo], mayo [ˈmaʝo]. Word-final ⟨y⟩ after a vowel is [j]: rei/rey [ˈrej]. The Cheso feature: ⟨ix⟩ is [ʃ] alone, with no preceding glide: caixa [ˈkaʃa], baixo [ˈbaʃo], naixer [naˈʃeɾ], not [ˈkajʃa].

Source: Martínez-Celdrán, Fernández-Planas & Carrera-Sabaté 2003, 'Castilian Spanish', JIPA 33(2), for the use of j/w and /ʝ/, simplified here to j/w in all positions; the Western realisation of ⟨ix⟩ = [ʃ] in Hecho follows Kuhn 1935 via es.wikipedia 'Aragonés cheso', overriding the base Aragonese variants [iʃ, jʃ].

## Other choices

### Voiced stops and b/v

Write /b d ɡ/ as [b d ɡ] in every position. Do not write the approximant allophones [β ð ɣ]. ⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩ are both [b]: bino [ˈbino], vida [ˈbida], fuego [ˈfweɡo].

Source: Broad-transcription practice of Martínez-Celdrán et al. 2003 for Ibero-Romance; Nagore Laín 2001 (phonology chapter: no b/v contrast).

### Rhotics

Write [r] for ⟨rr⟩, for word-initial ⟨r⟩ and for ⟨r⟩ after n, l, s. Write [ɾ] everywhere else, including word-finally: terra [ˈtera], rei [ˈrej], honra [ˈonra], cantar [kanˈtaɾ], piedra [ˈpjedɾa].

Source: Nagore Laín 2001 (phonology chapter).

### Distinctive consonants of the Western profile

⟨ll⟩ is [ʎ], with no yeísmo: fillo [ˈfiʎo]. ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ]: chent [ˈtʃent]. ⟨z⟩ and ⟨c⟩ before e/i are [θ], with distinción: zaga [ˈθaɡa], cielo [ˈθjelo]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ]: xada [ˈʃada]. ⟨ny⟩ and ⟨ñ⟩ are [ɲ]: anyo [ˈaɲo]. ⟨tz⟩ is [ts]. ⟨h⟩ is silent. ⟨qu⟩ and ⟨gu⟩ before e/i are [k] and [ɡ]. ⟨gü⟩ is [ɡw]. Initial F- is kept as [f]: feito [ˈfejto]. Write [n] before velars and labials unless the spelling has ⟨m⟩: cinco [ˈθinko].

Source: Nagore Laín 2001 (orthography and phonology); Kuhn 1935 via es.wikipedia 'Aragonés cheso' for the Hecho profile.

### Vowels

There are five vowels [a e i o u], with no length and no unstressed reduction. Unstressed vowels keep their full quality: casas [ˈkasas], fillos [ˈfiʎos]. Do not mark open or close mid allophones.

Source: Nagore Laín 2001 (phonology chapter: vowel system).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
