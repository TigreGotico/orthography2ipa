# Transcription conventions: Proto-Levantine Arabic (ar-x-levantine)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables. Leave monosyllables unmarked (beːt, ʔaːl, min). Syllabify the whole written word, including attached suffixes, the article and attached proclitics. Then apply this rule. (1) Stress a final superheavy syllable, which is CVːC or CVCC: kiˈtaːb, daˈrast, inˈnaːs. (2) Otherwise stress the penult if it is heavy, which is CVː or CVC: ˈmaktab, kaˈtabha, ˈʕallam. (3) Otherwise stress the antepenult: ˈmadrasa, ˈʃaʒara, ˈkatabu. Stress never falls further left than the antepenult. A long vowel makes its syllable heavy. A geminate closes the preceding syllable, so that syllable is heavy (ˈmʕallim, ˈʔawwal). Stress never falls on the article il-/l- or on the proclitics b(i)-, l(a)-, w-, ʕa-. If the rule would pick one of these, stress the first syllable of the stem instead: ilˈwalad, bilˈbeːt. Suffixes shift stress by the same rule: ˈbeːtu, beːˈtkum is not used; write ˈbeːtkum. Levantine has no case endings, so they play no part in stress.

Source: The standard Levantine stress rule as given for Damascus in Cowell 1964 (stress section; primary text not consulted here) and for Palestinian in the generative literature (Brame 1974; Kenstowicz 1994, Palestinian Arabic stress). This is the Levantine/Palestinian tradition (ˈmadrasa), not the Cairene tradition (madˈrasa).

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following items never carry a stress mark, even when they have two syllables. Article il-/l-. Conjunctions w(a)-/u, ʔaw, bas, ʔinnu, laʔinnu, ʔiza, law. Prepositions b(i)-, l(a)-/li-, ʕa-, fi, min, maʕ, ʕan, ʕala, ʕind, ʔilla. Relative ʔilli. Negators ma, miʃ/mu. Verbal particles b-, ʕam, raħ. Examples: ʕala lˈmaktab, ʔilli ˈʃuft, ma ˈbaʕrif, raħ ˈjiʒi. Attached pronominal suffixes belong to their host and take part in its stress computation: ˈʕindu, ʕalaˈjha is not used; write ʕaˈleːha. The following carry stress like content words: independent pronouns (ˈʔana, ˈʔinta, ˈhuwwa, ˈhijja, ˈnaħna); demonstratives (ˈhaːda, ˈhaːji); interrogatives (ˈleːʃ, ˈmiːn, ˈweːn take no mark because they are monosyllables; ˈʔeːmta is marked); the auxiliary/copular verb kaːn and its forms (ˈkaːnat).

Source: Cowell 1964 (particles and pronouns, as standard Damascene description); Watson 2002 ch. 5 on stressless proclitics.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply in the classical sense. Levantine has no case endings and no nunation, so each word has the same form at the end of the sentence, at a comma and inside the phrase. If the input writes MSA case vowels or tanwīn on a word, leave them out (ilˈbeːt, not ilbeːtu). The exception is the lexicalised adverbial -an, which is kept everywhere: ˈʃukran, ˈʔahlan, ˈtˤɑbʕan, ˈʔabadan. Word-final vowels are always written short (ˈʔana, ˈkatabu, fi). They lengthen only before a suffix inside the same word (ʔaˈbuːk, kataˈbuːha). This departs from the specification's input contract that every written short vowel surfaces.

Source: Cowell 1964 (loss of case and final-vowel shortening, as standard Damascene description); Fadda 2016:27-30 and Al-Wer 2020 describe the Ammani/urban Palestinian koine with no case endings.

## Connected speech

Each word stays in its own place and is written in its citation form, with three exceptions. (1) The article's l assimilates to a following coronal inside the same word: iʃˈʃams, innaːs → inˈnaːs, idˈdaːr, itˈtaːni. Before ʒ it is optional; keep il: ilˈʒaːmʕa. (2) The article loses its vowel after a vowel-final word, so the next word begins with l- or a geminate: fi lˈbeːt, ʕala ʃˈʃaːriʕ. After a consonant or a pause the article is il-, written without ʔ: ilˈbeːt ˈkbiːr. The same i/zero alternation applies to the prosthetic i- of initial clusters. (3) Nothing else is written. Do not write cross-word epenthetic vowels, nasal place assimilation, devoicing or resyllabification marks. Write word-initial ʔ wherever the word has it, even after a vowel: ˈhuwwa ʔaːl.

Source: Cowell 1964 (article allomorphy and sun-letter assimilation, as standard description); Watson 2002 (assimilation of the article l).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as the consonants j and w, never as i̯ u̯, and write geminate glides doubled: ˈhijja, ˈʔawwal, ˈʔajjaːm. Write long high vowels as iː uː, not ij uw. Monophthongise the inherited diphthongs /aj aw/ to eː oː wherever they precede a consonant or a word boundary: beːt, joːm, weːn, ˈmoːt, sˤoːtˤ. Keep aj aw only before a glide or geminate, as in ˈʔawwal and ˈʔajjaːm, and where the glide carries its own vowel: ħajaˈwaːn, ˈjawadd. Learned words that keep the diphthong in the urban koine also keep it: ˈdawla.

Source: Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 §1.1; Fadda 2016:30 (bajt → beːt, sˤawt → sˤoːtˤ, except before a glide); the specification's notes on وَيْ/يَوْ.

## Other choices

### qaf, jim and interdentals

Follow the urban prestige koine. ق is written ʔ (ʔaːl 'he said'), ج is written ʒ (ʒaːb), and θ ð ðˤ are written t d dˤ (tamaːnja, dahab, dˤuhr). In clearly learned Standard Arabic loans, write the koine sibilants s z zˤ: ˈmasalan, ˈsaːnawi, ˈzˤaːhir. Do not write the Bedouin or rural variants ɡ, dʒ, k or interdentals.

Source: Fadda 2016:27-28; Al-Wer & Herin 2011:60 (cited in Fadda); Al-Wer 2020:560; Cotter 2016:150-154.

### vowel symbols and emphasis

Write short vowels only as i a u. The mid and central allophones [e o ə] are written i and u in this broad transcription: ˈbeːtu, not ˈbeːto. Write long vowels as iː eː aː oː uː. Write /a aː/ as ɑ ɑː only when the vowel is directly adjacent to an emphatic consonant tˤ sˤ dˤ zˤ: sˤɑːr, ˈtˤɑbʕan, ˈmariːdˤ. Spreading further than one segment is not written.

Source: Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 §3-4 (three short, five long vowels); Watson 2002 (emphasis spreading), as modelled in the specification's notes.

### feminine ending (tāʔ marbūṭa)

At this ancestor node, write the absolute-state feminine ending as -a in every context: ˈmadrasa, ˈsˤɑːħa. Do not write the raised -e or -i, because imāla belongs to the city specifications. In construct state, write -it: ˈmadrasit ilˈbalad. Before a vowel-initial suffix, write -t-: madˈrasti.

Source: Cowell 1964 (feminine ending and construct -it, as standard); the specification's notes assign imāla to the per-city specifications.

### 3sg masculine suffix

Write the 3sg masculine suffix as -u (ˈbeːtu, ˈʃuftu), not Damascene -o. This follows the rule that short [o] is written u. After a vowel, write it as lengthening of that vowel: ʔaˈbuː.

Source: Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 §4 ([o] as an allophone of /u/); Fadda 2016:30.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
