# Transcription conventions: Fluminense Portuguese (pt-BR-x-fluminense)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable. Lexical stress falls on one of the last three syllables. An orthographic acute or circumflex marks the stressed syllable: café [kaˈfɛ], público [ˈpubliku], você [voˈse]. With no written accent, stress the penult if the word ends in -a, -e, -o (each optionally plus -s), or in -em, -ens, -am: casa [ˈkazɐ], cidade [siˈdadʒi], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. Otherwise stress the final syllable, as in words ending in -r, -l, -z, -x, -i(s), -u(s), -im, -om, -um, -ão or -ã: falar [faˈlah], Brasil [bɾaˈziw], aqui [aˈki], irmã [ixˈmɐ̃]. Mark stress on every polysyllable and on every stressed monosyllable (pé [ˈpɛ], mar [ˈmah], três [ˈtɾejʃ]). Leave unstressed clitic monosyllables unmarked (see function_words). Never mark secondary stress. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply. Spelled rr and ss are single consonants: carro [ˈkaxu], passo [ˈpasu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, chapter on word stress; Silva 2002, section on stress (acento); Callou & Leite 2001.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Leave these unstressed (no ˈ) and write them with reduced vowels. Definite articles: o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ]. Monosyllabic prepositions: de [dʒi], em [ẽj̃], com [kõ], por [poh], sem [sẽj̃], a [ɐ]. Their contractions: do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], ao [aw], pelo [pelu], num [nũ]. Conjunctions and complementisers: e [i], ou [o], que [ki], se [si], nem [nẽj̃], mas [majʃ]. Relative que [ki]. Clitic pronouns: me [mi], te [tʃi], se [si], lhe [ʎi], nos [nuʃ], o [u], a [ɐ], lo [lu]. Give lexical stress to these: indefinite articles um [ˈũ], uma [ˈumɐ], uns [ˈũʃ]. Tonic pronouns: eu [ˈew], você [voˈse], ele [ˈeli], nós [ˈnɔjʃ], mim [ˈmĩ]. Possessives: meu [ˈmew], sua [ˈsuɐ]. Demonstratives: isso [ˈisu]. Negation não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], always written in full, never reduced to [nũ]. Interrogatives and stressed quê: o quê [u ˈke], como [ˈkomu]. All forms of auxiliaries and copulas: é [ˈɛ], está [isˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], foi [ˈfoj], vai [ˈvaj]. Disyllabic prepositions: para [ˈpaɾɐ], sobre [ˈsobɾi], entre [ˈẽtɾi], até [aˈtɛ], desde [ˈdezdʒi]. Write para in full, and use [pɾɐ] only when the text spells pra. Write está and estar in full, never as [ta].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (clitic and prosodic word discussion); Callou & Leite 2001; Silva 2002.

## Pausal forms

Portuguese has no morphological pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so this topic does not apply. The only pause-conditioned choice is this: at a comma and at the end of the sentence, word-final /S/ is [ʃ] and word-final /R/ is [h], with no linking. Examples: ...os livros. [uʃ ˈlivɾuʃ], ...vou falar. [ˈvo faˈlah]. Final unstressed vowels keep their ordinary reduced forms [i u ɐ] at a pause and are not deleted: noite [ˈnojtʃi].

Source: Callou 2010:135 (word-final chiado) and 137-138 (word-final aspiration [h]); Callou & Leite 2001.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its own place in citation form, except for these boundary processes, which change only the final segment of the first word. (1) Word-final /S/ before a voiceless consonant or pause is [ʃ]: os pés [uʃ ˈpɛʃ]. Before a voiced consonant it is [ʒ]: mais doce [ˈmajʒ ˈdosi], as mães [ɐʒ ˈmɐ̃j̃ʃ]. Before a vowel it is [z]: os olhos [uz ˈɔʎuʃ], três anos [ˈtɾejz ˈɐ̃nuʃ]. (2) Word-final /R/ before a vowel is the tap [ɾ]: mar azul [ˈmaɾ aˈzuw], falar inglês [faˈlaɾ ĩˈɡlejʃ]. Before a consonant or pause it is [h]. (3) Do not write vowel sandhi (elision, degemination, diphthongisation across words, crasis). Both vowels stay in their own words: casa amarela [ˈkazɐ amaˈɾɛlɐ], de outro [dʒi ˈotɾu]. (4) Word-final /l/ stays [w] before a vowel: mal estar [ˈmaw isˈtah]. (5) Do not write any other assimilation across words, including nasal and place assimilation. /t d/ palatalisation is decided inside each word only.

Source: Callou 2010:135, 137-138 for /S/ and /R/; Callou & Leite 2001 for linking tap and /S/ voicing; Silva 2002. External vowel sandhi (as described for Brazilian Portuguese by Bisol) is deliberately not transcribed.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vowels as [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. Falling diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], meu [ˈmew], céu [ˈsɛw], foi [ˈfoj], and vocalised /l/ in alto [ˈawtu]. Rising sequences: quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], água [ˈaɡwɐ], and post-tonic -io/-ia, which is written as a glide: história [isˈtɔɾjɐ], sério [ˈsɛɾju]. Nasal diphthongs take a tilde on both elements: mão [ˈmɐ̃w̃], mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. Monophthongisation: always write ou as [o] (pouco [ˈpoku], vou [ˈvo]). Write ei as [e] before [ɾ ʃ ʒ] (primeiro [pɾiˈmeɾu], beijo [ˈbeʒu]) and as [ej] elsewhere (leite [ˈlejtʃi]). Write ai as [a] before [ʃ] (caixa [ˈkaʃɐ]). Yod epenthesis before word-final /S/ in stressed syllables is written as [j]: três [ˈtɾejʃ], faz [ˈfajʃ], nós [ˈnɔjʃ], arroz [aˈxojʃ], and also in mas [majʃ].

Source: Silva 2002 (diphthongs, monophthongisation, yod epenthesis); Callou & Leite 2001; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (glides as non-syllabic vowels).

## Other choices

### Coda /S/ (chiado)

Use the Carioca palatal coda everywhere, as the prestige default. Word-internally it is [ʃ] before a voiceless consonant and [ʒ] before a voiced one: festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ], mesmo [ˈmeʒmu], escola [isˈkɔlɐ] → [iʃˈkɔlɐ]. Word-finally the rules under connected_speech apply. The rural alveolar [s z] variants are never written. Note: the escola example in the pretonic-vowels entry follows this rule, so write [iʃˈkɔlɐ] and [iʃˈtah].

Source: Callou 2010:135, Tabela 1 (91% word-internal, 76% word-final palatalisation in Rio).

### Rhotics

Strong r (word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s) is [x]: rato [ˈxatu], carro [ˈkaxu], honra [ˈõxɐ]. Word-internal coda r is [x] regardless of the following consonant: porta [ˈpɔxtɐ], carne [ˈkaxni]. Word-final r is [h], including in infinitives, which are never deleted: mar [ˈmah], falar [faˈlah]. Before a vowel in the next word it is [ɾ]. Single intervocalic r and r in onset clusters are [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], prato [ˈpɾatu]. Never write the inland tap or retroflex coda.

Source: Callou 2010:137-138 (Callou 1987: velar [x] internal, aspiration [h] word-final); Callou & Leite 2001.

### /t d/ palatalisation

Write /t d/ as [tʃ dʒ] before every [i] or [j], including final unstressed -e and raised pretonic e: tia [ˈtʃiɐ], noite [ˈnojtʃi], cidade [siˈdadʒi], de [dʒi], desculpa [dʒisˈkuwpɐ]. Apply this categorically.

Source: Notes of the specification; Cardoso et al. 2014 (ALiB palatalisation isoglosses); Silva 2002.

### Coda /l/, lh, nh

Coda /l/ is always [w]: sol [ˈsɔw], Brasil [bɾaˈziw], calma [ˈkawmɐ]. Write lh as [ʎ] (filho [ˈfiʎu]) and nh as [ɲ] (vinho [ˈviɲu]), never as a nasal glide.

Source: Callou & Leite 2001; Silva 2002.

### Unstressed vowels

Final unstressed vowels: -e(s) [i(ʃ)], -o(s) [u(ʃ)], -a(s) [ɐ(ʃ)]. Examples: leite [ˈlejtʃi], livros [ˈlivɾuʃ], casa [ˈkazɐ]. Non-final post-tonic vowels: o → [u], e stays [e], a → [a]: pérola [ˈpɛɾulɐ], número [ˈnumeɾu], sábado [ˈsabadu]. Pretonic vowels: e and o are close [e o] by default, never open [ɛ ɔ] (menino [meˈninu], morar [moˈɾah]). The exceptions are word-initial unstressed es-/ex- before a consonant, written [iʃ]/[iʒ] (estar [iʃˈtah], explicar [iʃpliˈkah]), and the prefix des-, written [dʒiʃ]/[dʒiʒ]. Write open [ɛ ɔ] only under primary stress.

Source: Silva 2002 (following Câmara Jr.'s reduced unstressed vowel systems); Cardoso et al. 2014 (closed pretonic vowels in the Southeast); Callou & Leite 2001.

### Nasal vowels

Write orthographic vowel plus coda m/n, and ã, as a nasal vowel with tilde, with no nasal consonant: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], tempo [ˈtẽpu], fim [ˈfĩ], som [ˈsõ], mundo [ˈmũdu], lã [ˈlɐ̃]. Final -em/-ém/-ens is written [ẽj̃(ʃ)], and final -am/-ão is written [ɐ̃w̃]. A stressed vowel before a nasal consonant in the next onset is nasalised: cama [ˈkɐ̃mɐ], fome [ˈfõmi], banana [baˈnɐ̃nɐ]. Unstressed vowels before a nasal consonant are written oral.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (nasal vowels and diphthongs); Silva 2002.

### Epenthesis in consonant clusters

Insert [i] after a stop or fricative that has no following vowel in learned clusters. Stress is unchanged and the inserted [i] triggers palatalisation: advogado [adʒivoˈɡadu], ritmo [ˈxitʃimu], pneu [piˈnew], absurdo [abiˈsuxdu].

Source: Callou & Leite 2001; Silva 2002.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
