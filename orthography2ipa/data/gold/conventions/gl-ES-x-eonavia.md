# Transcription conventions: Eonavian (Galician-Asturian, gallego-asturiano) (gl-ES-x-eonavia)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable. Mark it in every word of two or more syllables. Mark it in every stressed monosyllable (see function_words). No secondary stress is marked. Placement is lexical, and the spelling gives it. A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel: cantéi [kanˈtej], caráuter [kaˈɾawteɾ], día [ˈdia], cómo [ˈkomo]. Without an accent, a word ending in a vowel, a falling diphthong is not included here, -n or -s is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkas̺a], llobo [ˈʎoβo], cantan [ˈkantan]. Without an accent, a word ending in another consonant or in a falling diphthong ⟨i u⟩ is stressed on the last syllable: cantar [kanˈtaɾ], rei [ˈrej], cantou [kanˈtow]. Enclitics written solid with the verb do not move the stress: díxoye [ˈdiʃoʝe]. The lect has no long vowels, geminates or case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply.

Source: ALLA (Secretaría Llingüística del Navia-Eo) 2025, Normas ortográficas del eonaviego, §1.5.1-1.5.2, pp. 20-21 (accentuation rules, accent on the more open vowel of a diphthong). Mark placement follows Regueira 1996, 'Galician', JIPA 26(2) (Illustrations of the IPA).

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and get no ˈ: definite articles and their contractions el [el], a [a], os [os̺], as [as̺], del [del], al [al], nel [nel], na [na], pol [pol]; monosyllabic prepositions de [de], en [en], a [a], con [kon], por [poɾ], pra [pɾa]; the conjunctions y [i], ou [ow], que [ke], se [s̺e], nin [nin]; the relative que [ke]; atonic clitic pronouns me [me], te [te], se [s̺e], ye [ʝe], yes [ʝes̺], lo [lo], la [la], nos [nos̺], vos [bos̺], including a clitic split off by a hyphen. The following are stressed and get ˈ: nouns, adjectives, adverbs, numerals and all verb forms, including auxiliaries and copulas: é [ˈɛ], ten [ˈten], hai [ˈaj], foi [ˈfoj]; tonic pronouns eu [ˈew], tu [ˈtu], el [ˈel] (pronoun), nós [ˈnɔs̺], mín [ˈmin]; possessives meu [ˈmew], súa [ˈs̺ua]; demonstratives; accented interrogatives qué [ˈke], ónde [ˈonde]; and disyllabic prepositions and conjunctions, which get ˈ on their lexical stress: pero [ˈpeɾo], desde [ˈdesðe]. Unstressed words only have the vowels [a e i o u], never [ɛ ɔ].

Source: ALLA 2025, §1.5.4 (diacritic accent separating tonic from atonic homographs: el/él type, vén/ven, pé/pe) and ALLA 2007 §1.3. The atonic/tonic word classes follow the Galician tradition, Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. There is no pausal form at the end of a sentence, at a comma or anywhere else, and the lect has no case endings or nunation. Final vowels and consonants are written the same at a pause as inside the phrase: casa [ˈkas̺a], can [ˈkan]. Only the post-pausal stop allophones of /b d g/ depend on a pause (see connected_speech).

Source: ALLA 2025, §1.1-1.3 (no pausal alternations described); Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2).

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its own place, with no linking marks and no syllable dots. Only these processes are written across word boundaries. (1) Spirantisation: /b d g/ are written [β ð ɣ] inside the phrase, word-initially too, when the previous sound is a vowel or any consonant other than a nasal, and for /d/ other than /l/: a boca [a ˈβoka], de dous [de ˈðows], os gatos [os̺ ˈɣatos̺]. They are written [b d g] after a pause (sentence start, comma), after a nasal and, for /d/, after /l/: con dous [kon ˈdows], el dedo [el ˈdeðo]. (2) Nasal place assimilation: a word-final /n/ is written [m] before /p b m/ and [ŋ] before /k g/: con boca [kom ˈboka], un can [uŋ ˈkan]. Otherwise it is [n], also before a vowel and at a pause. (3) A word-initial ⟨r⟩ is always the trill [r]: a roda [a ˈroða]. Not written: vowel elision, synalepha or contraction between words (que a [ke a]), /s/ voicing before voiced consonants, and resyllabification of a final consonant. Elisions that the spelling marks with an apostrophe are transcribed by their remaining segments as their own word, applying rule (1) to them: d'auga [ð ˈawɣwa] after a vowel.

Source: Spirantisation and nasal assimilation follow Regueira 1996, 'Galician', JIPA 26(2), the Galician base phonology the spec inherits. The phoneme inventory /m n ɲ/ without /ŋ/ follows ALLA 2007, §1.1. Contractions and apostrophe forms follow ALLA 2025 spelling.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vowels as [j] and [w], never i̯ u̯, in both falling and rising diphthongs. Falling: rei [ˈrej], hoi [ˈoj], hai [ˈaj], cantéi [kanˈtej], baxóu [baˈʃow], caráuter [kaˈɾawteɾ]. Rising: cuando [ˈkwando], ciencia [ˈθjenθja], augua [ˈawɣwa]. In a falling diphthong the nucleus has the quality of the unaccented diphthong even under an acute: [ej ow], not [ɛj ɔw]. A written accent on ⟨í ú⟩ marks hiatus, with two syllabic vowels: día [ˈdia], mía [ˈmia], faría [faˈɾia], súa [ˈs̺ua]. Keep [j] apart from the consonant /ʝ/ spelled ⟨y⟩ before a vowel: mayo [ˈmaʝo], muyer [muˈʝeɾ]. The conjunction y is the vowel [i].

Source: ALLA 2025, §1.3.2 (p. 17: hoi, hai, rei, penséi, cantái as diphthongs; ⟨y⟩ writes a vowel only as the conjunction), §1.2.5 (p. 16: /ʝ/ = y), §1.5.2 (p. 20). Symbol choice j/w follows Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2).

## Other choices

### tonic mid vowels

Seven stressed vowels /a e ɛ i o ɔ u/. Where the spelling has no timbre accent, open versus closed /e ɛ o ɔ/ follows the standard Galician lexical value of the cognate: terra [ˈtɛra], porta [ˈpɔɾta], home [ˈɔme], cedo [ˈθeðo], boca [ˈboka]. Where the Eonavian spelling marks timbre (vén, óso, pé, présa, coyémos), write [ɛ ɔ]: óso [ˈɔs̺o], coyémos [koˈʝɛmos̺]. Unstressed vowels are always [a e i o u].

Source: ALLA 2007, §1.3.1/§1.3.3 (seven-vowel tonic system); ALLA 2025, §1.5.4.2, p. 21 (timbre accent); Regueira 1996, JIPA 26(2).

### final unstressed vowels

Write final unstressed -o, -e as [o], [e], as spelled: llobo [ˈʎoβo], leite [ˈlejte]. The raising to [u], [i] found in the easternmost parishes is not transcribed.

Source: ALLA 2025 standard written forms; eastern raising noted as dialect-internal in the spec and in Frías Conde 2005, Ianua 5, pp. 93-106.

### consonant symbols

⟨ch⟩ [tʃ] (chover [tʃoˈβeɾ]); ⟨x⟩ [ʃ] (xabón [ʃaˈβon]); ⟨ll⟩ [ʎ] and ⟨l⟩ [l], as spelled, with no yeísmo; ⟨y⟩ before a vowel [ʝ]; ⟨ñ⟩ [ɲ]; ⟨z⟩ and ⟨c⟩ before e,i [θ] (distinción, no seseo); ⟨g⟩ [g]/[ɣ] (no gheada); ⟨j⟩ [χ] only in unadapted loans; ⟨b v⟩ both /b/ (betacism); ⟨h⟩ silent; ⟨s⟩ [s̺]; ⟨rr⟩, and ⟨r⟩ initially and after n, l, s, [r]; otherwise ⟨r⟩ [ɾ]. Intervocalic n lost in words like faría and mía is transcribed as spelled, with no nasalisation.

Source: ALLA 2025, §1.1-1.2.6, pp. 15-17; ALLA 2007, §1.1-1.2.6; Frías Conde 2005, Ianua 5, pp. 96-97 (denasalisation).

### word-final nasal

Word-final /n/ is [n] before a pause and before a vowel, not the Galician velar [ŋ]: can [ˈkan], cantan [ˈkantan]. [ŋ] and [m] arise only by assimilation (see connected_speech).

Source: ALLA 2007, §1.1 inventory (/m n ɲ/, no /ŋ/). This departs from the Galician base description, Regueira 1996, where implosive n is velar.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
