# Transcription conventions: Tunisian Arabic (ar-TN)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ on every content word, and only one per word. Count syllables over the whole written word, including the article, attached w-/b-/l-/f-, pronoun suffixes and negative -ʃ. Superheavy syllables are CVːC, CVCC and CVːCC. Heavy syllables are CVː and CVC. Light syllables are CV. (1) If the final syllable is superheavy, stress it: ktɛːb → ˈktɛːb, mʃaːw → ˈmʃaːw, ma ktibtʃ → ma ˈktibtʃ. (2) Otherwise stress the penultimate syllable: ˈmadrsa, ˈkassar, ˈmʕallim, ˈmɛːkla. (3) A word with only one vowel is stressed on that vowel: ˈdaːr, ˈktib, ˈbiːt. Final CVC and final short vowels never attract stress by themselves. Long vowels count only through syllable weight. A geminate closes the syllable before it, making it heavy: kas.sar → ˈkassar. Case endings do not exist in this lect, so they never affect stress. Placement of the mark: a word-initial consonant cluster belongs wholly to the first syllable, so the mark goes before it (ˈktɛːb, not kˈtɛːb). Word-medially the mark goes before the single consonant right before the stressed vowel (madˈrsa is not used; for final-stressed medial clusters write mak.ˈtuːb → makˈtuːb). Prothetic or epenthetic ə is never stressed.

Source: Weight-sensitive Maghrebi sedentary pattern as described for Tunis by Gibson 2009 (EALL vol. 4, 'Tunis Arabic', phonology/stress) and Singer 1984 (Medina of Tunis grammar, Lautlehre). Typology after Watson 2002, chapter on word stress. Page numbers were not verified for this pass.

## Stressed and unstressed words

No stress mark on these items: monosyllabic and clitic prepositions fi, ʕla, mʕa, mtaʕ, min, l-, b-; conjunctions w/u, willa, ama; the relative illi; the future marker bɛʃ; the negator ma when written as a separate word; the article l-, which is always inside its host word. Write these items in their short connected-speech form: fi, ʕla, bɛʃ, mtaʕ. These items DO take stress: independent pronouns (ˈana, ˈinti, ˈhuwwa, ˈhiyya); demonstratives (ˈhɛːða, ˈhɛːði); interrogatives (ˈʃnuwwa, ˈʃkuːn, ˈwiːn, kiːˈfɛːʃ); the negative copula ˈmuːʃ/ˈmɛːʃi; the auxiliaries kɛːn and qaːʕid (ˈkɛːn, ˈqaːʕid); and every noun, verb, adjective and adverb. Clitic pronouns and negative -ʃ are part of their host word and only shift its stress under the stress rule: ˈktɛːbi, ma ˈʒaːʃ.

Source: Gibson 2009 (EALL vol. 4, 'Tunis Arabic', morphology of particles and pronouns) and Singer 1984. Short forms follow Gibson's citation forms.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Tunis Arabic has no case endings, no nunation and no separate pausal form. Every word has the same shape at the end of a sentence, at a comma and medially. Word-final vowels are always written short in every position: ˈmʃa, ˈʃra, ˈmadrsa. A long vowel appears only before a suffix: ˈmʃaːh. A written tanwīn is read only in fossilised adverbs that keep it lexically (ˈʃukran, ˈabadan) and is otherwise ignored.

Source: Gibson 2009 (EALL vol. 4); Singer 1984. Loss of case and tanwīn in the Maghrebi dialects: Holes 2004 (chapter on the dialects).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its own place in a fixed form. Do not write cross-word assimilation (voicing, nasal place, emphasis spread), resyllabification or linking. The article assimilates inside its own word to a following coronal (t d s z ʃ ʒ θ ð n r l and their emphatics), written as a geminate: ˈʃʃams, ˈnnaːs, ˈddaːr. Before other consonants it is l: ˈlbɛːb, ˈlqamra. One boundary-sensitive rule applies: a word beginning with a consonant cluster (including article l-/geminate) gets a prothetic ə only when it is the first word of the sentence or follows punctuation, and has no prothetic vowel elsewhere. Examples: əlˈbɛːb ˈmsakkar vs fi lˈbɛːb; əˈʃʃams vs fi ˈʃʃams. No other elision is written.

Source: Article assimilation and prothesis as in Gibson 2009 (EALL vol. 4) and Singer 1984. The citation-form policy is a convention of this transcription.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as consonants j and w in onset and coda, never i̯ u̯. Geminate glides are doubled: ˈʃnuwwa, ˈhiyya written ˈhijja. Inherited /aj aw/ are monophthongised in Tunis and written as long vowels: ˈbiːt 'house', ˈluːn 'colour', ˈjuːm 'day'. Where a vowel-plus-glide sequence survives (morpheme boundaries, loans, verb forms like ˈmʃaːw), write it as V + j/w: ˈmʃaːw, ˈʕajni.

Source: Monophthongisation in Tunis: Singer 1984; Gibson 2009. Sahel retention of diphthongs (Talmoudi 1980; La Rosa 2021) is not followed.

## Other choices

### qāf

ق → q in every word, following urban Tunis: ˈqaːl, ˈqalb. ڨ → ɡ: ˈɡaːl. The Bedouin/rural ɡ for ق is not used. This departs from the note's mention of current ɡ variants.

Source: Singer 1984; Gibson 2009; Turki et al. 2024 (NOTA) on the q/g split. La Rosa 2021 p.9 for the Sahel ɡ variant, which is not followed.

### interdentals and ḍād

ث → θ, ذ → ð, ظ → ðˤ. ض also → ðˤ, since it is merged with ظ in Tunis. Examples: ˈθlɛːθa, ˈhɛːða, ˈðˤaww. Never stops, unlike the Sahel and Jewish dialects.

Source: Gibson 2009; Turki et al. 2024; Singer 1984. Sahel stops in La Rosa 2021 p.9 are not followed.

### imāla of long a

Long /aː/ → ɛː, except when adjacent to an emphatic (tˤ sˤ ðˤ) or to q χ ʁ ħ ʕ r. Examples: ˈbɛːb, ˈktɛːb, but ˈdaːr, ˈtˤaːb, ˈqaːl. Short a is written a in all positions.

Source: Singer 1984; Gibson 2009; La Rosa 2021 p.10.

### short vowels and epenthesis

Short vowels are written phonemically as a, i, u wherever a vowel is pronounced: ˈktib, ˈkassar, ˈmuxx is written ˈmuχχ. Vowels deleted by the dialect's syncope are not restored: ˈktbit, not katabat. ə is reserved for prothetic/epenthetic vowels.

Source: Gibson 2009. Vowel qualities as in Turki et al. 2024 are reduced here to /a i u/ in broad notation; descriptions that write e or ə for short i are not followed.

### ǧīm and loan letters

ج and چ → ʒ: ˈʒaːb, lˈfaʒr. ڢ = ف. ڤ → v, پ → p, گ/ڭ → ɡ. Word-final ڭ in borrowed names is read ɡ per the spec, not ŋ.

Source: Watson 2002 p.16 on ʒ; Turki et al. 2024 on پ ڤ گ; Saadane & Habash 2015 §4.1 p.70 on ڨ.

### emphasis, gemination, hamza

Emphatics are marked with ˤ on the consonant only (tˤ sˤ ðˤ). Emphasis spread to vowels and r is not written. Geminates are written as doubled symbols, not ː: ˈkassar. Hamza is written ʔ only in learned or MSA words where it is pronounced. Native words drop it: ˈana, ˈkla.

Source: Gibson 2009; Singer 1984.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
