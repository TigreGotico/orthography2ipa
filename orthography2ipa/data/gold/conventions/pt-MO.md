# Transcription conventions: Macau Portuguese (pt-MO)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable. Mark it in every lexical word, including monosyllables: mar [ˈmaɾ], pai [ˈpaj], casa [ˈkazɐ], estar [ɨʃˈtaɾ], Macau [mɐˈkaw]. Do not mark secondary stress. Placement is lexical and follows the orthography. A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed syllable: café [kɐˈfɛ], público [ˈpubliku], você [voˈse]. A tilde marks the stressed syllable unless another accent is present: irmã [iɾˈmɐ̃], órfão [ˈɔɾfɐ̃w̃]. Without an accent, words ending in a, e, o (optionally plus s), am, em or ens take penultimate stress: falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], homem [ˈɔmɐ̃j̃]. All other words take final stress: comer [kuˈmeɾ], papel [pɐˈpɛl], aqui [ɐˈki]. Place the mark using maximal legal onsets: obstruent plus l or ɾ stays together, as in outro [ˈotɾu]. s plus consonant splits, as in festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ]. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, no case endings and no geminates; ss and rr are single consonants: passo [ˈpasu], carro [ˈkaʁu]. This follows the European (Lisbon) standard, since Macau Portuguese is reported to follow it.

Source: Mateus, M. H. & E. d'Andrade (2000), The Phonology of Portuguese, OUP, chapter on word stress. Cruz-Ferreira, M. (1995), 'European Portuguese', JIPA 25(2), reprinted in Handbook of the IPA (1999), pp. 126–130. Adopting the European norm follows the flagged secondary source Wikipedia (2024), 'Macanese Portuguese', Phonology.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed: write them with no stress mark and with reduced vowels. Definite articles: o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ]. Monosyllabic prepositions and their contractions: de [dɨ], em [ɐ̃j̃], a [ɐ], à [a], por [puɾ], com [kõ], sem [sɐ̃j̃], do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], num [nũ], ao [aw], pelo [pɨlu], pela [pɨlɐ]. Monosyllabic conjunctions and complementizers: e [i], ou [o], mas [mɐʃ], nem [nɐ̃j̃], que [kɨ], se [sɨ]. The conjunction porque [puɾkɨ] and relative que [kɨ] are also unstressed. Clitic pronouns, hyphenated or free, are unstressed: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [sɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], nos [nuʃ], vos [vuʃ], lhes [ʎɨʃ], o [u], a [ɐ]. Thus dá-me is [ˈda] [mɨ], and in mesoclisis dar-lhe-ei is [ˈdaɾ] [ʎɨ] [ɐj]. The following keep lexical stress and are marked: disyllabic prepositions (para [ˈpɐɾɐ], sobre [ˈsobɾɨ], entre [ˈẽtɾɨ], contra [ˈkõtɾɐ]), indefinite articles (um [ˈũ], uma [ˈumɐ]), strong pronouns (eu [ˈew], ele [ˈelɨ], nós [ˈnɔʃ], mim [ˈmĩ]), possessives and demonstratives (meu [ˈmew], este [ˈeʃtɨ]), negation não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], interrogatives, and all verb forms, including auxiliaries (é [ˈɛ], foi [ˈfoj], tem [ˈtɐ̃j̃], há [ˈa], está [ɨʃˈta]).

Source: Vigário, M. (2003), The Prosodic Word in European Portuguese, Mouton de Gruyter, chapters on clitics and function words. Mateus & d'Andrade (2000) on vowel reduction in unstressed function words.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable as a distinct form: Portuguese has no case endings or nunation, and there is no pausal form at commas or sentence end. Each word before a pause is written in its citation form. Final unstressed vowels are written in full and not deleted: noite [ˈnojtɨ], livro [ˈlivɾu]. Word-final /S/ before a pause or at sentence end is [ʃ]: casas [ˈkazɐʃ]. Word-final /ɾ/ is kept as [ɾ], including in infinitives: comer [kuˈmeɾ]. The L2 infinitive r-drop reported for Macau is not written.

Source: Cruz-Ferreira (1995); Mateus & d'Andrade (2000). The infinitive r-drop is reported only in Wikipedia (2024), 'Macanese Portuguese', and is not adopted.

## Connected speech

Only sibilant voicing sandhi applies across word boundaries. It changes the final segment of the first word, and the words stay separate. Word-final /S/ takes these values: [z] before a vowel-initial word (os amigos [uz] [ɐˈmiɡuʃ]); [ʒ] before a voiced consonant (mais dias [ˈmajʒ] [ˈdiɐʃ]); [ʃ] before a voiceless consonant or a pause (as casas [ɐʃ] [ˈkazɐʃ]). Word-final /ɾ/ stays [ɾ] before a vowel; it is not written as [ʁ]. Word-final /l/ is written [l] everywhere. Do not write vowel elision, crasis or fusion across words: de um is [dɨ] [ˈũ], que eu is [kɨ] [ˈew], and a escola is [ɐ] [ɨʃˈkɔlɐ]. Do not write nasal place assimilation, and do not mark resyllabification or syllable boundaries.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), sections on fricative sandhi. Cruz-Ferreira (1995).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the non-syllabic parts of diphthongs as [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯: pai [ˈpaj], meu [ˈmew], dois [ˈdojʃ], Macau [mɐˈkaw]. In nasal diphthongs, nasalize both parts: mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], muito [ˈmũj̃tu]. Final -em is [ɐ̃j̃] and final unstressed -am is [ɐ̃w̃]. Orthographic ei is [ɐj], following the Lisbon norm: lei [ˈlɐj], primeiro [pɾiˈmɐjɾu]. Orthographic ou is [o]: outro [ˈotɾu]. Rising glides are written only after velars in qu and gu: quatro [ˈkwatɾu], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. Otherwise, sequences of vowel letters are written as hiatus with full vowels: dia [ˈdiɐ], rio [ˈʁiu], piano [piˈɐnu].

Source: Cruz-Ferreira (1995), vowel and diphthong sections (Lisbon [ɐj]). Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), chapter on glides and diphthongs.

## Other choices

### Unstressed vowel reduction

Write European reduction: unstressed a is [ɐ], e is [ɨ], and o is [u]. Examples: falar [fɐˈlaɾ], pequeno [pɨˈkenu], morar [muˈɾaɾ]. Initial unstressed e before s plus consonant is [ɨ]: escola [ɨʃˈkɔlɐ]. Lexical exceptions with unreduced vowels follow the standard dictionary pronunciation. Stressed a before a nasal consonant is [ɐ]: cama [ˈkɐmɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000); Cruz-Ferreira (1995).

### Rhotics

Write strong r as uvular [ʁ] word-initially, for rr, and after n, l or s: rua [ˈʁuɐ], carro [ˈkaʁu], honra [ˈõʁɐ]. Write a tap [ɾ] elsewhere, including codas: caro [ˈkaɾu], mar [ˈmaɾ]. Do not write the Cantonese-influenced non-rhotic L2 variants.

Source: Cruz-Ferreira (1995). The norm is retained per the spec notes. The non-rhotic variants are reported only in Wikipedia (2024) and are not adopted.

### Postalveolar voicing ([ʒ] devoicing)

Keep /ʒ/ voiced, as in the European standard: já [ˈʒa], hoje [ˈoʒɨ], gente [ˈʒẽtɨ]. The reported Macau-specific devoicing of [ʒ] to [ʃ] is not written.

Source: Cruz-Ferreira (1995). The devoicing claim appears only in the uncited Wikipedia (2024) and is not adopted.

### Coda sibilants within words

Write word-internal coda /S/ as [ʃ] before voiceless consonants and [ʒ] before voiced consonants: festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ], mesmo [ˈmeʒmu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade (2000); Cruz-Ferreira (1995).

### Nasal vowels

Write nasal vowels with a tilde and no following nasal consonant before a consonant or word end: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], tempo [ˈtẽpu], fim [ˈfĩ], som [ˈsõ], um [ˈũ].

Source: Cruz-Ferreira (1995); Mateus & d'Andrade (2000).

### Stop and lateral allophones

Write /b d ɡ/ as [b d ɡ] in all positions, not as spirants [β ð ɣ]. Write /l/ as [l] in all positions, without velarized [ɫ]. Write lh as [ʎ] and nh as [ɲ]: filho [ˈfiʎu], vinho [ˈviɲu].

Source: Cruz-Ferreira (1995), which describes the spirant and velarized allophones; they are omitted here as the broad-transcription choice.

### Chinese proper names

Read place and personal names in their Portuguese romanization with Portuguese letter values and Portuguese stress rules: Taipa [ˈtajpɐ], Coloane [kuluˈɐnɨ]. Do not use Cantonese tones or Cantonese segment values.

Source: Convention of this transcription set. No published description covers this; it follows the standard-European norm per Wikipedia (2024).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
