# Transcription conventions: Brasiliense Portuguese (pt-BR-x-brasilia)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and is read off the spelling. (1) A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed syllable: café [kaˈfɛ], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃padɐ], fácil [ˈfasiw], você [voˈse]. (2) With no accent mark, words ending in -a, -e, -o, -am, -em, -ens, or any of these plus -s, stress the penult: casa [ˈkazɐ], cidade [siˈdadʒi], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], homens [ˈõmẽj̃s]. (3) With no accent mark, all other endings stress the final syllable: -i, -u, -l, -r, -z, -x, -im, -um, -om, -ão, -ãe, -õe and the like. Examples: animal [aniˈmaw], falar [faˈlaɾ], feliz [feˈlijs], irmão [iɾˈmɐ̃w̃], aqui [aˈki]. A tilde does not override (2) when another accent is present: órfão [ˈɔɾfɐ̃w̃]. Write ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word of two or more syllables. Write no stress mark on monosyllables. Write no secondary stress. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings. Double rr and ss are single consonants: carro [ˈkaxu], massa [ˈmasɐ]. A long vowel is never written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (word stress); Silva 2002 (stress and accentuation); Brazilian orthographic accent rules.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed and never get a stress mark, even when they have two syllables. Definite articles: o [u], a [a], os [us], as [as]. Simple prepositions: de [dʒi], em [ẽj̃], a [a], com [kõ], sem [sẽj̃], por [poɾ], para [paɾɐ], pra [pɾa]. Preposition-article contractions: do [du], da [da], no [nu], na [na], ao [aw], pelo [pelu], pela [pelɐ], num [nũ], numa [numɐ]. Conjunctions and complementisers: e [i], ou [o], que [ki], se [si], mas [mas], nem [nẽj̃], como (conjunction) [komu]. Relative que [ki]. Atonic clitic pronouns: me [mi], te [tʃi], se [si], lhe [ʎi], nos [nus], vos [vus], o/a/os/as [u a us as], lo/la [lu la], no/na [nu na]. Unstressed function words take the reduced vowels of unstressed final syllables: e→[i], o→[u]. Written a stays [a]. A clitic split off by a hyphen is its own unstressed word: deu-me [ˈdew mi], vê-lo [ˈve lu]. The following are stressed. Indefinite articles: um [ũ], uma [ˈũmɐ], uns [ũs], umas [ˈũmɐs]. Subject and tonic pronouns: eu [ew], ele [ˈeli], você [voˈse], nós [nɔjs], mim [mĩ]. Possessives, demonstratives, interrogatives, porque [poɾˈke], and compound prepositions: sobre [ˈsobɾi], até [aˈtɛ]. All verb forms, including auxiliaries, are stressed: estava [isˈtavɐ], tinha [ˈtʃĩɲɐ], vai [vaj], é [ɛ]. Stressed monosyllables keep full vowels but carry no mark, following the stress section.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (clitics and stress); Callou & Leite 2001 (atonic monosyllables and vowel reduction).

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word before a pause or a comma is written as elsewhere. Final unstressed vowels keep [i u ɐ]. Final s is [s] before a pause: casas [ˈkazɐs].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000; Silva 2002 (no pausal morphology in Portuguese).

## Connected speech

Only sibilant sandhi is written across word boundaries. A word-final s/z/x is [z] before a word beginning with a vowel or a voiced consonant: os olhos [uz ˈɔʎus], as malas [az ˈmalɐs], dez anos [ˈdɛjz ˈɐ̃nus]. It is [s] before a voiceless consonant or a pause: os pés [us pɛjs]. The change is written on the first word only, and each word keeps its own place. Do not write vowel elision, degemination or vowel coalescence: casa amarela [ˈkazɐ amaˈɾɛlɐ], de ouro [dʒi ˈoɾu]. Do not write resyllabification beyond the [z]. Word-final coda r stays [ɾ] before a vowel: falar alto [faˈlaɾ ˈawtu]. Word-final l stays [w] before a vowel: mal amado [maw aˈmadu]. Nasal-final words keep their nasal vowel or nasal diphthong unchanged: bem aqui [bẽj̃ aˈki]. Palatalisation of t d before [i] applies inside each word only, so its trigger is always within the word.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 and 7 (sandhi, sibilant voicing); Callou & Leite 2001 (external sandhi); Silva 2002.

## Glides and diphthongs

Non-syllabic vowels are written [j w], never i̯ u̯. Nasal glides are written [j̃ w̃]. Oral falling diphthongs: pai [paj], lei [lej], boi [boj], céu [sɛw], viu [viw], mau [maw]. Coda l counts as [w]: sal [saw], Brasil [bɾaˈziw]. Nasal diphthongs: mão [mɐ̃w̃], mãe [mɐ̃j̃], põe [põj̃], final -em/-ens [ẽj̃]: também [tɐ̃ˈbẽj̃]. Final -am is [ɐ̃w̃]. Rising sequences after a velar are glide plus vowel: quatro [ˈkwatɾu], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. Unstressed i/u plus vowel in post-tonic position is written with a glide: história [isˈtɔɾjɐ], série [ˈsɛɾji]. In pre-tonic or stressed position, i/u plus vowel is a hiatus with a full vowel: piano [piˈɐ̃nu], saúde [saˈudʒi], país [paˈijs]. ou is written [o]: outro [ˈotɾu], falou [faˈlo]. ei before [ɾ ʃ ʒ] is written [e]: primeiro [pɾiˈmeɾu], beijo [ˈbeʒu], peixe [ˈpeʃi]. Elsewhere ei stays [ej]: leite [ˈlejtʃi]. A stressed vowel before word-final s/z takes an epenthetic [j]: três [tɾejs], nós [nɔjs], luz [lujs], arroz [aˈxojs]. Unstressed mas stays [mas].

Source: Silva 2002 (diphthongs, monophthongisation of ou/ei, glide insertion before /S/); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 (glides as non-syllabic vowels); Callou & Leite 2001.

## Other choices

### Vowel reduction

Word-final unstressed vowels are reduced, including before final -s or -m: e→[i], o→[u], a→[ɐ]. Examples: leite [ˈlejtʃi], livros [ˈlivɾus], casa [ˈkazɐ]. Non-final post-tonic vowels and pre-tonic vowels keep [a e o] unreduced, with no vowel-harmony raising: número [ˈnumeɾu], menino [meˈninu], colégio [koˈlɛʒju]. Stressed e/o are open [ɛ ɔ] or closed [e o] according to the lexical item: café [kaˈfɛ], avô [aˈvo], avó [aˈvɔ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2; Callou & Leite 2001 (pretonic and post-tonic vowels). This follows the SP/MG-leaning standard named in the spec notes. Pre-tonic raising, as in [miˈninu], is variable in Central-West speech and is not written.

### Nasal vowels

Vowels written with a tilde, or before a coda m/n, are nasal: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], lindo [ˈlĩdu], ponte [ˈpõtʃi], um [ũ]. Nasal a is [ɐ̃]. A stressed vowel before m, n or nh in the next syllable is also written nasal, and a becomes [ɐ̃]: cama [ˈkɐ̃mɐ], cena [ˈsẽnɐ], homem [ˈõmẽj̃], uma [ˈũmɐ]. An unstressed vowel before a heterosyllabic nasal stays oral: banana [baˈnɐ̃nɐ], camisa [kaˈmizɐ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 8 (nasality); Silva 2002.

### Palatalisation of t, d

t and d are [tʃ dʒ] before [i], including [i] from final e and epenthetic [i]: tia [ˈtʃiɐ], dia [ˈdʒiɐ], noite [ˈnojtʃi], cidade [siˈdadʒi]. Elsewhere they are [t d]: todo [ˈtodu].

Source: Spec notes (pt-BR base); Silva 2002; Callou & Leite 2001.

### Rhotics

Strong r is dorsal [x]: word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s. Examples: rio [ˈxiu], carro [ˈkaxu], honra [ˈõxɐ]. Intervocalic single r and r in onset clusters are the tap [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], prato [ˈpɾatu]. Coda r, including word-final r, is the tap [ɾ] and is always written, even in infinitives: porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ], falar [faˈlaɾ]. The [h] realisation of strong r, the retroflex/approximant coda r of the Goiás substratum, and the frequent deletion of infinitival -r are not written.

Source: Spec notes (dorsal onset /r/, tap coda /R/); Silva 2002 (rhotic variants); Callou & Leite 2001 (coda r deletion). The written form follows the notes, although published descriptions report a variable retroflex coda r in Central-West speech.

### Coda sibilants and coda l

Coda s/z/x is alveolar: [s] before voiceless consonants and pauses, [z] before voiced consonants. Examples: festa [ˈfɛstɐ], mesmo [ˈmezmu]. It is never palatal [ʃ ʒ]. Coda l is always [w]: alto [ˈawtu], papel [paˈpɛw]. Onset ch/x is [ʃ], j/g before e,i is [ʒ], lh is [ʎ], nh is [ɲ]: chave [ˈʃavi], filho [ˈfiʎu], tinha [ˈtʃĩɲɐ].

Source: Spec notes (alveolar coda /S/, coda /l/→[w]); Silva 2002.

### Epenthesis in consonant clusters

Write an epenthetic [i] after a stop or f that is not followed by a vowel, l or r. The epenthesis never moves the stress. Examples: advogado [adʒivoˈɡadu], ritmo [ˈxitʃimu], pneu [piˈnew], absurdo [abiˈsuɾdu], técnico [ˈtɛkiniku].

Source: Silva 2002 (epenthetic vowel in Brazilian Portuguese); Callou & Leite 2001.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
