# Transcription conventions: Mozambican Portuguese (pt-MZ)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write primary stress as ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: [ˈpaj], [ˈmar], [ˈkaza], [kafeˈ], no; write [kaˈfɛ]. Unstressed function words get no mark (see function_words). Do not write secondary stress. Stress is lexical and follows the orthography, as in European Portuguese. (1) A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed vowel: [ˈmedʒiku] is wrong; write médico [ˈmɛdiku], você [voˈse], avó [aˈvɔ], avô [aˈvo]. (2) Without one, a tilde marks it: irmã [irˈmɐ̃], coração [koɾaˈsɐ̃w̃]. If an acute is also present, the acute wins: órfão [ˈɔrfɐ̃w̃]. (3) Without any accent, words ending in -a, -e, -o, -as, -es, -os, -am, -em, -ens are stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkaza], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. (4) Without any accent, all other words are stressed on the last syllable: these end in -i, -u, -im, -um, -om, an oral diphthong, -r, -l, -z or another consonant. Examples: falar [faˈlar], papel [paˈpɛl], aqui [aˈki]. Long vowels: none; Portuguese has no length contrast, so never write ː. Geminates: none; <rr> and <ss> are single consonants [r] and [s]. Case endings: not applicable; Portuguese has none. Gonçalves describes PM stress as inherited unchanged from European Portuguese; Bantu-substrate pitch effects are unconfirmed and are not written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, The Phonology of Portuguese, ch. 6 (word stress); Cunha & Cintra 1984, Nova Gramática do Português Contemporâneo, ch. 5 (acentuação gráfica). Gonçalves 2010 ch. 2 via Barbosa 2011:203 for PM following the EP model.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed words carry no ˈ. Unstressed definite articles: o [u], a [a], os [us], as [as]. Unstressed prepositions: a [a], de [de], em [ẽj̃], por [por], para [paɾa], com [kõ], sem [sẽj̃]. Unstressed contractions: do [du], da [da], no [nu], na [na], ao [aw], à [a], pelo [pelu], pela [pela], num [nũ], dum [dũ]. Unstressed conjunctions: e [i], ou [o], que [ke], se [se], mas [mas], nem [nẽj̃]. Relative que [ke] is unstressed. Unstressed clitic pronouns, whether proclitic or hyphen-separated enclitic: me [me], te [te], se [se], lhe [ʎe], lhes [ʎes], nos [nus], vos [vus], o [u], a [a], os [us], as [as]. Stressed words carry ˈ: all nouns, verbs including auxiliaries (é [ˈɛ], está [esˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], foi [ˈfoj], vai [ˈvaj]), adjectives and adverbs. Also stressed: não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], sim [ˈsĩ], indefinite articles um [ˈũ] and uma [ˈuma], subject and tonic pronouns (eu [ˈew], ele [ˈele], nós [ˈnɔs], mim [ˈmĩ]), possessives (meu [ˈmew]), demonstratives (este [ˈeste], isso [ˈisu]), interrogatives (quem [ˈkẽj̃], onde [ˈõde]), and polysyllabic prepositions other than para (sobre [ˈsobɾe], entre [ˈẽtɾe]). In unstressed function words, final <e> is [e] and final <o> is [u], following the vowel rules under other.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 6–7 (clitics and prosodic words in Portuguese). Vowel qualities follow the weak-reduction PM norm of Gonçalves 2010 ch. 2 (via Barbosa 2011:203).

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A final vowel is written the same at a pause, a comma or sentence-internally. The one pause effect: a word-final sibilant before a pause or comma is [s] (see connected_speech). Nhatuve reports paragogic [ɨ] after a word-final <r> ([faˈlarɨ]). It is variable, so it is not written at pauses or anywhere else: falar [faˈlar].

Source: Nhatuve 2019:136 (paragoge noted but treated as variable, not transcribed).

## Connected speech

Every word keeps its citation form, with one exception: the voicing of a word-final sibilant (<s>, <z>, and <x> pronounced [s]). That sibilant is [z] when the next word begins with a vowel or a voiced consonant, and [s] before a voiceless consonant, a pause or punctuation. Examples: os amigos [uz aˈmiɡus]; mais nada [ˈmajz ˈnada]; dez casas [ˈdɛs ˈkazas]. It is never palatalised to [ʃ] or [ʒ]. Do not write vowel elision, vowel sandhi or crasis across word boundaries: de outro [de ˈotɾu], not [dˈotɾu]. Do not write resyllabification marks or linking marks; there is no ‿ and no dot across words. Word-final <r> stays [r] before a vowel: falar alto [faˈlar ˈalto] is wrong; write [faˈlar ˈaltu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 3 (external sandhi of /S/); Nhatuve 2019:136 and the spec note for the PM alveolar coda [s]/[z] and for no chiado.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vowels as the consonant symbols [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. Falling diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], seu [ˈsew], dói [ˈdɔj], primeiro [priˈmejɾu]. <ei> is [ej], not Lisbon [ɐj]. <ou> is the monophthong [o]: outro [ˈotɾu]. Nasal diphthongs carry the tilde on both elements: mão [ˈmɐ̃w̃], mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃]. Final <-em>/<-ens> is [ẽj̃], not EP [ɐ̃j̃], following the shared Lusophone-African pattern; this is tentative. Unstressed <i> or <u> before a vowel in the same word is a glide: história [isˈtɔɾja], água [ˈaɡwa], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], série [ˈsɛɾje].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 4 (glides and diphthongs). The PM [ej] and [ẽj̃] follow the African-Lusophone weaker-reduction pattern (Gonçalves 2010 ch. 2 via Barbosa 2011:203), flagged as modelled by analogy.

## Other choices

### unstressed vowels

Use weak reduction, not EP centralisation, and never write [ɨ]. Unstressed <a> is [a]: casa [ˈkaza], falar [faˈlar]. Unstressed <e> is [e] in every position, word-final included: pequeno [peˈkenu], grande [ˈɡɾɐ̃de], estar [esˈtar]. Unstressed <o> is [o] before the stress and [u] word-finally, with or without a following -s: obrigado [obɾiˈɡadu], livros [ˈlivɾus]. Unstressed <i> and <u> stay [i] and [u]. Stressed vowels follow the lexicon and accents: é [ɛ], ê [e], ó [ɔ], ô [o]. Stressed <a> before a nasal consonant is [ɐ]: cama [ˈkɐma]. Nasal vowels: [ɐ̃ ẽ ĩ õ ũ], as in campo [ˈkɐ̃pu] and lindo [ˈlĩdu]. This departs from EP: no [ɨ] and no [ɐ] in unstressed syllables.

Source: Gonçalves 2010 ch. 2 (phonic properties of educated PM), via Barbosa 2011:203. The spec note itself flags that the vowel values are modelled by analogy to Undolo 2014 (Angola).

### rhotics

The strong rhotic is the alveolar trill [r], never uvular [ʁ]. It occurs word-initially, for <rr>, and after a consonant in a different syllable: rio [ˈriu], carro [ˈkaro], honra [ˈõra]. Coda <r>, word-final or before a consonant, is also [r]: mar [ˈmar], porta [ˈpɔrta]. Single intervocalic <r> and <r> after a consonant in the same syllable are the tap [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], três [ˈtɾes]. This keeps the caro/carro contrast of the educated norm. Nhatuve reports a tendency to neutralise everything to [r]; that tendency is not written. Do not write the paragogic vowel.

Source: Nhatuve 2019:136–137 (alveolar multiple vibrant in all strong contexts, tendency to neutralisation). The tap is retained for the educated Maputo norm (Gonçalves 1996; Stroud & Gonçalves 1997).

### coda sibilants

Coda <s>/<z> is alveolar. Within a word it is [s] before a voiceless consonant and [z] before a voiced one: festa [ˈfɛsta], mesmo [ˈmezmu]. Word-finally it follows connected_speech. It is never [ʃ] or [ʒ]. Elsewhere: <ch> [ʃ], <j> and <g> before e/i [ʒ], intervocalic <s> [z], <ç> and <ss> [s].

Source: Spec note feature (3); Nhatuve 2019 (Maputo data).

### coda /l/ and <lh>

Coda <l> is written plain [l], neither velarised [ɫ] nor vocalised [w]: papel [paˈpɛl], Brasil [bɾaˈzil]. This disagrees with the spec's tentative [w], which the primaries do not support. <lh> is [ʎ] with no aspiration mark: filho [ˈfiʎu]. Nhatuve's voiced aspiration is narrow detail. <nh> is [ɲ]: vinho [ˈviɲu].

Source: Nhatuve 2019:137 (aspirated <lh>, not transcribed broadly). The spec note's under-sourced coda-l vocalisation is rejected.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
