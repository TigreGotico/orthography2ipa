# Transcription conventions: A Fala (Xalimego) (fax)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and falls on one of the last three syllables. Write IPA ˈ immediately before the onset of the stressed syllable in every stressed word of two or more syllables. Do not mark stressed monosyllables, and never mark unstressed function words. Place stress by these rules, in order. (1) A written acute or grave accent marks the stressed vowel: Xálima [ˈʃalima], cantórim [kanˈtoɾim]. (2) With no accent, a word ending in a vowel, -s or -n is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkaza], nossa [ˈnosa], queixu [ˈkejʃu], queiju [ˈkejʒu], caça [ˈkaθa]. (3) With no accent, a word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the final syllable: falar [faˈlaɾ], papel [paˈpel]. A diphthong counts as one syllable. A written accent on i or u breaks the diphthong into hiatus: día [ˈdi.a] (syllable dots are not written in the transcription). Long vowels: the lect has none, so length is never written. Geminates: the lect has none, because -LL- and -NN- degeminated. ⟨ss⟩ is single [s] and ⟨rr⟩ is single trill [r], so no doubled consonant symbols are written. Case endings: not applicable; the lect has no case morphology.

Source: Grupo de Estudo d'A Fala (Grupo Xálima) 2019, pp. 37-39 and 53 (accentuation and the degemination diagnostics). Carrasco González 2023 (Ibero-Romance penultimate default). Accent rules as in Alarcos Llorach 1994 for neighbouring Castilian and Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6, for Galician-Portuguese.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed clitics and never get ˈ, even when they have two syllables. Definite articles: u/o, a, us/os, as. Article-preposition contractions: du, da, nu, na, ó/ao, pulu/pola. Simple prepositions: a, de, en, con, pur/por, pa, sin. Polysyllabic prepositions: para [paɾa], entre [entɾe], desde, sobre [sobɾe]. Conjunctions and complementisers: e, ou, ni, que, se, mas, peru/pero [peɾu], porque [poɾke], cumu/como when it is a conjunction. Unstressed object and reflexive pronouns: me, te, se, lhe/li, lu/lo, la, nus, vus, whether proclitic or a hyphen-split enclitic. The relative que. The following keep lexical stress and take ˈ when polysyllabic. Indefinite articles and numerals: unha [ˈuɲa]. Possessives: meu, teu, nossu [ˈnosu]. Demonstratives: esti [ˈesti]. Tonic and subject pronouns: eli [ˈeli], nosoutrus. Interrogatives: cumu? [ˈkumu]. Adverbs. All verb forms, including auxiliaries and copulas: está [esˈta]. Example: para a casa → [paɾa a ˈkaza].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (Portuguese clitics and stress). Alarcos Llorach 1994 (palabras átonas in Castilian). The Galician-Portuguese clitic system of A Fala as described by Grupo Xálima 2019, pp. 37-56 and Carrasco González 2023.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: the lect has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written in the same form at the end of a sentence, at a comma and phrase-internally. Final unstressed vowels are written as spelled at all positions: queixu [ˈkejʃu], casa [ˈkaza]. Variety-specific final raising (-e > -i, -o > -u) is not treated as a pausal process. It is written only where the spelling shows it.

Source: Grupo de Estudo d'A Fala (Grupo Xálima) 2019, pp. 53-55 (final vowels are variety-lexical, not pausal). Carrasco González 2023.

## Connected speech

No cross-word process is written. Each word is transcribed in its citation form, in its own slot. Vowel elision or synaloepha is not written: de oru → [de ˈoɾu]. Resyllabification and linking are not written. Word-final s stays [s] before a vowel or a voiced consonant, with no Portuguese-style liaison [z]: as uvas → [as ˈubas]. Nasal place assimilation across a word boundary is not written: en Valverdi → [en balˈbeɾdi]. Inside a word, coda nasals take the place of the following consonant: cincu [ˈθiŋku], campu [ˈkampu], mañegu-type ⟨nf⟩ → [ɱf] is not written but kept [nf]. Variety overlays listed in the notes are not applied unless the spelling shows them: implosive -r/-l → [l], intervocalic -d- loss, final -z drop.

Source: Citation-form convention of the specification. The word-internal nasal assimilation is the general Ibero-Romance rule described in Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 and Alarcos Llorach 1994. The overlays are documented as variety traits in Grupo Xálima 2019, pp. 54-55.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vowels as consonant symbols [j] and [w], never i̯ or u̯. This applies in falling diphthongs: queixu [ˈkejʃu], queiju [ˈkejʒu], ⟨ou⟩ [ow], ⟨au⟩ [aw]. It applies in rising diphthongs: cuandu [ˈkwandu], ⟨ie⟩ [je]. Unaccented i or u next to another vowel is a glide. A written accent on i or u, or a morpheme boundary in verbs, keeps it syllabic in hiatus: día [ˈdia]. Word-initial and intervocalic ⟨y⟩ and ⟨hi⟩ + vowel are written [j], not [ʝ], in this broad transcription. Glides never take the stress mark themselves; ˈ goes before the whole syllable.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 (Portuguese glides as [j w]). Diphthong inventory of A Fala as in Grupo Xálima 2019, pp. 37-39 and Carrasco González 2023.

## Other choices

### Sibilants and interdentals

Follow the four-way medieval system. Intervocalic ⟨s⟩ is [z]: casa [ˈkaza]. ⟨ss⟩, and ⟨s⟩ in other positions, is [s]: nossa [ˈnosa]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ]: queixu [ˈkejʃu]. ⟨j⟩, and ⟨g⟩ before e or i, is [ʒ]: queiju [ˈkejʒu]. ⟨ç⟩, ⟨c⟩ before e or i, and ⟨z⟩ are all [θ]: cincu [ˈθiŋku], poçu [ˈpoθu]. This keeps the valverdeiru merger of z with [θ] rather than lagarteiru/mañegu [ð̞]. ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ]. There is no seseo or ceceo.

Source: Grupo de Estudo d'A Fala (Grupo Xálima) 2019, p. 38, table p. 53, pp. 54-55.

### Palatal lateral and nasal

⟨lh⟩/⟨ll⟩ is [ʎ] and ⟨nh⟩/⟨ñ⟩ is [ɲ], with no yeísmo: ovelha [oˈbeʎa], unha [ˈuɲa]. The specification lists ovelha among the [ʒ] examples. That is not followed: ⟨lh⟩ is always [ʎ], and [ʒ] is written only for ⟨j⟩ and for ⟨g⟩ before e or i.

Source: Grupo Xálima 2019, pp. 37-39 and 53 (palatal ʎ preserved). Carrasco González 2023.

### Betacism and voiced stops

⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩ are both /b/. Write [b d ɡ] in all positions. Approximant allophones [β ð ɣ] are not written: Valverdi [balˈbeɾdi], fala [ˈfala].

Source: Betacism per the specification notes and Carrasco González 2023. Broad-transcription convention as in Alarcos Llorach 1994.

### Rhotics

Write the trill [r] for ⟨rr⟩, for word-initial ⟨r⟩, and for ⟨r⟩ after n, l or s. Write the tap [ɾ] elsewhere, including in codas: terra [ˈtera], rabu [ˈrabu], falar [faˈlaɾ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2. Alarcos Llorach 1994.

### Mid vowels

Write only the five vowels [i e a o u]. The open-mid [ɛ ɔ] that some descriptions report in individual items are not written, because the contrast is not consistently phonemic across the three varieties. Unstressed vowels are written as spelled, with no Portuguese-style reduction: casa [ˈkaza], not [ˈkazɐ].

Source: Convention of this specification, following the inventory in Grupo Xálima 2019, pp. 37-56. Here it departs from the seven-vowel Galician-Portuguese system of Mateus & d'Andrade 2000.

### Nasal vowels and final nasals

Write a nasal vowel [ã ẽ ĩ õ ũ] only where the spelling marks it with a tilde, which reflects -N- loss. Vowels before a written nasal consonant are not marked nasal. Word-final ⟨n⟩ is written [n], although a velarised [ŋ] is reported.

Source: Grupo Xálima 2019, pp. 37-39 (-N- loss, nasal vowels). Carrasco González 2023.

### ⟨f⟩ and ⟨h⟩

⟨f⟩ is always [f]: fala [ˈfala]. ⟨h⟩ is silent. The lexical Extremaduran aspiration [h] is not written unless the spelling marks it.

Source: Grupo Xálima 2019, pp. 37-39 (F- preserved, as against Sierra de Gata /x-/). Penny 2002 (F- > h in Leonese/Extremaduran).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
