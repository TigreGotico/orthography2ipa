# Transcription conventions: Kabyle (kab)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is not marked. Kabyle has no phonemic long vowels, so no length mark is used for vowels. Geminates (tense consonants) are written with ː and do not affect stress, which is not written anyway. Kabyle has no case endings. The construct state (état d'annexion) is a prefix alternation, written segmentally as spelled, with no stress mark. Examples: argaz [arʝaz], wergaz [wərʝaz], tamurt [θamurθ], tmurt [θmurθ], abrid [aβrið].

Source: Kossmann & Stroomer 1997 (Berber Phonology, pp. 461-475), which treats Berber stress as weak and largely non-distinctive. Some descriptions note a tendency to the final or penultimate syllable but no contrast. This matches spec note (4).

## Stressed and unstressed words

No word carries a stress mark, so particles, prepositions, conjunctions, pronoun clitics and preverbs get no stress or destress mark. Each is written in its full citation form, with the same segmental rules as lexical words, including spirantization of lax stops. Do not reduce or delete their vowels. Examples: d 'and/it is' [ð], ad (future) [að], ur [ur], ara [ara], n 'of' [n], i 'to' [i], s 'with' [s], ɣer [ʁər], ɣef [ʁəf], deg [ðəʝ], seg [səʝ], akked [akːəð]. Hyphenated clitics are written as separate words: yenna-yas [jənːa jas], yusa-d [jusa ð], yeẓra-tt [jəzˤra tː].

Source: Naït-Zerrad 2001 (Grammaire moderne du kabyle) for the inventory and citation forms of particles and clitics. Kossmann & Stroomer 1997 for the absence of distinctive stress.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Kabyle has no case endings or nunation, and has no pausal alternation that a broad transcription writes. Final vowels and consonants keep their forms at commas and at the end of a sentence. Final lax stops remain spirantized, as in tamurt [θamurθ] and abrid [aβrið]. Final devoicing is not written.

Source: Kossmann & Stroomer 1997, whose Berber phonology chapter describes no pausal forms.

## Connected speech

Write every word in its isolation form. Write no cross-word sandhi, assimilation, elision or resyllabification. Spirantization and its blocking apply only inside the word. A word-initial lax stop is spirantized even after a nasal or consonant-final word: n tmurt [n θmurθ], d tamettut [ð θamətːutː], axxam n wergaz [aχːam n wərʝaz]. Do not insert schwas across word boundaries. Published descriptions record obligatory sandhi in normal speech, for example n + w → [bː] (n wergaz ≈ [bːərʝaz]) and d + t → [tː]/[tsː]. These are deliberately not written, following spec known-limit (2), so that every pass makes the same choice.

Source: Spec known-limit (2). The sandhi itself is described in Naït-Zerrad 2001 (phonology/morphophonology sections) and is acknowledged but not followed here.

## Glides and diphthongs

⟨y⟩ = [j] and ⟨w⟩ = [w] in all positions, treated as consonants. Vowel+glide sequences are written as vowel + [j]/[w], never with i̯ u̯ or diphthong symbols. Geminate glides take ː. Examples: yiwen [jiwən], awal [awal], ayen [ajən], ass-ayi [asː aji], tayri [θajri], ayyur [ajːur], iwwet [iwːəθ]. Glides do not spirantize. Do not write the morphological alternations ww→bb or yy→gg unless the spelling has them.

Source: Chaker 1996 (INALCO notation, consonant chart: y, w as semivowel consonants). Kossmann & Stroomer 1997 (semivowels pattern as consonants; i/u vs j/w).

## Other choices

### Spirantization of lax stops

Lax (single-letter) b t d k g ḍ are written [β θ ð ç ʝ ðˤ] in all positions, including word-initially and before i: tilawin [θilawin], deg [ðəʝ], kečč [çətʃː], ḍar [ðˤar]. Geminates are always stops with ː: bb [bː], tt [tː], dd [dː], kk [kː], gg [ɡː], ḍḍ [dˤː]. ṭ is always a stop [tˤ]. q, c, č, ǧ never spirantize.

Source: Kossmann & Stroomer 1997 p.466 §23.5.1 (spirantization culminates in Kabyle; never affects tense consonants). Chaker 1996 (lax stop/spirant pairs).

### Post-consonantal occlusivization (blocking), word-internal only

Follow the Chemini/Aït Mengellat pattern. b is a stop after n and m and written [mb]: zembil [zəmbil], anbaḍ [ambaðˤ]. b stays [β] after r and l. t, d, ḍ are stops after l, n and m: ldd/ld [ld], amdan [amdan], tamenta written with [nt]. They stay [θ ð] after r: tamurt [θamurθ]. k is a stop after any consonant, with nk written [ŋk]: askar [askar], tirkt [θirkθ]. g is a stop after any consonant except m, with ng written [ŋɡ]: argaz [arɡaz]. After m, g is [ʝ] before ə, i or a, and [h] before u (mg+u → [mh]).

Source: Bedar, Quellec & Tifrit 2022 (Chemini Kabyle). The categorical post-consonant k/g blocking follows Mokraoui 2026. Writing [mh] before u follows Bedar et al. and departs from spec known-limit (6), which leaves it unmodelled.

### Vowel quality

Write only the three phonemes [a i u] plus epenthetic [ə], in every context, including next to emphatics and pharyngeals. Do not write the allophones [æ ɑ e o ɪ]: abba [abːa], tirmitin [θirmiθin], aḍu [aðˤu]. Write [ə] exactly where the orthography has ⟨e⟩. Never add an unspelled schwa, and never split a geminate. This departs from the spec's illustrative [æβrið], [æbːæ] and [θɪrmiθin], which mix in allophonic detail.

Source: Kossmann & Stroomer 1997 p.463 §23.4.1-23.4.2. Wang 2020 (schwa epenthetic; geminates never broken).

### Pharyngealization (emphasis) spread

Write ˤ only on the emphatic letter itself: ṛ [rˤ], ṣ [sˤ], ẓ [zˤ], ṭ [tˤ], ḍ [ðˤ]/[dˤː]. Do not mark the spreading of emphasis to neighbouring vowels or consonants: aẓru [azˤru], ṭṭes [tˤːəs].

Source: Kossmann & Stroomer 1997 p.464 §23.4.3.1 (pharyngealization spreads automatically, so it is predictable and omitted in broad transcription).

### Realization of ɣ, x and other back consonants

ɣ = [ʁ], x = [χ], q = [q], ḥ = [ħ], ɛ = [ʕ], h = [h]. Geminates take ː: xx [χː], qq [qː]. Examples: aɣrum [aʁrum], axxam [aχːam], ɛlaya [ʕlaja].

Source: Spec grapheme table (uvular [ʁ] as the Kabyle norm). Karaoui, Djeradi & Djeradi 2024 (Kabyle fricative inventory).

### Tense tt/zz affrication

Write tt as [tː] and zz as [zː] throughout, including the intensive prefix tt- and the clitic -tt: ttawi [tːawi]. The affricate realizations [tsː]/[dzː] found in many Kabyle varieties are not written, because they are lexically and dialectally variable.

Source: Spec grapheme table (tt = /tː/). Naït-Zerrad 2001 notes the affricate realizations, which are not followed here.

### Labiovelarization

Not written. Write k g as [ç ʝ] or [k ɡ] per the rules above, never [kʷ ɡʷ ʝʷ].

Source: Spec note (standard orthography leaves labiovelarization unwritten). Karaoui et al. 2024 list it as a Kabyle feature.

### Loanwords and the letters p, v, o

Apply spirantization categorically to every word in Kabyle orthography, including integrated Arabic and French loans. Only words spelled with ⟨p⟩, ⟨v⟩ or ⟨o⟩, and foreign proper names, keep their stops, with p = [p] and v = [v]. Never write [v] for native ⟨b⟩: abrid [aβrið], not [avrið].

Source: Spec known-limit (5) and its 'v question' note. Kossmann & Stroomer 1997 p.466.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
