# Transcription conventions: Carioca Portuguese (pt-BR-x-rj)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and is read off the spelling. A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed vowel: café [kaˈfɛ], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃padɐ], você [voˈse]. With no accent mark, the stress falls on the penult when the word ends in -a, -e or -o (optionally plus -s), or in -em, -ens or -am: casa [ˈkazɐ], cidades [siˈdadʒiʃ], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. Otherwise it falls on the final syllable, as in words ending in -r, -l, -z, -x, -i(s), -u(s), -im, -um, -ão or -ões: falar [faˈla], Brasil [bɾaˈziw], aqui [aˈki], irmão [ixˈmɐ̃w̃]. Write ˈ before the stressed syllable of every content word, including monosyllables: mar [ˈmah], pé [ˈpɛ]. Do not mark secondary stress. The stressed /a/ before a nasal consonant is nasalised [ɐ̃]: cama [ˈkɐ̃mɐ]. Final unstressed vowels are [ɐ], [i] and [u]: noite [ˈnojtʃi], lado [ˈladu]. Pretonic e and o are [e] and [o], except that initial unstressed es-, ex-, en- and em- are [i] or [ĩ]: escola [iʃˈkɔlɐ], ensino [ĩˈsinu]. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings. Doubled rr and ss are single segments: carro [ˈkaxu], passo [ˈpasu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (word stress); Callou & Leite 2001 (vowel system and unstressed vowel reduction); final [ɐ] after Barbosa & Albano 2004 (JIPA illustration of Brazilian Portuguese).

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words are unstressed and written without ˈ:
- definite articles: o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ];
- the preposition a and contractions: ao [aw], à [a], do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], pelo [pelu], pela [pelɐ], num [nũ];
- simple prepositions: de [dʒi], em [ẽj̃], com [kõ], por [pox] (or [poh] or [poɾ] by the coda rules), para [paɾɐ], sem [sẽj̃];
- written pra [pɾɐ] and pro [pɾu];
- conjunctions and complementisers: e [i], ou [ow], que [ki], se [si], mas [maʃ], nem [nẽj̃];
- clitic pronouns: me [mi], te [tʃi], se [si], lhe [ʎi], nos [nuʃ], o [u], a [ɐ].
These words take ˈ:
- indefinite articles um [ˈũ] and uma [ˈumɐ];
- subject and prepositional pronouns: eu [ˈew], ele [ˈeli], você [voˈse], nós [ˈnɔʃ], mim [ˈmĩ];
- possessives, demonstratives and the negator não [ˈnɐ̃w̃];
- question words and adverbs;
- all verbs, including auxiliaries and copulas: é [ˈɛ], está [iʃˈta], tá [ˈta], tem [ˈtẽj̃], foi [ˈfoj], vai [ˈvaj].
Transcribe reduced forms only when the spelling has them (pra, tá, cê). Do not reduce para to [pɾɐ] or está to [ˈta] when the full form is written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (clitics and the prosodic word); Callou & Leite 2001 (atonic monosyllables); Silva 2002 (reduced vowels of clitics).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word before a pause (sentence end, comma or other punctuation) takes the same form it takes before a voiceless consonant. Coda /S/ is [ʃ]: paz [ˈpaʃ], dois [ˈdojʃ]. Word-final /R/ follows the rule under other.

Source: Not applicable to Portuguese; pre-pausal coda behaviour per Callou 2010:135-138.

## Connected speech

Write each word separately and do not elide vowels across word boundaries. Vowel sandhi such as casa amarela is not written: [ˈkazɐ amaˈɾɛlɐ]. Only two cross-boundary processes are written, and each changes only the final segment of the first word.
(1) Word-final /S/ before a vowel-initial word is [z]: os amigos [uz aˈmiɡuʃ], mais alto [ˈmajz ˈawtu]. Before a voiced consonant it is [ʒ]: mais de [ˈmajʒ dʒi], os bancos [uʒ ˈbɐ̃kuʃ], mesmo [ˈmeʒmu]. Before a voiceless consonant (including strong-r [x]) or a pause it is [ʃ]: os carros [uʃ ˈkaxuʃ], festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ]. Write this categorically, although Callou's figures are variable (91% word-internal, 76% word-final).
(2) Word-final /R/ before a vowel-initial word, with no punctuation between, is a tap [ɾ], for verbs and non-verbs alike: mar aberto [ˈmaɾ aˈbɛxtu], falar isso [faˈlaɾ ˈisu]. The /t,d/ palatalisation before [i] applies inside the word only: de [dʒi], noite [ˈnojtʃi]. Final l stays [w] before vowels: Brasil inteiro [bɾaˈziw ĩˈtejɾu].

Source: Callou 2010:135 (chiado carioca), 137-138 (coda /R/); Callou & Leite 2001 (sibilant voicing assimilation and resyllabification of /R/ before vowels); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 4 (coda /S/ and /R/).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as the consonant symbols [j] and [w], never i̯ or u̯.
- Oral diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], dois [ˈdojʃ], meu [ˈmew].
- Nasal diphthongs carry tilde on both members: mão [ˈmɐ̃w̃], bem [ˈbẽj̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃].
- Coda /l/ is vocalised [w]: alto [ˈawtu], Brasil [bɾaˈziw].
- Unstressed i or e before a vowel after the stress is [j]: história [iʃˈtɔɾjɐ], série [ˈsɛɾji].
- Stressed i or u before or after a vowel is a hiatus, with no glide: rio [ˈxiu], saúde [saˈudʒi].
- Spelled ou is [ow]: pouco [ˈpowku].
- Spelled ei is [ej]: primeiro [pɾiˈmejɾu].
- Do not insert an epenthetic glide before final /S/: paz [ˈpaʃ], três [ˈtɾeʃ], nós [ˈnɔʃ].

Source: Silva 2002 (diphthongs and glide status); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 (glides as non-nuclear vowels); Callou & Leite 2001.

## Other choices

### rhotics

Strong /r/ (word-initial, rr, and after n, l or s) is [x]: rio [ˈxiu], carro [ˈkaxu], honra [ˈõxɐ]. The notes give [χ~x]; use [x]. Intervocalic and cluster r is [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], prato [ˈpɾatu]. Non-final coda /R/ is [x] in every context: porta [ˈpɔxtɐ], carne [ˈkaxni]. Word-final /R/ before a consonant or pause is deleted in verbs (infinitives and future subjunctives): falar [faˈla], comer [koˈme]. In other words it is [h]: mar [ˈmah], amor [aˈmoh], por [poh]. Before a vowel it is [ɾ] (see connected_speech).

Source: Callou 2010:137-138, after Callou 1987 and Callou, Moraes & Leite 1996 (deletion favoured in verbs, aspiration elsewhere).

### palatalisation and nasal vowels

/t,d/ are [tʃ,dʒ] before every [i], including [i] from final unstressed e: tia [ˈtʃiɐ], cidade [siˈdadʒi], antes [ˈɐ̃tʃiʃ]. Vowels before a coda nasal are written as nasal vowels with no nasal consonant: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], tempo [ˈtẽpu], fundo [ˈfũdu]. Nh is [ɲ] and lh is [ʎ].

Source: Callou & Leite 2001; Cardoso et al. 2014 (ALiB palatalisation isogloss: categorical in Rio).

### yod epenthesis before final /S/

Write no epenthetic glide: mas [maʃ], vez [ˈveʃ]. Descriptions report variable [ˈpajʃ]-type forms in Rio; we follow the specification note, which writes paz [ˈpaʃ].

Source: Specification note following Callou 2010:135; the variable epenthesis is discussed in Callou & Leite 2001.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
