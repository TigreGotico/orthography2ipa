# Transcription conventions: Lisbon Portuguese (pt-PT-x-lisbon)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Write one primary stress mark ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included: seis [ˈsɐjʃ], pai [ˈpaj], casa [ˈkazɐ], café [kɐˈfɛ]. Onset clusters (obstruent + ɾ/l) stay together after the mark: livro [ˈlivɾu], outra [ˈotɾɐ]. A coda sibilant stays before the mark: espelho [ɨʃˈpɐʎu]. Placement follows the written word. A written accent (´ ^ `) marks the stressed syllable: árvore [ˈaɾvuɾɨ], você [vuˈse]. Without an accent, stress is penultimate if the word ends in -a -e -o (+s), -am, -em or -ens: falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], homem [ˈɔmɐ̃j̃], fala [ˈfalɐ]. Otherwise stress is final, on words ending in -i -u -l -r -z -ão -ã -im -om -um, a diphthong or a consonant: falar [fɐˈlaɾ], papel [pɐˈpɛɫ], irmão [iɾˈmɐ̃w̃]. Mark no secondary stress. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Stressed open vs close mid vowels (ɛ/e, ɔ/o) are lexical and follow the standard dictionary: pé [ˈpɛ], medo [ˈmedu], porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ], todo [ˈtodu]. The Lisbon deltas apply under stress: e/ɛ before a following onset palatal is [ɐ] (coelho [kuˈɐʎu], tenho [ˈtɐɲu], fecho [ˈfɐʃu], igreja [iˈɡɾɐʒɐ]). Stressed a before a nasal consonant is [ɐ]: cama [ˈkɐmɐ]. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Portuguese has no vowel length, no geminates (rr and ss are single consonants: carro [ˈkaʁu], passo [ˈpasu]) and no case endings.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (stress chapter; p.19 for stressed [ɐ]); Cunha & Cintra 1984 (accentuation chapter); Hristovsky 2008:239 for the pre-palatal [ɐ].

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed (no ˈ, reduced vowels) are the following. Definite articles: o a os as [u ɐ uʃ ɐʃ]. Clitic pronouns: me te se lhe nos vos lhes o a lo la no na [mɨ tɨ sɨ ʎɨ nuʃ vuʃ ʎɨʃ u ɐ lu lɐ nu nɐ]. Monosyllabic prepositions: de [dɨ], em [ɐ̃j̃], a [ɐ], por [puɾ], com [kõ], sem [sɐ̃j̃]. Contractions: do da dos das [du dɐ duʃ dɐʃ], no na [nu nɐ], ao [aw], à [a], pelo pela [pɨlu pɨlɐ], num [nũ]. The preposition para is unstressed [pɐɾɐ]. Conjunctions and relatives: e [i], que [kɨ], se [sɨ], mas [mɐʃ], ou [o], nem [nɐ̃j̃]. Stressed (marked ˈ) are the following. Indefinite articles: um uma [ˈũ ˈumɐ]. Subject and prepositional pronouns: eu [ˈew], tu [ˈtu], ele [ˈelɨ], ela [ˈɛlɐ], nós [ˈnɔʃ], mim [ˈmĩ], você [vuˈse]. Also stressed are possessives (meu [ˈmew]), demonstratives (este [ˈeʃtɨ], isso [ˈisu]), numerals, the negation não [ˈnɐ̃w̃], and polysyllabic prepositions and conjunctions other than para: sobre [ˈsobɾɨ], entre [ˈẽtɾɨ], como [ˈkomu], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], porque [ˈpuɾkɨ]. All verbs carry stress, auxiliaries included: é [ˈɛ], está [ɨʃˈta], tem [ˈtɐ̃j̃], há [ˈa], vai [ˈvaj], foi [ˈfoj]. A hyphenated clitic is its own unstressed word: dá-me [ˈda mɨ], amo-te [ˈɐmu tɨ], fazê-lo [fɐˈze lu]. In a mesoclitic verb, the parts are separate words: dar-lhe-ei [ˈdaɾ ʎɨ ˈɐj].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (clitics and prosodic word); Cunha & Cintra 1984 (pronouns, clitic placement, contractions).

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word is written identically at a comma, at sentence end and sentence-internally, except the word-final sibilant, which is [ʃ] before a pause (see connected_speech). Final unstressed vowels keep their reduced quality [ɐ ɨ u]. Their phonetic devoicing or deletion before a pause is not written.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (unstressed vowels); Cruz-Ferreira 1995:92.

## Connected speech

Only one cross-word process is written: word-final sibilant sandhi (orthographic -s, -z, -x). Before a vowel-initial word it is [z]: os olhos [uz ˈɔʎuʃ], mais alto [ˈmajz ˈaɫtu], dez anos [ˈdɛz ˈɐnuʃ]. Before a voiced-consonant-initial word it is [ʒ]: os dias [uʒ ˈdiɐʃ], mais nada [ˈmajʒ ˈnadɐ]. Before a voiceless consonant or a pause it is [ʃ]: os pés [uʃ ˈpɛʃ]. The sandhi value is written at the end of the first word, and the next word is unchanged. Two things are not written. First, vowel elision, crasis or glide formation across words: de amor stays [dɨ ɐˈmoɾ], o amigo stays [u ɐˈmiɡu], que eu stays [kɨ ˈew]. Second, resyllabification (no syllable dots). Word-final r stays [ɾ] before a vowel: falar alto [fɐˈlaɾ ˈaɫtu]. Word-final l stays [ɫ] in all contexts: mal assim [ˈmaɫ ɐˈsĩ]. Orthographic contractions (do, na, ao, à, pelo) are single words and are written as such.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (sibilant sandhi in the chapter on consonants and connected speech); Cruz-Ferreira 1995:92; native-speaker corroboration of prevocalic [z] in Portuguese With Leo 2021.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic glides as [j] and [w], never i̯ u̯. In nasal diphthongs, both elements carry the tilde: mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], bem [ˈbɐ̃j̃], pão [ˈpɐ̃w̃], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], põe [ˈpõj̃], muito [ˈmũj̃tu]. Falling diphthongs: ai [aj] (pai [ˈpaj]), au [aw] (mau [ˈmaw]), oi [oj] (foi [ˈfoj]), ói [ɔj], ui [uj], eu [ew], éu [ɛw], iu [iw]. Following Lisbon, ei is [ɐj] in every position: leite [ˈlɐjtɨ], primeiro [pɾiˈmɐjɾu], deixar [dɐjˈʃaɾ], lei [ˈlɐj]. Following Lisbon, ou is the monophthong [o]: ouro [ˈoɾu], outro [ˈotɾu], sou [ˈso]. Rising sequences after k/ɡ are written with [w]: quatro [ˈkwatɾu], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. Other vowel+vowel sequences are hiatus, not glides: dia [ˈdiɐ], rua [ˈʁuɐ], coelho [kuˈɐʎu], país [pɐˈiʃ].

Source: Cintra 1971 and Segura 2013 for [ɐj] and ou→[o] in the Centro-Litoral/Estremenho zone; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (diphthongs; lei [ˈlɐj] p.19).

## Other choices

### Stressed e before a palatal (Lisbon delta)

Stressed e/ɛ is [ɐ] when the next syllable's onset is ʎ ɲ ʃ ʒ (spelled lh nh ch x j g+e/i): espelho [ɨʃˈpɐʎu], vermelho [vɨɾˈmɐʎu], venho [ˈvɐɲu], vejo [ˈvɐʒu], cerveja [sɨɾˈvɐʒɐ]. It does not apply before a coda sibilant: mesmo [ˈmeʒmu], festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ]. It does not apply to unstressed e, which is [ɨ]: melhor [mɨˈʎɔɾ], fechar [fɨˈʃaɾ]. The conservative Coimbra-type norm keeps [e]/[ɛ] here; this convention follows Lisbon.

Source: Hristovsky 2008:239 (surveying Gonçalves Viana 1883, Cintra 1971, Barros 1994); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000:19.

### Unstressed vowel reduction

Unstressed a is [ɐ], e is [ɨ] and o is [u]: casa [ˈkazɐ], pequeno [pɨˈkenu], tomate [tuˈmatɨ]. Exceptions: (1) the conjunction e is [i]. (2) Word-initial unstressed e is [i] (elefante [ilɨˈfɐ̃tɨ]), and ex- before a vowel is [iz] (exame [iˈzɐmɨ]). Word-initial es-, ex- or des- before a consonant is [ɨʃ]/[dɨʃ] (estar [ɨʃˈtaɾ], explicar [ɨʃpliˈkaɾ]). (3) Unstressed nasal vowels do not reduce: entrar [ẽˈtɾaɾ]. Variable deletion of unstressed [ɨ] and [i] is never written: Filipa [fiˈlipɐ], telefone [tɨlɨˈfɔnɨ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (unstressed vowel system); Cruz-Ferreira 1995.

### Rhotics

Write [ʁ] for word-initial r, for rr, and for r after n, l or s: rato [ˈʁatu], carro [ˈkaʁu], honra [ˈõʁɐ]. Write the tap [ɾ] elsewhere, including the coda: caro [ˈkaɾu], mar [ˈmaɾ], porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ]. Never write [r].

Source: Cruz-Ferreira 1995:91; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (rhotics).

### Voiced stops and laterals

Broad transcription writes b d ɡ in all positions, with no spirants: abelha [ɐˈbɐʎɐ], cada [ˈkadɐ]. This departs from the specification note's [β] example, as spirantisation is narrow. Write onset l as [l] and coda l as [ɫ]: lado [ˈladu], mal [ˈmaɫ], alto [ˈaɫtu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000; Cruz-Ferreira 1995:91-92.

### Nasal vowels

Write a vowel before a coda nasal letter (m/n + consonant) as a nasal vowel with no nasal consonant: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], tempo [ˈtẽpu], ponte [ˈpõtɨ], mundo [ˈmũdu], fim [ˈfĩ]. Final -em/-ens is [ɐ̃j̃]/[ɐ̃j̃ʃ]: tem [ˈtɐ̃j̃], homens [ˈɔmɐ̃j̃ʃ]. Final unstressed -am is [ɐ̃w̃].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 (nasal vowels and diphthongs); Cruz-Ferreira 1995.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
