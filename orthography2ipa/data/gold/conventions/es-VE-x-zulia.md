# Transcription conventions: Maracucho (Zuliano) Spanish (es-VE-x-zulia)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is lexical and follows the standard Spanish orthographic rules. A written acute marks the stressed vowel. Without an acute, a word ending in a vowel, n or s is stressed on the penult, and any other word on the final syllable. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every stressed word, including stressed monosyllables: casa [ˈkasa], ciudad [sjuˈða], árbol [ˈaɾβol], sol [ˈsol], vos [ˈboh]. The syllable that takes ˈ is the one stressed in pronunciation, not the one spelled with the accent. Voseo verb forms take stress where the accent is written: tenéis [teˈnei̯h], estáis [ehˈtai̯h], sabés [saˈβeh]. Adverbs in -mente carry two stresses, written ˌ on the adjective and ˈ on -mente: rápidamente [ˌrapiðaˈmente]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so those points do not apply.

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 15 (stress); Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, stress chapter. Maracucho stress placement does not differ from general Spanish (Lipski 1994, ch. on Venezuela).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed words, written with no ˈ: definite articles el la lo los las [el la lo loh lah]; all simple prepositions except según (a de en con por para sin sobre entre hasta desde [a ðe en kon poɾ ˈpaɾa]). Note that para keeps its ˈ only if it is read as a polysyllabic preposition; it is normally unstressed, so write [paɾa]. Also unstressed: conjunctions y e o u ni que pero si porque como (non-interrogative) [i e o u ni ke peɾo si poɾke komo]; object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos los las les [me te se lo la le noh]; possessive determiners mi tu su mis tus sus [mi tu su mih]; and relatives que quien donde cuando. Stressed words, written with ˈ: nouns, adjectives, lexical verbs, and auxiliaries including all forms of haber and ser (ha [ˈa], he [ˈe], es [ˈeh]); adverbs; subject and prepositional pronouns yo tú vos él ella usted nosotros (vos [ˈboh], conmigo [koŋˈmiɣo]); interrogatives and exclamatives with an acute (qué [ˈke], dónde [ˈdonde]); demonstratives; numerals; indefinite un una unos [ˈuŋ ˈuna]; and según [seˈɣuŋ].

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, §15 (stressed vs. unstressed words); Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1967), Manual de pronunciación española, ch. on palabras inacentuadas.

## Pausal forms

Spanish has no pausal forms in the Arabic sense: no case endings, no nunation (tanwīn) and no dropping of final short vowels, so that part does not apply. Final vowels are always written in full. The only pause-conditioned rule concerns final nasals: a word-final /n/ before a pause (sentence end or comma) is written velar [ŋ], as it is before a vowel: pan [ˈpaŋ], también [tamˈbjeŋ]. Word-final /s/ is [h] at a pause as everywhere in the coda: más [ˈmah]. It is not deleted.

Source: Lipski, J. M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Venezuela (velarized final /n/, aspirated /s/ in the Caribbean and Zulian zone); Hualde (2005), §§ on nasal assimilation.

## Connected speech

Each word keeps its own place, and only the segments at a boundary change. (1) Nasal place assimilation across the boundary: final /n/ is written [m] before p b β m (un poco [um ˈpoko], en Maracaibo [em maɾaˈkai̯βo]); [ŋ] before k ɡ ɣ h w, before a vowel and at pause (con gusto [koŋ ˈɡuhto], un amigo [uŋ aˈmiɣo]); and [n] before all other consonants (en todo [en ˈtoðo]). (2) Spirantization: /b d ɡ/ are written as stops [b d ɡ] after a pause or a nasal, and /d/ also after l. Elsewhere, including after the final segment of the preceding word, they are [β ð ɣ]: la boca [la ˈβoka], el dedo [el ˈdeðo], con dos [kon ˈdoh], los gatos [loh ˈɣatoh]. (3) Word-initial r is always the trill [r]: la rosa [la ˈrosa]. (4) Final /s/ stays [h] before a vowel; it is not resyllabified as [s]: los amigos [loh aˈmiɣoh]. (5) There is no synalepha, glide formation or vowel elision across word boundaries: mi amigo [mi aˈmiɣo], la otra [la ˈotɾa]. Resyllabification is not marked, since no syllable dots are used.

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chs. on approximants, nasal assimilation and synalepha; Lipski, J. M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Venezuela, for treating prevocalic final /s/ as aspirated in the Caribbean zone.

## Glides and diphthongs

Prenuclear glides are written [j w]: bien [ˈbjeŋ], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [sjuˈða]. Postnuclear glides are written [i̯ u̯]: rey [ˈrei̯], aire [ˈai̯ɾe], aula [ˈau̯la], tenéis [teˈnei̯h]. An unstressed i or u next to another vowel in the same word is a glide. An acute on i or u marks hiatus, and both vowels are written full: país [paˈih], día [ˈdia]. Sequences of non-high vowels stay in hiatus within the word, even though casual Caribbean speech often reduces them: teatro [teˈatɾo], poeta [poˈeta]. Orthographic y and ll are the consonant [ʝ]: calle [ˈkaʝe], yo [ˈʝo]. Word-final y is a postnuclear glide: hoy [ˈoi̯]. The conjunction y is [i]. Glides are not formed across word boundaries.

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on glides and diphthongs; Quilis (1993) for diphthong vs. hiatus. Yeísmo /ʝ/ follows the specification note (Lipski 1994).

## Other choices

### Coda /s/

Seseo applies: c(e,i), z and s are all /s/. In the syllable coda, word-internally and word-finally, /s/ is written [h] and is never deleted: este [ˈehte], mosca [ˈmohka], vos [ˈboh], las casas [lah ˈkasah]. In onset position it is [s]: casa [ˈkasa]. This goes beyond the stub note, which models only seseo, but follows published descriptions of Zulian and Venezuelan Caribbean-zone Spanish.

Source: Lipski, J. M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Venezuela; Lipski (2011), in Hualde, Olarrea & O'Rourke (eds.), Handbook of Hispanic Linguistics, pp. 1-26 (Caribbean zone).

### Velar fricative

j and g before e or i are written glottal [h], as in the Venezuelan Caribbean norm, not velar [x]: gente [ˈhente], ojo [ˈoho], México [ˈmehiko]. Orthographic h is silent: hacer [aˈseɾ].

Source: Lipski, J. M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Venezuela.

### Word-final /d/

Word-final d is not written: verdad [beɾˈða], usted [uhˈte], Madrid [maˈðɾi]. Intervocalic d inside the word, including -ado, is kept as [ð]: cansado [kanˈsaðo].

Source: Lipski, J. M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Venezuela; Hualde (2005), ch. on /d/.

### Rhotics

The trill [r] is written for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l or s: perro [ˈpero], honra [ˈonra]. Elsewhere, including the coda, r is the tap [ɾ]: pero [ˈpeɾo], comer [koˈmeɾ], puerta [ˈpweɾta]. Coda r is not lateralized or deleted, and coda l stays [l].

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, ch. on rhotics. The non-neutralizing choice follows the educated Maracaibo norm (Lipski 1994, ch. on Venezuela).

### Other consonant letters

ch is the affricate [tʃ], which Zulian Spanish does not deaffricate: noche [ˈnotʃe]. b and v are both /b/, with the spirantization rule above: vaca [ˈbaka], uva [ˈuβa]. Intervocalic x is [ks]: taxi [ˈtaksi]. Preconsonantal x is [h], following the coda-s rule: extraño [ehˈtɾaɲo]. ñ is [ɲ]. Broad transcription writes no dental or labiodental nasal diacritics.

Source: Lipski, J. M. (1994), Latin American Spanish, ch. on Venezuela; Hualde (2005).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
