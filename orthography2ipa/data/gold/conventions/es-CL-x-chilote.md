# Transcription conventions: Chilote Spanish (es-CL-x-chilote)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable of every stressed word, monosyllables included. Place the mark before the whole onset of that syllable, which is syllabified inside the word only, and write no syllable dots. Placement follows standard Spanish orthographic stress. A written accent marks the stressed vowel: café kaˈfe, árbol ˈaɾbol, música ˈmusika. Without an accent, words ending in a vowel, n or s stress the penult: casa ˈkasa, hablan ˈablan, lunes ˈlunes. Other words stress the final syllable: ciudad sjuˈdad, comer koˈmeɾ. When the stressed vowel is a glide-adjacent vowel, the mark goes before the onset cluster: bueno ˈbweno, tierra ˈtjera. Adverbs in -mente keep both stresses, with secondary ˌ on the adjective and primary on -men-: rápidamente ˌrapidaˈmente. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so these do not apply. A doubled spelled vowel is written as two vowels: leer leˈeɾ, creer kɾeˈeɾ.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on word stress; Quilis, A. (1993) Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, stress chapter; RAE/ASALE (2011) Fonética y fonología, ch. on accent.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed and carry no ˈ: definite articles el la lo los las (el ˈpero); prenominal possessives mi tu su nuestro… (mi ˈkasa, nwestɾo ˈpadɾe); object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos os los las les; all prepositions except según (de, a, en, con, por, paɾa, sin, desde, asta; según is seˈɣun… written seˈgun); conjunctions y i, o, pero, ni, que, si, porque, como as conjunction; relative que, quien, donde, cuando (unaccented spellings); and the treatment word don/doña before a name. These carry ˈ: the indefinite article un una unos unas (ˈun ˈomβɾe… written ˈun ˈombɾe); subject and prepositional pronouns yo tú él ella usted nosotros mí ti (ˈʝo, ˈel, usˈted); stressed possessives mío tuyo suyo (ˈmio); demonstratives (ˈeste, aˈkel); interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent (ˈke, ˈkjen, ˈdonde); numerals; negative no (ˈno); all verbs, including the auxiliaries haber, ser and estar (ˈa ˈditʃo, esˈtaba koˈmjendo); and adverbs. The same word gets the same treatment in every row according to this list.

Source: Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1977) Manual de pronunciación española §§ on palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas; Quilis 1993, inventory of unstressed words; Hualde 2005, chapter on stress (function words).

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. Every word is written in the same form at a comma, at the end of the sentence and inside a phrase: casas ˈkasas everywhere. No pause-conditioned weakening or loss of final /s/ is written either (see other: coda /s/).

Source: Hualde 2005 (no pausal morphology in Spanish); Quilis 1993.

## Connected speech

Write no cross-word processes. Each word appears in its citation form, in its own place. The following are not written: resyllabification (los otros stays los ˈotɾos); synalepha or vowel merger across words (la escuela stays la esˈkwela; de el stays de el); nasal place assimilation (un poco stays ˈun ˈpoko, not ˈum); voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants (las manos stays las ˈmanos); and post-vocalic spirantization of /b d ɡ/ (la boca stays la ˈboka). These processes are regular and predictable in Chilean and Chilote speech, so a broad transcription leaves them to the reader. Spelled contractions al and del are single words written al, del.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on syllabification across word boundaries, nasal assimilation and spirantization (processes described, here left untranscribed as allophonic); Quilis 1993.

## Glides and diphthongs

Prevocalic (rising) non-syllabic high vowels are written j and w: tierra ˈtjera, bueno ˈbweno, ciudad sjuˈdad, cuatro ˈkwatɾo. Postvocalic (falling) ones are written i̯ and u̯: aire ˈai̯ɾe, peine ˈpei̯ne, causa ˈkau̯sa, hoy ˈoi̯, muy ˈmui̯, rey ˈrei̯. Word-final y after a vowel is i̯. The conjunction y is i. The consonant spelled y, ll or hi- before a vowel is the obstruent ʝ, not j: yo ˈʝo, calle ˈkaʝe, hielo ˈʝelo. hu- before a vowel is w: huevo ˈwebo. An accented high vowel is in hiatus and is written as a full vowel: día ˈdia, país paˈis, río ˈrio. Unaccented sequences of a high vowel and a vowel are diphthongs, written with glides (piano ˈpjano), even though some speakers have hiatus in a few words.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on glides and diphthongs and on the palatal obstruent; Quilis 1993; RAE/ASALE 2011 on diphthong/hiatus.

## Other choices

### seseo and yeísmo (spec baseline)

c before e/i and z are written s: cielo ˈsjelo, zapato saˈpato. ll and y are written ʝ: llave ˈʝabe. No θ and no ʎ are written. Some older dialectology reports lleísmo remnants in parts of Chiloé, but the standard modern description is yeísta. This agrees with the spec note.

Source: Lipski 1994, chapter on Chile; Lipski 2011 (overview); Oroz, R. (1966) La lengua castellana en Chile.

### coda /s/

Write /s/ in every coda: estos ˈestos, las casas las ˈkasas. Chilean and Chilote speech regularly aspirates or drops coda /s/ ([ˈehtoh]), but this is variable and allophonic and is not written. The spec note does not model it either.

Source: Lipski 1994, Chile chapter (aspiration of /s/); Oroz 1966; Hualde 2005 on /s/ weakening.

### velar fricative

j, and g before e/i, are written x in all positions: gente ˈxente, mujer muˈxeɾ. The Chilean fronting to [ç] before front vowels is allophonic and is not written. Silent h is not written: hacer aˈseɾ. México is written ˈmexiko.

Source: Lipski 1994, Chile chapter (palatalized /x/); Hualde 2005, chapter on /x/.

### voiced stops, b/v and -d

b and v are both b: vaca ˈbaka. Write b d ɡ in all positions, never β ð ɣ. Write intervocalic d in -ado/-ada: cansado kanˈsado. Write word-final d: usted usˈted, verdad beɾˈdad. The common Chilean loss of these is not written.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on voiced stops and spirantization; Lipski 1994, Chile chapter (loss of /d/).

### rhotics and ch

The tap is ɾ: pero ˈpeɾo, comer koˈmeɾ. The trill is r, used word-initially, after n l s and for rr: perro ˈpero, rosa ˈrosa, honra ˈonra. Rural and Chilote assibilated variants are not written. ch is tʃ: chico ˈtʃiko. The Chilean fricative [ʃ] variant is not written. tl in Mapudungun or Nahuatl-origin words is t.l within the word, unmarked: atlas ˈatlas.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on rhotics and affricates; Lipski 1994, Chile chapter (assibilated /r/, fricative /tʃ/); Oroz 1966.

### Mapudungun (Huilliche) loanwords

Transcribe loanwords and place names with Spanish phonemes and Spanish orthographic stress rules, following the Spanish spelling. curanto kuˈɾanto, Quellón keˈʝon, Ancud anˈkud, chilote tʃiˈlote. Do not use Mapudungun-specific vowels or consonants such as ɨ or ʃ.

Source: Cavada, F.J. (1914) Chiloé y los chilotes (lexicon); Lipski 1994, Chile chapter (Mapudungun contact).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
