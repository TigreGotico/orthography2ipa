# Transcription conventions: Beirão (Beira Portuguese) (pt-PT-x-beira)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable, including its onset, never before the vowel: casa [ˈkaz̺ɐ], lume [ˈlymɨ], not [lˈymɨ]. Place stress by Portuguese lexical rules. An orthographic accent fixes it: café [kɐˈfɛ], árvore [ˈaɾboɾɨ]. Otherwise a word ending in ⟨a e o⟩, optionally followed by ⟨s⟩, or in ⟨am em ens⟩ is stressed on the penult: cidade [s̺iˈdadɨ], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃]. Any other ending (⟨l r z i u im um ão⟩, consonants) takes final stress: papel [pɐˈpɛɫ], comer [kuˈmeɾ], azul [ɐˈz̺yɫ]. Every content monosyllable gets ˈ: pão [ˈpɐ̃w̃], sul [ˈs̺yɫ]. Secondary stress is not marked. Stress is the trigger for stressed-/u/ fronting (see other). Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings. ⟨ss rr⟩ are single consonants: passo [ˈpas̺u].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (word stress); Cunha & Cintra 1984, ch. 3–4 (accentuation and orthographic accents).

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed: write them without ˈ and with reduced vowels. Articles: o/os [u]/[uʃ], a/as [ɐ]/[ɐʃ], um/uns [ũ]/[ũʃ]. Simple prepositions: de [dɨ], em [ẽj̃], a [ɐ], por [puɾ], para [pɐɾɐ], com [kõ]. Contractions: do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], ao [aw], pelo [pɨlu], num [nũ]. Conjunctions and the complementiser: e [i], que [kɨ], se [s̺ɨ], mas [mɐʃ], nem [nẽj̃], ou [o]. Clitic pronouns: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [s̺ɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], lhes [ʎɨʃ], o/a [u]/[ɐ], nos [nuʃ], vos [buʃ]. These clitics never undergo stressed-/u/ fronting: do [du], not [ˈdy]. Stressed words: all nouns, adjectives, adverbs and lexical verbs; the auxiliaries ser, estar, ter, haver, ir in every form: é [ˈɛ], tem [ˈtẽj̃], foi [ˈfoj]; strong and prepositional pronouns: eu [ˈew], tu [ˈty], ele [ˈelɨ], nós [ˈnɔʃ], mim [ˈmĩ]; possessives and demonstratives: este [ˈeʃtɨ]; numerals other than um; negation não [ˈnɐ̃w̃]; disyllabic prepositions and conjunctions other than para: sobre [ˈs̺obɾɨ], entre [ˈẽtɾɨ], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6.3 (clitics and prosodic words) and ch. 2 (unstressed vowel reduction); Cunha & Cintra 1984, ch. on pronomes átonos.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable as a separate form. Portuguese has no case endings or nunation, and no pausal form is used at a comma or at the end of a sentence. Every word is written in the same form wherever it stands. Final unstressed ⟨e⟩ is always written [ɨ], never deleted: noite [ˈnojtɨ]. Final unstressed ⟨o⟩ is always [u]: tudo [ˈtydu]. Final ⟨s z⟩ before a pause is [ʃ]: luz [ˈlyʃ], casas [ˈkaz̺ɐʃ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 (final vowel reduction) and ch. 5 (coda /S/). Brissos & Saramago 2014, p. 57 report schwa for final ⟨o⟩ in parts of the Centro-Sul; that is not followed.

## Connected speech

Only the word-final sibilant changes across a boundary, and it is written on the first word; the next word is unchanged. Before a vowel-initial word it is [z̺]: os amigos [uz̺ ɐˈmiɡuʃ], dez anos [ˈdɛz̺ ˈɐnuʃ]. Before a voiced consonant it is [ʒ]: os dias [uʒ ˈdiɐʃ], mais bem [ˈmajʒ ˈbẽj̃]. Before a voiceless consonant or a pause it is [ʃ]: as casas [ɐʃ ˈkaz̺ɐʃ]. A sibilant-sibilant sequence is not degeminated: os sapatos [uʃ s̺ɐˈpatuʃ]. Word-final ⟨r⟩ stays the tap [ɾ] before a vowel: comer agora [kuˈmeɾ ɐˈɡɔɾɐ]. Word-final ⟨l⟩ stays [ɫ] in all positions. Vowel sandhi across words (elision of [ɨ], crasis, glide formation) is not written; each word keeps its own vowels: de amigo [dɨ ɐˈmiɡu], que eu [kɨ ˈew]. Spirantisation of /b d ɡ/ to [β ð ɣ] is not written, inside or across words: a vaca [ɐ ˈbakɐ], o dedo [u ˈdedu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 5 (coda /S/ voicing and resyllabification) and ch. 7 (sandhi). The apical value [z̺] of the resyllabified sibilant follows Cintra 1971, pp. 88, 93, and Álvarez Pérez 2014, pp. 37–38.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as the non-syllabic consonant symbols [j w], never i̯ u̯. In nasal diphthongs both elements carry the tilde: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], muito [ˈmũjtu], pão [ˈpɐ̃w̃], mãe [ˈmɐ̃j̃], põe [ˈpõj̃]. ⟨ei⟩ is [ej], not Lisbon [ɐj]: primeiro [pɾiˈmejɾu], leite [ˈlejtɨ]. Final ⟨em/ém⟩ is [ẽj̃], not Lisbon [ɐ̃j̃]: bem [ˈbẽj̃], também [tɐ̃ˈbẽj̃]. ⟨ou⟩ is monophthongal [o]: outro [ˈotɾu], falou [fɐˈlo]. After velars, ⟨qu gu⟩ plus a vowel is written [kw ɡw]: quatro [ˈkwatɾu], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. A glide [w] is never fronted to [y].

Source: Cunha & Cintra 1984, ch. 1–2 (Lisbon [ɐj] and [ɐ̃j̃] versus [ej] and [ẽj̃] elsewhere); Cintra 1971, pp. 92–93 (ou monophthongisation, ei preserved outside the south); Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 (glides as non-syllabic /j w/).

## Other choices

### sibilants

Onset ⟨s ss c(e,i) ç⟩ are apical [s̺], and onset ⟨z⟩ and intervocalic ⟨s⟩ are apical [z̺]: sol [ˈs̺ɔɫ], passo [ˈpas̺u], cidade [s̺iˈdadɨ], caça [ˈkas̺ɐ], casa [ˈkaz̺ɐ], zero [ˈz̺ɛɾu]. Coda ⟨s z⟩ keeps the palatal chiado [ʃ ʒ], as in standard EP: festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ], mesmo [ˈmeʒmu], luz [ˈlyʃ].

Source: Cintra 1971, pp. 11–13, 88, 93 (s beirão); Álvarez Pérez 2014, pp. 37–38.

### betacism

⟨v⟩ and ⟨b⟩ are both [b] everywhere: vinho [ˈbiɲu], vaca [ˈbakɐ], novo [ˈnobu], livro [ˈlibɾu]. [b] counts as a bilabial onset for the /u/ guard. The merger is applied across the whole Beira node, although it is weaker in the urban Litoral.

Source: Cintra 1971, p. 88; Álvarez Pérez 2014, pp. 35–37.

### stressed /u/ fronting

Stressed oral /u/ is written [y], also before a coda liquid: tudo [ˈtydu], lume [ˈlymɨ], sul [ˈs̺yɫ], azul [ɐˈz̺yɫ], uva [ˈybɐ], tu [ˈty]. It stays [u] after /p b m/: puro [ˈpuɾu], muro [ˈmuɾu], uvas but bufo [ˈbufu]. Unstressed /u/ stays [u]: futuro [fuˈtuɾu] is fronted only in the stressed syllable, giving [fuˈtyɾu]. Nasal ũ, glide [w] and the clitics listed under function_words are never fronted: nunca [ˈnũkɐ], um [ũ]. The Brissos 2014b central value [ʉ] is not used. This is the Beira-Baixa realisation, applied to the whole Beira node.

Source: Cintra 1971, p. 14 (CVC reflow); Brissos 2014, JPL 13(1) ([tˈyd], [lˈymɨ]); Brissos 2014b, pp. 87–89 (bilabial guard).

### unstressed vowels

Use standard EP reduction. Unstressed ⟨a⟩ is [ɐ], unstressed ⟨e⟩ is [ɨ] and unstressed ⟨o⟩ is [u]: pesado [pɨˈz̺adu], morar [muˈɾaɾ]. Pretonic ⟨e⟩ before a palatal or in initial ⟨es-⟩ is [i]: estar [iʃˈtaɾ]. Stressed ⟨a⟩ before a nasal consonant is [ɐ]: cama [ˈkɐmɐ]. The wider Beira-Baixa tonic chain shift is not written; only /u/ is fronted.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2; Cintra 1971, p. 14 (the chain shift is described but not transcribed here).

### rhotics

Strong r (initial ⟨r⟩, ⟨rr⟩, ⟨r⟩ after ⟨n l s⟩) is written [ʁ]: rio [ˈʁiu], carro [ˈkaʁu]. The conservative rural Beira alveolar trill [r] is not written. Weak r is the tap [ɾ] in onset and coda: caro [ˈkaɾu], mar [ˈmaɾ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 1 (/ʁ/ ~ [r] variation); the conservative trill is recorded by Vasconcelos 1901 (Beira section).

### laterals and ⟨ch⟩

⟨l⟩ is [l] in onset and [ɫ] in coda: lado [ˈladu], mal [ˈmaɫ]. ⟨ch⟩ is [ʃ], not the north-eastern affricate [tʃ]: chave [ˈʃabɨ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 1; Cintra 1971, p. 88 (the affricate is restricted to Trás-os-Montes and Alto Minho).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
