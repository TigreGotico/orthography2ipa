# Transcription conventions: Sendinês (Mirandese meridional) (mwl-x-sendim)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable on every stressed word, monosyllables included ([ˈlus] lhuç). Secondary stress is not marked. A written acute or circumflex accent fixes the stress on that vowel. Without an accent, stress falls on the penult when the word ends in a, e or o, alone or followed by s, or in verbal -n/-an/-en ([ˈkasɐ] cása, [ˈkurpu] curpo, [ˈtirɐ] tirra, [ˈkɐ̃tɐ̃] cántan). Otherwise it falls on the last syllable: endings -i, -u, -l, -r, -z, -ç, -on, -ion, nasal -in/-un and other consonants ([fɐˈzeɾ] fazer, [kɐ̃ˈsɐ̃w̃] cançon, [fɐˈzi] fazie). Sendinês monophthongs from ⟨ie, uo⟩ carry the stress that the diphthong had ([ˈfiru] firro, [ˈbunu] buno). The word exceptions quien, cien and bien are stressed on the i: [ˈkiɨ̃], [ˈsiɨ̃], [ˈbiɨ̃]. The lect has no phonemic vowel length and no case endings, so those do not apply. ⟨rr⟩ is the trill [r] and ⟨ss⟩ is [s̺]; neither is a geminate, and length is never written.

Source: Convenção Ortográfica da Língua Mirandesa (Ferreira & Raposo 1999), accentuation rules; Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §50 p. 219–220 and §58 p. 226 for the Sendinês forms.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed proclitics and enclitics. They get no ˈ mark and take the unstressed vowel values a [ɐ], e [ɨ], o [u].
- Definite articles: l [l], la [lɐ], ls [ls̺], las [lɐs̺].
- Indefinite articles: un [ũ], ũa follows its lexicon entry.
- Monosyllabic prepositions: de [dɨ], a [ɐ], an [ɐ̃], por [puɾ], pa [pɐ], cun [kũ].
- Conjunctions: i [i], que [kɨ], se [s̺ɨ], mas [mɐs̺], ni [ni].
- Clitic object and reflexive pronouns: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [s̺ɨ], le [lɨ] (initial lh > l), lo [lu], nos [nus̺], bos [bus̺].
- Proclitic possessives and the relative que.
- Proclitic quien and bien reduce to [kĩ] and [bĩ] with no stress.
Stressed words: all nouns, adjectives, adverbs and full verbs; auxiliaries and copula forms (they keep lexical stress); strong (tonic) pronouns; demonstratives; pronominal possessives; interrogatives; numerals; the negation non and the word exceptions tue, sue, which follow their lexicon values. A function word cited or emphasised in isolation takes its stressed form.

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I (phonetics of atonic words, §11 Obs. 4 p. 185 and §50 Obs. 1 p. 220 for proclitic -ien); unstressed vowel values inherited from Central Mirandese (Duas-Igrejas) per LdV 1900.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Mirandese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word is written the same at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside a phrase, and final unstressed vowels are always written in full ([ˈkurpu], [ˈnoitɨ]).

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I (no pausal alternations described); Convenção 1999.

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its citation form in its own place, and no cross-word process is transcribed:
- no vowel elision or synalepha between words;
- no voicing or resyllabification of final sibilants: final ⟨s⟩ is always [s̺], final ⟨z, ç⟩ always [s], e.g. las ourelhas [lɐs̺ owˈɾeʎɐs̺];
- no nasal or place assimilation.
Elision that the orthography shows with an apostrophe has already split the words. Write only the surviving segments: d' [d], l' [l], m' [m], s' [s̺].

Source: Convenção Ortográfica 1999 (apostrophe for written elision); citation forms per Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I.

## Glides and diphthongs

Non-syllabic vocoids are written j and w, never i̯ u̯.
- Falling diphthongs: ai [aj], ei [ej], oi [oj], ui [uj], ou [ow], au [aw]; e.g. [ˈowtɾu] outro, [pɾiˈmejɾu] primeiro. Mirandese keeps ou and ei as diphthongs.
- Nasal diphthongs carry tilde on both parts: [ɐ̃w̃], [jɐ̃w̃], [ɐ̃j̃]; e.g. [kɐ̃ˈsɐ̃w̃] cançon, [nɐˈsjɐ̃w̃] naçion.
- Rising: word-initial y and ⟨i, u⟩ before a vowel in the same syllable are [j] and [w].
- Sendinês has no Leonese rising diphthong: ⟨ie, iê⟩ is [i] and ⟨uo, uô⟩ is [u] (Central [jɪ] and [ʊ] are not used).
- Final ⟨ie⟩ is [i] and final ⟨ue⟩ is [u] ([ˈti], [ˈru]).
- The -ien exceptions are a hiatus [ˈiɨ̃], with no glide.

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §50 p. 219–220, §56 p. 225, §58 p. 226, §67 p. 232, §110 fn.

## Other choices

### Latin initial L / ⟨lh⟩

Word-initial ⟨lh⟩ or ⟨l⟩ from Latin L- is [l]: [ˈlobu] lhobo/lobo, [ˈlɐdu] lhado. Medial ⟨lh⟩ stays [ʎ]: [ˈuʎu] ulho, [muˈʎeɾ] mulher. The full depalatalisation reported for the Aditamento (alhá [ɐˈla]) is not followed.

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §111 p. 259 and p. 226 (ulho), against the Primeiro Aditamento 2000 as reported secondarily.

### Sibilants

Keep the four-way contrast. Apical ⟨s, ss⟩ is [s̺], intervocalic ⟨s⟩ is [z̺]. Predorsal ⟨c(e,i), ç⟩ is [s] and ⟨z⟩ is [z]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ], ⟨j, g(e,i)⟩ is [ʒ], ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ]. Examples: [ˈkaz̺ɐ] casa, [ˈpas̺u] passo, [fɐˈzeɾ] fazer, [tʃuˈbeɾ] chober.

Source: Convenção 1999; Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I.

### Unstressed vowels

Use the inherited Central values: unstressed a is [ɐ], e is [ɨ] (final included), o and u are [u]. Unstressed final -en is [ɨ̃]. The open/close quality of stressed e and o follows the accent (é, ó [ɛ ɔ]; ê, ô [e o]) or the lexicon entry.

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900 (Duas-Igrejas description; §77 p. 239 for Sendinês -en [ɨ̃]); no Sendinês-specific source differs.

### -on / -ion endings

Final -on is [ɐ̃w̃] and -ion is [jɐ̃w̃] (Sendinês, Portuguese-like), not Central [õ]: [kɐ̃ˈsɐ̃w̃] cançon.

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §110 footnote (hedged 'parece').

### Nasal vowels and rhotics

A vowel plus a coda nasal (⟨an en in on un⟩ before a consonant or finally) is a nasal vowel with no consonant: [ɐ̃ ẽ ĩ õ ũ], e.g. [kɐ̃ˈtaɾ] cantar. Initial ⟨r⟩ and ⟨rr⟩ are the trill [r]; other ⟨r⟩ is [ɾ]. ⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩ are written [b]; there is no /v/.

Source: Convenção 1999; Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
