# Transcription conventions: Peruvian Spanish (Andean) (es-PE)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable. The onset includes any consonant cluster and any prevocalic glide: [kaˈmino], [ˈtɾes], [ˈbweno], [ˈtjera].

The rule follows the spelling. A written accent marks the stressed vowel: [kaˈfe], [ˈarbol], [ˈmusika]. Without an accent, words ending in a vowel, n or s take penultimate stress: [ˈkasa], [ˈhoben] → write [ˈxoben], [ˈlunes]. All other words take final stress: [aˈmoɾ], [sjuˈdad], [ɾeˈloʝ]. Final y counts as a consonant here: [ɾeˈloʝ] reloj, but [ˈmui̯] muy.

Every lexically stressed word gets ˈ, including monosyllables: [ˈsol], [ˈes], [ˈmas], [ˈsi] sí. Unstressed words (see function_words) get no mark: [si] si, [de] de.

Adverbs in -mente keep both stresses: ˌ on the adjective's stressed syllable and ˈ on -men-: [ˌɾapidaˈmente].

Enclitics written together with the verb stay one word, and the written accent places the stress: [ˈdamelo].

Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings. Double letters are simply two segments: [inˈnato], [akˈsjon].

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on word stress (orthographic stress rules; double stress in -mente adverbs).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Unstressed, written without ˈ:
- definite articles el, la, lo, los, las: [la ˈkasa], [los ˈotɾos]
- all simple prepositions except según: [de], [en], [kon], [poɾ], [paɾa]; but [seˈɣun] → write [seˈɡun]
- conjunctions y, e, o, u, ni, que, pero, sino, aunque, si, porque: [ˈpeɾo] → write [peɾo], [poɾke]
- object and reflexive clitics me, te, se, lo, la, le, nos, les: [lo ˈbeo]
- prenominal possessives mi, tu, su, mis, tus, sus: [mi ˈmadɾe]
- relatives and relative adverbs without a written accent: que, quien, cual, donde, cuando, como

Stressed, written with ˈ:
- indefinite articles un, una, unos, unas: [ˈun ˈombɾe]
- subject and prepositional pronouns: [ˈʝo], [ˈel], [ˈmi] mí, [ˈti]
- demonstratives, numerals, nuestro and vuestro
- full possessives: [ˈmio]
- interrogatives and exclamatives with a written accent: [ˈke], [ˈdonde]
- all adverbs, nouns, adjectives
- all verb forms, including auxiliaries and copulas: [ˈa ˈdicho], [ˈes], [ˈesta] está
- según

Compound function words stay unstressed. Example: aunque [aunke] → written [au̯nke].

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, section on stressed and unstressed words. The list follows the traditional classification of Navarro Tomás's Manual de pronunciación española, which Hualde adopts.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation.

Words are written identically at a comma, at the end of the sentence and inside the phrase. Final /s/ stays [s] before a pause in this conservative highland lect: [ˈmas]. Final vowels are written in full; the variable Andean devoicing or loss of final unstressed vowels is not written.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Peru chapter (highland retention of final /s/). The dialect zones are as summarised in Lipski (2011), pp. 1–26.

## Connected speech

Each word keeps its citation form. No cross-word process is written.

- Resyllabification is not written. A final consonant before a vowel stays in its own word: [los ˈotɾos], [poɾ ˈeso], [kon ˈel].
- Synalepha and vowel elision across words are not written. Both vowels stay: [la ˈaɡwa], [de ˈesto], [mi ˈixo] → write [mi ˈixo]. This fits the Andean tendency to keep hiatus.
- Optional glottal onsets are not written.
- Nasal place assimilation is not written, within or across words: [en ˈkasa], [un ˈbeso]. Final /n/ is alveolar [n] in the highlands, not the coastal velar [ŋ].
- /s/ voicing before voiced consonants is not written: [ˈmismo], [los ˈdos].
- The conjunction y is always [i], also before a vowel: [i ˈel].

Source: Hualde (2005), chapters on syllabification across word boundaries and on nasal assimilation. Highland Peruvian specifics (alveolar final /n/, retained hiatus) follow Lipski (1994), Peru and Andean chapters.

## Glides and diphthongs

Rising diphthongs (unstressed i/u before another vowel) are written with [j w]: [ˈtjera], [ˈbweno], [ˈpje], [ˈkwando]. Words with ui and iu also take [j w] on the first element: [ˈrwido] ruido, [sjuˈdad] ciudad.

Falling diphthongs (unstressed i/u, or y, after a vowel) are written with [i̯ u̯]: [ˈai̯ɾe], [ˈkau̯sa], [ˈoi̯], [ˈmui̯], [ˈrei̯], [ˈbei̯nte].

The fricative y/hi before a vowel is the consonant [ʝ], not a glide: [ˈʝo], [ˈʝelo]. Spelled hu before a vowel is [w]: [ˈwebo].

An accented í/ú next to a vowel is a hiatus, with both vowels full: [ˈdia], [paˈis], [ˈɾio].

Vowel sequences without i or u (ea, eo, oa, ae) stay as two full vowels: [teˈatɾo], [ˈpoeta] → write [poˈeta]. They are never glided to [j] or [w].

Source: Hualde (2005), chapter on vowels, glides and diphthongs (notation [j w] vs. [i̯ u̯]; hiatus vs. diphthong).

## Other choices

### Quechua-substrate vowel raising

Write /e o/ as [e o] in every position: [ˈmesa], [ˈtodo]. The raising to [i u] mentioned in the spec note is not written. Published descriptions treat it as a trait of Quechua- or Aymara-dominant bilinguals, not of the standard pronunciation of highland Peruvian Spanish.

Source: Lipski (1994), Peru chapter. This departs from the spec note.

### ll vs. y (lleísmo)

Keep the distinction:
- ll = [ʎ]: [ˈkaʎe], [ˈʎama]
- y and consonantal hi = [ʝ]: [ˈmaʝo], [ˈʝelo]
The highland Andean zone is described as maintaining /ʎ/; Lima-coast yeísmo is not adopted.

Source: Lipski (1994), Peru chapter. Fontanella de Weinberg (1992), on lleísmo in the Andean region.

### Rhotics

Write the trill as [r] for initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s: [ˈrosa], [ˈpero] → write [ˈpero], [onˈrado]. Write every other r as the tap [ɾ], including word-finally before a vowel or a pause: [ˈkaɾa], [aˈmoɾ], [poɾ ˈeso]. The variable Andean assibilation mentioned in the spec note is not written.

Source: Hualde (2005), chapter on rhotics. Assibilation is as noted in Lipski (1994), Andean chapters, but treated as non-broad here.

### Voiced stops b d g

Write [b d ɡ] in all positions, without the spirant allophones [β ð ɣ]. Both b and v are [b]: [ˈbaka], [ˈlobo], [ˈnada], [ˈaɡwa]. Word-final d is written [d]: [sjuˈdad], [ˈbeɾdad] → write [beɾˈdad].

Source: Broad phonemic convention. Spirantization is described in Hualde (2005), chapter on obstruents.

### Sibilants and velar fricative

Seseo applies: z, ce and ci are [s], as in [ˈsapato], [ˈsjelo]. Coda /s/ is always written [s], never [h] or zero: [ˈestos]. Spelled j and ge/gi are velar [x]: [ˈxente], [ˈixo]. Spelled x is [ks] ([ekˈsamen]), except [x] in México and similar words. h is silent. ch is [t͡ʃ] and ñ is [ɲ].

Source: Hualde (2005), chapters on fricatives. Highland retention of /s/ and the velar [x] follow Lipski (1994), Peru chapter, consistent with the spec note.

### Unstressed vowel weakening

The Andean weakening, devoicing or loss of unstressed vowels (especially next to /s/) is not written. Unstressed vowels are always written in full: [ˈantes], [ˈpwes].

Source: Lipski (1994), Andean chapters. Treated as non-broad here.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
