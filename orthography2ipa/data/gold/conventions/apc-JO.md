# Transcription conventions: Jordanian Arabic (Ammani) (apc-JO)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables. Leave monosyllables unmarked (beːt, min, ʔalb). Count syllables after the fixed vowels are in place, including suffixes and the article. (1) If the final syllable is superheavy (CVVC or CVCC), stress it: kiˈtaːb, ilˈbeːt, ʃaˈribt. (2) Otherwise, if the penult is heavy (CVV or CVC), stress the penult: ˈkalbak, kiˈtaːbha, ˈʃaːfu. (3) Otherwise stress the antepenult: ˈkatabat, ˈmadrase is written ˈmadrasa (see the feminine ending under other), ˈʃajara. (4) In a two-syllable word with no superheavy final, stress the first syllable: ˈkatab, ˈʔana. A long vowel makes its syllable heavy. A word-final long vowel makes the final syllable only heavy, not superheavy, so it is not stressed unless rule 4 or other suffixes say so. A geminate closes the preceding syllable, which makes it heavy: ˈmʕallim, ˈħammaːm becomes ħamˈmaːm because the final CVVC wins. When the stressed syllable begins with the second half of a geminate, put ˈ between the two letters: ħamˈmaːm. Spoken Ammani has no case endings, so none are written or counted. Epenthetic vowels are never stressed. Do not mark secondary stress.

Source: Urban Levantine (Damascus–Amman–Jerusalem) stress type as summarised in Watson, J.C.E. (2011), 'Word stress in Arabic', Blackwell Companion to Phonology, ch. 124; Cowell 1964 for Damascus, as reported. The Bedouin Jordanian pattern of Irshied & Kenstowicz (1984, Bani Hassan) is not followed, because the Ammani koine aligns with urban Levantine (Fadda 2016:27-28; Al-Wer 2020).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Proclitics written as part of their host get no stress of their own, and stress is computed on the whole word. This covers the article il-/l-, b- 'with/in', la-/l- 'to', w-/u- 'and', the indicative b-/bi-, and the future ħa-: ilˈbeːt, bilˈbeːt, uˈkitaːb. The conjunction و joined to a word is [w] before a vowel and [u] before a consonant: wilˈwalad, ukiˈtaːb. When the following words stand as separate words, write them without a stress mark: prepositions fi, min, ʕala, ʕan, maʕ, la, ʕind; conjunctions w/u, ʔaw, bas, ʔinnu, ʔiza, law; the negators ma and miʃ; the progressive ʕam; the future raħ; the relative illi. Content-like function words keep normal stress by the stress rule: independent pronouns (ˈʔana, ˈʔinte written ˈʔinta, ˈhuwwa, ˈhijja), demonstratives (ˈhaːda, ˈhaːj, ˈhaðoːl written ˈhadoːl), the auxiliary kaːn (ˈkaːnat, monosyllable kaːn unmarked), and interrogatives (ˈʃuː is a monosyllable, so ʃuː; ˈleːʃ becomes leːʃ; ˈwein becomes weːn). Pronoun suffixes (-ak, -ik, -u, -ha, -na, -kum, -hum, -ni) belong to the host and shift stress by rule 1-3: ˈbeːtak, beːˈtha is wrong; write ˈbeːtha.

Source: Cowell 1964 (Damascus clitic and particle system, as reported by Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 §1.3); Levantine orthography and pronoun table in Wikipedia 'Levantine Arabic' (levantine_arabic_wiki, read 2026-09-13); Watson 2011 for stress over clitic groups.

## Pausal forms

No separate pausal form applies anywhere, at the sentence end, at commas or medially. Every word takes the same dialect form in all positions. MSA case vowels and tanwīn marks in the diacritized input are never pronounced: كِتَابٌ is kiˈtaːb, not kitaːbun. Exception: lexicalised adverbs in -an keep it everywhere (ˈʔabadan, taʔˈriːban, ˈʃukran). Ta marbuta is -a in all positions (ˈmadrasa, ˈʃajara). Before a possessor noun and before a pronoun suffix it is -it (madrasit ilˈbalad, madˈrasti). Write final long vowels as the orthography shows them (matres = long), following the specification's reading, for example لأبوي ˈlʔabwiː. Do not shorten them at a pause.

Source: Dialect morphology without case or nunation: Cowell 1964 as reported; Fadda 2016:27-30. The ta marbuta construct -it and the -iyye feminine follow Wikipedia 'Levantine Arabic' (levantine_arabic_wiki). The treatment of final long vowels follows the specification notes (positional keys, قَوِي awiː).

## Connected speech

Write each word in its own form, and write no process across a word boundary. Do not elide the article's helping vowel after a vowel-final word (fi ilˈbeːt, not fi lˈbeːt). Do not show cross-word epenthesis, voicing or place assimilation, and do not resyllabify. Within a word, write the article's assimilation to the coronal 'sun' consonants t d s z ʃ ʒ? no: ʒ does not assimilate; t d tˤ dˤ s z sˤ ʃ r l n do: iʃˈʃams, inˈnaːs, irˈraːdʒil written irˈraːʒil, but ilˈʒamal, ilˈʔamar. Write the article as il- without an initial ʔ when it begins the word, and as l- after a proclitic vowel (bilˈbeːt, lalˈbeːt). Write word-internal epenthetic i to break a three-consonant cluster where the dialect inserts it (ˈkalbik for kalb-k 'your (f.) dog').

Source: Levantine article assimilation and the epenthesis pattern in Cowell 1964 (as reported by Almbark & Hellmuth 2015) and Wikipedia 'Levantine Arabic' (levantine_arabic_wiki). The no-cross-boundary policy is this transcription's own choice, so that each word stays in its own place as the tool requires.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write glides as the consonants j and w, never i̯ or u̯. The old diphthongs /aj aw/ are monophthongised to eː and oː (beːt, ˈsˤoːtˤ, joːm, ˈleːla). They stay aj and aw before a glide or a geminate glide: ˈʔawwal, ˈħajja, ˈsˤajjaːd. For ⟨و⟩ and ⟨ي⟩ after a consonant letter, read in this order: the long vowel uː or iː, then oː or eː, then the glide. Word-initially and after a vowel they are always glides. Of two adjacent letters يو or وي, the first is the glide (bjuːt, not biːuːt). Before final ة the letter is a glide: -ijja (ʔaˈwijja, ʕaraˈbijja), -wa (ˈʔahwa).

Source: Fadda 2016:30 (/aj aw/ to eː oː except before a glide); Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 §1.1 (mid long vowels from coalescence); Wikipedia 'Levantine Arabic', table Vowels and the nisba -iyye (levantine_arabic_wiki). The matres ranking follows the specification notes.

## Other choices

### qaf

Write ق as ʔ in every word: ʔalb, ˈʔahwa, ʔaˈwijja, ʔaːl. The exception is MSA-learned vocabulary that the koine keeps with q, written q (ʔalˈqurʔaːn written ilˈqurʔaːn, qaˈraːr). This disagrees with the specification's note, which lists ɡ first. Al-Wer reports ɡ as the traditional and male Jordanian value, but the koine norm described for Amman is ʔ.

Source: Fadda 2016:27-28 (koine consonants = urban Palestinian ʔ); Al-Wer 2020:560 (third-generation women use ʔ consistently, men vary).

### jim

Write ج as ʒ: ˈʒamal, ʒiːb, ˈraːʒil.

Source: Fadda 2016:27-28; Al-Wer 2020:560 (women ʒ, men dʒ~ʒ).

### interdentals

Write ث ذ ظ as the koine stops t d dˤ in native vocabulary (tleːn written tneːn, ˈdahab, dˤuhr). In learned MSA words they are s z zˤ (ˈθaqaːfa becomes saˈqaːfa, ˈzˤaːhira). Do not write θ ð ðˤ.

Source: Al-Wer 2020:560 (both genders favour stops); Fadda 2016:27-28.

### feminine ending and short vowels

Write only the short vowels i u a, with no short e or o and no schwa. The feminine ending and other final -a are written a even where speech raises it to [e] (imāla): ˈmadrasa, ʔaˈwijja. Published Levantine descriptions give -e after non-emphatic consonants. The broad transcription keeps /a/, following the specification's ية [ja].

Source: Almbark & Hellmuth 2015 §3-4 (short e and o as allophones of /i u/); Fadda 2016:30 (short /i u a/); Wikipedia 'Levantine Arabic' (-iyye) for the raised realisation, not written here.

### long vowels, geminates, pharyngeals and emphatics

Write long vowels with ː (aː iː uː eː oː) and geminates as doubled letters, not with ː (ˈsitta, ħamˈmaːm). Use ħ ʕ x ɣ h ʔ. Write emphatics with ˤ (tˤ dˤ sˤ zˤ) and r and l as plain r and l.

Source: Specification notes; Fadda 2016:27-30.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
