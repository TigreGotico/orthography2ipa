# Transcription conventions: Northern Mexican Spanish (es-MX-x-norte)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is lexical and is predicted from standard spelling. (1) A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel: café [kaˈfe], árbol [ˈaɾβol], música [ˈmusika], día [ˈdia], país [paˈis]. (2) With no written accent, a word ending in a vowel, n or s is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkasa], hablan [ˈaβlan], lunes [ˈlunes]. (3) With no written accent, a word ending in any other consonant, including final y, is stressed on the last syllable: ciudad [sjuˈðað], comer [koˈmeɾ], estoy [esˈtoj], Uruguay [uɾuˈɣwaj]. (4) For syllable counting, unaccented i/u next to another vowel form a diphthong with it: tiene [ˈtjene]. Write ˈ immediately before the stressed syllable, inside the word. Write it on every stressed word, including monosyllables: sol [ˈsol], ve [ˈbe]. Never write it on unstressed function words (see function_words). Words in -mente take ˌ on the stressed syllable of the adjective and ˈ on -men-: rápidamente [ˌrapiðaˈmente]. No other secondary stress is written. Do not write syllable dots. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so these do not apply. Double vowels across a morpheme boundary are written as two vowels: leer [leˈeɾ], creemos [kɾeˈemos].

Source: Real Academia Española & ASALE (2011), Nueva gramática de la lengua española: Fonética y fonología, chs. on the accent; Hualde, J. I. (2005), The Sounds of Spanish (CUP), chapter on stress; Quilis, A. (1993), Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas (Gredos), on the accent. Mexican standard follows the general Spanish rule (Moreno de Alba 1994, La pronunciación del español en México).

## Stressed and unstressed words

Follow the classic division into tonic and atonic words.
Unstressed (no ˈ):
- definite articles el, la, lo, los, las;
- prenominal possessives mi, tu, su, nuestro, mis, tus, sus;
- all simple prepositions except según: a, ante, con, de, en, para, por, sin, sobre, hasta, desde;
- contractions al, del;
- conjunctions y, e, o, u, ni, pero, mas, que, si, aunque, porque, pues;
- non-interrogative relatives and relative adverbs que, quien, cual (with article), donde, cuando, como, cuanto;
- object and reflexive clitics me, te, se, nos, lo, la, le, los, las, les, whether free-standing or hyphen-split;
- the titles don, doña, san, fray, sor.
Stressed (ˈ):
- indefinite articles un, una, unos, unas;
- subject pronouns yo, tú, él, ella, usted, ustedes, nosotros;
- prepositional pronouns mí, ti, sí;
- demonstratives;
- postnominal possessives mío, tuyo, suyo;
- interrogatives and exclamatives qué, quién, cómo, dónde, cuándo, cuánto;
- numerals;
- según;
- all verb forms, including auxiliaries haber, ser and estar;
- adverbs, including no and sí.
Examples:
- la casa [la ˈkasa]
- un libro [ˈun ˈliβɾo]
- de mi padre [de mi ˈpaðɾe]
- lo vi [lo ˈβi]
- ha venido [ˈa βeˈniðo]
- qué quieres [ˈke ˈkjeɾes]
- que vengas [ke ˈβeŋgas]
- no sé [ˈno ˈse]

Source: Navarro Tomás, T. (1918), Manual de pronunciación española, chapter on palabras acentuadas e inacentuadas; Real Academia Española & ASALE (2011), Nueva gramática: Fonética y fonología, chapter on palabras tónicas y átonas; Quilis (1993).

## Pausal forms

Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings, nunation or pausal truncation, so these do not apply. Final vowels and consonants are written identically everywhere: final /s/ stays [s] and final vowels are written in full. The Central Mexican devoicing or weakening of unstressed vowels next to /s/ is not written. The only pause effect is on /b d g/ (see connected_speech). Treat these points as post-pausal: the start of the sentence, and the position after any punctuation mark (comma, semicolon, colon, dash, parenthesis, quotation mark). There /b d g/ are written as stops: Bueno, gracias [ˈbweno ˈɡɾasjas]; ..., dijo [ˈdixo].

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, sections on the stop/approximant alternation; Navarro Tomás (1918), §§ on b, d, g after pause.

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its own place. Only these two processes are applied, and they are applied at word boundaries as well as inside words.
(1) Spirantization. /b d g/ are written [b d ɡ] after a pause, after a nasal, and, for /d/ only, after /l/. Everywhere else they are written [β ð ɣ], including after a vowel-final or consonant-final preceding word:
- la boca [la ˈβoka]
- los dedos [los ˈðeðos]
- el gato [el ˈɣato]
- el dedo [el ˈdeðo]
- un beso [um ˈbeso]
- en Guadalajara [eŋ ɡwaðalaˈxaɾa]
Spellings b and v are both /b/.
(2) Nasal place assimilation. Syllable-final n is written [m] before /p b m/ and [ŋ] before /k ɡ x/, also across word boundaries:
- un perro [um ˈpero]
- con mucho [kom ˈmutʃo]
- en casa [eŋ ˈkasa]
- tengo [ˈteŋɡo]
Elsewhere write [n]: no [ɱ], dental or palatal nasal symbols.
Not written:
- voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants: mismo [ˈmismo], los mismos [los ˈmismos];
- resyllabification of a final consonant onto a following vowel: los otros [los ˈotɾos];
- vowel sinalefa or elision across words: la otra [la ˈotɾa], de esto [de ˈesto];
- word-final vowel gliding: casi alto [ˈkasi ˈalto], not [ˈkasj].
Only spelled contractions al, del are contracted.

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, sections on spirantization of /b d g/ and on nasal assimilation; Navarro Tomás (1918); Quilis (1993).

## Glides and diphthongs

Non-syllabic high vowels in diphthongs and triphthongs are written [j] and [w], not [i̯ u̯], in both rising and falling diphthongs:
- tiene [ˈtjene]
- bueno [ˈbweno]
- aire [ˈajɾe]
- aula [ˈawla]
- rey [ˈrej]
- ciudad [sjuˈðað]
- buey [ˈbwej]
An accented í or ú forms a hiatus and stays a full vowel: día [ˈdia], baúl [baˈul].
The palatal consonant from yeísmo (ll, consonantal y, word-initial hi+vowel) is [ʝ], kept distinct from glide [j]: calle [ˈkaʝe], yo [ˈʝo], hielo [ˈʝelo], mayo [ˈmaʝo].
The conjunction y is always [i], since it is its own word: y ella [i ˈeʝa].
Silent u in gue/gui/que/qui is not written: guerra [ˈɡera]. Spelled ü is [w]: pingüino [piŋˈɡwino].

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on glides and diphthongs; Real Academia Española & ASALE (2011), Fonética y fonología, chapters on diphthongs and on the palatal consonant; yeísmo as in the specification notes (Lipski 1994).

## Other choices

### Seseo and yeísmo (specification baseline)

Spellings c(e,i), z and s are all [s]: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato], casa [ˈkasa]. Spellings ll and y are both [ʝ], as in the notes. The phoneme [θ] is never written.

Source: Specification notes; Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Mexico chapter.

### Deaffrication of ch

Write [tʃ] for ch everywhere: mucho [ˈmutʃo], noche [ˈnotʃe]. Lipski reports [ʃ] for /tʃ/ in Chihuahua and Sonora, but it is socially and regionally variable within the north and is not written.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Mexico chapter (northern Mexico); Moreno de Alba (1994).

### Weakening of intervocalic /ʝ/

Always write [ʝ]: gallina [ɡaˈʝina], silla [ˈsiʝa], ella [ˈeʝa]. Northern Mexican loss or weakening of /ʝ/ next to front vowels (Lipski) is variable and is not written.

Source: Lipski (1994), Latin American Spanish, Mexico chapter.

### Velar fricative and spelling x

Spellings j, and g before e/i, are [x], not [h]: jefe [ˈxefe], gente [ˈxente]. Spelling x is [x] in México, Oaxaca and Texas: [ˈmexiko], [waˈxaka], [ˈtexas]. It is [s] in Nahuatl-origin names with initial x such as Xochimilco [sotʃiˈmilko]. Elsewhere it is [ks]: taxi [ˈtaksi], examen [eˈksamen]. The tl cluster of Nahuatl loans stays [tl]: tlapalería [tlapaleˈɾia], atlas [ˈatlas].

Source: Moreno de Alba (1994), La pronunciación del español en México; Lipski (1994), Mexico chapter; Hualde (2005) on Mexican tl.

### Rhotics

Write the trill [r] for word-initial r, for rr, and for r after n, l or s: rosa [ˈrosa], perro [ˈpero], honra [ˈonra], Israel [israˈel]. Write the tap [ɾ] for r in all other positions, including word-final and before a consonant: pero [ˈpeɾo], comer [koˈmeɾ], verde [ˈbeɾðe]. Assibilated variants of r are not written. A word-final r before a vowel-initial word stays [ɾ].

Source: Hualde (2005), The Sounds of Spanish, chapter on rhotics; Navarro Tomás (1918).

### Final /s/ and /d/

Word- and syllable-final /s/ is always [s]; northern Mexican Spanish retains /s/, so no [h] or deletion is written: estos [ˈestos]. Word-final d is written [ð]: ciudad [sjuˈðað], usted [usˈteð]. Medial d in -ado is written [ð], not deleted: cansado [kanˈsaðo].

Source: Lipski (1994), Mexico chapter (retention of /s/ in Mexico outside the coasts); Hualde (2005) on /d/.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
