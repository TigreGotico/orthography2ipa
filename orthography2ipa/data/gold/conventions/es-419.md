# Transcription conventions: Latin American Spanish (es-419)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every stressed word, monosyllables included. Unstressed words (see function_words) get no mark. Stress is lexical and is read off the orthography. A written accent marks the stressed vowel. Otherwise, a word ending in a vowel, n or s takes penultimate stress, and any other word takes final stress: casa [ˈkasa], comen [ˈkomen], ciudad [sjuˈðað], árbol [ˈaɾβol], café [kaˈfe], sol [ˈsol]. When counting syllables, a glide is not a syllable: tienen [ˈtjenen], paisaje [pai̯ˈsaxe]. Place ˈ before the whole onset cluster: hablo [ˈaβlo], otro [ˈotɾo]. Adverbs in -mente carry two stresses. Mark ˈ on -men- and ˌ on the stressed syllable of the adjective: rápidamente [ˌrapiðaˈmente], claramente [ˌklaɾaˈmente]. All other compounds take a single ˈ. Spanish has no phonemic vowel length, no lexical geminates and no case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply. Never write ː. A sequence of identical consonants across a word boundary is handled under connected_speech.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on word stress (penultimate default for vowel/n/s-final words, two stresses in -mente adverbs); Harris 1969, ch. 4 on stress placement.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Write the following words without ˈ (unstressed). Definite articles el la los las lo: la casa [la ˈkasa]. Prenominal possessives mi tu su mis tus sus: mi padre [mi ˈpaðɾe]. Object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos os los las les: lo veo [lo ˈβeo]. These clitics stay unstressed also when written as separate words after a hyphen. All simple prepositions except según: a ante bajo con contra de desde en entre hacia hasta para por sin sobre tras: de Lima [de ˈlima], para ti [paɾa ˈti]. Conjunctions y e o u ni que pero sino aunque porque si: pan y vino [ˈpan i ˈβino]. Unaccented relatives que quien cual donde cuando como: el libro que leí [el ˈliβɾo ke leˈi]. Also the unaccented particles tan, aun, mas and the forms of address don and doña before a name. Write the following words with ˈ (stressed). Indefinite articles un una unos unas: un perro [ˈum ˈpero]. Subject and prepositional pronouns yo tú él ella usted nosotros ellos mí ti sí: para mí [paɾa ˈmi]. Demonstratives este ese aquel and their forms. Stressed possessives mío tuyo suyo. Prenominal nuestro and vuestro: nuestra casa [ˈnwestɾa ˈkasa]. Accented interrogatives and exclamatives qué quién cuál dónde cuándo cómo cuánto: ¿qué quieres? [ˈke ˈkjeɾes]. Numerals, según, negative no, and all verbs including auxiliaries: forms of haber, ser and estar in periphrases, as in he comido [ˈe koˈmiðo], ha llegado [ˈa ʝeˈɣaðo], está cantando [esˈta kanˈtando]. Accent pairs follow the orthography: tú [ˈtu] against tu [tu], sí [ˈsi] against si [si], él [ˈel] against el [el].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on word stress, section on stressed and unstressed words (articles, prenominal possessives, clitics, prepositions, conjunctions and relatives unstressed; indefinites, interrogatives, pronouns, demonstratives and verbs stressed). Nuestro/vuestro are treated as stressed, following the common description of them as fully stressed polysyllables.

## Pausal forms

Spanish has no pausal forms in the Semitic sense: there are no case endings, no nunation (tanwīn) and no deletion of final short vowels. Final vowels are always written in full: casa [ˈkasa]. The only thing a pause conditions is the realisation of /b d ɡ/ at the start of an utterance or after a comma or other punctuation. Treat both of these positions as post-pausal and write a stop there: Bueno, gracias. [ˈbweno ˈɡɾasjas]; ..., dime [ˈdime]. Final /d/ before a pause is written [ð], not deleted: verdad [beɾˈðað], Madrid [maˈðɾið]. Final /s/ and /ɾ/ before a pause are written [s] and [ɾ]: tres [ˈtɾes], comer [koˈmeɾ].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on the voiced obstruents (stop realisation of /b d ɡ/ after pause and after nasals); Harris 1969, ch. 2 on spirantization.

## Connected speech

Each word keeps its own place, and only the segments at its edges may change. Apply these processes across word boundaries exactly as inside words. (1) Spirantization: /b d ɡ/ are stops [b d ɡ] after a pause, after a nasal, and for /d/ also after /l/. Everywhere else they are approximants [β ð ɣ], including after a vowel-final or consonant-final preceding word: la boca [la ˈβoka], un beso [ˈum ˈbeso], el dedo [el ˈdeðo], el gato [el ˈɣato], los dos [los ˈðos]. (2) Nasal place assimilation: a word-final or coda nasal is written [m] before p b m, [ŋ] before k ɡ x, and [n] everywhere else: un peso [ˈum ˈpeso], en casa [eŋ ˈkasa], con José [koŋ xoˈse], un año [ˈun ˈaɲo]. Do not write ɱ, n̪ or palatalised variants. (3) Resyllabification of a final consonant onto a following vowel (los otros, por eso) is not shown. Do not write syllable dots or move segments between words: por eso [poɾ ˈeso], los otros [los ˈotɾos]. A final r before a vowel stays the tap [ɾ]. (4) Synalepha (vowel contact across words) is not written. Each vowel keeps its full form and no non-syllabic marks are added: la amiga [la aˈmiɣa], mi hijo [mi ˈixo], su uso [su ˈuso]. (5) Identical consonants across a boundary are both written: las sillas [las ˈsiʝas], el lado [el ˈlaðo]. (6) Voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants is not written: los mismos [los ˈmismos], desde [ˈdesðe]. (7) Lateral assimilation is not written. Word-initial r is the trill [r] whatever precedes it: la rosa [la ˈrosa].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on the voiced obstruents (spirantization), nasals (place assimilation), syllable structure and resyllabification, and vowel sequences (synalepha); Harris 1969, ch. 2.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write prenuclear (rising) glides as [j w] and postnuclear (falling) glides as [i̯ u̯]. Rising: tierra [ˈtjera], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [sjuˈðað], cuidado [kwiˈðaðo]. Falling: aire [ˈai̯ɾe], causa [ˈkau̯sa], hoy [ˈoi̯], rey [ˈrei̯], muy [ˈmui̯], deuda [ˈdeu̯ða]. Treat unaccented i, u, and word-final y, as glides whenever they are next to another vowel in the same word. Write them as such even in words with variable hiatus such as cliente [ˈkljente], dueto [ˈdweto], piano [ˈpjano]. An accented í or ú is a full vowel in hiatus: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis], baúl [baˈul]. A sequence of non-high vowels is always in hiatus: teatro [teˈatɾo], poeta [poˈeta]. Orthographic y and ll before a vowel are the consonant [ʝ] (yeísmo), not a glide: yo [ˈʝo], calle [ˈkaʝe], ya [ˈʝa], hielo [ˈʝelo]. Write [ʝ] also after a nasal or lateral, never [ɟʝ]: cónyuge [ˈkonʝuxe]. The conjunction y is [i] (unstressed): Juan y Ana [ˈxwan i ˈana].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on glides and diphthongs ([j w] onglides against [i̯ u̯] offglides; variable hiatus) and chapter on palatals (yeísmo, [ʝ]); Penny 2002, ch. 2 on the history of yeísmo.

## Other choices

### Coda /s/

Write coda /s/ as [s] everywhere, word-internally, word-finally and before a pause: estos [ˈestos], más tarde [ˈmas ˈtaɾðe], los años [los ˈaɲos]. Never write [h] or deletion. This departs from the specification note, which records aspiration in its allophone map. Aspiration and deletion are regional (Caribbean, Rioplatense, Chilean and other lowland varieties), whereas the general Latin American standard (highland Mexico, the Andes, the norm of broadcast speech) keeps [s].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on the fricatives and on dialect variation (aspiration of /s/ as regional); Penny 2002, ch. on dialectal variation in American Spanish.

### Seseo

Write c before e or i, and z, as [s]. There is no /θ/: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato], hacer [aˈseɾ], cazar [kaˈsaɾ].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on dialect variation (seseo); Penny 2002, ch. 5 on the origin of seseo.

### Velar fricative

Write j, and g before e or i, as [x]. Write x as [x] in the toponyms México, Oaxaca and Texas: ajo [ˈaxo], gente [ˈxente], México [ˈmexiko]. Otherwise x is [ks]: taxi [ˈtaksi], examen [ekˈsamen]. Do not use the Caribbean or Central American [h]. h is silent: hablar [aˈβlaɾ].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on the fricatives (/x/ realised [x] in Mexico and the Andes, [h] in the Caribbean).

### Rhotics

Write [r] for word-initial r, for rr, and for r after n, l or s: rosa [ˈrosa], perro [ˈpero], honra [ˈonra], Israel [israˈel]. Write [ɾ] elsewhere, including in codas: pero [ˈpeɾo], carta [ˈkaɾta], comer [koˈmeɾ]. Do not write assibilated or retroflex regional variants.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on the rhotics; Harris 1969, ch. 2.

### Other consonants and vowels

Write b and v alike as /b/, realised [b] or [β] as set out under connected_speech: vaca [ˈbaka], la vaca [la ˈβaka]. Write ch [tʃ]: mucho [ˈmutʃo]. Write ñ [ɲ]. Write qu and c before a, o, u as [k], and gu before e or i as [ɡ]/[ɣ]: guerra [ˈɡera]. Write ü as [w]: pingüino [piŋˈɡwino]. Write intervocalic /d/, including in -ado, as [ð] and never delete it: cansado [kanˈsaðo]. Use the five vowels [a e i o u] with no open or closed allophone marks and no nasalisation marks.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on the phoneme inventory, the vowels and the stops; Harris 1969, ch. 2.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
