# Transcription conventions: Ifanês (Mirandese septentrional) (mwl-x-ifanes)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Primary stress is lexical and falls on one of the last three syllables. Read it from the spelling, using the rules of the official orthography. A written acute, circumflex or grave accent marks the stressed syllable. Without an accent, words ending in ⟨a e o⟩, in these plus ⟨s⟩, or in ⟨am em en ens⟩ stress the penult. Words ending in ⟨i u⟩, ⟨is us⟩, ⟨l r z ç⟩, other consonants, or a nasal vowel written ⟨an in on un⟩ stress the final syllable. Write ˈ before the stressed syllable, before its onset and any onset glide. Separate syllables with a dot, as in the gold calibration [ˈfwo.ʊ]. Examples: casa [ˈka.z̺ɐ], falar [fɐˈlaɾ], lhobo [ˈʎo.bu], pássaro [ˈpa.s̺ɐ.ɾu], tierra [ˈtjɪ.rɐ], Infainç [ĩˈfajns]. Content monosyllables take ˈ, as in pan [ˈpɐ̃], ye [ˈjɛ]. Unstressed function monosyllables take no mark (see function_words). Write no secondary stress. Mirandese has no contrastive vowel length, so write no ː. There are no phonological geminates: ⟨rr⟩ is one trill [r], and ⟨ss⟩ is one apical [s̺]. There are no case endings. Unstressed vowels: ⟨a⟩ [ɐ], ⟨e⟩ [ɨ], ⟨o u⟩ [u]. Stressed vowels: ⟨a/á⟩ [a], ⟨é⟩ [ɛ], ⟨ê⟩ [e], ⟨ó⟩ [ɔ], ⟨ô⟩ [o].

Source: Ferreira & Raposo (coords.) 1999, Convenção Ortográfica da Língua Mirandesa (accentuation rules). Unstressed vowel values follow Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, Estudos de Philologia Mirandesa vol. I (Duas-Igrejas, central norm), which Ifanês inherits per vol. II pp. 28-31.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words are proclitic or enclitic and carry no stress mark. They take reduced vowels. Definite articles: l [l], la [lɐ], ls [ls̺], las [lɐs̺]. Indefinite articles: un [ũ], ua [ˈu.ɐ]→ write [wɐ]. Simple prepositions: de [dɨ], a [ɐ], an [ɐ̃], cun [kũ], por [puɾ], pa [pɐ], para [ˈpɐ.ɾɐ]→ unstressed [pɐ.ɾɐ]. Contractions: al [ɐl], ne [nɨ], na [nɐ], pul [pul]. Conjunctions and the relative: i [i], que [kɨ], se [s̺ɨ], ou [ow], mas [mɐs̺], nin [nĩ]. Clitic object pronouns: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [s̺ɨ], le [lɨ], lo [lu], la [lɐ], nos [nus̺], bos [bus̺], les [lɨs̺], los [lus̺]. Preverbal negation nun is [nũ]. These words carry stress: all verbs, including auxiliaries and the copula (ye [ˈjɛ], era [ˈɛ.ɾɐ], hai [ˈaj]); subject and strong pronouns (you [ˈjow], tu [ˈtu], el [ˈel], eilha [ˈej.ʎɐ], mi [ˈmi]); demonstratives; possessives used as pronouns; interrogatives; adverbs, including Ifanês aquina [ɐˈki.nɐ] and quina [ˈki.nɐ]; and numerals. When un/ua means 'one', and when nun stands alone ('no'), they carry stress: [ˈũ], [ˈnũ]. A clitic split off by a hyphen is unstressed: dá-le → [ˈda] [lɨ].

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, Estudos de Philologia Mirandesa vol. I (treatment of atonic words and the central vowel reduction), and Ferreira & Raposo 1999, Convenção Ortográfica (lists of articles, contractions and clitics).

## Pausal forms

Pausal forms do not apply to this lect: there are no case endings and no nunation. Every word takes the same form at the end of a sentence, at a comma and inside the phrase. Final unstressed vowels keep their reduced values before a pause: [ɐ], [ɨ], [u], and [ə̃] for final unstressed ⟨en em⟩. Examples: noite [ˈnoj.tɨ], ome [ˈɔ.mɨ]. Final vowels are not dropped or devoiced, and final ⟨s⟩ stays [s̺].

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §§76-77, pp. 238-239 (final unstressed vowels, including ⟨en em⟩ → [ə̃]). No published description of Mirandese reports a distinct pausal form.

## Connected speech

Write each word in its citation form. Mark no assimilation, voicing of final ⟨s⟩, vowel elision or resyllabification across a word boundary: ls homes → [ls̺] [ˈɔ.mɨs̺], not [lz̺]. Write only elision that the spelling marks with an apostrophe. The fragment before the apostrophe is its own word and keeps only its consonant: d'el → [d] [ˈel], j'era → [ʒ] [ˈɛ.ɾɐ]. Inside a word, the vowel before a nasal plus stop is nasalised, and the nasal consonant is kept with its place assimilated to the stop: campo [ˈkɐ̃m.pu], cuntar [kũnˈtaɾ], branco [ˈbɾɐ̃ŋ.ku]. Write /b d ɡ/ as plain stops in every position; mark no spirantisation in this broad register.

Source: Nasal before a stop: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, pp. 187-188. Word-boundary sandhi is left unwritten by convention of this broad register; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 is cited only for spirantisation and place assimilation as shared Ibero-Romance phonetic processes, which are not written at the phonological level.

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic high vowels as [j] and [w], never as i̯ or u̯. This holds for on-glides and off-glides alike. Write nasal off-glides as [j̃] and [w̃]. The Leonese diphthong ⟨ie/iê⟩ is [jɪ]: tierra [ˈtjɪ.rɐ], cielo [ˈsjɪ.lu]. This follows the human-gold calibration of the spec. The Convenção writes it [je]. ⟨uo/uô⟩ is [wo], as in the Raiano gold sample fuogo [ˈfwo.ʊ] and the [wo] of the Convenção: puorta [ˈpwoɾ.tɐ]. The spec note gives a bare [ʊ] for this value; I follow the gold sample and the published [wo] instead. Write the falling diphthongs with off-glides: ⟨ai⟩ [aj], ⟨ei⟩ [ej], ⟨oi⟩ [oj], ⟨ou⟩ [ow], ⟨au⟩ [aw], ⟨eu⟩ [ew], as in pai [ˈpaj], cousa [ˈkow.z̺ɐ], muito [ˈmuj.tu]. ⟨ye⟩ is [jɛ]: ye [ˈjɛ], yerba [ˈjɛɾ.bɐ]. A glide belongs to its syllable; the stress mark goes before it, as in [ˈtjɪ.rɐ].

Source: Ferreira & Raposo 1999, Convenção Ortográfica, p. 62 ([je]/[wo] for Central and Raiano), and Leite de Vasconcellos 1901, vol. II, pp. 28-31 (S. Martinho tierra, cielo as in the centre). The [jɪ] value follows the spec's Central gold calibration.

## Other choices

### Ifanes monophthongisation of [je]

Do not reduce ⟨ie/iê⟩ to [e] in Ifanês. Keep [jɪ] everywhere, including the final syllable of mie, tenie and die (tenie [tɨˈnjɪ]).

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1901, vol. II, pp. 28-31: raiano speech 'não offerece differenças phoneticas em relação á do centro'. This is followed against Ferreira & Raposo 1999, fn. 13 (PDF p. 18) and fn. 19 (PDF p. 20), which report [e] at Ifanes.

### Sibilants

Keep three series apart. ⟨s ss⟩ is apical: [s̺] initially, after a consonant and word-finally, and [z̺] between vowels (sal [ˈs̺al], passo [ˈpa.s̺u], casa [ˈka.z̺ɐ]). ⟨ç⟩ and ⟨c⟩ before e/i are laminal, written plain [s] (cinco [ˈsĩŋ.ku]). ⟨z⟩ is plain [z] (fazer [fɐˈzeɾ]). ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ], ⟨j⟩ and ⟨g⟩ before e/i are [ʒ], and ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ] (chober [tʃuˈbeɾ]).

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I (central sibilant system), and the spec note on the apical series.

### Laterals, nasals, rhotics, labials

⟨lh⟩ is [ʎ], including word-initially (lhobo [ˈʎo.bu]). ⟨nh⟩ is [ɲ]. ⟨l⟩ is [l]. ⟨r⟩ between vowels and in clusters is a tap [ɾ]. ⟨rr⟩ and word-initial ⟨r⟩ are a trill [r]. ⟨b⟩ and ⟨v⟩ are both [b], since there is no /v/.

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I. Ferreira & Raposo 1999, Convenção Ortográfica.

### Unstressed nasal ⟨en em⟩

Unstressed ⟨en em⟩ is [ɨ̃] inside the word and [ə̃] word-finally: home [ˈɔ.mɨ] vs. órfen-type finals [ə̃]; antender [ɐ̃n.tɨ̃nˈdeɾ]. These are the central values, not the Sendinês ones.

Source: Leite de Vasconcellos 1900, vol. I, §§76-77, pp. 238-239.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
