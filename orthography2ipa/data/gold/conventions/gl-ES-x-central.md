# Transcription conventions: Central Galician (gl-ES-x-central)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Galician stress is lexical and can fall on any of the last three syllables. Take its place from RAG orthography. A word with a written accent is stressed on the accented vowel. Otherwise, a word ending in a vowel, in -n or in -s is stressed on the penult, and a word ending in any other consonant (including -ns) is stressed on the final syllable. Write ˈ immediately before the onset of the stressed syllable, using maximal onsets that are legal in Galician (pl, pɾ, bɾ, kɾ, tɾ, kl, fɾ…). Write ˈ on every lexically stressed word, monosyllables included. Never write ˈ on the unstressed function words listed under function_words. Mark only primary stress: no ˌ, no syllable dots. Adverbs in -mente get one ˈ, on -men-. A stressed mid vowel takes its lexical open or closed quality from DPLG; the orthography does not show it. Examples: casa [ˈkasa], cantar [kanˈtaɾ], fábrica [ˈfaβɾika], cidade [θiˈðaðe], pé [ˈpɛ], ver [ˈbeɾ], sol [ˈsɔl], porta [ˈpɔɾta], rapidamente [rapiðaˈmente]. Long vowels, geminates and case endings do not apply: Galician has no phonemic length, no geminates and no case endings. Orthographic cc is a cluster: acción [akˈθjoŋ].

Source: Regueira, X.L. (1996), 'Galician', Journal of the IPA 26(2):119–122 (stress and vowels); Regueira (coord.) (2010), Dicionario de pronuncia da lingua galega (DPLG), ILG, for lexical vowel quality; Freixeiro Mato (1998), Gramática da Lingua Galega I, chapter on accent.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following are unstressed (no ˈ) and take reduced, closed vowel quality. Definite articles: o [o], a [a], os [os], as [as]. Contractions: do [do], da [da], no [no], na [na], co [ko], coa [koa], polo [polo], pola [pola], ao/ó [ɔ], á [a]. Simple prepositions: a, de [de], en [eŋ], con [koŋ], por [poɾ], para [paɾa], sen [seŋ], ata [ata], desde [dezde], contra [kontɾa], entre [entɾe], sobre [soβɾe]. Conjunctions and complementizers: e [e], ou [ou̯], nin [niŋ], que [ke], se [se], pero [peɾo], mais [mai̯s], porque [poɾke]. Relative que, cando and onde, and como as a conjunction. Atonic clitic pronouns: me, te, se, lle [ʎe], lles [ʎes], che [tʃe], nos, vos, o, a, os, as, lo/la/no/na, and their combinations mo, cho [tʃo], llo [ʎo]. A clitic split off by a hyphen is still unstressed. The following are stressed (ˈ): all verbs, including auxiliaries and copulas: ser, estar, haber, ter, ir; é [ˈɛ], foi [ˈfoi̯], ten [ˈteŋ], hai [ˈai̯]. Also stressed: indefinite articles un [ˈuŋ] and unha [ˈuŋa]; subject and prepositional pronouns eu [ˈeu̯], ti [ˈti], el [ˈel], ela [ˈɛla], min [ˈmiŋ]; possessives meu [ˈmeu̯] and súa [ˈsua]; demonstratives este [ˈeste] and ese [ˈese]; interrogatives que, quen, cando, onde, como in questions; negation non [ˈnoŋ]; and adverbs. Example: non llo dixen [ˈnoŋ ʎo ˈðiʃeŋ].

Source: Freixeiro Mato (1998), Gramática da Lingua Galega I–II (chapters on article, pronoun and clitic forms); Regueira (2010) DPLG for atonic forms. The choice of [ɔ] for ao/ó follows Freixeiro Mato (1998) on the crasis a + o.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable. Galician has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so a word has the same form at a comma or at sentence end as inside the sentence. The one thing a pause conditions is the choice between stop and approximant after it; see connected_speech.

Source: Regueira (1996), 'Galician', JIPA 26(2); Freixeiro Mato (1998) vol. I.

## Connected speech

Write one process across word boundaries: the stop/approximant choice for /b d g/. These are [b d g] at the start of a sentence, after a comma or other punctuation pause, and after a nasal; /d/ is also [d] after /l/. Everywhere else they are [β ð ɣ]: after vowels, glides and /ɾ r s θ ʃ l/. The rule applies inside words and across word boundaries, and the condition is the final segment of the preceding word. Examples: o gato [o ˈɣato], un gato [ˈuŋ ˈgato], de Lugo [de ˈluɣo], a vaca [a ˈβaka], casa de pedra [ˈkasa ðe ˈpɛðɾa], el dixo [ˈel ˈdiʃo]. The spec note mentions only /g/ → [ɣ]. The published description treats /b d g/ alike, so all three are written. Everything else is left in citation form. Do not write synaloepha or vowel elision: write both vowels, and do not turn a final vowel into a glide (de outro [de ˈoutɾo]). Do not resyllabify across words and do not write links. A word-final nasal stays [ŋ] before a vowel and before any consonant: un home [ˈuŋ ˈɔme], en Lugo [eŋ ˈluɣo]. Do not write voicing of /s/ or /θ/ before voiced consonants: os nenos [os ˈnenos]. Each word keeps its own IPA form, in its own place.

Source: Regueira (1996), 'Galician', JIPA 26(2):119–120 (consonants: approximant allophones of /b d g/, velar nasal); Freixeiro Mato (1998) vol. I, fonética sintáctica.

## Glides and diphthongs

A prenuclear glide is written [j w]: cuarto [ˈkwaɾto], piano [ˈpjano], acción [akˈθjoŋ], igual [iˈɣwal]. A postnuclear glide (second element of a falling diphthong) is written [i̯ u̯]: pai [ˈpai̯], meu [ˈmeu̯], auga [ˈau̯ɣa], moito [ˈmoi̯to], ou [ou̯]. Where a written accent shows a hiatus, write two full vowels and no glide: día [ˈdia], saía [saˈia], país [paˈis]. Unaccented sequences of i or u with another vowel inside one word are written as diphthongs. Never create a glide across a word boundary.

Source: Freixeiro Mato (1998) vol. I, ditongos e hiatos; Regueira (2010) DPLG, which separates crescentes from decrecentes; RAG Normas ortográficas (2003) for hiatus marking.

## Other choices

### unstressed vowels

Use seven vowels /i e ɛ a ɔ o u/ only in stressed syllables. In unstressed syllables, pretonic and posttonic, the mid vowels are written [e o]. Final unstressed vowels are written [e o a], not narrow [ɪ ʊ ɐ]. Examples: pedriña [peˈðɾiɲa], porto [ˈpɔɾto], noite [ˈnoi̯te].

Source: Regueira (1996), JIPA 26(2):120; Freixeiro Mato (1998) vol. I; Fernández Rei (1990) on the central area keeping full final vowels.

### nasals

Word-final n and the n of nh are the velar [ŋ]: pan [ˈpaŋ], unha [ˈuŋa], algunha [alˈɣuŋa]. A nasal before a consonant inside a word takes the place of that consonant: campo [ˈkampo], canto [ˈkanto], cinco [ˈθiŋko]. ñ is [ɲ]: viño [ˈbiɲo].

Source: Regueira (1996), JIPA 26(2):119; Freixeiro Mato (1998) vol. I.

### consonant symbols

Use these symbols. z and c before e/i: [θ]. x: [ʃ], but [ks] in learned words (taxi [ˈtaksi]). ch: [tʃ]. ll: [ʎ]. Initial r, rr, and r after n/l/s: [r]; other r: [ɾ]. v and b: one phoneme [b ~ β], following the rule under connected_speech. g and gu before e/i: [g ~ ɣ]; there is no gheada, so never write [ħ x h]. s: [s]. h is silent. There is no seseo and no yeísmo: [θ] stays distinct from [s], and [ʎ] stays distinct from [j] and [ʝ].

Source: Spec notes; Regueira (1996), JIPA 26(2); Fernández Rei (1990) for the central dialect bloc.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
