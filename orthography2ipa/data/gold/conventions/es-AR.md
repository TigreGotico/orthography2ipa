# Transcription conventions: Rioplatense Spanish (es-AR)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and follows the spelling. A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel. Without one, a word ending in a vowel, ⟨n⟩ or ⟨s⟩ is stressed on the penult, and any other word on the final syllable. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable on every stressed word, monosyllables included: casa [ˈkasa], ciudad [sjuˈdad], árbol [ˈaɾbol], sol [ˈsol]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Rioplatense voseo verb forms keep their own final or penultimate stress: vos tenés [ˈbos teˈnes], tomá [toˈma], decime [deˈsime], sentate [senˈtate]. Enclitics written solid with the verb do not move its stress: dámelo [ˈdamelo]. Adverbs in -mente carry two stresses: write ˈ on -men- and ˌ on the stressed syllable of the adjective, as in rápidamente [ˌrapidaˈmente]. Stress is never written on more than one syllable per word except in -mente. Spanish has no phonemic long vowels, geminates or case endings, so those parts of the topic do not apply.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on word stress (lexical stress, spelling correspondence, two stresses in -mente adverbs); voseo forms after Fontanella de Weinberg 1992, section on voseo in the Río de la Plata.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These are unstressed and carry no ˈ: definite articles el la los las lo; prepositions a ante bajo con contra de desde en entre hacia hasta para por sin sobre tras; conjunctions y e o u ni pero sino que si como (conjunction) cuando (conjunction) porque aunque pues; relatives que quien donde cuando cual (without accent); prenominal possessives mi mis tu tus su sus; object and reflexive clitics me te se nos lo la los las le les, whether free or attached; the titles don doña san. Examples: el libro [el ˈlibɾo], de la casa [de la ˈkasa], me lo dijo [me lo ˈdixo], mi perro [mi ˈpero]. These are stressed and take ˈ: all verb forms, including auxiliary haber, ser and estar; subject and prepositional pronouns; numerals; demonstratives; nuestro and vuestro; the indefinite articles un una unos unas; según; and interrogatives and exclamatives with an accent. Examples: ha venido [ˈa beˈnido], un día [ˈun ˈdia], yo [ˈʃo], vos [ˈbos], usted [usˈted], ¿qué querés? [ˈke keˈɾes], para mí [paɾa ˈmi]. A clitic split off by a hyphen is still unstressed.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on stress (inventory of stressed and unstressed words), which follows Navarro Tomás and Quilis in the standard classification.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply. Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation (tanwīn). Words have the same form at a comma and at the end of a sentence as anywhere else, and final vowels are never dropped or changed: casa [ˈkasa] is the same in every position.

Source: Hualde 2005 (no pausal alternations in the description of Spanish segmental phonology).

## Connected speech

Cross-word processes are not written. Each word is transcribed in its citation form in its own place, separated by single spaces. Synalepha and vowel fusion are not written: la amiga [la aˈmiɡa], de esto [de ˈesto], lo hizo [lo ˈiso]. Resyllabification of final consonants is not written, and no syllable dots are used: los otros [los ˈotɾos]. Nasal place assimilation is not written within or across words, so ⟨n⟩ is always [n]: un poco [ˈun ˈpoko], invierno [inˈbjeɾno]. Spirantization of /b d ɡ/ is not written: la boca [la ˈboka], cada [ˈkada], lago [ˈlaɡo]. Voicing of /s/ before a voiced consonant is not written: mismo [ˈmismo]. Word-final ⟨r⟩ before a vowel stays a tap: por eso [poɾ ˈeso].

Source: Hualde 2005, chapters on synalepha, the allophones of /b d ɡ/ and nasal assimilation, which describe these as predictable allophonic processes. A broad transcription leaves them out.

## Glides and diphthongs

An unstressed ⟨i, u, y⟩ next to another vowel in the same word forms a diphthong. Before the nucleus write [j w]: bien [ˈbjen], bueno [ˈbweno], cuidado [kwiˈdado], ciudad [sjuˈdad]. After the nucleus write [i̯ u̯]: baile [ˈbai̯le], hoy [ˈoi̯], muy [ˈmui̯], rey [ˈrei̯], hay [ˈai̯], auto [ˈau̯to]. Stressed í or ú next to another vowel is in hiatus and is written as a full vowel: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis], río [ˈrio]. Sequences of a, e and o stay in hiatus: teatro [teˈatɾo], poeta [poˈeta]. Word-initial ⟨hie-, hia-, hio-⟩ keeps the glide [j] and does not merge with ⟨ll, y⟩: hielo [ˈjelo], hierba [ˈjeɾba]. Word-initial ⟨hue-, hui-⟩ is [w]: huevo [ˈwebo]. ⟨y⟩ before a vowel is the consonant [ʃ], not a glide (see other). The conjunction y is [i] everywhere.

Source: Hualde 2005, chapter on diphthongs and hiatus ([j w] prenuclear, [i̯ u̯] postnuclear); the hielo [ˈjelo] treatment follows the specification note citing Coloma 2018, p. 244.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
