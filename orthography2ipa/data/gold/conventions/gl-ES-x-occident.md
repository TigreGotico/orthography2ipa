# Transcription conventions: Western Galician (gl-ES-x-occident)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress only, with ˈ placed before the onset of the stressed syllable. Do not mark secondary stress or syllable dots. Onsets are maximal, so the mark goes before a muta cum liquida cluster: 'libro' [ˈliβɾo], 'pedra' [ˈpɛðɾa]. Stress is lexical but follows the spelling. A written acute accent marks the stressed vowel: 'café' [kaˈfɛ], 'árbore' [ˈaɾβoɾe]. Without an accent, a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the penult: 'casa' [ˈkasa], 'galego' [haˈleho], 'cantan' [ˈkantaŋ]. Any other ending takes final stress: 'cantar' [kanˈtaɾ], 'papel' [paˈpɛl]. A diacritic-free monosyllable that is a stressed word gets ˈ: 'pan' [ˈpaŋ], 'mar' [ˈmaɾ]. Unstressed words (see function_words) get no mark. Enclitics written solid with the verb do not move the verb's stress: 'dígocho' [ˈdihotʃo]. Galician has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings; ⟨rr⟩ is the single phoneme /r/ and ⟨ll⟩ is /ʎ/. The seven-vowel contrast /a ɛ e i ɔ o u/ holds only in stressed syllables. In unstressed syllables write only [a e i o u], so final unstressed vowels are [e o a] and are not raised: 'leite' [ˈlejte], 'gato' [ˈhato]. The open or close quality of a stressed e/o is taken word by word from Regueira's dictionary. This corrects the specification's 'galego' [haˈlɛho] to [haˈleho], since the stressed e is close. Before a tautosyllabic nasal write only [o], never [ɔ]: 'bon' [ˈboŋ], 'conta' [ˈkonta].

Source: Freixeiro Mato 1998 (Gramática da Lingua Galega, vol. I, Fonética e fonoloxía: acento) for the stress rule; Regueira 2010 (Dicionario de pronuncia, introduction and entries) for stressed mid-vowel quality and for the absence of [ɔ] before a nasal.

## Stressed and unstressed words

These words are unstressed and get no ˈ. Their mid vowels are written close [e o]. Definite articles and their contractions: o a os as, ao á aos ás, do da dos das, no na nos nas, polo pola polos polas, co coa cos coas: 'o can' [o ˈkaŋ], 'da casa' [ða ˈkasa]. Simple prepositions: a de en con por para sen ata desde cara. Conjunctions: e ou nin que se mais pero porque como (when it is the conjunction). Relatives que quen onde cando when they are not interrogative. Atonic object, reflexive and dative clitics: me te che o a lo la no na lle nos vos os as lles se. This holds whether the clitic stands before the verb or after a hyphen: 'non o sei' [ˈnoŋ o ˈsej]. These words are stressed and get ˈ: indefinite articles un unha uns unhas ('un home' [ˈuŋ ˈɔme], 'unha' [ˈuŋa]); tonic pronouns eu ti vostede el ela nós vós eles elas mi min; possessives, including before a noun ('o meu pai' [o ˈmew ˈpaj]); demonstratives; interrogative and exclamative words ('que?' [ˈke], 'quen?' [ˈkeŋ]); the negation non [ˈnoŋ]; all verb forms, including the auxiliaries ser, estar, haber, ter and ir ('é' [ˈɛ], 'hai' [ˈaj], 'está' [esˈta]); and all adverbs.

Source: Freixeiro Mato 1998 (vol. I, átonos e tónicos; vol. II, artigo, pronomes persoais); Regueira 2010 (introduction, unstressed words), which treats the indefinite article and possessives as tonic.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply as a pausal-form system: Galician has no case endings and no nunation (tanwīn), and final short vowels are never dropped or changed at a pause. Only two boundary effects apply. A pause is the start of the text or anything after . , ; : ? ! or a dash. First, a word right after a pause starts with the stops [b d], not [β ð]: 'Bo día.' [ˈbo ˈðia], 'Dime, bebe.' [ˈdime ˈbeβe]. Gheada /ɡ/ is [h] in every position, so it is never [ɡ] after a pause: 'Galicia' [haˈliθja]. Second, a word-final nasal before a pause is velar [ŋ]: 'ten.' [ˈteŋ].

Source: Regueira 2010 (introduction: stops after a pause, velar word-final nasal); Fernández Rei 1990 (gheada).

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its own place and is never fused, deleted or respelled for vowel sandhi. Do not write elision, vowel merger, liaison or resyllabification: 'de este' [de ˈeste], 'a auga' [a ˈawha]. Only two cross-boundary processes are written, both on the word whose segment is affected. (1) Lenition: /b d/ are stops [b d] after a pause or a nasal and approximants [β ð] everywhere else, including word-initially after a word ending in a vowel, liquid or other consonant. /d/ also lenites after /l/. Examples: 'a boca' [a ˈβoka], 'un barco' [ˈum ˈbaɾko], 'o dedo' [o ˈðeðo], 'mil días' [ˈmil ˈðias]. Gheada replaces /ɡ/ and its approximant [ɣ] with [h] in every position: initial, intervocalic, after a nasal or liquid, and in gr/gl clusters. Examples: 'o gato' [o ˈhato], 'un gato' [ˈuŋ ˈhato], 'domingo' [doˈmiŋho], 'grande' [ˈhɾande]. (2) Nasal place assimilation: a coda nasal takes the place of the following consonant, within a word and across a word boundary: 'campo' [ˈkampo], 'un peixe' [ˈum ˈpejʃe], 'con fame' [koɱ ˈfame], 'en Vigo' [em ˈbiho]. A word-final nasal before a vowel or a pause is [ŋ]: 'un home' [ˈuŋ ˈɔme]. Do not write /s/ voicing before voiced consonants.

Source: Regueira 2010 (introduction (a) lenition of /b d ɡ/, (b) nasal place assimilation and velar final nasal); Fernández Rei 1990 (gheada, scope in western dialects).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the non-syllabic parts of diphthongs as [j w], never i̯ u̯. Falling diphthongs: 'pai' [ˈpaj], 'rei' [ˈrej], 'boi' [ˈboj], 'muito' [ˈmujto], 'meu' [ˈmew], 'ouro' [ˈowɾo], 'auga' [ˈawha]. Rising diphthongs: 'piano' [ˈpjano], 'cuarto' [ˈkwaɾto], 'Galicia' [haˈliθja], 'igual' [iˈhwal]. The u of ⟨gu qu⟩ before e/i is silent: 'que' [ke], 'guerra' [ˈhɛra]. A written accent on i/u marks hiatus, with both vowels full: 'día' [ˈðia] after a vowel, 'país' [paˈis]. ⟨nh⟩ is the velar nasal [ŋ], not a glide: 'unha' [ˈuŋa]. Where unaccented i/u next to a vowel varies between hiatus and diphthong, follow the Regueira 2010 entry.

Source: Freixeiro Mato 1998 (vol. I, ditongos e hiatos); Regueira 2010 (dictionary entries).

## Other choices

### Gheada scope versus the specification note

The note says ⟨gu⟩ before e/i keeps /ɡ/. The published dialect descriptions treat gheada as phonological: it affects every /ɡ/ whatever the spelling. This document follows them, so 'guerra' [ˈhɛra], 'seguir' [seˈhiɾ]. Write the glottal [h] throughout, never [ħ] or [x].

Source: Fernández Rei 1990 (gheada); Dubert García 2006.

### Seseo versus distinción

Keep the standard contrast: ⟨z⟩ and ⟨c⟩ before e/i are [θ], ⟨s⟩ is [s]: 'cazar' [kaˈθaɾ], 'cinco' [ˈθiŋko]. Western coastal seseo is not written, because it covers only part of the area.

Source: Regueira 2010; Fernández Rei 1990 (seseo areas).

### Palatal lateral and other consonant symbols

⟨ll⟩ is written [ʎ], not the yeísta [ʝ]: 'cabalo' [kaˈβalo], 'muller' [muˈʎɛɾ]. ⟨x⟩ is [ʃ] ('xente' [ˈʃente]), ⟨ch⟩ is [tʃ], ⟨ñ⟩ is [ɲ]. ⟨rr⟩, word-initial r, and r after n, l or s are [r]; any other r is [ɾ]: 'terra' [ˈtɛra], 'honra' [ˈonra], 'caro' [ˈkaɾo]. ⟨h⟩ is silent; the only [h] comes from gheada.

Source: Regueira 2010 (introduction and entries); Freixeiro Mato 1998 (vol. I, consoantes).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
