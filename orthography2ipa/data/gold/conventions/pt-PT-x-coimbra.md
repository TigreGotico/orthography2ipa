# Transcription conventions: Conimbricense Portuguese (pt-PT-x-coimbra)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and is placed by the orthography. A written acute or circumflex marks the stressed syllable: café [kɐˈfɛ], lâmpada [ˈlɐ̃pɐdɐ], você [voˈse]. A tilde does not mark stress if another accent is present: órfã [ˈɔɾfɐ̃]. Without a written accent, stress is penultimate in words ending in -a, -e, -o (with or without -s), -am, -em or -ens: casa [ˈkazɐ], cantam [ˈkɐ̃tɐ̃w], homens [ˈɔmɐ̃jʃ]. It is final in words ending in -i, -u, -im, -um, -om, -l, -r, -z, -x, -ão, -ãe, -ões or an oral diphthong: comer [kuˈmeɾ], papel [pɐˈpɛɫ], feliz [fɨˈliʃ], irmão [iɾˈmɐ̃w], Peru [pɨˈɾu]. Write ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every stressed word, including monosyllabic content words: mar [ˈmaɾ], pé [ˈpɛ]. Never mark the unstressed function words listed under function_words. Do not mark secondary stress. Each hyphen-split part is its own word and keeps its own stress: guarda-chuva → [ˈɡwaɾdɐ] [ˈʃuvɐ]; dá-lo → [ˈda] [lu]. Vowel length is not phonemic and is never written. There are no geminates: rr is one [ʁ] and ss is one [s], as in carro [ˈkaʁu] and passo [ˈpasu]. Portuguese has no case endings, so that part does not apply.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, The Phonology of Portuguese, ch. 6 (word stress); Acordo Ortográfico accentuation rules.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following words are unstressed: they get no ˈ and their vowels are written reduced. Definite articles: o [u], a [ɐ], os [uʃ], as [ɐʃ]. Simple prepositions: a [ɐ], de [dɨ], em [ɐ̃j], por [puɾ], com [kõ], para [pɐɾɐ]. Preposition-article contractions: do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], ao [aw], à [a], pelo [pɨlu], pela [pɨlɐ], num [nũ]. Conjunctions: e [i], ou [o], que [kɨ], se [sɨ], mas [mɐʃ], nem [nɐ̃j], porque [puɾkɨ]. The relative que [kɨ]. Clitic pronouns, whether standing alone or split off by a hyphen: me [mɨ], te [tɨ], se [sɨ], lhe [ʎɨ], lhes [ʎɨʃ], nos [nuʃ], vos [vuʃ], o/a/os/as [u ɐ uʃ ɐʃ], lo/la [lu lɐ], no/na [nu nɐ]. The following words are stressed: tonic pronouns (eu [ˈew], tu [ˈtu], ele [ˈelɨ], ela [ˈɛlɐ], nós [ˈnɔʃ], mim [ˈmĩ], si [ˈsi]), demonstratives, possessives, numerals including um/uma [ˈũ] [ˈumɐ], não [ˈnɐ̃w], interrogatives (quê [ˈke], quem [ˈkɐ̃j]), and all verb forms including auxiliaries (é [ˈɛ], tem [ˈtɐ̃j], está [ɨʃˈta], foi [ˈfoj], há [ˈa]). Polysyllabic prepositions, conjunctions and adverbs keep their lexical stress: sobre [ˈsobɾɨ], entre [ˈẽtɾɨ], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], como [ˈkomu]. The only exceptions are para and pelo/pela/pelos/pelas, which are unstressed as listed above.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 6 (clitics and unstressed words) and ch. 2 (vowel reduction); Cunha & Cintra, Nova Gramática do Português Contemporâneo (1984), on átonos.

## Pausal forms

Pausal forms in the Arabic sense do not apply to this lect: it has no case endings and no nunation, and final vowels do not change before a pause. At the end of a sentence and before a comma, write the word in its isolation form. Final vowels stay as they are, and final unstressed [ɨ] and [u] are kept, not deleted: noite [ˈnojtɨ], livro [ˈlivɾu]. A final sibilant is [ʃ]: lápis [ˈlapiʃ]. Final r is [ɾ]: mar [ˈmaɾ]. Final l is [ɫ]: sal [ˈsaɫ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 2 and ch. 3 (coda sibilants, coda /l/, unstressed vowels).

## Connected speech

The only cross-word process written is the voicing and place of a word-final sibilant (s, z, x), decided by the first sound of the next word in the same phrase. Before a pause or a voiceless consonant, write [ʃ]: os pais [uʃ] [ˈpajʃ]. Before a voiced consonant, write [ʒ]: os dias [uʒ] [ˈdiɐʃ]. Before a vowel, write [z]: os olhos [uz] [ˈɔʎuʃ]; dez anos [ˈdɛz] [ˈɐnuʃ]. This [z] follows the published standard. It departs from the spec note, which models a Coimbra prevocalic [ʒ] ([uʒ] [ˈɔʎuʃ]); that [ʒ] is attested only by a popular native-speaker source, which itself calls it variable. A comma counts as a pause. No other sandhi is written. Vowel elision, vowel fusion and glide formation across words are not written, so each word keeps its full form: de uma [dɨ] [ˈumɐ], casa amarela [ˈkazɐ] [ɐmɐˈɾɛlɐ]. Final r stays [ɾ] and final l stays [ɫ] before a vowel: mar azul [ˈmaɾ] [ɐˈzuɫ]. Words are never joined.

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 (final /s/ in external sandhi: [ʃ]/[ʒ]/[z]); the Coimbra [ʒ] variant is from Portuguese With Leo 2023 (not followed).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write the non-syllabic part of a diphthong as [j] or [w], never as i̯ or u̯. Oral diphthongs: pai [ˈpaj], mau [ˈmaw], meu [ˈmew], céu [ˈsɛw], dói [ˈdɔj], foi [ˈfoj], fui [ˈfuj], lei [ˈlej]. Nasal diphthongs carry the tilde on the vowel only: mão [ˈmɐ̃w], mãe [ˈmɐ̃j], bem [ˈbɐ̃j], põe [ˈpõj], muito [ˈmũjtu]. qu and gu before a pronounced u are [kw] and [ɡw]: quatro [ˈkwatɾu], água [ˈaɡwɐ]. Inside a word, other unstressed i or u before a vowel stays a syllabic vowel: piano [piˈɐnu], suave [suˈavɨ], real [ʁiˈaɫ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000, ch. 3 (diphthongs and glides).

## Other choices

### <ei> diphthong

Write <ei> as [ej], not the Lisbon [ɐj]: fevereiro [fɨvɨˈɾejɾu], primeiro [pɾiˈmejɾu], seis [ˈsejʃ]. Nasal word-final -em/-ens stays [ɐ̃j], as in the standard: bem [ˈbɐ̃j], também [tɐ̃ˈbɐ̃j].

Source: Cintra 1971, Boletim de Filologia 22:81-116 (central conservative zone); Portuguese With Leo 2023; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 3 for nasal [ɐ̃j].

### Stressed e before a palatal

Keep the mid front vowel before [ʎ ɲ ʃ ʒ]; do not centralise it to [ɐ] as Lisbon does: coelho [kuˈeʎu], espelho [ɨʃˈpeʎu], vermelho [vɨɾˈmeʎu], telha [ˈteʎɐ], tenho [ˈteɲu], igreja [iˈɡɾeʒɐ], fecho [ˈfeʃu]. The note's [ˈkɔɛʎu] for coelho is not followed. Standard pronunciation has unstressed [u] in the first syllable and stress on the e.

Source: Portuguese With Leo 2023; Cintra 1971; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 2.

### <ou>

Write <ou> as the monophthong [o]: pouco [ˈpoku], roupa [ˈʁopɐ], ou [o].

Source: Portuguese With Leo 2023; Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 3.

### OI/OU doublets

Transcribe each doublet as it is spelled in the text: toiro [ˈtojɾu], touro [ˈtoɾu], loiça [ˈlojsɐ], louça [ˈlosɐ].

Source: Portuguese With Leo 2023 (Coimbra preference for oi, left to the lexicon).

### Unstressed vowels

Reduce unstressed vowels following the standard pattern. Unstressed a is [ɐ], unstressed e is [ɨ], and unstressed o is [u]. Never delete [ɨ] or [u]: pequeno [pɨˈkenu], telefone [tɨlɨˈfɔnɨ]. Keep unstressed written i as [i] and do not delete it: Filipa [fiˈlipɐ], piscina [piʃˈsinɐ], disciplina [diʃsiˈplinɐ]. Word-initial unstressed es- or ex- before a consonant is [ɨʃ]: estar [ɨʃˈtaɾ]. Other word-initial unstressed e is [i]: elefante [ilɨˈfɐ̃tɨ]. Stressed mid vowels are written as given in the lexicon: [e ɛ o ɔ].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 2; Portuguese With Leo 2023 (retention of /i/).

### Rhotics

Write strong r (word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s) as uvular [ʁ]: rato [ˈʁatu], carro [ˈkaʁu], honra [ˈõʁɐ]. Write r everywhere else, including in the coda, as [ɾ]: caro [ˈkaɾu], porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ]. Do not write the regional alveolar trill [r].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 1 and ch. 3 (uvular as the predominant urban standard).

### Laterals and coda sibilants

Write l as [ɫ] in the coda and [l] in the onset: sal [ˈsaɫ], alto [ˈaɫtu], lado [ˈladu]. A coda s inside a word is [ʃ] before voiceless consonants and [ʒ] before voiced ones: festa [ˈfɛʃtɐ], mesmo [ˈmeʒmu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 3.

### Nasals and stressed a

Write nasal vowels [ɐ̃ ẽ ĩ õ ũ] with no following nasal consonant: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], tempo [ˈtẽpu], som [ˈsõ]. Stressed a before a nasal consonant is [ɐ]: cama [ˈkɐmɐ], ano [ˈɐnu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 2 and ch. 3.

### Voiced stop lenition

Do not write the approximant allophones [β ð ɣ]. Always write [b d ɡ]: cada [ˈkadɐ], lago [ˈlaɡu].

Source: Mateus & d'Andrade 2000 ch. 1 (allophonic, omitted in broad transcription).

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
