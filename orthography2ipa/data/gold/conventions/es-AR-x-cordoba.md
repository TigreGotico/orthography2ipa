# Transcription conventions: Cordobés Spanish (es-AR-x-cordoba)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Stress is lexical and follows standard spelling (RAE accent rules). Words ending in a vowel, n or s are stressed on the penult unless they have a written accent; other words are stressed on the final syllable unless they have a written accent. Voseo verb forms follow their spelling: vos tenés [ˈbos teˈnes], cantá [kanˈta], sos [ˈsos]. Put ˈ before the onset of the stressed syllable in every stressed word, monosyllables included: casa [ˈkasa], ciudad [sjuˈðað], árbol [ˈaɾβol], sol [ˈsol]. Unstressed function words (see function_words) get no mark. Do not mark syllable boundaries. Adverbs in -mente take secondary ˌ on the adjective and primary ˈ on -mente: rápidamente [ˌrapiðaˈmente]. Clitic-hosting verbs keep their own stress and nothing more: dámelo [ˈdamelo]. Spanish has no contrastive long vowels, no geminates and no case endings. The Cordobés tonada lengthens the pretonic vowel phonetically, but that lengthening is not phonemic and is not written: the city is [la siuˈðað], not with ː.

Source: Quilis, A. (1993) Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, ch. on accent; Hualde, J. I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 12; RAE/ASALE (2010) Ortografía, §II.3. Tonada as pretonic lengthening: Lipski (1994) Latin American Spanish, chapter on Argentina; Fontanella de Weinberg (2000), 'El español de la Argentina', in Alvar (ed.) Manual de dialectología hispánica: El español de América.

## Stressed and unstressed words

Follow Quilis's list of unstressed (átonas) words. These are written with no stress mark: definite articles el la lo los las [el la lo los las]; all simple prepositions except según (a de en con por para sin sobre hasta desde entre hacia: de [de], para [paɾa]); the conjunctions y e o u ni que pero si porque como (comparative) cuando (non-interrogative) [i e o u ni ke peɾo si poɾke komo]; relatives que quien donde cual; object and reflexive clitics me te se lo la le nos les los las; prenominal possessives mi tu su mis tus sus; and the titles don doña. These are written with ˈ: indefinite articles un una unos unas [ˈun ˈuna]; subject and prepositional pronouns yo vos él ella usted nosotros mí ti [ˈʒo ˈbos ˈel]; interrogatives and exclamatives qué cómo dónde cuándo quién [ˈke ˈkomo]; demonstratives, numerals, según, postnominal and full possessives (nuestro, mío); negation no [ˈno]; and all verb forms, auxiliaries included (ha, he, está, fue: [ˈa], [ˈfwe]). Example: lo vi en la casa [lo ˈβi en la ˈkasa].

Source: Quilis, A. (1993) Tratado de fonología y fonética españolas, ch. on accent (palabras acentuadas y inacentuadas); Navarro Tomás, T. (1918/1977) Manual de pronunciación española, §§ on stressed and unstressed words.

## Pausal forms

Does not apply: Spanish has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation, so every word is written in the same form at a pause, at a comma and sentence-medially. A pause matters only as the trigger for stop allophones of /b d ɡ/ after it (see other). Final /s/ aspiration or loss before a pause is not written: más [ˈmas].

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish; no pausal morphology is described for Spanish in Quilis (1993).

## Connected speech

Each word is written in its own place. The only cross-word process written is the choice of allophone at the edge of the following word. (1) /b d ɡ/ at the start of a word are written as stops [b d ɡ] after a pause, at the start of the sentence, after a comma, and after a word ending in a nasal; /d/ is also a stop after a word ending in l. Everywhere else they are approximants [β ð ɣ]: la boca [la ˈβoka], con dos [kon ˈdos], el dedo [el ˈdeðo], las dos [las ˈðos], Dios [ˈdjos] at sentence start. (2) A word-initial r is always the trill [r]: la rosa [la ˈrosa]. A word-final r stays the tap [ɾ] before a vowel: ver a [ˈbeɾ a]. Not written: synalepha and vowel merger across words (de agua [de ˈaɣwa], no glide or elision); resyllabification (los otros [los ˈotɾos]); nasal place assimilation across words (un poco [ˈun ˈpoko], not [um]); and voicing of /s/ before voiced consonants (mismo [ˈmismo]).

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish, chs. 8–9 (spirantization, synalepha); Quilis (1993) on the distribution of /b d ɡ/ allophones; Navarro Tomás (1918) Manual de pronunciación española.

## Glides and diphthongs

Rising diphthongs are written with [j w]: bien [ˈbjen], bueno [ˈbweno], ciudad [sjuˈðað], cuatro [ˈkwatɾo]. Falling diphthongs are written with [i̯ u̯]: aire [ˈai̯ɾe], hoy [ˈoi̯], muy [ˈmui̯], rey [ˈrei̯], auto [ˈau̯to]. In ui/iu the second vowel is the nucleus and the first is a rising glide: muy is the exception written [ˈmui̯] as in its spelling with y; fui [ˈfwi], viuda [ˈbjuða]. Hiatus marked by a written accent keeps two full vowels: día [ˈdia], país [paˈis]. The conjunction y is [i]. The consonantal ll/y and initial hi+vowel are the fricative [ʒ], not a glide: hielo [ˈʒelo], calle [ˈkaʒe]. Initial hue- is [w]: huevo [ˈweβo]; an epenthetic [ɣ] is not written.

Source: Hualde, J. I. (2005) The Sounds of Spanish, ch. 10 (glides and diphthongs); Quilis (1993) on diphthongs and hiatus.

## Other choices

### ll/y (yeísmo with rehilamiento)

Write ll and consonantal y as voiced [ʒ]: calle [ˈkaʒe], yo [ˈʒo], lluvia [ˈʒuβja]. After n or l, also [ʒ]: cónyuge [ˈkonʒuxe]. Córdoba keeps the voiced variant; Buenos Aires-style devoiced [ʃ] is not written. This departs from the specification note's /ʝ/, because published descriptions of Argentine and Cordobés Spanish give [ʒ].

Source: Lipski (1994) Latin American Spanish, chapter on Argentina; Fontanella de Weinberg (2000), 'El español de la Argentina', in Alvar (ed.) Manual de dialectología hispánica.

### seseo and coda /s/

c before e/i, z and s are all [s]: cielo [ˈsjelo], zapato [saˈpato]. Coda /s/ is written [s] in every position. Its common aspiration before consonants ([h]) and its weakening at a pause are allophonic and are not written: los dos [los ˈðos], este [ˈeste].

Source: Lipski (1994), chapter on Argentina, on /s/ aspiration in Argentina; broad phonemic practice as in Hualde (2005).

### rhotics

Trill [r] for rr, for word-initial r, and for r after n, l or s: perro [ˈpero], honra [ˈonra]. Tap [ɾ] elsewhere, including codas: pero [ˈpeɾo], comer [koˈmeɾ]. Do not write the assibilated [ř] described for NW Argentina, since it is not the Córdoba-city standard.

Source: Hualde (2005) ch. 11; Lipski (1994), chapter on Argentina (assibilated r as a northwestern and Andean trait).

### other consonant symbols

j and g before e/i are velar [x]: gente [ˈxente]. ch is [tʃ]: chico [ˈtʃiko]. ñ is [ɲ]. Within a word, n before a velar is [ŋ]: tengo [ˈteŋɡo], ángel [ˈaŋxel]. Word-final and intervocalic d is written [ð] and is never deleted: verdad [beɾˈðað], cansado [kanˈsaðo]. Inside a word, /b d ɡ/ follow the same stop/approximant rule as in connected_speech: hombre [ˈombɾe], caldo [ˈkaldo], algo [ˈalɣo]. Both b and v are written with the same /b/ symbols.

Source: Quilis (1993); Hualde (2005) chs. 8 and 11; Fontanella de Weinberg (2000) on the Argentine velar [x].

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
