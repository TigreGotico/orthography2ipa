# Transcription conventions: Sulista/Gaúcho Portuguese (pt-BR-x-sul)

Every pass of `scripts/correct_silver_ipa.py` reads this sheet. Correct it before the run. A change to the text changes the cache key, so the rows of the lect run again.

## Stress

Mark primary stress with ˈ immediately before the onset of the stressed syllable in every word of two or more syllables, except the unstressed proclitics listed under function_words. Monosyllables carry no mark, and secondary stress is never marked. The stressed syllable is found as follows. (1) If the word has a written acute, circumflex or tilde, that syllable is stressed: café [kaˈfɛ], fácil [ˈfasiw], você [voˈse], irmão [iɾˈmɐ̃w̃]. If a word has a tilde and another accent, the other accent wins: órfão [ˈɔɾfɐ̃w̃]. (2) Otherwise, a word ending in a, e or o (with or without s), or in am, em or ens, is stressed on the penult: casa [ˈkazɐ], leite [ˈlejte], falam [ˈfalɐ̃w̃], homens [ˈɔmẽj̃s]. (3) All other words are stressed on the final syllable, including words ending in i, u, is, us, r, l, z, x, im, um, om or ão: falar [faˈlaɾ], Brasil [bɾaˈziw], aqui [aˈki], arroz [aˈrojs]. Syllabify with maximal onsets, so obstruent plus liquid stays together: outro [ˈotɾu], quatro [ˈkwatɾu]. A verb with a hyphenated clitic keeps its own stress: deixa-me [ˈdeʃɐ me]. Portuguese has no phonemic long vowels, no geminates and no case endings, so these do not apply. Orthographic rr and ss are single consonants: carro [ˈkaru], passo [ˈpasu].

Source: Standard Portuguese lexical stress as described in Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 (word stress), and in Silva (2002), the section on acento.

## Stressed and unstressed words

The following words are unstressed proclitics or enclitics and never take ˈ, even when they have two syllables. Definite articles: o [u], os [us], a [ɐ], as [ɐs]. Prepositions, and their contractions with articles: de [de], em [ẽj̃], com [kõ], sem [sẽj̃], por [poɾ], a [ɐ], para [paɾɐ], do [du], da [dɐ], no [nu], na [nɐ], ao [aw], pelo [pelu], pela [pelɐ], num [nũ]. Conjunctions: e [i], que [ke], se [se], mas [majs], nem [nẽj̃], ou [o]. Relative que [ke]. Object and reflexive clitics, whether attached by a hyphen or standing free: me [me], te [te], se [se], lhe [ʎe], lhes [ʎes], nos [nus], o [u], a [ɐ]. Everything else is stressed and takes ˈ when it has two or more syllables. This includes subject and stressed pronouns (ele [ˈele], você [voˈse], comigo [koˈmiɡu]), indefinite articles (uma [ˈumɐ]), demonstratives, possessives, adverbs, wh-words, polysyllabic conjunctions (porque [poɾˈke], quando [ˈkwɐ̃du], como [ˈkomu]), and all verbs, including auxiliaries (está [esˈta], tinha [ˈtʃiɲɐ] is wrong; write [ˈtiɲɐ], foi [foj]). Write clitic vowels with the lect's unraised final e ([de], [ke], [me]). Exceptions: conjunction e is [i], and o/os are [u]/[us]. Never write colloquial contractions that are not in the spelling: para stays [paɾɐ], not pra; está stays [esˈta], not tá.

Source: Clitic status of Portuguese articles, prepositions and pronouns follows Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 6 (stress and clitics). Unraised final /e/ in Gaúcho clitics follows Vieira (2002), 'As vogais médias postônicas', in Bisol & Brescancini (eds.), Fonologia e variação, EDIPUCRS.

## Pausal forms

Not applicable: Portuguese has no pausal forms, case endings or nunation. A word before a comma, before other punctuation or at the end of a sentence has its citation form. The only effect of a pause is that it blocks the cross-word processes under connected_speech. So final /S/ is [s] before a pause (casas. [ˈkazɐs]), final /R/ is [ɾ] (vou falar. [vo faˈlaɾ]), and final vowels are neither devoiced nor deleted ([ˈlejte], [ˈtudu]).

Source: Silva (2002), the chapters on syllable structure and coda consonants; no pausal phenomenon is described for Brazilian Portuguese in Mateus & d'Andrade (2000).

## Connected speech

Each word keeps its own segments in its own place. Only the final consonant of a word changes, according to the first sound of the next word in the same phrase; there is no change across punctuation. (1) Word-final /S/ is [z] before a vowel or a voiced consonant, and [s] before a voiceless consonant or a pause: os amigos [uz aˈmiɡus], as casas [ɐs ˈkazɐs], mais doce [majz ˈdose], dez anos [dɛjz ˈɐ̃nus]. (2) Word-final /R/ stays the tap [ɾ] before a vowel; resyllabification is not written: mar azul [maɾ aˈzuw]. (3) Word-final /l/ stays [w] before a vowel: mal assado [maw aˈsadu]. (4) Vowel sandhi is not written. There is no elision, degemination or crasis merger across words, and each word keeps its vowel: de água [de ˈaɡwɐ], casa amarela [ˈkazɐ amaˈɾɛlɐ], o homem [u ˈɔmẽj̃]. (5) No nasal place assimilation and no liaison consonants are written across words. Inside a word, /S/ voicing applies the same way: mesmo [ˈmezmu], rasgar [razˈɡaɾ].

Source: Coda /S/ voicing and sandhi in Brazilian Portuguese follow Silva (2002), the sections on the archiphoneme /S/ and sândi, and Callou & Leite (2001). Alveolar (non-palatal) /S/ in Rio Grande do Sul follows the note and the ALiB coda-/S/ maps, Cardoso et al. (2014).

## Glides and diphthongs

Write non-syllabic vowels as [j] and [w], and nasal glides as [j̃] and [w̃]. Never use i̯ or u̯. Falling diphthongs: pai [paj], lei [lej], céu [sɛw], viu [viw], mãe [mɐ̃j̃], não [nɐ̃w̃], põe [põj̃], também [tɐ̃ˈbẽj̃], muito [ˈmũjtu]. Rising sequences after a consonant are also written with a glide: história [isˈtɔɾjɐ], água [ˈaɡwɐ], quatro [ˈkwatɾu], série [ˈsɛɾje]. An accented i or u marks a hiatus and is written as a full vowel: saúde [saˈude], país [paˈis]. Monophthongization is written in fixed contexts. ou always becomes [o]: outro [ˈotɾu], pouco [ˈpoku], vou [vo]. ei becomes [e] before [ɾ ʒ ʃ]: primeiro [pɾiˈmeɾu], beijo [ˈbeʒu], deixa [ˈdeʃɐ]. ai becomes [a] before [ʃ]: caixa [ˈkaʃɐ]. All other diphthongs are kept: leite [ˈlejte], noite [ˈnojte].

Source: Glide analysis and monophthongization follow Bisol (1989), 'O ditongo na perspectiva da fonologia atual', DELTA 5, and Silva (2002), the section on ditongos.

## Other choices

### /t d/ before [i]

/t/ and /d/ stay dental stops [t d] before [i], as the specification's conservative default requires: tia [ˈtiɐ], dia [ˈdiɐ], tipo [ˈtipu], cidade [siˈdade], pode [ˈpɔde]. Never write [tʃ dʒ]. This departs from published descriptions: Battisti et al. (2007) and Bisol report advancing palatalization in Porto Alegre, especially among younger speakers. That palatalization is not written here.

Source: The specification note, consistent with the conservative Gaúcho norm. Variation is documented in Battisti, Dornelles Filho et al. (2007), 'Palatalização das oclusivas alveolares e a rede social dos informantes', ReVEL, and in the ALiB palatalization maps, Cardoso et al. (2014).

### Unstressed final vowels

Word-final unstressed e is [e]: leite quente [ˈlejte ˈkẽte], noite [ˈnojte], pontes [ˈpõtes]. Word-final unstressed o is raised to [u]: tudo [ˈtudu], livros [ˈlivɾus]. Word-final unstressed a is [ɐ]: casa [ˈkazɐ]. The specification note says final /e/ only 'often' stays [e]; this convention applies [e] in every row. Raising of final /o/ follows the published finding that it is near-categorical in Rio Grande do Sul.

Source: Vieira (2002), 'As vogais médias postônicas', in Bisol & Brescancini (eds.), Fonologia e variação: recortes do português brasileiro, EDIPUCRS.

### Rhotics

Strong R is written as the alveolar trill [r]. This covers word-initial r, rr, and r after n, l or s: rua [ˈruɐ], carro [ˈkaru], honra [ˈõrɐ], Israel [izraˈɛw]. Everywhere else, write the tap [ɾ]: between vowels, in clusters, in syllable codas and word-finally, including infinitives: caro [ˈkaɾu], prato [ˈpɾatu], porta [ˈpɔɾtɐ], falar [faˈlaɾ]. Never write [x h χ] and never delete final r. Monaretto (1997) also reports velar or glottal strong R and infinitive r-deletion among Porto Alegre speakers; these are not written.

Source: Monaretto (1997), Um reestudo da vibrante: análise variacionista e fonológica (PhD thesis, PUCRS), and the specification note.

### Coda /l/

Coda l is vocalized to [w], both inside and at the end of a word: sol [sɔw], alto [ˈawtu], Brasil [bɾaˈziw]. Velarized [ɫ], which survives in border and German-settled areas, is not written.

Source: Quednau (1993), A lateral pós-vocálica no português gaúcho (MA thesis, UFRGS).

### Diphthongization before /S/

Insert [j] between a stressed word-final a, e, ɛ, o or ɔ and a final /S/: faz [fajs], mas [majs], três [tɾejs], dez [dɛjs], nós [nɔjs], arroz [aˈrojs]. Do not insert it after i or u (luz [lus], quis [kis]), in unstressed syllables (lápis [ˈlapis]), or inside a word (mesmo [ˈmezmu]).

Source: Bisol (1994, 2012), based on Porto Alegre speech, as cited in Silva (2014:6-7), ALFAL XVII #4817-4818.

### Pretonic mid vowels

Pretonic e and o are written close, as [e o], never open [ɛ ɔ]. Variable vowel harmony is not applied: pequeno [peˈkenu], menino [meˈninu], coruja [koˈruʒɐ], colégio [koˈlɛʒju]. Stressed mid vowels follow the spelling and the standard lexicon: é [ɛ], ê [e], ó [ɔ], ô [o]. Exception: pretonic e at the start of a word before s plus a consonant is [i]: estar [isˈtaɾ].

Source: Nascentes (1953:25), falares do Sul with closed pretonics. Harmony variation is described in Bisol (1981), Harmonização vocálica (PhD thesis, UFRJ), and is not written here.

### Nasality and palatals

A vowel before a nasal coda is written as a nasal vowel with no nasal consonant: campo [ˈkɐ̃pu], tempo [ˈtẽpu], fim [fĩ], som [sõ]. Stressed a before m, n or nh is [ɐ̃]: cama [ˈkɐ̃mɐ], ano [ˈɐ̃nu]. Other vowels before a nasal onset are written oral: tema [ˈtemɐ]. nh is written [ɲ] and lh [ʎ]: vinho [ˈviɲu], filho [ˈfiʎu]. ch and x are [ʃ], and j and g before e or i are [ʒ].

Source: Silva (2002), the sections on vogais nasais and consoantes palatais; Mateus & d'Andrade (2000), ch. 2.

### Consonant clusters

No epenthetic vowel is written inside heterosyllabic or word-initial consonant clusters: pneu [pnew], advogado [advoˈɡadu], ritmo [ˈritmu].

Source: Collischonn (2002), on epenthesis in Brazilian Portuguese, in Bisol & Brescancini (eds.), Fonologia e variação. The epenthesis described there is variable and is not written here.

## Word segmentation

The engine splits a text into words at spaces, at hyphens and at punctuation. An apostrophe splits words, as punctuation does. A clitic joined to its host by a hyphen is a word of its own. Each pass gets the words of its text in this split, and writes one IPA word for each word, in the same order, separated by single spaces. A process across a word boundary changes the words on each side of the boundary; it does not join them.
